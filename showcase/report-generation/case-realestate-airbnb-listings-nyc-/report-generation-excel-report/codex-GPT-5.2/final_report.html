Key Takeaways

Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

  1. Pricing leaders (where available): The highest overall median nightly price is in Bangkok ($1,400). The lowest is Rome ($131). (Paris and Sydney are NA due to 100% missing price.)
  2. Professionalization is highest in Bangkok (60.4% of listings hosted by 5+ listing hosts), followed by Barcelona (58.3%). This suggests stronger multi-unit competition and potentially higher regulatory sensitivity in those markets.
  3. Commercial whole-unit intensity: Paris has the highest “Entire home/apt” share (88.2%). Markets with high whole-unit shares typically face greater policy scrutiny (housing supply impacts) and can offer more standardized product for operators.
  4. Demand proxy (reviews): Rome leads on average reviews/month (1.77), indicating comparatively stronger booking velocity. Pair this with review-recency: cities with higher 90-day recency tend to show more active, currently-booking supply.
  5. Availability signals differ sharply: Bangkok has the highest mean availability (253 days/year), which can reflect either commercial openness or softer demand requiring more open calendars. Use in conjunction with review metrics before inferring revenue potential.

Strategic caution: Because New York, Tokyo, Los Angeles are absent from data.csv and pricing is missing for Paris and Sydney, cross-city conclusions about “global top 10” pricing cannot be fully benchmarked from this file alone.
