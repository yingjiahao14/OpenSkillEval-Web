Supply Structure & Host Concentration

Professional host concentration is calculated at the listing level using the exact threshold in the brief: calculated_host_listings_count >= 5. This metric indicates the share of supply controlled by multi-listing operators rather than casual hosts.

Highest concentration: Bangkok at 60.4% of listings.
Lowest concentration: Amsterdam at 6.8% of listings.

Commercialization is strongest where professional host share and entire-home share are both elevated. These markets are more likely to exhibit portfolio management, standardized operations, and regulatory scrutiny over housing conversion.

Guest Demand & Review Activity

Review metrics are supporting demand proxies. Average reviews per month approximates booking velocity, last-twelve-month reviews capture recent engagement volume, and review recency indicates whether listings are still active with guests.

Demand readout: Rome has the highest average reviews per month at 1.77. Bangkok has the strongest review recency, with 0.0% of listings reviewed in the 180 days before scrape.

Availability & Booking Constraints

Availability and minimum-night settings help distinguish frequently bookable short-stay inventory from listings that may be constrained by regulation, owner preference, or monthly-stay positioning.

Bangkok is the most available market on average with 253 days/year, while Amsterdam is lowest at 92 days/year. Barcelona has the highest share of listings requiring 30+ nights at 40.1%.

Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

  1. Pricing power is concentrated at the top: Bangkok has the highest median nightly price at $1,400, 10.7x Rome's $131 median. Bangkok posts the highest average price at $2,337, reflecting stronger high-end skew than the median alone shows.
  2. Professionalized supply is not uniform: Bangkok has 60.4% of listings tied to hosts with 5+ listings, versus 6.8% in Amsterdam. That 53.7-point spread is material for regulatory benchmarking and competitive intensity.
  3. Entire-home exposure varies sharply: Paris is the most entire-home-oriented market at 88.2%, while Barcelona is lowest at 60.2%. Higher entire-home shares signal more investable, stand-alone rental supply but also greater housing-policy sensitivity.
  4. Guest-demand proxies point to Rome: it leads on average reviews per month at 1.77, while Bangkok has the highest share of listings reviewed within 180 days at 0.0%. These review metrics support, but do not replace, booking and occupancy data.
  5. Booking accessibility differs by market: Bangkok averages 253 available days per year, compared with 92 in Amsterdam. Barcelona has the highest 30+ night minimum share at 40.1%, a potential marker of regulation, monthly-stay strategy, or constrained short-stay availability.
  6. Data coverage is incomplete relative to the brief: the file contains 7 cities (Amsterdam, Bangkok, Barcelona, London, Paris, Rome, Sydney) and no rows for New York, Tokyo, Los Angeles. Price analysis is based on 24,087 priced listings; 25,913 listings (51.8%) have missing prices and are excluded from price statistics.

Methodology & Data Integrity

  • Primary source: /app/benchmark/data.csv, 50,000 rows and 26 columns.
  • Scrape date: 2026-03-29; this is a cross-sectional snapshot, not a time series.
  • City coverage: present cities are Amsterdam, Bangkok, Barcelona, London, Paris, Rome, Sydney. Missing from the expected 10-city brief: New York, Tokyo, Los Angeles.
  • Price handling: missing prices are excluded from all price medians, averages, percentiles, and room-type price comparisons. No outlier trimming is applied to KPI calculations.
  • Professional host definition: listing-level share where calculated_host_listings_count >= 5, exactly matching the brief.
  • Demand limitation: reviews per month, last-twelve-month reviews, and review recency are proxies for demand; they are not confirmed bookings or occupancy.
