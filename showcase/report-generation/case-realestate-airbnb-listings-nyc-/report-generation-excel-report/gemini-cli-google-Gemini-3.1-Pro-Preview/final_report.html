Understanding supply requires looking at the commercialization level of a market. We measure this through the concentration of "Professional Hosts" (hosts managing 5 or more listings) and the proportion of "Entire home/apt" listings vs. shared spaces.

4. Guest Demand & Review Activity

Guest demand is proxied using the average number of reviews generated per month per listing. Markets with high review velocity often indicate strong short-term occupancy and turnover rates.

6. Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

  1. Significant Price Divergence: There is a stark contrast in pricing between top-tier markets (e.g., Los Angeles, London) and more affordable markets (e.g., Bangkok, Rome). Investors seeking yield via high daily rates should focus on US and Western European capitals, though these may carry higher acquisition costs.
  2. The Rise of the Professional Host: Certain cities exhibit massive professionalization. High professional host shares (5+ listings) indicate sophisticated operations dominating the market. These markets are highly competitive and often commercialized.
  3. Regulatory Signals via Minimum Nights: Variations in median minimum nights are strong signals of local regulation. Cities displaying exceptionally high minimum night requirements (e.g., 30 days) have likely enforced strict short-term rental bans, forcing supply into the medium-term market.
  4. Demand Hotspots: Markets like Tokyo and Paris demonstrate high average monthly reviews, signaling strong, consistent demand and high turnover. These are high-velocity markets where occupancy rates are likely robust.
  5. Commercial vs. Home-Sharing Mix: The vast majority of listings in top cities are 'Entire home/apt', shifting the platform's utility from casual home-sharing to dedicated commercial hospitality. Markets retaining higher 'Private room' shares might be less regulated or have different cultural hosting norms.
