Global Airbnb Market Comparison

Pricing, Supply & Guest Demand Across Major Cities

Report Date: April 2026  |  Data Source: Inside Airbnb (scrape date 2026-03-29)  |  Audience: Hospitality Industry Analysts & Short-Term Rental Strategists

Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

  1. Pricing hierarchy is extreme: Among cities with price data, Bangkok commands the highest median nightly rate at $1,400, while Rome is the most affordable at $131 — a 10.7x gap. Bangkok shows exceptionally high average prices ($2,337) driven by extreme outliers, but its median ($1,400) is more moderate. Paris and Sydney have no usable price data in this dataset and are excluded from price rankings.
  2. Professional host concentration varies dramatically: Bangkok leads with 60.4% of listings controlled by hosts with 5+ properties, followed by Barcelona at 58.3%. At the other extreme, Amsterdam has only 6.8% professional-host share, suggesting a more individual-host market. For super-professional hosts (20+ listings), Barcelona tops the list at 36.0%.
  3. Entire-home dominance signals commercialization: Paris has the highest entire-home share at 88.2%, indicating a heavily commercialized supply base. Barcelona is the most balanced market with only 60.2% entire homes and 38.9% private rooms — a profile closer to traditional homesharing.
  4. Rome leads on guest demand intensity: With 1.77 average reviews per month and 73.4% of listings receiving reviews in the last 12 months, Rome shows the strongest guest engagement. Bangkok lags on both metrics (0.96 reviews/month, 51.5% reviewed LTM), suggesting either weaker demand or lower review propensity.
  5. Availability patterns reveal regulatory and commercial divergence: Bangkok listings are open an average of 253 days per year — the highest in the dataset — while Amsterdam averages only 92 days. High minimum-night requirements (30+) are most prevalent in Barcelona (40.1%), potentially signaling long-term rental regulations or host preference for extended stays.
  6. Data gaps limit cross-city completeness: Price data is entirely missing for Paris (13927 listings) and Sydney (3013 listings), and partially missing for London (35.2%), Amsterdam (44.2%), and Bangkok (19.0%). All price comparisons should be interpreted with this coverage bias in mind.
