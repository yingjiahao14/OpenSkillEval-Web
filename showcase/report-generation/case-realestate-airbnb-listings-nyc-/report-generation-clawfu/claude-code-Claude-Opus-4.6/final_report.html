5. Availability & Booking Constraints

Mean annual availability is the simplest indicator of commercial intent: a listing open more than 270 days/year is unlikely to be a casual host's spare room. Minimum-nights distributions reveal regulatory pressure — a heavy 30+ nights tail typically indicates either a long-stay specialty market or a regulatory ceiling that pushes hosts out of short-term rental classification.

Regulatory signal. Barcelona has the largest share of listings with a minimum stay of 30+ nights (40.1%), a pattern consistent with short-term-rental restrictions that incentivize hosts to reposition as medium-term rentals.

6. Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

  1. Bangkok is the priciest market by a wide margin. A median nightly rate of $1,400 — roughly 10.7× the floor set by Rome ($131) — makes it the highest-yield market on a per-night basis but also the most exposed to demand softening and regulatory scrutiny.
  2. Professional supply is concentrated in Bangkok (60%) and Barcelona (58%). These markets read as the most commercially operated and are the natural targets for institutional entry — but also the most likely to face caps on multi-listing operators. Amsterdam (7%) remains predominantly individual-host territory.
  3. Whole-unit rentals dominate the highest-pricing markets. Paris leads at 88% entire-home share; Barcelona sits lowest at 60%. The entire-home share moves in tandem with median price across the sample, suggesting commercialization and pricing power are jointly determined.
  4. Demand intensity is highest in Rome. 1.77 avg reviews/month per active listing implies the strongest booking turnover among the cities analyzed — a signal that occupancy, not pricing, is the dominant return driver there.
  5. Supply scale ≠ pricing power. London carries 16,674 listings (33% of supply) but ranks 3 of 7 on median price — large markets dilute pricing power, and entrants should expect competition on volume rather than yield.
  6. Regulatory pressure is visible in minimum-nights data. Barcelona stands out with 40% of listings requiring 30+ night minimums — the clearest fingerprint in the dataset of short-term-rental rules pushing supply toward medium-stay segments. Strategists evaluating new markets should treat this column as an early-warning indicator.
  7. Data caveat for decision-makers. 52% of listings lack a price; the dataset is also a single snapshot rather than a time series. Cross-city comparisons here are directionally robust for medians and shares, but absolute revenue projections require external pricing data and seasonal calibration.
