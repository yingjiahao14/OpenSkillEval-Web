6. Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

  1. Bangkok commands the highest median price ($1,400/night), while Rome is the most affordable market at $131/night — a 10.7x premium. Investors seeking high revenue per booking should prioritize Bangkok; value-focused entrants should evaluate Rome. Paris, Sydney listings lacked price data entirely and could not be included in pricing comparisons.
  2. Professional host dominance varies sharply: Bangkok leads with 60.4% of listings from multi-listing hosts (5+ properties), while Amsterdam sits at just 6.8%. High professional concentration signals mature, commercialized markets — and potentially tougher regulatory scrutiny.
  3. Entire-home share is a commercialization bellwether: Paris (88.2% entire homes) and Barcelona (60.2%) sit at opposite ends. Markets with high entire-home shares tend to correlate with higher prices and professional host activity.
  4. Guest demand (reviews/month) peaks in Rome (1.77), suggesting strongest booking velocity. Bangkok shows the lowest engagement (0.96), which may reflect seasonal patterns or market saturation.
  5. Availability patterns reveal distinct regulatory footprints: Bangkok averages 253 available days/year — suggesting a commercially-oriented supply — while Amsterdam averages only 92 days, likely reflecting strict short-term rental caps (e.g., Amsterdam's regulatory environment).
  6. Minimum-night requirements are highest in cities with regulatory constraints on short stays, with Paris averaging 43.9 nights — a red flag for investors targeting short-stay tourists.
