Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

  1. Bangkok is the clear premium-priced market in this dataset (median price 1500 on available listings) and also has the highest professional host share (60.4% of listings have calculated_host_listings_count ≥ 5).
  2. Barcelona shows “commercialized supply” signals: high professional host share (58.3%) plus the lowest Entire‑home share among cities in-file (60.2%), indicating a relatively larger private-room segment versus peers.
  3. Rome leads demand proxies: highest average reviews_per_month (1.77) and the highest median number_of_reviews_ltm (6), consistent with strong recent guest activity.
  4. Availability patterns differ sharply: Bangkok has the highest mean availability (252.5 days/year) while Amsterdam is lowest (92.5), suggesting materially different booking openness/commercial intensity (or regulatory constraints).
  5. Price analytics are incomplete for Paris and Sydney: price is entirely missing for both cities in this file (0 non‑null prices), so cross‑city pricing ranks should not be interpreted as “cheaper” for those markets.

Methodology & Definitions

  • Median Nightly Price by City: median of non‑null price per city.
  • Professional Host Share: share of listings where calculated_host_listings_count ≥ 5 per city (computed on all listings, regardless of missing price/reviews).
  • Average Reviews per Month: mean of reviews_per_month per city (pandas mean skips nulls).
  • Mean Availability: mean of availability_365 per city.
  • Entire Home Share: share of listings with room_type == "Entire home/apt".

All computations were derived from the provided file only; no external enrichment or imputation was applied.
