This analysis covers 50,000 Airbnb listings across seven major global cities. Paris and Sydney are present in the dataset but lack usable price data, so pricing comparisons are limited to the five remaining cities. The sample is heavily weighted toward European capitals, with London and Paris alone accounting for 61% of all listings.

Room-Type Composition

Paris and Amsterdam are the most commercialized markets: 88% and 83% of listings are entire homes/apartments, respectively. Bangkok and Barcelona show more diversity, with private rooms making up 32% and 39% of supply. Shared rooms and hotel rooms remain marginal everywhere (<4% combined).

Price by Room Type

Entire homes command a premium over private rooms in every market. The gap is widest in London (2.9x) and Barcelona (2.8x), and narrowest in Rome (1.4x) and Bangkok (1.4x). Hotel rooms in London and Barcelona actually exceed entire-home medians, likely reflecting boutique properties in premium locations.

Price Dispersion

London exhibits the highest price dispersion (standard deviation $536), followed by Bangkok ($4,647 in absolute terms but driven by a high mean). Amsterdam is the most tightly distributed market. For strategists, high dispersion signals opportunities for both budget and luxury positioning; low dispersion suggests a commoditized market.

Host Portfolio Distribution

Barcelona and Bangkok are dominated by multi-listing operators: more than 30% of listings belong to hosts with 20+ properties. In contrast, Amsterdam is overwhelmingly individual-host driven, with 93% of listings from hosts managing a single unit. London and Paris sit in the middle, with high absolute counts of professional hosts but lower concentration.

Strategic note: Cities with high professional-host concentration (Bangkok, Barcelona) are more likely to face regulatory tightening. Amsterdam’s low concentration may reflect strict short-term rental laws that cap the number of days or listings per host.

4. Guest Demand & Review Activity

Reviews serve as the best available proxy for booking frequency. Rome leads on every demand metric: highest average reviews per month (1.77), highest mean total reviews (60), and highest last-twelve-month reviews (14.4). Sydney and Barcelona follow closely. Bangkok, despite its high prices, shows the weakest review velocity (0.96/month), suggesting either lower occupancy or fewer guests leaving reviews.

6. Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

  1. Bangkok is a premium outlier, not a value market. With a median nightly price of $1,400—10× Rome’s $131—it commands the highest rates in the sample. Yet its review velocity is the weakest (0.96/month), suggesting either lower occupancy or a guest base less inclined to review. Investors should treat Bangkok as a high-revenue, potentially lower-turnover market.
  2. Barcelona and Bangkok are the most commercially concentrated markets. Professional hosts control 60% (Bangkok) and 58% (Barcelona) of supply, and more than 30% of listings in both cities come from hosts with 20+ properties. These are the markets most exposed to regulatory crackdowns on multi-listing operators.
  3. Rome offers the strongest demand fundamentals. It leads in reviews per month (1.77), total reviews (60 mean), and last-twelve-month review activity (14.4). Two-thirds of Rome listings have received a review within the past year. For operators prioritizing occupancy over rate, Rome is the standout.
  4. Paris and Sydney are data blind spots. Zero usable price data for 16,940 listings (34% of the sample) means pricing conclusions for these major markets cannot be drawn from this dataset. Any investment or pricing strategy relying on this report must source external price benchmarks for Paris and Sydney.
  5. Amsterdam is the most regulated-looking market. The lowest professional-host share (6.8%), the lowest mean availability (93 days), and the tightest price distribution (CV 0.83) all point to a supply pool constrained by short-term rental rules. Market entry here likely requires navigating licensing and day-cap regulations.
  6. High minimum-night requirements in Paris and Barcelona signal regulatory adaptation. Paris’s mean minimum stay of 44 nights and Barcelona’s 40% share of 30+ night listings suggest hosts are repositioning toward medium-term lets—possibly to evade short-term rental limits. This reduces addressable market for typical tourists and shifts competitive dynamics toward corporate and relocation demand.
  7. London dominates scale but not performance. With 16,674 listings (33% of the sample), London is the largest market, yet its demand metrics are mid-tier and its price dispersion is the highest (CV 2.55). This suggests a fragmented market with room for both budget and luxury positioning, but also intense competition.
