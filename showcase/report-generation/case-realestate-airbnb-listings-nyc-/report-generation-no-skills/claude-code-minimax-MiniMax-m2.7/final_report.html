Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

1
Amsterdam commands premium pricing but limits commercial scale. With the highest median price ($222) yet the lowest professional host share (6.78%), Amsterdam's market is dominated by individual hosts operating under restrictive regulations. Investors seeking high-value, low-volume operations should prioritize Amsterdam.
2
Bangkok and Barcelona represent highly commercialized markets. Both cities exceed 58% professional host concentration, indicating mature supply chains. Bangkok's high availability (252.5 days) signals strong commercial intent, while Barcelona's regulatory environment continues to evolve.
3
Rome shows the strongest guest demand signals. Rome leads in average reviews per month (1.77), mean total reviews (59.95), and LTM review activity (14.35). This indicates either the most active guest market or the most established review culture. The 218.6 mean availability suggests active commercial participation.
4
Paris and London represent balanced, large-scale markets. Paris leads in entire home share (88.23%) but lacks price data — limiting analysis. London offers the largest sample with complete data (16,674 listings), providing reliable market benchmarks at 64.72% entire home share.
5
Data gaps are critical for NYC, Tokyo, and LA. The absence of any data for New York, Tokyo, and Los Angeles — three of the most significant global markets — severely limits the report's global competitiveness analysis. Decision-makers should treat the current analysis as partial and seek supplementary data sources for these markets.
