Global Market Overview

Supply is highly uneven: London and Paris account for 61.2% of all observed listings. Smaller markets such as Amsterdam have fewer listings but materially different composition and availability patterns.

Entire-home/apartment listings dominate every city except none; Paris has the highest entire-home share at 88.2%, followed by Amsterdam at 82.8%. Barcelona has the largest private-room share at 38.9%, signaling a less fully whole-unit-oriented mix than Paris, Amsterdam, Sydney, and Rome.

Pricing Dynamics Across Cities

Pricing is the centerpiece of cross-market differentiation. Among cities with populated prices, Bangkok is the clear premium outlier at $1,400 median, while Rome is lowest at $131. Bangkok's values are far above other observed markets and should be interpreted as dataset-normalized prices exactly as provided, with no currency conversion or correction applied.

Price Dispersion

Mean prices exceed medians in every priced city, indicating right-skewed distributions with high-end listings lifting averages. Bangkok shows the widest absolute spread, with a median of $1,400, average of $2,337, and interquartile range from $919 to $2,250. Amsterdam is the highest-priced non-Bangkok market at $222 median.

Supply Structure & Host Concentration

Professional host concentration is calculated as the share of listings where calculated_host_listings_count >= 5. This listing-weighted metric captures the supply controlled by multi-listing operators, not the share of host accounts.

Bangkok (60.4%) and Barcelona (58.3%) are the most professionally concentrated markets. Amsterdam is the clear low-concentration outlier at 6.8%, despite a high entire-home share, suggesting supply is more fragmented among smaller hosts.

Commercialization is strongest where professional host share, entire-home share, and availability are jointly high. Bangkok combines the highest professional concentration with the highest mean availability (252.5 days/year). Barcelona pairs high professional concentration with the highest 30+ night minimum-stay share (40.1%), a likely regulatory or operating-constraint signal.

Guest Demand & Review Activity

Reviews are used as directional demand proxies, not direct bookings. The all-listing average reviews-per-month treats listings without reviews as zero activity; this avoids overstating demand in markets with many unreviewed listings.

Rome has the strongest observed review intensity at 1.51 average reviews per month and 14.4 reviews in the last twelve months per listing. Sydney ranks second on average reviews per month (1.22) despite missing all price data, while Bangkok's high prices coexist with lower review intensity (0.62) and the highest zero-review share (35.6%).

Availability & Booking Constraints

Availability and minimum-night rules reveal how accessible supply is to short-stay travelers and how commercial or regulation-constrained a market may be.

Bangkok leads availability at 252.5 days/year, reinforcing its commercially available supply profile. Amsterdam has the lowest mean availability at 92.5 days/year, a sharp contrast with its premium pricing and high entire-home share. Barcelona's 30+ night minimum-stay share of 40.1% is the clearest booking-constraint signal in the dataset; Paris follows at 20.4%, though price data is unavailable for Paris.

Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

Bangkok is the priced-market premium and commercialization outlier. It has the highest median price ($1,400), highest professional host share (60.4%), and highest mean availability (252.5 days/year), but demand proxies are weaker than Rome and Sydney.
Amsterdam is premium-priced but capacity-constrained. Its median price ($222) ranks second among priced cities while mean availability is lowest (92.5 days/year) and professional host share is only 6.8%.
Barcelona has the strongest regulatory/constraint signature. Professional hosts control 58.3% of listings and 40.1% require 30+ nights, the highest share observed.
Rome shows the strongest demand proxy. Rome leads all cities on average reviews per month (1.51), average LTM reviews (14.4), and recent-review share (66.3%), while maintaining mid-tier median pricing ($131).
London is the scale benchmark, not the price leader. London contributes 16,674 listings, the largest observed market, but its median price ($135) is close to Barcelona and Rome.
Paris and Sydney require non-price benchmarking until price data is repaired. Both have 100% missing prices in data.csv; use their room mix, professional share, review activity, availability, and minimum-night metrics, but do not rank them on pricing.

Methodology & Data Notes

All metrics are derived directly from /app/benchmark/data.csv. Price statistics exclude blank prices. Professional host share is listing-weighted and equals listings where calculated_host_listings_count >= 5 divided by total city listings. Average reviews per month uses all listings as the denominator, assigning zero to blank reviews_per_month values because those blanks correspond to listings with no reviews. Recent review share is the share of listings with last_review within 365 days of the scrape date (2026-03-29).

Documentation mismatch: source_brief.md describes 292,802 listings across 10 cities, but the provided data.csv contains 50,000 listings across 7 cities. No missing cities or prices were inferred.
