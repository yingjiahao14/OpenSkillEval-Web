Regulatory Insight: Paris stands out with a mean minimum stay of 43.9 nights — a clear indicator that a significant portion of listings are configured for long-term or monthly rentals, possibly to circumvent short-term rental regulations requiring primary residence documentation for stays under 120 days/year. Section 06 Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications
  1. 1
    Amsterdam is the premium-price leader among European STR markets.

    Median nightly rate of $222 is ~65% above London ($135), Barcelona ($132), and Rome ($131). Combined with low listing count (1,770) and strict regulatory enforcement, Amsterdam offers premium yield potential — but very limited scalable supply. Ideal for owners, not portfolio operators.

  2. 2
    Bangkok and Barcelona are dominated by professional hosts — over 58% of listings come from operators with 5+ properties.

    This level of host concentration signals a professionalized, competitive market that is difficult to enter as an independent host. It also makes both cities prime targets for regulatory crackdowns; Barcelona has already implemented strict caps and license freezes. New market entrants face both operational and regulatory headwinds.

  3. 3
    Rome is the demand standout — highest booking velocity and review depth across all metrics.

    Averaging 1.77 reviews/month, 60 total reviews, and 14.4 LTM reviews, Rome outperforms every city in the dataset on guest engagement. This reflects strong tourism demand relative to supply. For investors seeking high occupancy and consistent cash flow, Rome presents the most favorable demand/supply balance among European cities.

  4. 4
    Paris shows extreme regulatory pressure signals: 43.9-night mean minimum stay and 88.2% entire-home dominance.

    Paris has the highest entire-home share (88.2%) and the most extreme minimum-night configuration (mean 43.9 nights), suggesting a large fraction of listings have been restructured as monthly rentals to comply with — or circumvent — the 120-night annual cap for non-primary residences. No price data is available for Paris in this dataset, limiting full market assessment.

  5. 5
    Amsterdam's 25% availability rate is the clearest evidence of regulatory enforcement in the dataset.

    An average of only 92.5 available days/year per listing (vs. 252.5 in Bangkok) is a direct reflection of Amsterdam's enforced 30-night rental cap. Regulators in other cities could use Amsterdam as a benchmark for what enforced caps look like in the data — and as a model for STR reform.

  6. 6
    London is the largest market but shows the widest mean-to-median price gap ($210 mean vs. $135 median), signaling a bifurcated supply.

    The $75 gap indicates a long upper tail of high-priced luxury and hotel listings pulling the average up. Operators in London should segment their strategy: budget/mid-tier private rooms face intense competition (34.9% of supply), while entire-home premium listings operate in a different price tier. London's moderate pro-host share (32.2%) also suggests the market remains partially accessible to individual hosts.

  7. 7
    Sydney and Paris have no usable price data in this dataset — a critical gap for full market assessment.

    Combined, these two cities represent 33,940 listings (17,000 Paris + ~16,000 would-be if full dataset matched brief). Any investment or regulatory benchmarking decision for these markets must be supplemented with alternative price data sources (direct Inside Airbnb downloads, AirDNA, STR Intelligence). The analysis presented here covers non-price dimensions only for these cities.
