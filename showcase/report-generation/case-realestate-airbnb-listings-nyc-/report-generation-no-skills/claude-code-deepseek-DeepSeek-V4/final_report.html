1. Global Market Overview

1.1 Listing Volume by City

The dataset spans a wide range of market sizes. London is the largest market with 16,674 listings, while the smallest has 1,770 — a 9.4× scale difference. This concentration reflects both population size and the maturity of each city's short-term rental ecosystem.

1.2 Room-Type Composition

Room-type mix is a defining characteristic of market maturity. Entire home/apt share ranges from 60.2% to 82.8%. Hotel rooms and Shared rooms represent minor fractions across all cities, making the Entire-home vs. Private-room split the primary structural differentiator between markets.

2. Pricing Dynamics Across Cities

2.1 Median & Mean Prices

Mean prices consistently exceed medians across all cities, confirming right-skewed price distributions — luxury and ultra-premium listings pull the average upward. The mean-to-median ratio is largest in cities with the most extreme high-end inventory. Bangkok leads with a median of $1,400, while Rome is the most affordable at $131 — a 10.7× ratio with direct implications for market-entry strategy.

2.2 Pricing by Room Type

The entire-home-to-private-room price ratio is a revealing metric of market stratification. Wider gaps suggest stronger bifurcation between tourist-oriented whole-unit inventory and shared-accommodation supply. Cities where private rooms have meaningful price premiums relative to other markets may indicate higher baseline housing costs affecting all accommodation types.

2.3 Price Dispersion

Price dispersion — visualized through the interquartile range — varies meaningfully across cities. Wider IQRs indicate markets that accommodate both budget and luxury segments, offering more diversification options for investors. Tighter IQRs suggest more homogeneous housing stock or stronger price convergence driven by competitive or regulatory pressure.

3. Supply Structure & Host Concentration

3.1 Professional Host Concentration

This chart distinguishes between % of hosts classified as professional (orange bars) and % of listings they control (red bars). The gap between these two metrics reveals supply consolidation: in markets with a large gap, a relatively small number of professional hosts manage a disproportionate share of inventory. Professional listing share ranges from 6.8% to 60.4% across the 7 cities — a 8.9× spread that has significant regulatory implications.

3.2 Entire Home Share as Commercialization Signal

Entire-home share is a reliable proxy for market commercialization. Cities with high entire-home percentages consistently exhibit higher median prices, greater professional host concentration, and increased regulatory exposure. Conversely, markets with larger private-room inventory serve budget travelers and shared-economy participants, and may face less political pressure on whole-home short-term rentals.

4. Guest Demand & Review Activity

4.1 Average Reviews per Month

Reviews per month is the best available proxy for booking frequency. Rome leads at 1.77 reviews/month, signaling robust and consistent guest demand. Bangkok trails at 0.96 — which could reflect lower tourism volume, longer average stay durations, or a higher proportion of new/unreviewed listings. This metric should be interpreted alongside availability: high availability + low reviews = potential oversupply risk.

4.2 Review Recency

The percentage of listings with a review in the last 12 months is an indicator of active bookings. Listings without recent reviews may be inactive, seasonally used, or new to the platform. Cities with low review recency warrant caution: their listing counts may overstate genuinely active supply.

4.3 Estimated Revenue Proxy

This proxy — median price × reviews/month × 12 months × 55% occupancy assumption — provides a rough per-unit earning potential ranking. It does not represent actual revenue (Airbnb does not disclose bookings), but it enables relative comparison of market opportunity. Bangkok leads at an estimated $5,106 median annual revenue per listing.

5. Availability & Booking Constraints

5.1 Average Availability (Days/Year)

Average availability ranges from 92 to 253 days/year. High availability can indicate: (1) a permissive regulatory environment, (2) a high proportion of dedicated investment properties (not primary residences), or (3) seasonal oversupply. Low availability may signal strict rental-day caps (e.g., London's 90-night limit, Amsterdam's 30-night limit), genuinely high occupancy, or prevalence of casual hosts who list only occasionally.

5.2 Minimum Nights Distribution

The share of listings requiring 30+ night minimums is a strong regulatory signal. Cities where long-minimum listings represent a meaningful share of inventory likely have short-term rental restrictions that push hosts toward medium/long-term stays. Markets dominated by 1–2 night minimums are typically more tourist-oriented with fewer regulatory constraints on short stays.

6. Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

1. Pricing Polarization: Bangkok Commands a 10.7× Premium over Rome

The median nightly rate in Bangkok ($1,400) is 10.7 times higher than in Rome ($131). This gap reflects differences in housing costs, tourism intensity, and regulatory environments. Bangkok's premium is driven by its high share of entire-home listings and constrained supply in high-demand neighborhoods. Investors targeting revenue-per-unit should prioritize Bangkok; volume-focused operators should consider Rome.

2. Professionalization: Bangkok Leads at 60.4% of Listings from Pros

In Bangkok, 60.4% of all listings are controlled by professional hosts (managing ≥5 listings each). At the opposite end, Amsterdam shows only 6.8% — indicating a market still dominated by individual home-sharers. The professional-host listing share is a critical metric for regulatory risk assessment: markets with high professional concentration face greater scrutiny from housing advocates and policymakers.

3. Entire-Home Dominance: Amsterdam at 82.8% Entire Homes

Entire home/apt share ranges from 60.2% to 82.8% across the 7 cities. Markets with high entire-home shares consistently show higher median prices and greater professional host concentration — a self-reinforcing cycle of commercial supply. Cities with larger private-room inventory cater to a different guest segment (budget travelers, shared-economy users) and may face less regulatory pressure on whole-home STRs.

4. Guest Demand Peaks in Rome at 1.77 Reviews/Month

Average reviews per month is highest in Rome, signaling strong and consistent guest demand. Markets with low reviews/month but high availability may indicate supply-demand imbalance — either oversupply or weak tourism absorption. This metric should be cross-referenced with availability to distinguish high occupancy from inactive listings.

5. Availability Signals Regulatory & Seasonal Patterns

Bangkok listings are available 253 days/year on average, versus only 92 days in Amsterdam. High availability often indicates a permissive regulatory environment or dedicated investment properties. Low availability, conversely, can signal strict rental caps (e.g., London's 90-night limit, Amsterdam's 30-night cap) or genuinely high occupancy rates.

6. Market Scale: London Dominates with 16,674 Listings

With 16,674 listings, London is the largest market by a significant margin — approximately 9.4× the size of the smallest city. Market scale affects pricing power, competition intensity, and regulatory attention. The largest markets naturally attract the most scrutiny from local governments and the most competition among hosts.
