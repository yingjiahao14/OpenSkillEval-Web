Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

  1. Pricing spread is substantial: Bangkok posts the highest median nightly price ($1400) while Rome is lowest ($131), creating clear premium-vs-value positioning across markets.
  2. Professional supply concentration varies sharply: professional-host share ranges from Amsterdam (6.8%) to Bangkok (60.4%), materially changing competitive intensity and likely regulatory exposure.
  3. Commercialization profile differs by city: entire-home share diverges significantly, indicating different market structures between investor-led inventory and host-occupied supply.
  4. Demand momentum is not identical to price leadership: Rome leads review velocity (1.77 reviews/month), whereas Bangkok is lowest (0.96), suggesting occupancy proxies and pricing power do not always move together.
  5. Booking friction differs across markets: higher minimum-night norms in some cities imply constrained short-stay accessibility and potential regulation-linked operating models.
  6. Data integrity note: missing prices are city-dependent (see pricing table), so all comparisons use available observations without synthetic fills.
