A comprehensive cross-city analysis of Airbnb listings across New York, London, Paris, Tokyo, Barcelona, Los Angeles, Amsterdam, Bangkok, Rome, and Sydney, examining pricing dynamics, room-type distribution, host concentration, and guest engagement patterns.

Executive Key Takeaways

  1. 1. Pricing spread: Bangkok has the highest median nightly price ($1,400; n=4,018 priced listings) while Rome is the lowest among cities with price data ($131). Pricing metrics are unavailable for Paris, Sydney due to 100% missing `price`.
  2. 2. Professionalization: Bangkok shows the highest professional-host share (60.4% of listings have `calculated_host_listings_count` ≥ 5), versus Amsterdam at 6.8%.
  3. 3. Commercial listing mix: Entire-home supply is most dominant in Paris (88.2% of listings) and least dominant in Barcelona (60.2%).
  4. 4. Demand proxy: Rome leads on average `reviews_per_month` (1.77), suggesting comparatively higher booking velocity (subject to review-rate differences).
  5. 5. Access & constraints: Bangkok has the highest mean availability (253 days/year). Minimum-night constraints are most pronounced in Paris (p90=365 nights; median=3).

All figures are computed strictly from benchmark/data.csv. Where metrics are unavailable (e.g., missing prices), the report flags the gap explicitly.

Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

Action-oriented implications aligned to pricing, professionalization, and demand proxies.

  • Pricing benchmarking: Use median price as the primary cross-market comparator; mean price can be distorted by outliers (especially visible in Bangkok).
  • Regulatory exposure proxy: Cities with high professional-host share (≥5 listings) may face more regulatory scrutiny and institutional competition.
  • Commercialization via room mix: Very high entire-home share suggests more investor-style supply; pair with availability for a clearer commercialization read.
  • Demand screening: Combine reviews/month with last-review recency to prioritize markets with both high velocity and recent activity.
  • Constraint-driven strategy: High minimum-night requirements can shift feasible strategies from short-stay tourism toward mid/long-stay positioning.
