Key Findings

  • Bangkok has the highest professional host concentration (60.4%), with 64.6% of entire home listings owned by multi-property hosts.
  • Amsterdam has the lowest professional host share (6.8%), suggesting a market dominated by individual hosts — likely reflecting strict short-term rental regulations.
  • Barcelona shows high professionalization (58.3%), particularly in shared rooms (87.5% controlled by multi-listing hosts).
  • London has moderate professionalization (32.2%), with entire homes at 37.4% professional ownership.
  • Paris shows relatively low professional host share (21.4%) despite having the highest entire home share (88.2%), suggesting individual owners dominate even entire-home supply.

Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

  1. Bangkok is the highest-priced market by median ($1,400), driven by luxury serviced apartments and a high concentration of professional hosts (60.4%).   2. Amsterdam has the most regulated market structure. With only 6.8% professional host share — the lowest in the dataset — and the lowest mean availability (93 days), Amsterdam's market appears heavily shaped by short-term rental restrictions that limit commercial-scale operations.
  3. London offers the largest total supply (16,674 listings) with moderate pricing ($135 median), balanced host professionalization (32.2%), and the highest absolute number of fully-available listings (660), making it attractive for both individual hosts and mid-scale operators.
  4. Barcelona shows European peak professionalization (58.3%), with shared rooms almost entirely controlled by multi-listing hosts (87.5%). Combined with moderate pricing ($132 median), this suggests a maturing market with strong commercial competition.
  5. Rome demonstrates the strongest guest demand signals — highest average reviews per month (1.77), highest median total reviews (19), and lowest zero-review percentage (14.6%) — indicating robust booking activity across the market.
  6. Paris and Sydney lack price data, limiting cross-market pricing comparisons. For Paris, the combination of the highest entire home share (88.2%), lowest professional host share among entire homes (21.0%), and highest mean minimum nights (43.9) suggests a market dominated by individual owners under regulatory constraints.
  7. Regulatory signals are visible in availability patterns. High minimum-night averages (Paris: 43.9, Barcelona: 15.5, Sydney: 18.4) correlate with markets that have implemented restrictions on short-term rentals, while markets with low minimum nights (Amsterdam: 4.1, London: 5.5) maintain more flexible booking structures.
