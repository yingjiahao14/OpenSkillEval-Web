2. Pricing Dynamics Across Cities

Pricing data is incomplete. Paris (13,927 listings) and Sydney (3,013 listings) have 0% price coverage. Bangkok prices appear to be in Thai Baht rather than USD despite dataset documentation claiming normalization. Cross-city price comparisons should be interpreted with these caveats in mind.

Among cities with available price data, Bangkok reports the highest median nightly rate at 1,400 nominal units, followed by Amsterdam at $222 and London at $135. Barcelona and Rome cluster near $130–$132. However, the Bangkok figures are likely denominated in Thai Baht (1,400 THB ≈ $40 USD), which would make it the most affordable market rather than the most expensive. Without verified currency normalization, the rankings below reflect raw data as reported.

Price Dispersion

All markets exhibit significant right-skew, with mean prices substantially above medians—indicating a small number of high-value luxury listings pulling averages upward. Bangkok shows the widest dispersion (CV: 199%), while Amsterdam is the most compact (CV: 83%).

In London, the gap is even starker: $176 vs. $61—a 189% premium. This consistent pattern confirms that room type is the primary price differentiator within each market.

3. Supply Structure & Host Concentration

Host concentration is a critical indicator of market professionalization and has direct regulatory implications. The share of listings managed by hosts with 5+ properties (defined here as "professional hosts") ranges from just 6.8% in Amsterdam to 60.4% in Bangkok—nearly a tenfold difference.

Figure 6. Percentage of listings where the host manages 5+ properties. Higher values indicate greater market professionalization.

Host Portfolio Distribution

The composition of host portfolios reveals distinct market structures. Amsterdam is overwhelmingly individual-host dominated (82% single-listing hosts), while Bangkok and Barcelona are dominated by professional operators (60% and 58% of listings belong to hosts with 5+ properties respectively). London and Rome fall in a middle zone with roughly one-third of listings under professional management.

Figure 7. Distribution of listings by host portfolio size. Markets with high "5+ listings" shares indicate professional/commercial operation.

Room Type as a Commercialization Signal

The share of entire-home listings is a complementary indicator of commercialization. Paris leads at 88.2%, despite having a relatively low professional host share (21.4%)—suggesting many individual hosts who rent out their complete property. Conversely, Barcelona has a moderate entire-home share (60.2%) but the second-highest professional host concentration (58.3%), indicating that professional operators manage a diverse portfolio including both entire homes and private rooms.

4. Guest Demand & Review Activity

Reviews serve as a proxy for booking activity, though they undercount actual stays (not all guests leave reviews). Rome stands out with the highest average reviews per month (1.77) and the highest mean reviews in the last twelve months (14.4), suggesting strong and recent demand. Barcelona and Sydney also show elevated review activity. Bangkok has the lowest review velocity (0.96/month) despite having the second-largest listing count in the dataset after London.

Figure 8. Average reviews per month across listings with at least one review. Higher values suggest stronger booking frequency.

Review Recency

The median last-review date provides a signal of market freshness. Barcelona (median: August 2025) and Sydney (median: August 2025) show the most recent review activity, while Amsterdam (median: June 2025) has the oldest median—possibly reflecting seasonal patterns or lower booking velocity.

Zero-Review Listings

The proportion of listings with zero reviews is a proxy for dormant or newly listed properties. Bangkok has the highest share at 35.6%, followed by Barcelona (26.2%) and London (24.5%). Amsterdam (11.1%) and Sydney (14.9%) have the fewest zero-review listings, indicating a more consistently active supply base.

5. Availability & Booking Constraints

Annual availability (days a listing is bookable in the next 365 days) reveals stark differences in market accessibility and regulatory environments. Bangkok leads with a mean of 253 available days/year, followed by Rome (219) and Barcelona (194). Amsterdam has the lowest mean availability at 93 days—consistent with the Netherlands' strict short-term rental regulations limiting rentals to 30 nights/year for non-licensed properties.

Figure 9. Mean annual availability by city. Lower availability may indicate regulatory restrictions or high occupancy rates.

Availability Buckets

Segmenting listings into availability tiers reveals Amsterdam's regulatory footprint most clearly: 53% of Amsterdam listings are available 30 days or fewer per year, consistent with the Dutch 30-night annual cap for unregistered properties. Paris shows a similar pattern (44% at 0–30 days), reflecting its 120-day annual rental limit (which may account for the mid-tier share as well). Bangkok, Rome, and Barcelona have the highest shares of listings available 181+ days, indicating either lighter regulation or more commercially oriented supply.

Minimum Night Requirements

Minimum-night requirements also vary significantly. Amsterdam's median is just 3 nights, while Barcelona's is also 3 but with a 75th percentile at 31 nights—reflecting the Catalan government's enforcement of minimum stays for certain license categories. Paris has the highest mean (43.9 nights) and a long right tail, suggesting many properties operate as medium-term rentals rather than nightly stays, potentially as a regulatory workaround (e.g., "mobility leases").

6. Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

  1. Amsterdam is the most regulated market in the dataset. With 53% of listings available 30 days or fewer and the lowest mean availability (93 days/year), Amsterdam's short-term rental cap is clearly reflected in the data. For market entrants, this means limited supply but potentially higher per-night yields for licensed operators. The low professional host share (6.8%) also suggests less competitive pressure from commercial operators.
  2. Bangkok and Barcelona are the most professionalized markets. Over 58% of listings in both cities are managed by hosts with 5+ properties. This concentration creates barriers for individual hosts entering the market and suggests regulatory attention may be warranted. For investors, it signals that competing requires scale; for policymakers, it raises questions about housing stock conversion.
  3. Paris has the highest entire-home share (88%) but relatively low professional host concentration (21%). This suggests a market dominated by individual property owners renting out their complete homes rather than commercial operators managing portfolios. The high minimum-night requirement (mean: 44 nights) indicates that many Paris listings operate as medium-term rentals, likely as a regulatory adaptation to France's strict short-term rental rules.
  4. Rome shows the strongest guest demand signals. With the highest average reviews per month (1.77) and the highest mean LTM reviews (14.4), Rome stands out as the most actively booked market. Combined with high availability (219 days/year mean) and moderate professional host share (31%), it presents as an accessible market with strong demand and less entrenched commercial competition.
  5. Pricing data gaps are a major analytical limitation. Paris and Sydney—the 2nd and 7th largest markets by listing count—have zero price data, making definitive cross-market pricing conclusions impossible. Bangkok's prices appear to be in Thai Baht rather than USD, further complicating comparisons. Among cities with reliable price data, Amsterdam commands the highest median ($222), while Barcelona and Rome are the most affordable (median ~$131).
  6. London dominates by volume but is middling on most quality metrics. With 16,674 listings (33% of the dataset), London is by far the largest market, but its professional host share (32%), review velocity (0.99/month), and price levels (median $135) are all near the middle of the pack. Its scale offers opportunity but also implies competitive saturation.
  7. Low-availability markets (Amsterdam, Paris) and high-availability markets (Bangkok, Rome) represent fundamentally different operating environments. Low availability correlates with regulatory stringency and may produce higher occupancy and per-night yields for compliant operators. High availability suggests either lighter regulation or excess supply, which may pressure pricing but offers more booking flexibility for guests.
