Global Airbnb Market Comparison

Pricing, Supply & Guest Demand Across Major Cities
Audience: Hospitality Analysts & Market Strategists Data Source: Inside Airbnb (2026-03-29) 50,000 Listings Analyzed

Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

Synthesized findings for investors, hosts, and policymakers based on 50,000 Airbnb listings across seven cities.

Data Coverage Note: The dataset contains 50,000 listings across 7 cities (London, Paris, Rome, Bangkok, Barcelona, Sydney, Amsterdam). New York, Tokyo, and Los Angeles are absent from this sample and are therefore excluded from all cross-market comparisons.
  1. Bangkok commands the highest median nightly price ($1,400) — more than 6× Amsterdam ($222) and 10× London ($135). However, Bangkok's price distribution is heavily skewed by extreme outliers (max $102,858), suggesting possible local-currency artifacts or ultra-luxury inventory. The 99th-percentile-capped mean ($2,164) still confirms Bangkok as the premium market in this sample.
  2. Professional host concentration is highest in Bangkok (60.4%) and Barcelona (58.3%), signaling heavily commercialized supply. Amsterdam (6.8%) and Paris (21.4%) remain comparatively individual-host markets. For regulators, Bangkok and Barcelona should be priority markets for enforcement of short-term rental caps.
  3. Rome leads on guest-demand intensity with the highest average reviews per month (1.77), highest median reviews in the last 12 months (6.0), and the strongest review-recency profile (66.3% of listings reviewed within the past year). Rome's combination of high demand and moderate professional-host share (30.9%) suggests a healthy mix of commercial and organic supply.
  4. Paris has the highest entire-home share (88.2%), followed by Amsterdam (82.8%), indicating these markets are functionally apartment-rental platforms rather than home-sharing networks. Conversely, Barcelona's lower entire-home share (60.2%) reflects a more diverse room-type mix, partly due to higher private-room supply.
  5. Booking-constraint patterns reveal regulatory footprints: Barcelona has the highest share of listings requiring ≥30 nights (40.1%), likely reflecting Catalonia's strict short-term rental laws. Sydney follows with 16.1% requiring ≥90 nights, pointing to New South Wales' 180-day caps and minimum-stay incentives. Paris (20.4% ≥30 nights) also shows constraint clustering consistent with its 120-day legal limit.
  6. Amsterdam stands out as the most constrained market: lowest availability (92.5 days/year), lowest professional-host share (6.8%), and highest share of listings with zero availability (39.6%). This aligns with Amsterdam's progressive crackdown on short-term rentals in residential zones — the market is shrinking from the supply side.
  7. London dominates by scale (33.3% of all listings) but shows middling performance on most metrics: median price $135, professional-host share 32.2%, and only 44.0% of listings with a recent review. Its large, fragmented supply suggests a maturing market with saturation risk in outer boroughs.

Methodology & Data Quality

This report is based on a single scrape of Inside Airbnb data dated 2026-03-29. All statistics are computed directly from the provided data.csv file (50,000 rows). No external data or assumptions were used to fill gaps.

Known Limitations

  • Missing cities: New York, Tokyo, and Los Angeles are documented in the source brief but absent from data.csv. All cross-market rankings are therefore limited to the seven cities present.
  • Missing prices: 25,913 of 50,000 rows (51.8%) have null prices. Price statistics are computed only on non-null values and flagged accordingly.
  • Currency normalization: The source brief notes that prices are "assumed to be normalized to USD" but warns that original data uses local currencies. Bangkok's extreme outliers (max $102,858) suggest possible normalization gaps.
  • Review proxies: Reviews are an imperfect demand proxy. Not all guests leave reviews, and review frequency varies by culture and platform nudging.
  • neighbourhood_group and license columns: Largely empty across all cities and excluded from this analysis.
