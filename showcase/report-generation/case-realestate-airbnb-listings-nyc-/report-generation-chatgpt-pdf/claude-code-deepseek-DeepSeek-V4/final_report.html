1. Global Market Overview

At the lower end, Amsterdam (1,770) and Sydney (3,013) represent smaller markets. The dominant room type globally is Entire home/apt at 73.9% of listings, signaling a largely commercialized supply base rather than traditional home-sharing.

6. Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

Synthesizing the analysis across pricing, supply structure, guest demand, and availability, the following data-driven insights emerge for investors, hosts, and policymakers.

  1. Pricing Hierarchy is Clear Among Cities With Data. Bangkok commands the highest median at $1,400, followed by Bangkok ($1,400) and Barcelona ($132). Rome is the most affordable at $131 median — a 10.7x gap between extremes. Paris and Sydneyhave no pricing data available, leaving a critical blind spot in this analysis.
  2. Professionalization is Concentrated in Southeast Asia. Bangkok leads with 60.4% of supply controlled by professional hosts (5+ listings), followed by Barcelona (58.3%). At the other end, Amsterdam (6.8%) has the most fragmented, individual-host dominated market. Markets with high professional host concentration face greater regulatory exposure to multi-listing caps.
  3. Amsterdam & Rome are the Most Commercialized by room type: Paris leads in entire-home share at 88.2% of listings, followed by Amsterdam (82.8%). This means very little traditional "sharing economy" inventory — for policymakers, this signals potential housing stock displacement; for investors, it signals a mature, standardized market.
  4. Guest Demand is Highest in Rome. At 1.77 reviews/month on average, Rome shows 1.9x the booking velocity of Bangkok (0.96/mo). Barcelona (1.43/mo) and Sydney (1.44/mo) also show strong demand. However, these metrics should be cross-referenced with average length-of-stay — longer stays produce fewer reviews per unit time.
  5. Bangkok Shows the Strongest Commercial Supply Signal. Bangkok listings average 253 available days/year — suggesting full-time STR operations rather than occasional hosting. Combined with Bangkok's 60.4% professional host share and its pricing patterns (raw median: ฿1,400, likely ~$42 USD when adjusted), this market shows strong commercial maturity with affordable pricing.
  6. Regulatory Fragmentation Creates Market Arbitrage. The wide variance in minimum-night requirements (avg from 4.1 to 43.9 days across cities) and availability profiles points to highly fragmented local regulations. Barcelona has 38.5% of listings requiring 30+ nights — suggesting regulatory constraints on short-duration stays. Hosts operating in less-regulated markets can capture demand restricted elsewhere.
  7. Pricing Data Gaps Undermine Complete Market Comparison. Paris (13,927 listings) and Sydney (3,013 listings) — two of the world's highest-value real estate markets — have zero valid price entries in this dataset. Any global market analysis must address this data gap before drawing investment or regulatory conclusions about these cities.
Bottom Line: For investors, Rome offers the strongest demand signal (1.77 rev/mo) with moderate pricing ($131 median). For policymakers, Bangkok (60.4% pro host share) and Barcelona (58.3%) represent the most urgent cases for supply-side regulation. For hosts, London provides the deepest market (16,674 listings) with balanced metrics across price, demand, and availability. Caveat: The absence of price data for Paris and Sydney, and the likely currency mismatch for Bangkok, means this analysis should be supplemented with verified USD-normalized pricing before making investment decisions.
