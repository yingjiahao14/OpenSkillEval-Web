Global Airbnb Market Comparison: Pricing, Supply & Guest Demand Across 10 Major Cities

A comprehensive cross-city analysis of Airbnb listings across New York, London, Paris, Tokyo, Barcelona, Los Angeles, Amsterdam, Bangkok, Rome, and Sydney, examining pricing dynamics, room-type distribution, host concentration, and guest engagement patterns.

Data scrape date: 2026-03-29 • Generated: 2026-05-05 (UTC)

Purpose

Equip the reader with a clear understanding of how Airbnb markets differ across the world's top tourist destinations — identifying which cities command premium pricing, where supply is most concentrated among professional hosts, how room-type mix varies, and where guest demand (as proxied by reviews) is strongest — ultimately enabling data-driven decisions for market entry, investment, or regulatory benchmarking.

Data coverage note: Dataset contains 7 of 10 expected cities. Missing: New York, Tokyo, Los Angeles. All rankings and comparisons are limited to cities present in the file.

Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

  1. Pricing leader: Bangkok has the highest median nightly price ($1,400), while Rome is lowest ($131).
  2. Commercialization signal: Professional host share is highest in Bangkok (60.4%) and lowest in Amsterdam (6.8%), shaping competitive and regulatory exposure.
  3. Product mix: Paris has the highest entire-home share (88.2%), indicating stronger whole-unit supply orientation.
  4. Demand proxy: Review velocity is strongest in Rome (1.77 reviews/month on average) and weakest in Bangkok (0.96).
  5. Availability posture: Bangkok shows the highest mean availability (252.5 days/year), consistent with more open calendars and/or softer booking constraints.

Supply Structure & Host Concentration

Professional host share is defined as calculated_host_listings_count ≥ 5. This is a listing-level share, not a host-level share.

Interpretation notes

  • High professional share often signals commercialization and tighter competitive dynamics.
  • Entire-home heavy mixes can correlate with regulatory scrutiny and displacement concerns.

Guest Demand & Review Activity

Demand is proxied by review velocity (reviews_per_month) and recency (days since last_review at scrape date). Listings without reviews are excluded from recency calculations.

Why reviews are only a proxy

Review propensity varies by culture, trip type, and platform prompts. Use these signals directionally — not as a substitute for booking data.

Availability & Booking Constraints

Availability indicates openness to short-stay bookings; minimum nights can reflect regulation or host strategy.

Methodology & Data Integrity Notes

  • Cities missing from this extract are explicitly reported; no backfilling was performed.
  • All price statistics exclude rows with missing price; missing-price share is reported per city.
  • Professional host share is calculated at the listing level using calculated_host_listings_count ≥ 5.
  • Review-recency metrics are based on last_review; listings with no reviews have null last_review and are excluded from recency calculations.
  • Minimum_nights can have extreme outliers; the report emphasizes medians for stability.
