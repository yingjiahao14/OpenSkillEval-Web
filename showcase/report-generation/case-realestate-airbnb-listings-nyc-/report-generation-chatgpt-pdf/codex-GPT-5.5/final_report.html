Data Integrity Notes

No inferred values

The report uses only values derived from data.csv. Although the brief references 10 major cities, this file contains 7 cities: Amsterdam, Bangkok, Barcelona, London, Paris, Rome, Sydney. New York, Tokyo, Los Angeles are absent from the delivered dataset and are therefore not ranked or estimated.

Pricing analysis excludes listings with null price. This is material: 25,913 of 50,000 listings (51.8%) have no price. Paris and Sydney have 0 priced listings, so their price medians, averages, and price-by-room-type metrics are reported as unavailable rather than imputed.

The price column is labeled as a common price field in the source documentation; however, the unusually high Bangkok values indicate that cross-currency normalization should be validated before using absolute price rankings for investment decisions. The report therefore labels figures as dataset price units.

Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

Executive summary
  1. Do not treat the delivered file as a complete 10-city benchmark. New York, Tokyo, and Los Angeles are absent, while Paris and Sydney have no usable prices. Strategic pricing comparisons should be limited to the five priced cities unless corrected data is supplied.
  2. Bangkok is the clear premium-priced outlier in the current price field. Its median price of 1,400 is over 6x Amsterdam’s 222, but the magnitude warrants currency-normalization validation before capital allocation decisions.
  3. Professionalized supply is concentrated in Bangkok and Barcelona. Professional-host listing shares of 60.4% and 58.3% point to markets where operators and regulators face stronger multi-listing dynamics.
  4. Paris, Amsterdam, and Sydney are the most entire-home oriented markets. Entire-home shares of 88.2%, 82.8%, and 79.0% signal higher exposure to whole-unit rental policy debates.
  5. Rome has the strongest demand-quality signal. It leads reviews per month at 1.77 and recent-review share at 66.3%, suggesting broad active guest engagement rather than only legacy review accumulation.
  6. Amsterdam looks structurally constrained. Its low professional-host share (6.8%) and low mean availability (92 days) suggest limited scalable supply access despite a high observed median price.

Methodology

Derived calculations

Professional host share equals the percentage of listings where calculated_host_listings_count >= 5. Entire home share equals listings with room_type = Entire home/apt divided by total city listings. Review recency equals listings with a non-null last_review within 365 days of the listing's scrape_date.

Pricing metrics are computed only on non-null price values. No medians, means, or room-type prices are imputed for cities or listings with missing prices. Tables retain “N/A” where the required input data is unavailable.
