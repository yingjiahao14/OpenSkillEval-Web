2. Pricing Dynamics Across Cities

Cross-market price comparisons — median, mean, distribution, and room-type breakdown

Pricing is the most direct signal of market positioning and demand-supply balance. Across the 10 cities, median nightly prices range from $131 in Rome to $1,376 in Bangkok — a 10.5x spread that reflects differences in tourism spending power, housing costs, and regulatory constraints on supply.

Figure 2: Median nightly price by city. Bangkok commands the highest premium.

Figure 3: Price distribution (box plot, capped at $1,000). Wide interquartile ranges in cities like Bangkok indicate high price dispersion.

Key Pricing Insight: The gap between mean and median prices reveals skew in the pricing distribution. Cities where the mean significantly exceeds the median (e.g., check the overview table above) have a long tail of luxury listings pulling the average up — a critical consideration for revenue projections.

3. Supply Structure & Host Concentration

Room-type mix, professional host prevalence, and market commercialization

The room-type composition of each city's Airbnb market is a direct indicator of commercialization. Markets dominated by "Entire home/apt" listings tend to be more professionalized and face greater regulatory scrutiny, while private-room-heavy markets reflect a more traditional home-sharing model.

Figure 5: Room type distribution by city. Paris has the highest entire-home share at 88.2%.

Professional Host Concentration

Professional hosts — defined as those managing 5 or more listings — are a key indicator of market industrialization. Their share of supply directly impacts competitive dynamics for individual hosts, regulatory pressure, and overall market pricing behavior.

Figure 6: Share of listings from professional hosts (5+ listings). Bangkok leads at 60.4%, while Amsterdam is lowest at 6.8%.

Regulatory Signal: Cities with high professional host concentration (like Bangkok) are prime candidates for tighter short-term rental regulation. Markets where a small number of hosts control large portions of supply face greater political pressure to enforce licensing, occupancy limits, or outright bans on commercial short-term letting.

Figure 7: Host portfolio size distribution by city. The yellow (5-19 listings) and red (20+ listings) segments indicate professional operators.

4. Guest Demand & Review Activity

Review-based demand proxies: frequency, recency, and engagement intensity

While Airbnb does not publish booking data, review metrics serve as widely accepted demand proxies. Average reviews per month (RPM) approximates booking frequency, while the share of listings with reviews in the last 12 months indicates what portion of supply is actively generating guest engagement.

Demand Interpretation: A high RPM coupled with a high LTM-active percentage signals a market where supply is genuinely being consumed by guests. Markets with low active-listing shares may have significant "ghost inventory" — listings that appear available but generate no bookings.

5. Availability & Booking Constraints

Annual availability, minimum-stay requirements, and regulatory fingerprints

Availability patterns and minimum-night requirements reveal both host strategy and regulatory environment. Low average availability may indicate hosts who block dates for personal use (true home-sharers) or regulatory caps on short-term rental days. High minimum-night requirements (30+ days) often signal markets where regulations push listings toward medium-term rentals.

6. Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

Data-driven insights for investors, hosts, and policymakers

  1. Bangkok commands the highest median nightly price at $1,376, making it the premium market for short-term rental revenue. At 10.5x the price of Rome ($131), this spread highlights the enormous cross-market variation that investors must factor into yield models.
  2. Bangkok has the most professionalized host base (60.4% of listings from 5+ listing hosts), signaling an industrialized market where individual hosts face stiff competition from portfolio operators. Conversely, Amsterdam (6.8%) retains a more grassroots, home-sharing character.
  3. Room-type mix is a reliable commercialization indicator. Paris leads with 88.2% entire-home listings, while cities with higher private-room shares (check Figure 5) maintain stronger home-sharing profiles. Markets with high entire-home + high professional-host overlap are most vulnerable to regulatory tightening.
  4. Rome shows the strongest guest demand signal with an average RPM of 1.77, suggesting high booking turnover. Markets with strong demand but constrained supply (low availability, high minimums) represent potential pricing-power opportunities.
  5. Barcelona's 40.1% share of 30+ night minimums is the clearest regulatory fingerprint in the dataset, indicating that a significant portion of its Airbnb supply operates under medium- or long-term rental rules. Short-stay investors should model regulatory risk carefully in this market.
  6. Ghost inventory varies significantly: Sydney has 73.4% of listings receiving reviews in the last 12 months, while London sits at just 50.4%. Markets with low active-listing shares may overstate effective supply — a critical nuance for competitive analysis.
  7. Availability as a supply signal: Bangkok averages 253 available days per year — close to full-time commercial availability — while Amsterdam averages just 92 days, suggesting either strong personal-use patterns or regulatory day caps.
  8. London dominates in sheer scale with 16,674 listings, making it the largest Airbnb market in this comparison. Scale markets offer volume opportunity but also face the most intense competition and regulatory attention.
