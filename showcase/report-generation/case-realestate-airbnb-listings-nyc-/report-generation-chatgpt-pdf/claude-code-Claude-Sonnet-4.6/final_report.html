Amsterdam Commands the Highest Median Price Among Cities With USD-Denominated Data

At a median of $222/night, Amsterdam is the most expensive market with comparable currency data — 64% higher than Rome's median of $131. London's hotel rooms (median $246) command a premium over its entire-home listings ($176), an unusual inversion suggesting the hotel segment serves a distinct high-end niche.

Bangkok & Barcelona: Dominated by Professional Operators

In Bangkok, 60.4% of listings are held by hosts managing 5+ properties — the highest concentration in the dataset. Barcelona follows at 58.3%, suggesting both cities are characterized by property management companies rather than casual hosts. Notably, a single Paris host manages 816 listings — indicative of institutional-scale hospitality operators in that market. Amsterdam, by contrast, shows the hallmarks of strong regulatory enforcement: only 6.8% professional host share, 1.06 listings per host, and a maximum portfolio of just 35 properties.

Amsterdam's Low Availability Reflects Regulatory Cap Compliance

Amsterdam's mean availability of just 92.5 days/year is consistent with its 30-night/year cap on short-term rentals in many districts. This is the clearest regulatory fingerprint in the dataset. Barcelona's extreme reading — 40.1% of listings require a 30+ night minimum — is a different regulatory adaptation: hosts setting long minimum stays to reclassify properties as medium-term rentals, partially circumventing Airbnb-specific restrictions. Paris's anomalously high mean minimum nights (43.9 days vs. a median of 3) is driven by a small number of listings with extreme values (up to 1,000 nights), suggesting data quality issues or deliberate blocking strategies.

Section 06
Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

Data-derived findings for investors, market entrants, and policymakers. All conclusions are drawn directly from the dataset.

  • Amsterdam is the highest-priced market (in USD-comparable terms) with the most regulated supply. At a median of $222/night and only 92.5 mean availability days/year, Amsterdam combines premium pricing with severe supply constraint — a direct result of its 30-night/year cap. For investors, this creates both opportunity (pricing power) and risk (volume limits). For policymakers, it's the clearest example in this dataset of regulation successfully compressing short-term rental availability.
  • Bangkok and Barcelona are most dominated by professional operators — over 58% of listings are held by hosts with 5+ properties. This signals that both markets have already transitioned from a peer-to-peer sharing model toward a commercial property management model. Regulatory bodies in both cities face a concentrated operator base; Barcelona's 40.1% min-30-night share suggests regulation is already forcing adaptation tactics by these operators.
  • Rome delivers the strongest guest demand signals of any city in the dataset. With the highest average reviews/month (1.77), highest LTM review count (14.35/listing), and 73.4% active listing rate, Rome shows sustained, broad-based booking demand. Combined with relatively modest pricing (median $131) and low minimum-night requirements, Rome appears accessible and high-turnover — attractive for operators seeking volume over margin.
  • Paris and Sydney have no price data in this extract — a critical gap for cross-market investment decisions. Analysts relying on this dataset cannot benchmark Paris or Sydney pricing. Given that Paris has 13,927 listings (27.9% of the dataset), this absence materially limits the report's pricing conclusions. Any investment or competitive analysis involving these cities must be supplemented with additional data sources.
  • London's market is large but shows signs of demand softness relative to its size. With 16,674 listings — 33% of the dataset — London has only a 50.3% active listing rate and 0.99 average reviews/month, below Rome, Sydney, and Barcelona. This divergence between supply scale and per-listing demand intensity suggests a surplus of inventory relative to actively booked demand, potentially compressing yields for average operators.
  • Bangkok prices are denominated in Thai Baht, not USD. The median of 1,400 THB (~$42 USD at current exchange rates) places Bangkok as a value market when correctly interpreted — not the extreme-high-price outlier it appears in raw data. The high professional host concentration (60.4%) and high availability (252.5 days/year) together indicate a lightly regulated, commercially active market with strong operator infrastructure but lower per-night revenue in USD terms.
  • Amsterdam's market profile is an anomaly that should not be generalized. Its combination of the lowest listings/host ratio (1.06), lowest professional host share (6.8%), lowest max portfolio (35 listings), lowest availability (92.5 days), and highest pricing creates a market that is structurally distinct from all other cities — shaped almost entirely by regulation rather than natural market dynamics. It is not a replicable model without equivalent regulatory infrastructure.
