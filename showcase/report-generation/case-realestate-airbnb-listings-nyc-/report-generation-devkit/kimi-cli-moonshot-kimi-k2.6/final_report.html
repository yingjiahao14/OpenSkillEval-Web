1. Global Market Overview

This analysis covers 50,000 Airbnb listings across seven major global cities. The dataset represents a snapshot scraped on 2026-03-29 and includes listing details, host information, pricing, review activity, and availability metrics.

Data Quality Note

Price data is completely missing for Paris (13,927 listings) and Sydney (3,013 listings). All price-related comparisons exclude these two cities. Bangkok price values appear to be recorded in local currency (THB) rather than USD-equivalent, making direct cross-city price comparisons unreliable for Bangkok. These limitations are flagged throughout the report.

Price Dispersion

Room-Type Price Premium

Across all price-reporting cities, entire homes command a substantial premium over private rooms:

  • Amsterdam: Entire homes are 77% more expensive than private rooms (median $250 vs $141).

  • London: Entire homes are 189% more expensive than private rooms (median $176 vs $61).
  • Rome: Entire homes are 35% more expensive than private rooms (median $140 vs $104).

6. Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

Synthesized findings for hospitality analysts, investors, and policymakers.

  1. Amsterdam commands the highest verified median price ($222) among cities with reliable USD-equivalent data, followed by Barcelona ($132), London ($135), and Rome ($131). Bangkok’s recorded median of $1,400 appears to be denominated in local THB and is not directly comparable. Paris and Sydney lack price data entirely.
  2. Professional host concentration is a two-speed market. Bangkok (60.4%) and Barcelona (58.3%) are dominated by multi-listing operators, creating competitive dynamics that favor scale and professional operations. Amsterdam (6.8%) and Paris (21.4%) remain more individual-host driven, offering entry opportunities for boutique operators.
  3. Rome is the demand leader. With 1.77 average reviews per month and 66.3% of listings receiving a review in the last 12 months, Rome shows the strongest guest engagement. Bangkok lags with 35.6% zero-review listings, suggesting oversupply relative to current demand.
  4. Barcelona’s regulatory footprint is unmistakable. The 38.5% share of listings requiring 31+ night minimums, combined with high professional host share, indicates a market that has shifted toward medium-term rentals in response to licensing restrictions.
  5. Availability patterns reveal regulatory intensity. Bangkok’s 49.1% highly-available listings (>300 days) signal a permissive, commercial environment. Amsterdam’s 39.6% zero-availability listings and low mean availability (92.5 days) reflect strict rental caps and owner-occupancy requirements.
  6. Paris is the most commercialized by room-type mix. Despite missing price data, Paris leads in entire-home share (88.2%), suggesting a mature, professionally operated market. Combined with moderate professional-host share (21.4%), this implies consolidation among fewer, larger operators rather than fragmentation.
  7. Price dispersion is highest in London (CV 2.55) and Barcelona (CV 2.30), indicating heterogeneous inventory from budget to luxury. Amsterdam’s low dispersion (CV 0.83) suggests a more standardized, mid-market offering.
  8. For market entry, Rome and Sydney offer the best demand-availability balance: strong review activity, moderate professional-host concentration, and reasonable availability windows. Barcelona and Bangkok are better suited for scaled operators with compliance expertise.
Methodology Note

All statistics are derived directly from the provided dataset (50,000 listings, scrape date 2026-03-29). Missing price data for Paris and Sydney, and apparent currency inconsistency for Bangkok, are reported transparently. No imputation or assumption-based gap-filling was performed.
