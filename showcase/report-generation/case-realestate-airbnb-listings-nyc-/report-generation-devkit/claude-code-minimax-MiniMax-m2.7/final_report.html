Executive Summary

  1. Bangkok commands the highest median prices ($1,400/night), driven by a concentration of high-end entire-home listings and a 60.4% professional host presence — signaling a highly commercialized market.
  2. Barcelona and Bangkok exhibit the highest professional host concentration (58-60% of supply), raising regulatory scrutiny concerns and suggesting mature, institutionalized short-term rental markets.
  3. Paris and Sydney have no valid price data in this sample, limiting pricing comparisons — this data gap should be addressed before making market-entry decisions for those cities.
  4. Rome shows the strongest guest demand with 1.77 avg reviews/month and the highest total review count (382,872), indicating robust booking activity despite moderate pricing.
  5. Amsterdam has the lowest professional host share (6.8%), suggesting a market dominated by individual hosts — potentially more vulnerable to regulatory changes affecting small operators.

6. Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

1

Premium Pricing Markets Carry Higher Entry Barriers

Bangkok's $1,400 median price is 10× Rome's $131, reflecting a fundamentally different market segment. Investors entering Bangkok should plan for higher per-unit investment and potentially longer payback periods, while Rome offers accessible entry for budget-conscious operators.

2

Host Concentration Predicts Market Maturity

Bangkok (60.4%) and Barcelona (58.3%) professional host shares indicate institutionalized markets with established operating models. Amsterdam (6.8%) remains fragmented, presenting opportunities for individual hosts but also vulnerability to regulatory shifts.

3

Data Gaps Limit Paris/Sydney Analysis

The absence of price data for 16,940 listings (33.9% of sample) in Paris and Sydney precludes definitive pricing comparisons. Market-entry decisions for these cities require supplementary data sources.

4

Rome Offers Highest Demand Intensity

With 1.77 reviews/month and 382,872 total reviews, Rome demonstrates the strongest guest engagement per listing despite mid-range pricing. This suggests favorable unit economics for operators entering that market.

5

Availability Patterns Reveal Regulatory Environments

Amsterdam's 92-day average vs Bangkok's 253 days reflects divergent regulatory approaches. Markets with lower availability (Amsterdam, Paris) may face upcoming supply constraints if regulations tighten further.
