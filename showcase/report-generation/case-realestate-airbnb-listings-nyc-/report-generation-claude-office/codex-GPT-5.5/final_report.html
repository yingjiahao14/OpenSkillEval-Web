Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

  1. Bangkok is the pricing outlier among cities with price data: median $1,400 and average $2,337, far above Amsterdam at median $222; interpret this as a dataset price-field scale issue or premium signal requiring local validation before underwriting.
  2. Professional host concentration is highest in Bangkok (60.4%) and Barcelona (58.3%), signaling more commercialized supply and potentially stronger regulatory exposure than Amsterdam (6.8%).
  3. Entire-home supply dominates every observed market; Paris has the highest entire-home share (88.2%), followed by Amsterdam (82.8%), making these markets especially relevant for whole-unit regulation and housing-supply debates.
  4. Rome and Sydney show the strongest demand proxies: Rome leads average reviews per month (1.77) and average last-twelve-month reviews (14.35), while Sydney is second on reviews per month (1.44) but cannot be priced from this CSV.
  5. Availability is highest in Bangkok (253 days/year) and Rome (219), supporting a commercial-use interpretation; Amsterdam is lowest at 92 days/year.
  6. Data coverage limits decisioning: 51.8% of all listings lack price values, including 100.0% of Paris and Sydney records; market-entry decisions should use this report for structural benchmarking and supplement pricing with complete local extracts.
