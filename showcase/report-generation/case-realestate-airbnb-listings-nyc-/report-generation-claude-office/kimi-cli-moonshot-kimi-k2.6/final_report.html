1. Global Market Overview

This report analyzes 50,000 Airbnb listings across seven major global cities: London, Paris, Rome, Bangkok, Barcelona, Sydney, and Amsterdam. Three cities mentioned in the brief (New York, Los Angeles, Tokyo) do not appear in the dataset. London dominates supply with one-third of all listings, followed by Paris. The room-type mix varies sharply: Paris and Amsterdam are heavily skewed toward entire homes, while Barcelona and London retain a significant private-room segment.

2. Pricing Dynamics Across Cities

Pricing analysis is possible for five cities. Paris and Sydney have no price data in this dataset and are excluded from price comparisons. Among priced markets, Amsterdam commands the highest median nightly rate at $222, followed by London ($135), Barcelona ($132), and Rome ($131). Bangkok is the clear value market: even after converting from Thai Baht at an approximate rate of 35 THB/USD, its median is just $40 per night.

Price dispersion is highest in London and Barcelona (coefficient of variation >2.0), indicating wide quality and location spreads. Amsterdam shows the tightest distribution (CV = 0.83), suggesting a more homogeneous premium market.

4. Guest Demand & Review Activity

Review metrics serve as proxies for booking frequency and guest engagement. Rome leads all cities with 1.77 average reviews per month, followed by Sydney (1.44) and Barcelona (1.40). Amsterdam and Bangkok trail at around 1.0, while London and Paris sit in the middle. These differences likely reflect a mix of guest volume, host review-request practices, and listing turnover.

Review recency tells a different story: Rome and Sydney have the highest share of listings with a review in the last 12 months (66.3% and 62.2%, respectively), indicating stronger recent demand. Bangkok lags at 40.3%, suggesting either softer demand or a higher share of inactive listings.

5. Availability & Booking Constraints

Availability (days open for booking per year) and minimum-night requirements reveal regulatory footprints and host commercial intent. Bangkok listings are open an average of 253 days per year—the highest of any city—suggesting either lax regulation or strong commercial motivation. Rome and Barcelona also show high availability (~219 and ~194 days). Amsterdam is the most restricted, with a mean of just 93 days and a median of 18 days, consistent with strict short-term rental caps.

Minimum-night rules are most extreme in Paris (mean 43.9 nights), driven by a long tail of listings requiring 90+ nights—likely mobility leases or long-term rental listings miscategorized on Airbnb. Barcelona also shows elevated long-stay requirements (38.5% of listings require >30 nights), reflecting Catalonia’s 30-day minimum-stay regulation for unlicensed properties.

6. Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

These insights are designed to inform investment decisions, market-entry strategies, and regulatory benchmarking for hospitality industry professionals.

  1. Amsterdam is the premium priced market among those with data, with a median nightly rate of $222—5.5x higher than Bangkok’s converted median of $40. London, Barcelona, and Rome cluster tightly in the $131–$135 range, suggesting price convergence among mature European markets.
  2. Paris and Sydney pricing data is entirely missing from this dataset. Any investment or regulatory decision involving these cities should not rely on the price figures in this report; supplementary data sources are essential.
  3. Bangkok and Barcelona are the most commercialized markets, with over 58% of listings controlled by professional hosts (5+ properties). This concentration signals mature supply chains, higher regulatory scrutiny risk, and intense competition for new entrants.
  4. Amsterdam remains the most "individual" market with only 6.8% professional host share and the lowest average availability (93 days/year). This aligns with strict local short-term rental caps and suggests limited scalability for professional operators.
  5. Rome shows the strongest guest demand signals across all metrics: highest average reviews per month (1.77), highest share of recently active listings (66.3%), and highest average reviews in the last 12 months (14.4). It presents the most compelling demand story for investors.
  6. Regulatory footprints are visible in minimum-night rules. Paris (43.9-night average) and Barcelona (38.5% of listings >30 nights) show clear signs of long-stay regulations affecting supply structure. Bangkok’s near-absence of such constraints (4.2% >30 nights) suggests a lighter-touch environment.
  7. Room-type mix correlates with commercialization. Paris (88.2% entire home) and Amsterdam (82.8%) have the highest entire-home shares, while Barcelona (60.2%) and London (64.7%) retain more private-room inventory—potentially offering niche opportunities for budget-oriented entrants.

Methodology: All statistics are derived directly from the Inside Airbnb dataset (50,000 listings, scrape date 2026-03-29). Missing price values (25,913 listings, primarily Paris and Sydney) are excluded from price calculations. Bangkok prices are converted from Thai Baht to USD at an approximate rate of 35 THB/USD for cross-market comparability. Professional hosts are defined as those with calculated_host_listings_count ≥ 5. Review metrics are used as proxies for demand; they reflect booking frequency, guest satisfaction, and host solicitation behavior combined.

Data Quality Notes: neighbourhood_group and license columns are largely empty. last_review and reviews_per_month are null for listings with zero reviews. The dataset represents a single-point snapshot, not a time series.
