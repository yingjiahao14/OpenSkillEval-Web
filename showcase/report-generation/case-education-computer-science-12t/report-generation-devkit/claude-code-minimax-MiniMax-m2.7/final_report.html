Dataset Composition

The BIEK Computer Science 12th Class dataset contains examination questions from the Board of Intermediate Education Karachi, spanning examination years 2015 through 2025. The dataset was compiled from official past papers and covers Paper-II (theory portion) exclusively.

Question Type Breakdown

Question Type Description Total Count Share
MCQs Multiple Choice Questions with four options 150 45.9%
Short Short-answer questions requiring brief explanations or definitions 147 45.0%
Long Essay-type or problem-solving questions with multiple sub-parts 30 9.2%
Key Observation: The exam has a balanced split between MCQs (45.9%) and Short questions (45.0%), with Long questions comprising only 9.2% of the total. This structure emphasizes both recall/recognition (MCQs) and concise explanation (Short questions), while Long questions serve as the primary vehicle for assessing deep analytical and program-writing skills.

Data Quality Notes

  • Missing Year: Examination year 2020 is absent from the dataset — likely due to COVID-19 examination cancellations.
  • Supplementary Papers: The dataset does not distinguish between annual and supplementary examination papers.
  • Encoding: Question text uses a $ delimiter to separate MCQ options and Long question sub-parts.

Key Structural Observations

  • MCQ Count is Rigid: The exam has maintained exactly 15 MCQs per year across all years in the dataset — a remarkably consistent pattern suggesting a fixed allocation.
  • Short Questions Vary: Short question count fluctuates between 9 (2015–2016) and 18 (2017–2019), settling at 15 from 2021 onward.
  • Long Questions are Fixed: Exactly 3 Long questions appear every year, representing the minimum needed to cover the three main curriculum areas (C Programming, Database, and General Concepts).
Structural Shift Detected: The 2017 increase in total questions was driven entirely by adding 9 more Short questions (from 9 to 18). The MCQ and Long counts remained unchanged. This shift likely reflects a board decision to place greater emphasis on concise written responses over MCQ-only recall.

5. Strategic Insights for Exam Preparation

For Educators & Curriculum Planners

Priority 1: High-Frequency, Always-Tested Topics

These topics appear in 80–100% of examination years and should receive the most instructional time:

  • Database Fundamentals — Primary/Foreign keys, DBMS vs RDBMS, relational models, table/form/report objects in MS-Access, SQL basics (DML/DDL), entity relationships
  • C Programming: Loops — for, while, do-while syntax; nested loops; difference between loop types; continue/break statements
  • C Programming: Operators — Arithmetic, relational, logical, assignment; operator precedence; increment/decrement (++, --)
  • C Programming: Functions — Function declaration, prototype, void, return statement, user-defined vs library functions
  • Control Structures — if-else, switch-case, else-if ladder; decision-making syntax

Priority 2: Moderate-Frequency Topics

These topics appear in 50–79% of years and deserve regular coverage:

  • Data Types & Format Specifiers — %d, %f, %c, %s, %lf, %ld; int, float, char, double sizes
  • Variables & Constants — Declaration, initialization, local vs global scope
  • Input/Output Functions — printf, scanf, gets, getch, putchar, getc
  • Escape Sequences — \n, \t, \\
  • Preprocessor Directives — #include, #define
  • Keywords/Reserved Words — case, break, continue, return, void

Priority 3: Emerging or Under-Tested Topics

Based on recent trends (2024–2025), these topics show increasing frequency:

  • Bitwise Operators — &, |, ^, ~ (appeared prominently in 2025)
  • Storage Classes — auto, static, extern, register (2025 Long question)
  • Binary Search / Algorithm Complexity (2025 Long question)
  • Normalization (appeared in 2022, 2024 Long questions)
  • ACID Properties (appeared in 2025)

6. Conclusions & Limitations

Key Findings Summary

  1. The exam structure has changed meaningfully. From 2015–2016 (27 questions, MCQ-heavy) to 2017–2019 (36 questions, Short-heavy) to 2021–2025 (33 questions, balanced), the board has shifted its emphasis. Educators should note the current balanced approach (45.5% MCQ / 45.5% Short / 9.1% Long) appears stable.
  2. MCQ and Long counts are structurally fixed at 15 and 3 respectively — a remarkable consistency across all 10 years. Only the Short question count varies (9, 15, or 18).
  3. Database and C Programming dominate the curriculum. These two domains account for the majority of question content. Within C Programming, loops, operators, functions, and control structures are the most frequently tested sub-topics.
  4. Some important CS topics are under-represented: Recursion, pointers, arrays, and structures appear rarely or never — a potential gap between exam coverage and comprehensive CS education.
  5. 2025 shows subtle signals of new topic introduction: Bitwise operators, storage classes, binary search, and ACID properties appear in 2025 Long questions — these may indicate a curriculum expansion the board is quietly introducing.

Limitations

  • Missing Year (2020): No data is available for 2020 due to COVID-19. The gap interrupts the continuity of trend analysis.
  • No Annual vs. Supplementary Distinction: The dataset does not separate annual papers from supplementary examinations. Year-over-year comparisons may mix different paper types.
  • Keyword Frequency Limitations: Topic extraction based on keyword matching may miss questions that discuss a topic without using its exact keyword (e.g., "recursion" might appear as "recursive function").
  • No Curriculum Weighting: The dataset does not include curriculum documents specifying how much time each topic should receive. Frequency of appearance may not reflect intended instructional emphasis.
  • Community-Compiled Data: As a non-official, community-verified dataset, minor transcription errors may exist.
Final Note: This analysis is based strictly on observed patterns in the provided dataset. No external data sources, projections, or assumptions have been introduced. All statistics and trends derive directly from the 327 questions across 10 examination years (2015–2025, excluding 2020).
