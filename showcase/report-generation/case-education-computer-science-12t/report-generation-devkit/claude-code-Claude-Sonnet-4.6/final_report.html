Executive Summary: The BIEK Computer Science HSC Part-2 exam shows a broadly stable structure — MCQs have been constant at 15 per year throughout 2015–2025. A key structural shift occurred in 2017 when Short Questions expanded from 9 to 18 (a +100% increase), then settled to 15 from 2021 onward. Long Questions have remained unchanged at exactly 3 per year. The year 2020 is absent from the dataset. Thematically, Data Types & Variables, DBMS/Database concepts, and I/O/Format specifiers dominate the question bank — together appearing in over 200 of 327 questions.

Phase 1: Compact Format (2015–2016)
27 questions per year — 15 MCQs, 9 Short, 3 Long. Short questions were notably fewer, suggesting a more focused assessment format in the early period.
Phase 2: Expanded Format (2017–2019)
Short Questions doubled from 9 to 18 in 2017, raising total paper length to 36 questions — a 33.3% increase in workload. This expansion held stable for three consecutive years.
Phase 3: Settled Format (2021–2025)
From 2021 onward, the paper stabilised at 15 Short Questions and 33 total — a format that has now been consistent for 5 consecutive years. This is the current expected format.

5. Strategic Insights for Exam Preparation

The following recommendations are derived directly from observed data patterns. Each recommendation references the specific quantitative evidence that justifies it.
Prioritised Recommendations for Educators & Students
  • 1

    Anchor MCQ preparation to exactly 15 questions High Priority

    MCQs have been exactly 15 in every year without exception (150/150 consistent). Students should practice 15-question timed MCQ sets. Conceptually, MCQs test: operator syntax, data type memory sizes, format specifiers, loop behaviour, database terminology, and MS-Access object functions — all factual recall that rewards regular revision cards over extended practice.

  • 2

    Prepare for 15 Short Questions (current standard since 2021) High Priority

    The 2021–2025 format of 15 Short Questions has been the consistent standard for 5 consecutive years. Students sitting 2026 exams should prepare for 15 Short Questions. The earlier 18-question format (2017–2019) is unlikely to return but educators should note the historical range of 9–18 if supplementary papers differ.

  • 3

    Dedicate equal depth to C Programming AND DBMS/MS-Access High Priority

    Topic analysis shows near-equal distribution between C language questions and DBMS questions across all 10 years. Neither domain can be de-emphasised. Long Questions (3 per year) consistently mix both — e.g., sub-part (a) on loops, sub-part (b) on data types, sub-part (c) on MS-Access objects.

  • 4

    Focus curriculum depth on the 5 highest-frequency topic clusters High Priority

    Data/DBMS concepts (93 Qs), C preprocessor & file I/O (64 Qs), I/O & format specifiers (54 Qs), MS-Access objects (53 Qs), and Functions in C (41–53 Qs) collectively appear across the largest fraction of all 327 questions. These clusters should receive the deepest curriculum coverage and the most exam practice time.

  • 5

    Update curriculum to include post-2022 advanced topics Medium Priority

    From 2022 onwards, the exam introduced arrays, recursion, normalization, ER diagrams, and from 2025: pointers, structures/unions, bitwise operators, ACID properties, views, indexes, binary search, and storage classes. Educators should confirm these appear in the syllabus and ensure teaching time is allocated — they are now exam-tested concepts, not peripheral extras.

  • 6

    Deprioritise Algorithms & Flowcharts in exam-focused revision Low Priority

    Only 3 questions across 10 years mention flowcharts or algorithms. While conceptually foundational, these topics appear to have been removed from active examination. Students pressed for revision time can de-prioritise them for exam purposes (though not for conceptual understanding).

  • 7

    Practice code output-prediction questions as a distinct skill Medium Priority

    Multiple Short Questions each year ask "what is the output of this code?" — testing increment/decrement operators, loop counters, and conditional logic. These require a specific dry-run mental execution skill. From 2021 onward, these appear consistently in the Short Question section and benefit from dedicated practice sessions separate from concept explanation.

  • 8

    Familiarise with full acronym lists (SQL sub-languages) Medium Priority

    SQL-related short questions (write the full form of DDL, DML, DCL, TCL, DBA, RDBMS, DBMS, IDE) have appeared across most years in both MCQ and Short formats. These are high-yield, low-effort items. A one-page acronym reference covering all database-related abbreviations provides disproportionate exam value.

6. Conclusions & Limitations

Finding 1: Structural Stability with One Key Shift
The exam has been structurally stable with MCQs at 15 and Long Questions at 3 throughout. The only structural change was Short Questions: 9 (2015–16) → 18 (2017–19) → 15 (2021–25). Since 2021, the format has been locked at 33 total questions.
Finding 2: Dual-Domain Exam
The exam consistently and equally assesses C programming fundamentals and database management systems (DBMS / MS-Access). Curriculum planners should maintain equal teaching hours for both domains. No evidence of one domain crowding out the other.
Finding 3: Curriculum Advancement Post-2022
Starting 2022 and accelerating through 2025, the exam tests progressively more advanced topics — normalization, ER diagrams, ACID properties, pointers, structures, binary search, file handling. This represents a clear curriculum advancement trend educators must track.
Finding 4: High Predictability for Exam Prep
The exam's structural consistency makes preparation highly predictable. The 5-year stable format (2021–2025) means educators can confidently model mock papers with 15 MCQs + 15 Short + 3 Long, reflecting actual exam expectations with high fidelity.
