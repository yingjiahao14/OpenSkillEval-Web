Year-by-Year Question Volume Analysis

Question volume is not random: it follows three clear phases. The earliest phase, 2015–2016, contains 27 questions per year. The 2017–2019 phase rises to 36 questions per year, driven entirely by an increase in Short Questions. From 2021–2025, the structure settles at 33 questions per year. Because 2020 is absent, the change from 2019 to 2021 should be interpreted as the next available-year comparison, not necessarily a direct calendar-year shift.

Question Type Composition & Trends

The exam structure is stable in two dimensions and variable in one. MCQs remain constant at 15 per present year, while Long Questions remain constant at 3 per present year. The overall volume changes come from Short Questions: 9 in 2015–2016, 18 in 2017–2019, and 15 in 2021–2025.

Strategic Insights for Exam Preparation

  • Protect the fixed-format baseline: plan annual practice around 15 MCQs and 3 Long Questions, because both counts are constant in every present year of the dataset.
  • Prioritize Short Questions as the flexible section: Short Questions explain all observed volume shifts, moving from 9 to 18 to 15 across the three phases. Teachers should maintain a larger short-answer bank than the minimum needed for any one year.
  • Emphasize C programming fundamentals: category analysis finds 157 question-level hits for C programming/control/syntax keywords. Loops, operators, variables, functions, arrays, pointers, printf, and format specifiers should be practiced in both MCQ and short-answer forms.
  • Keep database concepts central: database-related keywords appear in 102 question-level hits, with repeated attention to tables, keys, records, fields, MS Access, DBMS, primary key, and foreign key concepts.
  • Use long-answer preparation for synthesis: Long Questions are few but consistent. They often align with broader explanations, examples, and comparative topics, so students should practice structured responses rather than only definitions.
  • Do not infer 2020 behavior: since no 2020 rows exist in data.csv, preparation decisions should be based on the observable phases rather than assuming continuity through that missing year.

Conclusions & Limitations

The BIEK Computer Science HSC Part-2 exam structure appears partly stable and partly shifted. The stable parts are MCQs and Long Questions, which remain fixed at 15 and 3 respectively for every present year. The meaningful structural shifts occur in Short Questions, which drive the total volume from 27 in 2015–2016, to 36 in 2017–2019, and to 33 in 2021–2025.

From a curriculum-planning perspective, the data supports a consistent focus on C programming and database foundations. Topic analysis indicates repeated coverage of programming control/syntax and database concepts across the period, while algorithms/flowcharts/data structures and object-oriented programming appear less frequently in the keyword groups used here.

Limitations: the dataset is community-compiled, 2020 is absent from the actual file, annual versus supplementary examinations are not separately encoded, and keyword analysis cannot fully capture semantic equivalence or curriculum chapter boundaries.
