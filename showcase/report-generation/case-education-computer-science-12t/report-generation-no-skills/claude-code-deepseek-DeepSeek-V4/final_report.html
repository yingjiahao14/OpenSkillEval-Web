This dataset covers 10 examination years from 2015 to 2025, comprising 327 individual questions drawn from BIEK Computer Science Paper-II (Theory). The three question types — Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs), Short Questions, and Long Questions — are all represented, with MCQs and Short Questions dominating the dataset.

Note: The year 2020 is absent from the dataset. This is likely due to the COVID-19 pandemic, which disrupted the regular examination schedule across Pakistan. Any trend comparisons across the 2019→2021 boundary should be interpreted with this gap in mind. The dataset technically spans 11 calendar years (2015–2025) but contains data for only 10 of them.

The overall composition shows MCQs making up 45.9% of all questions, Short Questions at 45.0%, and Long Questions at 9.2%. While MCQs and Short Questions are roughly balanced in count, Long Questions are substantially fewer — consistent with typical exam formats where each long question carries significantly more marks and encompasses multiple sub-parts.

MCQ Analysis

MCQs are the most stable component of the exam by a wide margin. Every single year in the dataset — all 10 years from 2015 through 2025 — contains exactly 15 MCQs. There is zero variance: the mean is 15.0 per year, and the count has never deviated from 15. This absolute consistency makes the MCQ section the most predictable part of the BIEK exam.

Short Question Analysis

Short Questions show the greatest volatility across the dataset. The count moved from 9 (2015–2016) to 15 (2017–2019) to 12–18 (the 2017 peak was 18 in one year). The post-2021 period settled at 15 Short Questions per paper. The mean is 14.7 per year. This category is the primary lever the exam board has used to adjust overall paper length.

Long Question Analysis

Long Questions are the bedrock constant: every single year in the dataset has exactly 3 Long Questions. The mean is 3.0 — zero variance. Each Long Question contains 2–3 sub-parts (indicated by the $ delimiter in the question text), meaning students face approximately 6–9 distinct long-form tasks per paper regardless of year.

Structural Insight: The BIEK exam blueprint has two completely fixed anchors (always 15 MCQs, always 3 Long Questions) and one flexible component (Short Questions, ranging 9–18). This means every paper has a guaranteed core of 18 questions, with Short Questions used as the sole mechanism for adjusting overall paper length.

Topic Pattern Findings

  1. Database / DBMS is the single most pervasive topic, appearing in questions across all three formats and all 10 years. Every long question paper includes at least one DBMS-related sub-question. This reflects BIEK's curriculum emphasis on database theory alongside C programming.
  2. C Language Basics (identifiers, variables, constants, data types, preprocessor directives) forms the foundation of the MCQ section. Questions testing knowledge of #include, data type sizes (int = 4 bytes, char = 1 byte), and identifier naming rules appear almost every year.
  3. Control Structures and Loops are consistently tested in both Short and Long formats. Writing programs using for, while, do-while, and if-else constructs appears in the Short Question section of every year from 2017 onward.
  4. Functions and I/O & Formatting show moderate frequency but are heavily concentrated in Short and Long questions. The function topic includes recursion, which appears in 2022–2025 papers — a notable recent addition.
  5. Algorithms & Flowcharts appears primarily in earlier years (2015–2017) and has diminished in recent papers. Conversely, Arrays, Structures & Pointers appears more frequently from 2022 onward, suggesting a gradual shift toward more advanced C programming concepts.
Curriculum Alignment Note: The dataset reveals a C-language-heavy exam (roughly 60–70% of content) with database concepts making up the remaining 30–40%. Within the C portion, basic syntax and control structures dominate, with advanced topics (pointers, structures, file handling) receiving less coverage — particularly in the MCQ and Short Question sections.

5. Strategic Insights for Exam Preparation

For Teachers & Curriculum Planners

  1. Align with the settled blueprint (15/15/3). Since 2021, the exam has consistently used 15 MCQs, 15 Short Questions, and 3 Long Questions. Mock exams and classroom assessments should mirror this structure. Even before 2021, MCQs and Long Questions were fixed (always 15 and 3 respectively); only the Short Question count varied.
  2. Prioritize Database/DBMS topics across all formats. Database concepts (keys, relationships, normalization, MS Access objects, SQL/DDL/DML) appear pervasively — in MCQs, Short, and Long questions. A student weak in DBMS loses marks in all three sections, not just one.
  3. Teach control structures and loops as a unified block. These topics appear together in programming questions, and the data shows that years emphasizing for loops in Short Questions also test if-else/switch in Long Questions. Integrating them improves student coherence.
  4. Monitor the shift toward advanced C topics. The 2022–2025 papers introduce recursion, structures, unions, pointers, and file handling more prominently than earlier years. If this trend continues, curriculum adjustments may be warranted to ensure students are prepared.
  5. Address the flowchart/algorithm gap. The decline in flowchart-related questions since 2017 may indicate either a curriculum de-emphasis or an exam board choice. Curriculum planners should verify whether algorithmic thinking is still a stated learning outcome — if so, the exam may be under-testing it.

For Students

  1. Master Database concepts first. With DBMS appearing in approximately 28% of all questions across all three sections, it offers the highest return on study time investment.
  2. Practice writing C programs by hand. Short Questions in every year from 2017 onward include at least one "write a program" task. Common patterns include: sum/average calculations, number swapping, factorial computation, area calculations, and table printing.
  3. Don't neglect MS Access. Questions about MS Access objects (tables, queries, forms, reports) and data types appear consistently in MCQs and occasionally in Long Questions. These are factual recall questions — high marks for modest effort.
  4. Prepare for output-tracing questions. Short Questions frequently ask students to determine the output of a given C program fragment (nested loops, increment operators, conditional statements). This tests deeper understanding beyond rote memorization.
  5. Learn the acronyms. Full-form questions (DML, DDL, SQL, DBA, RDBMS, IDE, DCL, TCL) appear in at least 7 of the 10 available years. These are consistent, low-effort marks.

6. Conclusions & Limitations

Summary of Key Findings

  1. The exam has stabilized post-COVID. After structural changes in 2017 (expansion) and 2021 (contraction), the paper format has been perfectly consistent at 15 MCQs + 15 Short + 3 Long for five consecutive years (2021–2025). This represents a settled blueprint that educators can rely on for planning.
  2. Long Questions are the fixed pillar, and MCQs are equally stable. Exactly 3 Long Questions and exactly 15 MCQs appear in every single year of the dataset — zero deviation across 10 years for either. Together, these two sections form a completely invariant core of 18 questions per paper.
  3. Short Questions are the sole adjustment valve. All year-over-year changes in total question count are attributable entirely to changes in the Short Question section (ranging from 9 to 18). MCQs have been exactly 15 and Long Questions exactly 3 in every year — only Short Questions have ever varied.
  4. Database/DBMS is the dominant topic, followed by C Language Basics and Control Structures/Loops. These three topic clusters account for the majority of exam content across all question types.
  5. A gradual curriculum evolution is detectable. Recent papers (2022–2025) show increased emphasis on recursion, structures, pointers, and file handling — topics that were rare or absent in 2015–2017 papers — suggesting the BIEK curriculum may be slowly modernizing.
  6. The exam is highly structured and predictable in both format and topic distribution. This is valuable information for educators: the historical patterns strongly indicate what future papers will look like.

Limitations

  1. Missing 2020 data. The absence of the 2020 examination year (COVID-19 disruption) means the pre- vs. post-pandemic comparison is based on 2019→2021, not a continuous year-over-year transition. The nature of any 2020 exam (if one was held) is unknown from this dataset.
  2. No distinction between annual and supplementary exams. The dataset does not encode whether questions come from the main annual examination or the supplementary (retake) examination. Some year-to-year variation may reflect the inclusion or exclusion of supplementary papers rather than changes in exam design.
  3. Community-compiled, not official. The question bank was manually transcribed from scanned papers. Minor transcription errors (typos, formatting inconsistencies) may exist, though the dataset appears well-curated overall.
  4. Limited sample size for trend detection. With only 327 questions across 10 data-years, fine-grained statistical analysis (e.g., detecting subtle shifts in specific sub-topics) has limited power. Topic trends should be treated as indicative rather than definitive.
  5. Paper-I content is excluded. This dataset covers Paper-II (Theory) only. Practical/lab examination content (Paper-I) is not represented, so the full examination experience is only partially captured.
  6. Topic classification is keyword-based. The topic grouping uses keyword-synonym matching, which may miss questions that discuss a topic using unanticipated terminology, or may over-count questions that mention a keyword in passing without the topic being the primary focus.
  7. No mark-weight data. The dataset contains question text only — no information about how many marks each question carries. A Short Question with a complex program-writing task may be weighted differently than a simple definition Short Question, but this analysis treats all questions within a type equally.
Bottom Line: Has the BIEK Computer Science exam structure remained stable? Largely, yes — especially since 2021. The post-COVID period shows a firmly settled format. The pre-2020 period saw one major structural adjustment (2017 expansion), but the core architecture — 3 Long Questions anchoring the paper, with MCQs and Short Questions filling the balance — has been consistent throughout. Educators can plan with confidence that the 15/15/3 blueprint is the current standard, while remaining attentive to the gradual shift toward more advanced programming topics in recent years.
