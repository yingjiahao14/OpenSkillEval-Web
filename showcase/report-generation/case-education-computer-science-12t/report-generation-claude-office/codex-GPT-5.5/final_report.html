Year-by-Year Question Volume Analysis

The exam structure is highly patterned. Total annual volume begins at 27 questions in 2015–2016, rises to 36 questions in 2017–2019, and settles at 33 questions for every observed year from 2021 through 2025. The only source of these volume changes is the number of Short questions.

Main trend: MCQs remain fixed at 15 questions every observed year and Long questions remain fixed at 3. Short questions shift from 9 in 2015–2016, to 18 in 2017–2019, then to 15 in 2021–2025.

Question Type Composition & Trends

The composition changes materially even though two components are stable. MCQs and Long questions are constant in absolute count, so their percentages fall or rise inversely with Short-question volume.

  • MCQs Count is stable at 15 per observed year; the overall total is 150, or 45.9% of all rows.
  • Short Count is the structural variable: 9–18 per observed year, with 147 total rows (45.0%).
  • Long Count is stable at 3 per observed year; the overall total is 30, or 9.2% of all rows.
  • The largest positive observed shift is 2017, when total volume increases by 9 questions (33.3%) versus 2016.
  • The only negative observed shift is 2021, when total volume decreases by 3 questions (-8.3%) versus 2019 because 2020 is absent from the CSV.

Strategic Insights for Exam Preparation

The data indicates a stable exam framework with one meaningful adjustment area: Short-question volume. Preparation should therefore balance predictable format coverage with deeper practice on the topics that recur across question text.

  • Plan for fixed-format anchors: include MCQ drills every year because the dataset shows exactly 15 MCQs in each observed year, making them a predictable component of the paper.
  • Prioritize Short-question readiness: Short questions are the only type that changes annual volume; teachers should maintain banks of concise definitions, comparisons, syntax explanations, and output-tracing prompts.
  • Keep Long-answer preparation focused but consistent: Long questions appear at 3 per observed year, so students need structured answer practice without letting it crowd out MCQ and Short-question breadth.
  • Reinforce database concepts throughout the year: database-related terms are the largest topic family by coverage in this keyword model, including tables, records, keys, relationships, and MS Access objects.
  • Integrate C-language fundamentals with control flow: repeated references to functions, statements, loops, operators, format specifiers, and data types suggest that syntax knowledge and program-behavior reasoning should be taught together.
  • Use year bands for mock-paper design: a 27-question mock reflects 2015–2016, a 36-question mock reflects 2017–2019, and a 33-question mock reflects 2021–2025; avoid inventing a 2020 pattern from this dataset.

Conclusions & Limitations

The central finding is that the BIEK Computer Science exam structure appears stable in its MCQ and Long-question anchors, while Short-question volume has shifted in clear blocks. Educators should respond to the Short-question variability and the persistent recurrence of database and C-programming topics.

  • Stable elements: MCQs are fixed at 15 and Long questions at 3 in every observed year in data.csv.
  • Meaningful shift: Short questions move from 9 to 18 to 15 across the observed year bands, changing both total volume and question-type percentages.
  • Recurring content: database/DBMS vocabulary and C-language concepts dominate the extracted keywords and topic families.
  • Missing year: no 2020 rows are present, so the report does not claim what happened in the 2020 paper.
  • Dataset limitation: the CSV does not distinguish annual vs. supplementary papers, and the source is community compiled; minor transcription or delimiter inconsistencies may exist.
  • Method limitation: topic analysis is keyword-based, not a full semantic curriculum mapping; terms can overlap across concepts and question types.
