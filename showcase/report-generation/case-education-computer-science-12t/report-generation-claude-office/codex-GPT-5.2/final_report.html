Question Type Composition & Trends

Composition is highly structured. Across all observed years, MCQs = 15 and Long = 3 appear every year. The remaining variation is in Short questions (9 in 2015–2016, 18 in 2017–2019, and 15 in 2021–2025). This yields three distinct composition regimes.

Interpretation for educators

  • Exam practice sets should reliably include 15 MCQs and 3 long questions as a stable core.
  • Short-question weight is the main lever that changes total paper volume across years in this dataset.
  • Because MCQs and Long questions are constant, shifts in “difficulty feel” may be driven more by content than by section counts.

Strategic Insights for Exam Preparation

The most actionable patterns in this dataset are (1) stable section counts for MCQs and Long questions and (2) database + programming-fundamentals dominance in recurring keywords. Recommendations below connect directly to these observed signals.

For teachers

  • Align practice papers to the stable core: build drills that always include 15 MCQs and 3 Long questions, then vary Short count depending on target years.
  • Prioritize database fundamentals: the strongest recurring signals are database, table, and key (e.g., “primary key”, “foreign key”).
  • Reinforce programming mechanics: frequent vocabulary includes function, loop, statement, operator, and printf, suggesting repeated assessment of core C concepts.
  • Short-question strategy: since total volume changes are explained by Short questions, prepare students for rapid, definition/difference-style responses (e.g., “format specifier”, “data type”).

For curriculum planners

  • Stability signal: repeated fixed counts (MCQs=15, Long=3) suggest a consistent blueprint in the observed data; review whether official policy mirrors this.
  • Coverage check: database and programming tokens dominate frequency lists; if the curriculum intends broader breadth, ensure underrepresented topics are not being crowded out.
  • Data-aware interpretation: dataset does not label annual vs supplementary papers; do not treat year-to-year changes as definitive policy shifts without cross-verification.

Conclusions & Limitations

Based strictly on data.csv, the BIEK Computer Science paper structure appears mostly stable across observed years, with variation concentrated in the number of Short questions. Question content signals emphasize databases (keys, tables, relational model) and programming fundamentals (functions, loops, statements, operators, printf).

Limitations (important for interpretation)

  • Missing year: 2020 is absent in the dataset, so the 2019 → 2021 transition may be misleading if 2020 differs.
  • Annual vs supplementary: the dataset does not encode exam session type; counts per “year” could mix papers.
  • Text parsing: $ delimiters are used for MCQ options / subparts; keyword analysis replaces them with spaces, but nuanced phrasing may still be lost.
  • Topic inference: keyword frequency reflects vocabulary recurrence, not a full syllabus map; it should complement (not replace) curriculum standards mapping.
