Dataset & Examination Overview

This report is based on a community-compiled dataset of 327 questions drawn from BIEK Computer Science Paper-II (HSC Part-2) examinations spanning 2015 to 2025. The dataset covers 10 distinct exam years — 2020 data is absent from the source dataset.

Structural Architecture of the Exam

Each examined year follows a consistent three-part structure: a fixed block of 15 Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs), a variable block of short-answer questions, and exactly 3 long-answer questions. The most notable structural distinction across the dataset's timeline is the evolution of the short-question count — which increased and then contracted, marking three identifiable phases.

Key structural constant: MCQs have been exactly 15 per year and Long Questions exactly 3 per year throughout the entire 11-year span. The only variable element is the short-question count, which changed twice: from 9 (2015–2016) to 18 (2017–2019), then settling at 15 (2021–present).

Data Source & Quality

The dataset (cs-12th-question-bank-biek-su.csv) was sourced from Kaggle (user: sadamumer), derived from official BIEK papers and publicly scanned past papers. It contains 4 columns: id, year, type, and question. The question text uses a $ delimiter to separate MCQ options and long-question sub-parts. The dataset does not distinguish between annual and supplementary examination sittings, and 2020 data is entirely absent.

Key Observations

The 2017 spike is the single most significant structural event in this dataset. The short-question count doubled from 9 to 18, increasing the total paper length by 33.3% (from 27 to 36 questions). This expansion was sustained for three years (2017–2019) before being trimmed to 15 short questions in 2021 — a reduction of 3 questions (−8.3%) that has held stable through 2025.

The data reveals that once the board sets a question count pattern, it maintains it for multi-year periods. The current 33-question format (15 MCQ + 15 Short + 3 Long) represents the longest unbroken stable run in the dataset, now in its 5th consecutive year since 2021.

MCQ Proportion Evolution

In 2015–2016, MCQs comprised 55.6% of each paper — meaning more than half the marks came from multiple choice. When the short-question block doubled in 2017, MCQ share fell sharply to 41.7%, where it remained through 2019. The current phase (2021–2025) sits at 45.5% — MCQs recovering slightly but still below the early baseline.

Long Question Stability

Long questions have been remarkably stable: exactly 3 per year, always structured as three multi-part questions numbered Q3, Q4, and Q5. In earlier years (2015–2018) each long question had 2 sub-parts (a, b); from 2017 onward a 3-sub-part format (a, b, c) became common. The 3-question, multi-part long format persists unchanged through 2025 — indicating that the board treats this as a fundamental structural constant.

Curricular implication: The current exam format (2021–2025) gives equal proportional weight to MCQs and short questions (~45.5% each). This equal split — a departure from the MCQ-heavy early years — signals that educators should invest nearly equal preparation time in factual recall (MCQs) and written explanation (short questions).

Perennial Topics (Present in 9–10 of 10 Years)

FFunctions in C
Appears in all 10 examined years. Consistently tested as Short questions (define, prototype, types) and Long questions (explain with example, user-defined functions, recursion). This is the single highest-frequency topic.
LLoops & Iteration
Present in 9/10 years. for, while, do-while, and nested loops are recurring across all question types. Loop-related output tracing (predict the output) appears in Short questions from 2015 onward.
KDatabase Keys
Present in all 10 years. Primary key, foreign key, composite key, candidate key, and alternate key are all tested. Earlier years focus on primary/foreign key; 2023–2025 introduce candidate, super key, and referential integrity.
DDatabase & DBMS
Present in 9/10 years. Covers database models (hierarchical, network, relational), DBMS advantages, DBA roles, DDL/DML, and MS Access objects. One of the two dominant content pillars alongside C programming.
OOperators in C
Present in 9/10 years. Arithmetic, relational, logical, assignment, and increment/decrement operators appear routinely. 2025 introduces bitwise and ternary operators — an expansion of the operators subtopic.
SSwitch Statement
Present in 7/10 years, often paired with break/continue. Appears in both Short (syntax questions) and Long (explain with example). A reliable exam staple in C control structures.

Long Question Sub-Topic Patterns

Across all 30 long questions (10 years × 3 questions), the structural pattern is highly consistent: one C-programming topic + one database/DBMS topic + one control-structures/loops topic per paper. This tri-part thematic balance has been maintained every single year, providing a reliable framework for targeted preparation.

Section 05

Strategic Insights for Exam Preparation

The following recommendations are drawn directly from the data patterns identified above. They are organized by audience and priority level based on historical question frequency and structural stability.

For Students: Preparation Priorities

1Master C Functions (Highest Priority)
Functions appear in all 10 years across all three question types. Know: defining functions, function prototypes, call-by-value vs. call-by-reference, user-defined functions, and recursion. Program-writing on functions is a near-certain short/long question.
2All Loop Types in C
for, while, do-while, and nested loops appear in 9/10 years. Practice writing programs with each, converting between loop types, and tracing output. Nested loops and loop-based programs (tables, series, patterns) are perennial staples.
3Database Keys (Primary, Foreign, Composite)
Present in all 10 years. Questions frequently ask to distinguish between key types, explain usage, and relate to referential integrity. Since 2023, candidate key, super key, and alternate key have also appeared — expand prep accordingly.
4C Operators (All Categories)
Arithmetic, relational, logical, assignment, and increment/decrement operators appear in 9/10 years. From 2025, bitwise and ternary operators are also being tested. Know operator precedence — a recurring short question.
5Database Models & DBMS Concepts
Hierarchical, relational, and network models appear repeatedly. DBMS advantages, DBA roles, DDL/DML/DCL/SQL, and MS Access objects (table, query, form, report, macro, module) appear across all phases. This is the second dominant content pillar.
62025+ Emerging Topics
Arrays, structures, recursion, normalization, pointers, file handling, and ACID properties are all appearing with increasing frequency. For 2026 preparation, include these topics — especially arrays (3/5 recent years) and structures/unions.

For Teachers: Curriculum Alignment Guidance

ABalanced MCQ + Short Prep is Now Essential
Since 2021, MCQs and short questions each carry ~45.5% of the question count. Instruction should allocate roughly equal time to fact-level recall (MCQs) and written conceptual explanation (short questions) — the earlier MCQ-heavy preparation strategy no longer reflects exam reality.
BLong Questions Follow a Predictable Template
Every year's three long questions reliably cover: (1) a C control structure or loops topic, (2) a functions or data types topic, (3) a database/DBMS topic. Teachers can use this template to design mock exams that closely mirror the actual examination pattern.
CThe 2025 Curriculum Shift Warrants Attention
The 2025 paper introduced more new topic areas than any other single year in the dataset. If this reflects a curriculum update, future papers will likely repeat these topics. Recommend updating lesson plans to include: binary search algorithms, file handling operations, ER diagrams, ACID properties, and storage classes.
DMS Access Remains Consistently Tested
MS Access objects (table, query, form, report, macro, module) and their uses appear in every phase of the exam. This topic is frequently tested at the MCQ level (object identification) and in short/long questions (explain uses, create table steps). Do not deprioritize it despite being "older" software.

Section 06

Conclusions & Limitations

The 11-year record reveals a broadly stable examination framework with two notable structural inflection points, a consistently dual-pillar content strategy, and recent signs of curriculum broadening that merit close monitoring.

Key Findings Summary

Structural Stability with Defined Phases
MCQs (always 15) and Long Questions (always 3) have never changed. The exam has passed through three short-question phases: 9 (2015–16), 18 (2017–19), 15 (2021–present). The current 33-question format is the most stable phase in the dataset.
Two Immovable Content Pillars
C Programming and Database/DBMS collectively account for nearly all question content across every year. This dual focus has remained constant regardless of question count changes — it is the invariant core of BIEK Computer Science Paper-II.
2025: Broadening Scope
The 2025 paper introduced more new sub-topics than any prior year: binary search, ACID properties, bitwise operators, ER diagrams, storage classes, file handling, structures/unions. Whether this is a one-off or signals a curriculum update warrants ongoing monitoring.
Exam Predictability is High
The consistent tri-part long-question structure (C + Database + Control Structures) and the high multi-year presence of 6–8 core topics mean well-targeted preparation can cover the vast majority of likely exam content with high confidence.

Data Limitations

1. Missing 2020 data. The source dataset contains no entries for 2020. This represents a gap in the 11-year timeline and means any trend line across 2019–2021 is based on the two flanking years only. The true 2020 pattern — whether it continued the Expansion phase format or anticipated the Consolidation phase — is unknown from this dataset.
2. Annual vs. Supplementary papers. The dataset does not distinguish between annual and supplementary BIEK sittings. Some years may aggregate questions from both, which could artificially inflate question counts and topic breadth for those years.
3. Transcription accuracy. As a community-compiled dataset, minor transcription errors in the question column are possible. Topic frequency analysis is based on keyword matching in question text — typographical variants may cause slight undercounting of some topics.
4. Statistical power. With only 327 rows across 10 years and many sub-topic categories appearing just once or twice, fine-grained frequency differences should be interpreted cautiously. Broad patterns (the dual C+Database pillar, the three structural phases) are robust; single-year emergence of new topics is a signal, not a confirmed trend.

Recommendations for Follow-Up Analysis

Future work that would meaningfully extend this analysis includes: (1) sourcing the 2020 paper to fill the dataset gap; (2) coding annual vs. supplementary paper labels to separate their question sets; (3) tracking whether 2025's new topics (ACID, ER diagrams, binary search, storage classes) recur in 2026; and (4) conducting a marks-weighting analysis if official mark schemes become available, since question counts alone do not reflect marks allocation within long questions.
