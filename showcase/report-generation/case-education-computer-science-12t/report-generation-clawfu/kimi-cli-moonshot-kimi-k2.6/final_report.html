✅ Conclusions & Limitations

Key Findings Summary

  1. Structural Evolution, Not Stagnation: The BIEK Computer Science exam has undergone meaningful structural shifts — a 33% expansion in 2017 and an 8.3% contraction in 2021 — before stabilizing. Educators should not assume the format is permanently fixed.
  2. Anchor-and-Lever Design: MCQs (15) and Long Questions (3) serve as fixed anchors, while Short Questions (9–18) act as the adjustable lever. This hierarchical stability model allows controlled evolution.
  3. Topic Concentration: Four topic clusters (Data Types, Operators, Functions, Keys & Relationships) dominate the assessment landscape, accounting for the majority of question mentions.
  4. Balanced Modern Format: The 2021–2025 period achieves near-equal weighting between MCQs and Short Questions (45.5% each), suggesting a deliberate move toward balanced assessment of recall and explanatory skills.

Data Limitations

  • Missing Year (2020): No data exists for 2020, preventing a complete 11-year continuous trend line. The gap coincides with the COVID-19 pandemic and likely reflects examination cancellations.
  • Annual vs. Supplementary Ambiguity: The dataset does not distinguish between annual and supplementary examination papers. Years with both may have higher question counts, though the observed stability within periods suggests this is not a major confounder.
  • Community-Compiled Source: The dataset is non-official and may contain minor transcription errors. The non-standard $ delimiter requires careful parsing and may occasionally misrepresent question boundaries.
  • Small Sample Size: With 327 questions across 10 years, fine-grained topic-level trend detection has limited statistical power. Year-to-year topic fluctuations should be interpreted cautiously.
  • Paper-I Exclusion: Only Paper-II (theory) is represented. A complete curriculum picture would require Paper-I data.
