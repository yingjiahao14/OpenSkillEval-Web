1. Dataset & Examination Overview

The dataset comprises 327 individual examination questions drawn from BIEK Computer Science Paper-II (theory) across a spanning period from 2015 to 2025. The Board of Intermediate Education Karachi (BIEK) conducts these HSC Part-2 examinations annually, with a supplementary round occasionally offered.

Key Scope Notes

Two important structural facts must be stated upfront:

  • 2020 is absent — The dataset contains no questions for the 2020 examination year. This almost certainly reflects the cancellation of in-person BIEK examinations during the COVID-19 pandemic. This gap must be factored into any year-over-year trend interpretation.
  • Supplementary vs. annual papers are not distinguished — The dataset does not label whether a question originated from an annual or supplementary examination. Some years may aggregate questions from both paper sessions.
Data integrity reminder: This is a community-compiled dataset. While it has been manually verified, minor transcription errors may exist. All statistics in this report are derived exclusively from the 327 rows in data.csv.

Structural Findings

Three remarkable patterns emerge from the type-level analysis:

  • MCQs are perfectly invariant: Exactly 15 MCQ questions appear in every single year without exception — the most stable element of the entire exam structure across all 10 years.
  • Long Questions are structurally fixed: Exactly 3 Long Questions per year have appeared in every year, meaning the long-answer component has never changed in volume.
  • Short Questions drive all structural change: Every change in total question count is attributable entirely to the Short Question section (9 → 18 → 15), which acts as the exam's primary sizing dial.

Across all years, MCQs constitute 45.9% of all questions (150/327), Short Questions 45.0% (147/327), and Long Questions 9.2% (30/327). The Long Question share is consistently the smallest at roughly 1/11th of the paper.

5. Strategic Insights for Exam Preparation

The patterns in this dataset, while derived from a single subject's question bank, carry actionable implications for both students and teachers. The insights below are tied directly to observed frequencies and structural facts.

1. MCQs Are a Rock-Solid Bet

Exactly 15 MCQ questions appear every single year without variation. Students should treat MCQ preparation as non-negotiable and can rely on the count being stable. The topics循环 (loops), operators, data types, and database basics appear reliably in MCQ form.

2. Short Questions Carry the Most Weight in Era 3

In the current format (2021–2025), Short Questions make up 45.5% of the paper. With 15 Short Questions per session, students must practise writing concise, accurate definitions and explanations. Topics like "difference between X and Y" and "write a program" are perennial.

3. Focus Revision on Database & DBMS First

Database & DBMS content accounts for 19.6% of all questions — the single largest topic block. Key sub-topics include: primary/foreign key distinctions, DML vs. DDL vs. DCL, relational model characteristics, and MS Access objects. These appear across MCQ, Short, and Long formats.

4. Loops + Control Structures Together Dominate

Combining Loops (12.2%) and Control Structures (17.1%) yields nearly 30% of all questions. The while/for/do-while triumvirate and if/switch branching are the most tested programming concepts. Nested loops, break/continue distinctions, and loop-output prediction questions appear regularly in Short format.

5. Three Long Questions — Three Fixed Themes

Long Question 3 consistently covers loops and/or control structures; Question 4 covers operators, I/O functions, or database models; Question 5 covers functions or MS Access objects. This three-part structure has remained consistent across all eras. Teachers can use this as a reliable framework for extended-answer practice.

6. Program-Writing Is a Recurring Short Question Theme

Short Questions requesting C program code appear in nearly every year's Short section (radius/area, swapping, tables, factorial, series). The printf/scanf syntax and loop constructs are essential building blocks for these responses.

7. Emerging Topics: Pointers, Structures, Normalization

Advanced topics such as pointers, structures, and normalization appear only in the 2022 and 2025 papers. This may signal a curriculum expansion in the current era. Students should not neglect these newer additions despite their historically low frequency.

8. Expect No Major Format Changes

The exam has maintained structural stability from 2021 onwards (33 questions, 15 MCQ + 15 Short + 3 Long). Given this five-year stability, the current format is likely to persist. Teachers can confidently plan around the Era 3 structure for upcoming examination cycles.

6. Conclusions & Limitations

Key Findings Summary

  • The BIEK Computer Science exam has not remained structurally stable — it has undergone two distinct structural transitions (2017 expansion, 2021 contraction).
  • MCQs are perfectly stable at 15 per year — the most reliable element of the paper. Long Questions are equally fixed at 3 per year.
  • Short Questions are the sole variable in the exam structure, accounting for all year-to-year volume changes (9 → 18 → 15).
  • The current format (2021–2025) appears fully stabilised at 33 questions with an MCQ/Short/Long split of 15/15/3.
  • Database & DBMS is the single most-tested topic area (19.6%), followed by Control Structures (17.1%) and Loops (12.2%).
  • Advanced topics such as File Handling, Pointers, and Structures appear very infrequently and may be under-represented in the community-compiled dataset.

Data Limitations

  • 2020 missing entirely: No data exists for the 2020 examination year. Any trend line spanning 2019→2021 has a gap that cannot be interpolated with confidence.
  • No annual/supplementary distinction: The dataset does not label papers as annual or supplementary. Some year counts may combine two paper sessions.
  • Community-compiled source: While the dataset is typed and verified, it is not an official BIEK publication. Minor transcription errors in question text are possible.
  • Compact size: With only 327 data points, fine-grained statistical tests (e.g., detecting subtle topic shifts) have limited power. Patterns should be interpreted as directional indicators rather than statistically validated findings.
  • Paper scope: The dataset covers only Paper-II (theory). Practical questions and Paper-I content are excluded unless they overlap with Paper-II topics.
