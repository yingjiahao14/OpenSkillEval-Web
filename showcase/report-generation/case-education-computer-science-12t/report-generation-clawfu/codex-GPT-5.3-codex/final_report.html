Question Type Composition & Trends

Across the full period, the exam composition is dominated by Short questions (44.95%), followed by MCQs (45.87%) and Long questions (9.17%). Mean yearly counts are MCQs 13.64, Short 13.36, and Long 2.73.

  • MCQs trend around a narrow band in most years, indicating recurring objective-assessment presence.
  • Short questions form the largest instructional weight, so preparation should prioritize concise conceptual articulation.
  • Long questions remain the smallest share, but are consistently present in nearly every year.

Strategic Insights for Exam Preparation

  • Allocate time by historical weight: Short-question practice should receive the largest share of classroom drills because it is the dominant historical format.
  • Maintain MCQ readiness all year: MCQs are consistently represented and should be reinforced through periodic mixed-topic quizzes.
  • Target recurrent concepts: High-frequency terms (e.g., top-ranked keywords above) should anchor revision plans, chapter sequencing, and formative assessments.
  • Use year-aware mock papers: Build mock exams using each year’s observed type mix rather than fixed assumptions, especially when YoY changes are positive or negative outliers.
  • Balance short and deep responses: Even with fewer long questions, long-form answering remains a persistent requirement and should be trained with rubric-based marking.

Conclusions & Limitations

The 2015–2025 record indicates a broadly persistent three-part exam structure (MCQs, Short, Long), with moderate year-to-year volume variation rather than structural discontinuity. For educators, this supports a stable preparation model weighted toward short answers, with continuous MCQ and long-answer integration.

  • The dataset does not explicitly separate annual and supplementary papers.
  • The source is community-compiled; minor transcription inconsistencies may exist.
  • Keyword analysis is frequency-based and does not fully disambiguate context/synonyms.
  • No missing years were found in the 2015–2025 range in data.csv.
