1. Dataset & Examination Overview

The dataset comprises 327 examination questions spanning the academic years 2015 to 2025, covering 10 out of 11 possible years. The examinations are organized by the Board of Intermediate Education Karachi (BIEK) for Computer Science Paper-II (HSC Part-2 / Intermediate Second Year).

Data Gap: No data is available for the year(s): 2020. The 2020 examinations were likely affected by the COVID-19 pandemic, which disrupted the academic calendar across Pakistan. All analyses account for this gap; trend lines may show discontinuities at these points.

Stability Assessment

Across the 11-year period, the proportion of MCQs has ranged from 42% to 56%, Short Questions from 33% to 50%, and Long Questions from 8% to 11%.

Critical Finding: MCQ and Long Question Counts Are Perfectly Fixed

Across all 10 years with data, MCQs are exactly 15 per year and Long Questions are exactly 3 per year — with zero deviation (CV = 0.0% for both). All year-to-year variation in total question volume is driven entirely by Short Questions, which range from 9 to 18 per year. This structural rigidity in MCQs and Long Questions reflects a deliberate BIEK examination design choice, not random variation. The exam's total length fluctuates solely based on how many Short Questions are included.

The coefficient of variation (CV) confirms this: MCQs and Long Questions are perfectly stable (CV = 0.0% each), while Short questions show meaningful year-to-year fluctuation (CV = 22.5%).

The BIEK examination structure has a rigid MCQ/Long framework (fixed counts) with a flexible Short Question component that adjusts total paper length. Short question counts shifted from 9 (2015–2016) to 18 (2017–2019) and settled at 15 (2021–2025), suggesting a mid-period expansion followed by a moderation. Educators and students should be aware that total paper length depends primarily on the Short Question section, which is the only variable component.

5. Strategic Insights for Exam Preparation

The following recommendations are derived directly from the data patterns identified in the preceding sections. They are intended to help educators align their teaching strategies and students prioritize their preparation efforts.

1. Prioritize Short-Answer Questions — They Dominate Every Year

Short-answer questions represent 45.0% of all questions across the dataset and have the MCQs year-to-year distribution (CV = 22.5%). In the most recent year (2025), Short questions accounted for 45% of the paper. Students should allocate a proportional amount of preparation time to practicing concise, accurate short-form answers covering both C programming and database topics.

2. C Programming Fundamentals Are Non-Negotiable

"Function" appears 44 times, "program" 46 times, and "loop" 34 times — making them the highest-frequency technical keywords overall. Control structures (if-else, switch, loops), functions (definition, prototype, recursion), and I/O functions (printf, scanf) are tested consistently every year. Every student must have working knowledge of writing, reading, and debugging C programs.

3. Database Concepts Are the Second Pillar

"Database" appears 52 times, "data" 59 times, and MS Access-specific terms (table, query, form, report) recur consistently. The primary–foreign key distinction, relational database models, and DDL/DML definitions are among the most frequently tested specific concepts. Teachers should ensure equal coverage of DBMS theory alongside C programming.

4. Recent Papers Show Curriculum Modernization

Questions about pointers, structures, unions, ER diagrams, and normalization (1NF) appear predominantly in 2023–2025 papers. This suggests the curriculum is evolving to include more advanced topics. Educators should review the most recent 2–3 years of papers carefully when preparing students for upcoming examinations, as these are the strongest indicators of the current exam format.

5. MCQ Preparation: Focus on Precision and Terminology

MCQs constitute 45.9% of all questions. The most common MCQ topics center on: format specifiers (e.g., %d, %f, %s), operator types, loop characteristics, database keys, MS Access objects, and C syntax rules. MCQ questions test precise recall — students should drill terminology and syntax specifics rather than relying on general conceptual understanding.

6. Long Questions Reward Structured, Multi-Part Answers

Long questions (averaging 3 per year) consistently contain multiple sub-parts separated by the "$" delimiter. The most frequent long-question topics are: function definitions with examples, database models, control structure explanations, and data type descriptions. Students should practice writing structured answers that address each sub-part clearly and separately.

6. Conclusions & Limitations

Key Findings

  1. Exam structure has a rigid core with a flexible component. MCQs (15 per year) and Long Questions (3 per year) are perfectly fixed across all years. All variation in total paper length — from 27 to 36 questions — is driven entirely by the Short Question count, which has ranged from 9 to 18.
  2. Two knowledge domains dominate: C programming and database management. Keyword analysis confirms these are the twin pillars of the BIEK Computer Science Paper-II. Topics outside these domains (e.g., networking, web technologies, cybersecurity) are effectively absent from the dataset.
  3. Question volume varies by over 40% year-to-year. The range from 27 to 36 questions per year indicates inconsistent paper length, which may affect student time management expectations.
  4. Recent years (2023–2025) show evidence of curriculum modernization with the introduction of more advanced topics like pointers, structures, normalization, and ER diagrams.
  5. MCQs serve a distinct assessment purpose: testing precise factual recall of syntax, terminology, and definitions, while Short and Long questions assess explanatory depth and programming ability.

Limitations

  • Missing 2020 data: No examination paper data is available for 2020, likely due to COVID-19 pandemic disruptions. This creates a gap in the 11-year trend series and limits the statistical power of year-over-year comparisons around that period.
  • No distinction between annual and supplementary papers: The dataset does not encode whether a question comes from the main annual examination or a supplementary/second-chance paper. This may inflate question counts for certain years if both papers were compiled.
  • Topic analysis is keyword-based, not semantic: The keyword frequency approach captures surface-level term occurrences but does not account for question difficulty, cognitive depth, or the specific context in which a term is used.
  • Small sample size for fine-grained analysis: With only 327 total questions, sub-analyses (e.g., per-topic trends within a single question type) may lack statistical robustness.
  • Community-compiled dataset: Minor transcription errors may exist. The data was manually extracted from scanned papers and typed by community contributors.
  • Text encoding conventions: The $ delimiter used within the question text column was accounted for during preprocessing, but edge cases in parsing may exist for complex question structures.
Recommendation for Future Data Collection: To enable more robust analysis, future iterations of this dataset should include a column distinguishing annual vs. supplementary examinations, a question-level difficulty rating, and explicit topic tags assigned by subject matter experts rather than relying on keyword extraction.
