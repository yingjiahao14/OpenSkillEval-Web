Trend Lines by Question Type

MCQs: MCQ counts have been remarkably stable at 15 questions per year from 2015 through 2025, forming a fixed backbone of the exam. This consistency means MCQ preparation is always essential — students can reliably expect 15 MCQs.
Short Questions: This category has seen the most variability — ranging from 9 in the early years to 18 at peak. The growth in total questions is almost entirely attributable to the expansion of short-answer questions. Teachers should allocate proportionally more practice time to this format.
Long Questions: Long questions have remained consistently at 3 per paper across all years, indicating a stable essay component. The emphasis is always on depth of understanding — typically covering C programming, database concepts, and MS Access in equal measure.

Recommendations for Teachers

1. Prioritize Short-Answer Preparation
Short questions have grown from ~9 per paper (2015) to ~15 per paper (2025). This is where students face the most variety and where marks are densest. Practice answering concisely — definitions, syntax, small programs, and comparisons are the dominant formats.
2. MCQ Mastery is Non-Negotiable
With a fixed count of 15 MCQs every year, this section offers the most predictable marks. Focus on: data types and memory sizes, operator identification, MS Access object names, database terminology (keys, tuples, fields), and format specifiers.
3. Long Questions Follow a Pattern
Exactly 3 long questions appear each year, typically covering: (a) a C programming concept with code, (b) a database/DBMS theory question, and (c) an MS Access or practical application question. Prepare template answers for loops, functions, switch statements, data types, database models, and MS Access objects.
4. Track Emerging Topics
Recent papers (2024–2025) have introduced concepts like structures, unions, pointers, file handling, binary search, ER diagrams, ACID properties, storage classes, and normalization. These represent a broadening of the syllabus that teachers should begin incorporating into preparation materials.

Recommendations for Curriculum Planners

Structural Evolution: The exam has evolved from a compact 24-question format to a more expansive 30+ question format. This expansion was entirely in the Short Question category, suggesting the board values broader topic coverage with concise answers. Curriculum planners should ensure teaching time reflects this — more breadth, more practice with definitions and short programs. The core syllabus pillars (C programming + DBMS/MS Access) remain unchanged across the full 11-year span, confirming they are board-mandated fundamentals that should never be deprioritized.

6. Conclusions & Limitations

Summary of key findings and important caveats about the dataset.

Key Findings

  1. The exam structure shifted decisively around 2017, expanding from ~24 to ~33+ questions per paper, driven entirely by growth in Short Questions.
  2. MCQs (15/paper) and Long Questions (3/paper) have remained fixed across all 11 years — a remarkably stable exam backbone.
  3. Two content pillars dominate every paper: C programming fundamentals and DBMS/MS Access concepts. Together, these account for the vast majority of all questions.
  4. The 2020 exam year is absent from the dataset, most likely due to COVID-19 disruptions. This creates a one-year gap in the trend line.
  5. Recent papers (2024–2025) show syllabus broadening, with new topics like structures, pointers, file handling, ER diagrams, ACID properties, and normalization appearing for the first time.
  6. Short Questions offer the highest return on study investment — they comprise the largest and most variable portion of the exam, covering the widest range of topics.
