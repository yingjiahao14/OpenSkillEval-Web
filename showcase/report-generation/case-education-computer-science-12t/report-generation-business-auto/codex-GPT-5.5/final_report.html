1. Dataset & Examination Overview

The dataset contains 327 question rows with four fields: id, year, type, and question. It covers observed examination years from 2015 to 2025, but no 2020 records appear in data.csv. All statistics in this report are calculated only from the provided CSV.

Data integrity note: the source brief describes an 11-year span, but the file itself contains rows for 10 distinct years. The year 2020 is therefore treated as missing, not as a zero-question exam year.

5. Strategic Insights for Exam Preparation

  • Plan around a stable MCQ base: every observed year contains exactly 15 MCQ rows, so rapid-recall practice should be a permanent preparation component rather than a year-specific adjustment.
  • Do not underweight Short questions: Short-question volume is the main source of structural variation and has remained at 15 rows in every observed year from 2021 to 2025.
  • Teach Long answers as synthesis tasks: Long questions are only 3 rows per observed year, but they repeatedly intersect with programming and database concepts; students need explanation, comparison, and multi-part response practice.
  • Prioritize programming fundamentals: terms such as program, statement, loop, operator, printf, and syntax recur frequently, indicating sustained assessment of C/C++ mechanics and output reasoning.
  • Maintain strong database coverage: database, data, key, table, primary key, foreign key, and relational database are prominent; curriculum pacing should reserve repeated practice time for these concepts.
  • Treat smaller topic areas as targeted review: web/internet, object-oriented, data-structure, operating-system, and number-system signals appear, but at much lower frequencies than programming and databases in this dataset.

6. Conclusions & Limitations

Answer to the central question: the exam structure is partly stable and partly shifted. MCQs and Long questions are highly stable in row count, but Short questions changed meaningfully across periods: 9 rows in 2015–2016, 18 rows in 2017–2019, and 15 rows in 2021–2025. Educators should therefore treat the exam as structurally stable in its fixed components, while remaining responsive to Short-question workload changes.

Limitations

  • Missing 2020 data: data.csv contains no 2020 rows, so no 2020 statistics or trends are inferred.
  • No annual/supplementary flag: the dataset may mix annual and supplementary questions, but the CSV does not encode that distinction.
  • Keyword-based topic detection: topic patterns are derived from text frequencies and curated keyword groups, so they should be read as signals rather than official syllabus labels.
  • Community-compiled source: the dataset is compact and may contain transcription inconsistencies, although no missing values are present in the core columns.
