Executive Summary

The data shows a clear disconnect between price and satisfaction among Amazon bestsellers: the full-dataset Pearson correlation between price and user rating is -0.133, a weak negative relationship. Lower-priced books appear more often and often rate better, while premium-priced titles can still enter the bestseller list when category authority or reference value is high.

Fiction vs. Non-Fiction: Pricing and Rating Strategies

Fiction delivers the stronger perceived-value profile: it has a lower average price ($10.85 vs. $14.84 for Non-Fiction), a higher average rating (4.648 vs. 4.595), and higher average review count (15,684 vs. 9,065).

Year-over-Year Trends in Pricing and Ratings

This is the key trend story: average prices remained elevated through 2014, then dropped sharply in 2015 while ratings continued improving. By 2019, the list reached its lowest average price ($10.08) and highest average rating (4.740).

Strategic Recommendations

1
Use accessible pricing as the default bestseller posture. The price-rating correlation is -0.133, 2019 combined the lowest average price with the highest average rating, and the $6–10 segment had the most appearances.
2
Price Fiction for volume and satisfaction. Fiction averages $10.85, below Non-Fiction, while posting higher average rating and review count; maintain lower friction pricing for Fiction catalog bets.
3
Reserve premium pricing for validated Non-Fiction utility. Non-Fiction sustains a higher average price of $14.84 and more appearances, but lower average satisfaction means premium positioning should be tied to clear authority, reference, or problem-solving value.
4
Track review velocity as a leading signal. Average reviews rose from 4,710 in 2009 to 15,898 in 2019; engagement growth is a material part of bestseller durability.
5
Build around repeatable author/franchise economics. The top author, Jeff Kinney, appears 12 times with a 4.800 average rating and $9.25 average price, indicating repeat demand can outperform price escalation.

Methodology and Data Integrity

All statistics, counts, averages, trend values, and the Pearson correlation coefficient were computed directly from /app/benchmark/data.csv. Annual rows are treated as bestseller appearances, not deduplicated books, because the source data represents the top 50 books for each year.

Dataset checks: 550 rows, 7 columns, 0 missing values detected. Genres are reported as Fiction and Non-Fiction; the source column stores Non-Fiction as “Non Fiction”.
