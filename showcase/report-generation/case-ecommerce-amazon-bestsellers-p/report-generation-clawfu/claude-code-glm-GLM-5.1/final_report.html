Strategic Recommendations

Data-grounded insights for pricing strategy and catalog curation

  • 1. Target the $8–$15 Price Window for New Titles The data shows that the most commercially successful bestsellers — and the authors with the most repeated appearances — cluster in the $8–$15 range. The Pearson correlation of -0.133 confirms no positive price-rating relationship. Pricing above $20 does not yield higher ratings and reduces the likelihood of sustained bestseller status.
    Evidence: Top authors under $20 average $11.61 per appearance; Budget segment achieves 4.67 avg rating.
  • 2. Invest in Fiction for Higher Engagement and Perceived Value Fiction achieves higher ratings at lower prices than the other genre, with more review engagement (15,683 vs. 9,065 avg reviews). For catalog curation, Fiction titles offer better value-per-rating returns.
    Evidence: Fiction avg rating 4.65 at $10.85; Non Fiction avg rating 4.60 at $14.84.
  • 3. Capitalize on the Downward Pricing Trend Average bestseller prices declined from $15.40 in 2009 to $10.08 in 2019, while ratings remained stable or improved. This signals market acceptance of lower price points. New titles should enter at competitive prices rather than premium positions.
    Evidence: 2019 avg price $10.08 vs. 2009 avg price $15.40; ratings held at 4.74.
  • 4. Leverage Free and Low-Price Titles as List-Building Tools 12 free titles appear in the bestseller dataset. Free and budget-priced books achieve ratings comparable to mid-range titles, suggesting they can be used to build audience and drive reviews for an author's broader catalog.
    Evidence: Free titles ($0) and Budget ($1–$10) segment avg ratings remain above 4.67.
  • 5. Prioritize Review Volume as a Leading Indicator Review counts have surged across the decade while prices declined. High review volume correlates with repeated bestseller appearances. Marketing investment should focus on generating early reviews rather than defending price points.
    Evidence: Avg reviews grew from 4,710 (2009) to 15,898 (2019).
