Price vs. User Rating: Correlation Analysis

The central question of this analysis is whether price drives user satisfaction. Our findings show a very weak, slightly negative correlation between price and user rating. The Pearson correlation coefficient is -0.133.

This suggests that paying a premium for a bestseller does not guarantee higher satisfaction. In fact, many lower-priced books achieve near-perfect ratings, likely driven by promotional pricing on established favorites or highly accessible fiction.

Fiction vs. Non-Fiction: Pricing and Rating Strategies

When segmenting by genre, distinct strategies emerge. Non-Fiction titles command a higher average price but receive fewer reviews on average. Fiction titles, despite being priced lower, tend to generate significantly more engagement (reviews) and maintain competitive ratings.

Year-over-Year Trends in Pricing and Ratings

A temporal analysis reveals significant shifts in pricing strategy over the decade. Notably, average prices for both genres saw a steep decline from 2009 to 2014, stabilizing in recent years. Meanwhile, user ratings have steadily climbed, indicating that readers are paying less but reporting higher satisfaction—a win for the consumer but a margin compression challenge for publishers.

Strategic Recommendations

  • Avoid Overpricing for Perceived Value: The negligible correlation (-0.133) between price and rating indicates that readers do not equate a higher price with a better book. Keep prices competitive, especially for Fiction titles where volume drives engagement.
  • Leverage the Fiction Review Engine: Fiction books generate substantially higher review volumes. Use aggressive promotional pricing (or free first-in-series tactics) on Fiction to build an insurmountable review moat, which algorithmically sustains sales.
  • Protect Non-Fiction Margins: Non-Fiction readers are historically less price-sensitive and willing to pay a premium. Maintain higher price points for authoritative Non-Fiction, but ensure quality is uncompromised to maintain high ratings in a lower-volume review environment.
  • Adapt to the New Price Baseline: The temporal data shows the era of $15+ average prices is largely behind us. Catalog planning must assume lower per-unit revenue and optimize for higher volume or alternative monetization streams.
