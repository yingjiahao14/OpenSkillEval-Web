Price vs. User Rating: Correlation Analysis

Statistical result: Pearson correlation coefficient r = -0.133, indicating a weak negative relationship between price and rating.
Interpretation: Higher price points do not systematically improve user satisfaction among bestsellers; value perception appears to be driven by factors beyond list price.

Year-over-Year Trends in Pricing and Ratings

Critical inflection #1 (2014→2015): Average price fell from $14.64 to $10.42 (-$4.22, largest single-year drop), while average rating increased from 4.622 to 4.648 (+0.026).
Critical inflection #2 (2018→2019): Ratings jumped from 4.668 to 4.740 (+0.072, largest increase), while price declined to the decade low ($10.08).

Strategic Recommendations

1) Prioritize value pricing bands ($0–$10) for broad-market bestsellers: this range represents 266 of 550 entries and sustains strong ratings (4.65–4.72 average).
2) De-risk premium pricing as a satisfaction lever: weak negative correlation (r = -0.133) indicates price increases alone are unlikely to raise customer ratings.
3) Lean catalog investment toward Fiction-like value profiles: lower average price ($10.85) with higher satisfaction (4.648) and stronger engagement (15,684 reviews) than Non-Fiction.
4) Use 2015 and 2019-style playbooks as pricing benchmarks: both years show favorable rating outcomes with reduced average prices, indicating elasticity-friendly positioning on bestseller outcomes.
5) Build recurring-author programs in high-rating cohorts: repeatedly appearing authors combine audience trust and predictable performance, enabling portfolio-level demand stability.
