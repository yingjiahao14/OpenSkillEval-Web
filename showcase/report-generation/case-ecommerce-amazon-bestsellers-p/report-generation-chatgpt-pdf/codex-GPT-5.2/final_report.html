Price vs. User Rating: Correlation Analysis

Finding (quantified)
Pearson correlation between Price and User Rating across all 550 bestseller appearances is r = -0.133. This indicates a weak negative relationship: higher prices are not consistently associated with higher ratings in this dataset.

Strategic Recommendations

Actionable guidance (data-traceable)
  1. Prioritize mid-market pricing as the default for bestseller targeting: the $6–10 and $11–15 segments account for the largest share of annual bestseller appearances, while maintaining strong average ratings.
  2. Treat premium pricing as a niche play: the Pearson correlation between price and rating is r = -0.133 (weak/negative), indicating higher prices do not reliably translate into higher ratings.
  3. Segment strategy by genre: Non-Fiction sustains higher average prices than Fiction across most years, while Fiction leads on average review volume and slightly higher ratings in later years.
  4. Use inflection windows as benchmarks: average prices reset sharply in 2015 and remain lower through 2019 while ratings trend upward (notably in 2014 and 2019).

Management implications

  • Set genre-specific price architecture (Non-Fiction premium is structurally supported; Fiction wins on engagement density).
  • Benchmark future pricing tests against the 2015–2019 lower-price regime, where ratings improved rather than declined.
  • When pushing premium SKUs, justify price via format bundles / institutional use cases (observed among recurring high-price manuals), not assumed rating lift.
