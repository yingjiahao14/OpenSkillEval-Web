Price vs. User Rating: Correlation Analysis

Higher prices do not translate into higher customer ratings in this bestseller dataset. The full-sample Pearson correlation between price and user rating is -0.133 (p = 0.0018), indicating a statistically detectable but weak negative relationship.

Weak negative associationEach additional dollar is associated with a regression slope of -0.00279 rating points, so price explains little of rating variation.
Premium outliers existThe highest-priced record is $105, but premium pricing is uncommon: only 26 appearances are in the $31+ segment.
Low price does not suppress satisfactionThe $1–5 and $6–10 segments average 4.680 across their segment means, above the overall average of 4.618.

Temporal movement is the most decision-relevant signal: average prices peaked early, reset sharply in 2015, and ended the window at the decade low, while ratings improved to the decade high in 2019. This indicates that bestseller success increasingly concentrated in lower-priced, highly rated titles.

Strategic Recommendations

Prioritize value-priced bestseller candidates.

Because price and rating have a weak negative correlation (r = -0.133) and the $6–10 segment has the highest appearance count (179), catalog and promotional planning should treat accessible pricing as a volume-friendly default, not as a satisfaction trade-off.

Do not justify premium pricing through rating expectations alone.

Non-Fiction earns a $3.99 average price premium over Fiction but posts a lower average rating (4.595 vs. 4.648); premium positioning should be tied to category need, authority, or reference value rather than expected rating lift.

Invest in repeatable Fiction franchises for engagement.

Fiction generates 15,684 reviews per appearance versus 9,065 for Non-Fiction, and leading Fiction authors such as Jeff Kinney and Rick Riordan show high repeat-list frequency at moderate average prices.

Monitor annual price resets as a strategic signal.

The 2015 drop from $14.64 to $10.42 coincided with ratings continuing upward to 4.648; management should benchmark new-list pricing against post-2015 levels rather than the higher 2009–2014 period.

Use rating strength as a curation filter, not a price ladder.

Ratings reached the decade high of 4.740 in 2019 while average price was the dataset low of $10.08; for bestseller targeting, high satisfaction and accessible pricing are compatible.
