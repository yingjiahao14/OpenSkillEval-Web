Price vs. User Rating: Correlation Analysis

Pearson Correlation: -0.1331

To understand if price drives satisfaction, we calculated the Pearson correlation coefficient between book price and user rating. The correlation coefficient is -0.1331.

This slight negative correlation suggests that higher prices do not equate to higher customer satisfaction. In fact, more expensive books tend to have very slightly lower ratings, potentially due to higher customer expectations.

Year-over-Year Trends in Pricing and Ratings

The following temporal trends illustrate how pricing strategy and customer sentiment evolved from 2009 to 2019.

Key Insight: Average price dropped significantly around 2014-2015 while review volumes exploded post-2017.

Inflection Points:

  • Price Trend: A general downward trajectory in average prices over the decade, with a notable drop around 2014-2015, possibly correlating with the rise of digital reading and Kindle pricing strategies.
  • Rating Trend: Ratings have steadily trended upwards over the decade, breaking the 4.6 average consistently in the latter half.
  • Review Volume: There is a massive acceleration in customer reviews in later years, especially post-2017, highlighting increased engagement and the growing importance of the review ecosystem.

Strategic Recommendations

Key Takeaways for Management

  • Optimize Pricing for Volume, Not Just Margin: The negative correlation between price and ratings suggests that premium pricing does not enhance perceived value. Lower-priced items often satisfy broader audiences better.
  • Genre-Specific Pricing Strategies: Non-fiction sustains higher price points than fiction. Ensure pricing reflects the genre expectations—do not underprice premium non-fiction, but remain highly competitive with fiction.
  • Capitalize on the Review Economy: The explosive growth in review volumes post-2014 indicates that market visibility is driven by community validation. Encourage reviews early in the book launch lifecycle to build momentum.
  • Digital First Pricing: The long-term downward trend in average bestseller prices underscores the shift toward accessible, mass-market digital pricing. High-volume strategies at lower price points dominate the bestseller lists.
