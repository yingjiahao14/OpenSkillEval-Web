2. Price vs. User Rating: Correlation Analysis

The Pearson correlation coefficient between book price and user rating is -0.133. This indicates a very weak, slightly negative relationship. Higher prices do not guarantee higher ratings; in fact, there is a marginal tendency for more expensive books to receive slightly lower ratings, or for highly rated books to be more affordably priced.

6. Strategic Recommendations

  • Pricing Strategy: A high price does not guarantee a high rating (Pearson correlation: -0.133). Avoid artificially inflating prices under the assumption that it signals premium quality. Instead, price competitively, particularly in Fiction, where lower prices align with higher review volumes.
  • Genre Focus: If the goal is consistent, premium pricing, Non-Fiction offers a higher average price ceiling. However, if the goal is rapid audience engagement and review velocity, Fiction is the superior vehicle.
  • Temporal Adaptation: The steady decline in average bestseller prices over the decade highlights increased price sensitivity and competition. Publishers must optimize production and marketing costs to maintain profitability at the $10-$14 price point, which is becoming the new norm for bestsellers.
  • Author Cultivation: Top authors like Jeff Kinney and Rick Riordan demonstrate that consistent output of highly-rated, moderately-priced books drives repeated bestseller appearances. Invest in long-term author franchises rather than relying solely on one-off high-priced hits.
