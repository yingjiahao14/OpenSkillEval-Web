Price vs. User Rating: Correlation Analysis

Pearson correlation coefficient (Price, User Rating): -0.13. This indicates a weak negative relationship: higher price does not systematically produce higher user ratings within this bestseller set.

  • The rating spread remains relatively tight across most price bands, reinforcing that content fit and audience resonance matter more than price alone.
  • $16–20 titles show the lowest average ratings (4.50), while both low-price ($0–5) and select premium tiers ($21–30) sustain strong ratings.

Strategic Recommendations

  • Decouple quality targets from price hikes: With r = -0.13, raising price is unlikely to improve satisfaction outcomes on its own.
  • Prioritize value-optimized price bands: Maintain strong title pipelines in $6–15 where volume is highest and ratings remain competitive.
  • Use genre-differentiated strategy: Sustain premium framing in Non-Fiction where willingness to pay is higher; reinforce engagement-led pricing in Fiction.
  • Exploit post-2015 trend signal: The period shows lower average prices coexisting with improving ratings; test targeted price elasticity without sacrificing sentiment.
  • Invest in repeat-author franchises: Frequent-appearance authors demonstrate durable bestseller mechanics that can de-risk catalog planning.
