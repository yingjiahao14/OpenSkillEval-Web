Year-over-Year Trends in Pricing and Ratings

This is the most actionable view for pricing strategy. The market shows a visible reset after 2014: average price dropped from $14.64 in 2014 to $10.42 in 2015, while ratings rose from 4.622 to 4.648.

Inflection points

  • 2012 satisfaction low: Average rating reached the period low at 4.532, while average reviews had already climbed to 13,091.
  • 2014–2015 price reset: Average price fell $4.22 year over year (-28.8%), the largest visible pricing shift in the series.
  • 2016–2019 rating strength: Ratings stayed at or above 4.678 and peaked in 2019 at 4.740.
  • Review engagement growth: Average reviews rose from 4,710 in 2009 to 15,898 in 2019, a 237.5% increase.

Strategic Recommendations

Prioritize value-priced bestseller positioning. The most common segment is $6–10 with 179 appearances, and the overall correlation between price and rating is weakly negative (r = -0.133); higher price should not be treated as a satisfaction lever.
Use Non-Fiction premium pricing selectively. Non-Fiction averages $14.84 versus Fiction’s $10.85, but its average rating is lower (4.595 vs. 4.648); maintain premiums where the title has clear authority, utility, or repeat-demand signals.
Invest in Fiction series and repeatable franchises. Fiction has higher average ratings and 73.0% more reviews per listing, while top recurring Fiction authors such as Jeff Kinney and Rick Riordan show sustained appearances at accessible prices.
Treat 2015 as a pricing benchmark shift. After the 2014–2015 price reset, ratings improved and stayed elevated through 2019; management should compare new catalog pricing against the post-2015 norm rather than the higher 2009–2014 range.
Track review velocity alongside rating. Review volume increased 237.5% from 2009 to 2019, making engagement a critical companion KPI for catalog curation; high ratings with low review volume should be evaluated differently from high-rating, high-review titles.
