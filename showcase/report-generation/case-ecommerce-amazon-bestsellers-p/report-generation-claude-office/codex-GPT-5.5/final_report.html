Executive summary

Price is not a reliable proxy for customer satisfaction.

Across 550 annual bestseller entries, the Pearson price-rating correlation is -0.133, indicating a weak negative relationship. The clearest strategic pattern is temporal: late-decade bestseller pricing moved down while ratings and review intensity moved up.

Strategic Recommendations

Each recommendation is grounded in observed dataset patterns and avoids assumptions not supported by the data.

Do not use higher price as a satisfaction signal.

The Pearson correlation is -0.133, and Fiction achieves higher average ratings at a lower average price than Non-Fiction. Price optimization should be managed separately from expected star-rating performance.

Prioritize the mid-low price corridor for volume-oriented bestseller positioning.

The $6–10 segment contains the most bestseller entries (179), and the 2015–2019 period combines lower average prices with higher average ratings and reviews.

Use genre-specific pricing guardrails.

Non-Fiction averages $14.84 versus Fiction at $10.85. Fiction can sustain strong perceived value at lower prices, while Non-Fiction has historically appeared more often at higher prices.

Invest in repeatable author and franchise assets.

The top author list shows repeated appearances from series-driven Fiction authors and durable Non-Fiction/reference brands. Catalog curation should track repeat appearances, average rating, and review counts together.

Monitor annual inflection points, not just full-period averages.

2012, 2015, and 2019 materially change the narrative: reviews jumped in 2012, price reset in 2015, and ratings peaked in 2019. Annual dashboards should flag these shifts for pricing and catalog decisions.
