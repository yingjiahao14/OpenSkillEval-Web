1Publication Volume & Temporal Distribution

The dataset spans January 2024 through March 2026, with a pronounced concentration in 2024 reflecting PubMed's indexing lifecycle — newer publications take time to be fully indexed and assigned MeSH terms. Of the 4,886 sampled records, 3,924 (80.3%) are from 2024, 737 (15.1%) from 2025, and 225 (4.6%) from Q1 2026. Extrapolated to the full dataset of 126,832 records, this yields approximately 101,860 articles in 2024, 19,131 in 2025, and 5,840 in early 2026.

Indexing Lag Note: The heavy 2024 skew (~80% of records) is a well-known artifact of PubMed's retrospective indexing pipeline, not an indicator of declining research output in 2025–2026. Year-over-year raw count comparisons should be interpreted with this context.

Key Observations

  • January 2024 peak (1,665 publications) dominates the timeline — this reflects the large batch of articles indexed retrospectively with January publication dates.
  • Post-Q1 2024, monthly volumes stabilize between 30–100 publications per month, representing ongoing real-time indexing.
  • A notable uptick appears in December 2025 (94 publications) and a rising trend into Q1 2026 (51 → 76 → 98), suggesting accelerating indexing of recent publications.
  • 225 records from 2026 cover only January–March, yet Q1 2026 volume (225 total) already shows strong throughput.

2Geographic Landscape of Biomedical Research

Research output is concentrated among a small number of nations. The top 10 contributing countries account for 51.3% of all publications, with China and the USA together comprising 31.7% of the dataset. China leads with 1,028 publications (21.0%), followed by the USA with 521 (10.7%).

Geographic Shifts Over Time

China's dominance fluctuates: its share drops from 22.6% in 2024 to 13.2% in 2025 before rebounding to 20.4% in Q1 2026. The USA maintains a relatively stable contribution (~10% each year). India's share notably increases from 3.3% (2024) to 8.0% (2025), indicating growing research output. Iran also shows increased representation in 2025 (3.7%, up from lower levels in 2024).

4Open Access Adoption & Journal Landscape

Open access (OA) availability — defined as presence in PubMed Central (PMC) — stands at 65.1% across the full sample. This rate varies significantly by year: 64.1% in 2024, rising to 74.1% in 2025, before dropping to 53.8% in early 2026. The 2026 decline likely reflects the lag between publication and PMC deposit rather than a true retreat from OA publishing.

Open Access by Country

OA rates vary substantially across contributing nations, reflecting different funding mandates and institutional publishing norms.

Iran leads with an 86.9% OA rate, likely reflecting national policies encouraging or mandating open-access deposit. The UK (80.8%), Japan (77.4%), and the USA (76.4%) all exceed the global average. China, despite being the largest contributor by volume, has a below-average OA rate of 55.7%, suggesting room for policy-driven improvement.

6Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

1. PubMed Indexing Lag Distorts Year-Over-Year Comparisons

~80% of the dataset is from 2024 due to retrospective indexing. Funding strategists should avoid interpreting declining 2025–2026 raw counts as reduced output — rate-normalized metrics are essential for temporal comparisons.

2. Open Access Is Trending Upward — But Unevenly

OA rates rose from 64.1% (2024) to 74.1% (2025), driven by OA-native journals dominating the top-10 list. However, China (55.7% OA) and subscription-model journals remain significant holdouts. Policy interventions targeting deposit mandates could accelerate this trend.

3. China and the USA Anchor Global Output

Together comprising ~31.7% of publications, these two nations drive the global research agenda. India's rapid share increase (3.3% → 8.0%) signals a rising force that funders and collaboration programs should engage proactively.

4. COVID-19 Research Persists but Is Declining

Still the #1 MeSH major topic (43 articles), but concentrated in early 2024. Month-over-month contribution is falling sharply, indicating a pivot toward chronic disease, microbiome, and neurodegeneration topics.

5. Emerging Topics Signal New Investment Frontiers

PTSD, COPD, and EEG-based neuroscience research show the strongest growth rates (+255%, +255%, +166% respectively). These accelerating areas may represent high-value targets for strategic funding allocation.

6. Preprints Drive the Largest Collaborations

With a median of 9 authors (vs. 6 overall), preprints reflect large-team, computationally intensive research. At 100% OA, they also represent the most accessible segment of the literature — though only ~0.8% of total output.

Strategic Recommendation: Funding agencies should (1) adopt rate-normalized metrics for portfolio analysis across PubMed data, (2) monitor the PTSD/COPD/neuroscience acceleration as potential priority areas, (3) incentivize OA deposit in high-volume countries like China, and (4) track India's rising research trajectory as a signal for expanded bilateral research agreements.

Methodology & Data Notes

This analysis is based on a representative sample of 4,886 records drawn from a full dataset of 126,832 biomedical research abstracts indexed in PubMed from January 2024 through March 2026. All statistics, percentages, and trends are computed directly from the sample data. Full-dataset estimates are extrapolated proportionally and marked as approximate (~).

  • Temporal bias: ~80% of records are from 2024 due to PubMed's indexing lag. This is a known artifact, not a data error.
  • MeSH coverage: 52.9% of records have assigned major topics; newer articles are less likely to be indexed.
  • Unknown months: 754 records (15.4%) have unknown publication months and are excluded from monthly trend charts.
  • Country normalization: "United States" and "USA" were merged; "PR China" and "China" were merged. 144 records have unknown country affiliations.
  • Open access: Defined as availability via PubMed Central (PMC). Recent publications may not yet be deposited, leading to potential underestimation of true OA rates for 2025–2026.
  • Emerging topic growth: Computed as rate-normalized year-over-year change (publications per 10,000 records) between 2024 and 2025, filtered to topics with ≥3 publications in 2024.
