Publication Volume & Temporal Distribution

The dataset covers January 2024 through March 2026, with a pronounced skew toward 2024 (80% of records). This reflects a well-documented PubMed indexing artifact: older publications accumulate more indexed entries as MeSH metadata and downstream references propagate. All cross-year comparisons should treat this imbalance as a structural feature, not a real-world publication surge.

Indexing Lag Advisory: The January 2024 cohort contains 1,665 records — approximately 4× the February 2024 volume. This spike almost certainly reflects backlog indexing, not an actual January publication surge. Policy analysts should normalize by excluding or discounting this entry when modeling publication velocity.

Geographic Landscape of Biomedical Research

Geographic distribution reveals a highly concentrated research landscape. The top 10 nations collectively account for the vast majority of indexed output. China's dominance is striking but should be interpreted alongside data quality notes: three country name variants in the dataset ("China", "PR China", "People's Republic of China") inflate apparent fragmentation — combined, they represent ~22% of all records.

Research Topic & MeSH Keyword Analysis

MeSH indexing is the backbone of PubMed topic classification. However, the major_topic field is sparsely populated — only ~970 of 4,886 records (~19.8%) carry a primary major topic, reflecting indexing lag for recent publications. MeSH keyword coverage is broader. Topic analysis should be understood as a partial signal, likely most representative of 2024 publications.

Coverage Limitation: Major topic field populated for ~970 records (19.8%). Keyword field populated for a larger share. Conclusions below are directionally valid but should not be generalized to the full 126,832-record population without caution.

Open Access Adoption & Journal Landscape

Open access (OA) availability via PubMed Central is a key policy metric. The dataset shows a headline OA rate of 65.1%, with a measurable upward trend from 2024 to 2025. Month-level OA rates are volatile, in part due to batch indexing into PMC, but the directional signal is clear: OA adoption is increasing.

Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

The following findings represent the highest-signal insights for research policy and funding strategy, synthesized across all analytical dimensions.

80%
2024 Dominates Due to Indexing Lag
About 80% of records are from 2024. This is a structural PubMed artifact, not a publication surge. Policy benchmarks must normalize for this skew to avoid misreading growth trajectories.
+10pp
Open Access Rate Is Rising
OA rate grew from 64.1% (2024) to 74.1% (2025) — a 10 percentage-point gain. Funding mandates (e.g., NIH public access, European open access policies) appear to be taking effect.
~22%
China Is the Dominant Producer
China-affiliated institutions account for ~22% of all records (combining name variants). USA (~11%) follows, with India (4%) and Japan (2.8%) in the next tier. Strategic partnerships with China remain high-value.
65%
COVID-19 Research Still Tops MeSH Rankings
Despite declining volume (37→5→1 across 2024-2026), COVID-19 remains the #1 indexed major topic. This signals ongoing long-COVID and pandemic-preparedness work — a sustained funding priority.
6.0
Median 6 Authors — Strong Collaboration
Median authorship of 6 (mean 7.1) indicates robust multi-institutional collaboration is the norm, not the exception. Funding models should support consortium-based research structures.
19.8%
MeSH Coverage Gap Is a Real Limitation
Only ~20% of records have a populated major_topic field, reflecting indexing lag for recent publications. Topic analysis is directional; trend detection requires monitoring of 6–12 month cohorts after publication.
Top 3
High-Volume Journals Are OA-Native
Scientific Reports, Heliyon, and Cureus — the top 3 journals by volume — are all open-access or mega-journals with APC models. This signals that high-volume publication increasingly routes through OA venues.
86.7%
Case Reports & Systematic Reviews Lead OA
Systematic reviews (89.3% OA) and case reports (86.7% OA) far exceed standard journal article rates (66.1%). High-synthesis, high-impact publication types are increasingly OA-first — an important signal for evidence-based medicine funders.
