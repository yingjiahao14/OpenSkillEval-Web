Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

Prioritized findings for resource allocation, institutional positioning, and research monitoring.

Temporal interpretation is the central constraint.

The file is strongly weighted to 2024 (80.3% of records), while 2025 and early-2026 are visibly incomplete. Use later-year values as indexing/pipeline signals, not final output totals.

Open access is substantial but volatile.

Overall OA is 65.1%. The annual rate rises from 64.1% in 2024 to 74.1% in 2025, then falls to 53.8% in the partial 2026 window; March 2026 is notably low at 23.5%.

Geographic concentration is meaningful but label quality matters.

The top 10 country labels account for 48.1% of records. China is the largest single label (952), but variants such as USA/United States and China/PR China should be normalized before formal benchmarking.

Topic intelligence is constrained by missing MeSH indexing.

47.1% of records lack major-topic indexing. Dominant observed topics include COVID-19, type 2 diabetes, breast neoplasms, gastrointestinal microbiome, and Alzheimer disease, but emerging-topic claims need cautious low-count framing.

Collaboration intensity varies by publication type.

The median article has 6 authors; clinical-trial-labelled records have the highest median among tracked types (11), consistent with more complex study coordination.

Fast-rising topic signals are best treated as watchlist triggers.

Keyword-level Q1-2026 signals flag environmental monitoring, chemical water pollutants, artificial intelligence, qualitative research, and skin neoplasms, but counts are small and should guide surveillance rather than immediate prioritization alone.

High-collaboration signals

Clinical-trial-labelled records have median 11 authors, and preprints have median 9. These labels indicate organizationally complex or fast-dissemination workstreams.

Strategic reading

Use author count as a directional proxy only. It does not distinguish single-institution teams from multi-site collaborations; funding analysis would benefit from author affiliation normalization.

Methodology & Limitations

All computations were performed directly from /app/benchmark/data.csv.

  • Coverage mismatch: The available CSV contains 4,886 records, while the documentation references 126,832. The report intentionally uses the available CSV only.
  • Temporal lag: 2025–2026 records are incomplete and should be interpreted as indexing/currentness signals rather than final publication output.
  • Known-month analysis: Monthly charts exclude 754 records with pub_month_num = 0; annual KPIs include all records.
  • MeSH missingness: 47.1% of records lack major-topic indexing, limiting emerging-topic precision.
  • Country normalization: Labels such as USA/United States and China/PR China are not merged to avoid altering source data; formal policy benchmarking should standardize these labels first.
