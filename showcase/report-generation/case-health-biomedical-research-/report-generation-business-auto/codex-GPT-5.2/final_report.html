Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

  • Temporal skew is substantial: 3,924 publications are dated 2024 vs. 737 in 2025 and 225 in 2026 (through the latest month present). Interpret 2025–2026 levels as incomplete due to indexing lag.
  • Open access signals are volatile month-to-month: overall OA rate is 65.1%, with notable within-year variation in the monthly trend line; policy tracking should use smoothed windows rather than single-month readings.
  • Geographic concentration: the top 10 countries contribute 48.1% of all records in this dataset slice, indicating a highly concentrated publication landscape.
  • MeSH coverage is a limiting factor for “emerging topics”: 47.1% of records lack major_topic, reducing sensitivity for topic acceleration KPIs in the most recent months.
  • Collaboration proxy is stable: median authors per publication is 6 across 2024–2026 in this dataset, suggesting no detectable shift in team size with the current sample.

Implication: for funding strategy, treat 2026 patterns as early signals only; prioritize moving-average trend monitoring (publication volume, OA rate) and ensure topic acceleration is computed on a MeSH-complete subset or supplemented by text-based topic modeling.

Interpretation guidance (indexing lag)

Because PubMed indexing accumulates over time, earlier years typically have higher observed counts. In this dataset, 2024 dominates the counts; 2025 and 2026 should be treated as partial-year / partial-indexing snapshots rather than comparable full-year totals.

Methodology & Limitations

  • Source file: /app/benchmark/data.csv (17 columns, 4,886 rows in this environment).
  • Monthly trends: computed using rows with known months (pub_month_num 1–12). Unknown-month rows excluded from monthly charts.
  • Open access rate: mean of boolean open_access, aggregated by month/year.
  • Emerging topic KPI: attempted using month-over-month growth in major_topic counts; results may be empty when MeSH coverage is incomplete and monthly topic counts are sparse.
  • Entity normalization: no attempt was made to reconcile country synonyms (e.g., “USA” vs “United States”) or journal name variants; doing so would change some rankings and shares.
