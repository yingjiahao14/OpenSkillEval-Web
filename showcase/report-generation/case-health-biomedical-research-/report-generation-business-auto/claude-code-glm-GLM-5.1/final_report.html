2. Geographic Landscape of Biomedical Research

Country of first-author affiliation reveals the global distribution of biomedical research output. China leads by a significant margin, followed by the USA and India. The top 10 countries account for 55.7% of all publications with known affiliations, underscoring the concentration of output in a small number of research-intensive nations.

Year-over-year trends for the top five contributing countries show that China's dominance is sustained across all years. India's output appears to grow proportionally, while European and North American contributions hold relatively steady in the available data.

Country Consolidation Note: Raw data contains multiple name variants (e.g., "China", "PR China", "People's Republic of China", "P R China"). These have been consolidated into standardized names for this analysis. Records with "Unknown" country affiliation are excluded from geographic analyses.

6. Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

The following priorities synthesize the most actionable findings from this analysis for research policy analysts and funding strategists.

  1. China dominates biomedical research output. With 1,119 publications — more than double the USA's 555 — China's position as the leading producer of PubMed-indexed biomedical research is unambiguous. Funding bodies and policy institutions should factor this geographic concentration into collaboration and competitiveness strategies.
  2. Open access adoption is rising but uneven. The OA rate increased from 64.1% in 2024 to 74.1% in 2025, a meaningful shift toward open dissemination. However, OA rates vary significantly by country, suggesting that national funder mandates and infrastructure investment remain key drivers. Policymakers should monitor whether this trajectory sustains through 2026.
  3. Emerging research topics signal strategic opportunity. Topics showing the strongest growth in the latter half of the study period — including Coleoptera (+-14%) — represent areas where research momentum is accelerating. Funding agencies should evaluate whether their portfolio allocations match these demonstrated growth trajectories.
  4. COVID-19 research remains prevalent but is normalizing. COVID-19 is still the single most common major MeSH topic, yet its relative share is declining as the research frontier shifts toward post-pandemic sequelae (Long COVID), chronic disease, and emerging therapeutic modalities.
  5. Collaboration intensity varies by geography. Median author counts range from 5 to 8 across the top 10 countries, suggesting that multi-institutional and international collaboration norms differ substantially. Countries with lower median author counts may benefit from targeted programs encouraging consortium-based research models.
  6. Indexing lag must be accounted for in real-time monitoring. The ~80/20 temporal skew toward 2024 is a structural feature of PubMed's indexing pipeline, not a signal of declining output. Any near-real-time monitoring system should apply normalization or lag-adjustment models before drawing inferences about output trends.
