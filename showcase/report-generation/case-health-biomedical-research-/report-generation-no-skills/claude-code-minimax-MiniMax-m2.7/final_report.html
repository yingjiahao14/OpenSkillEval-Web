Executive Summary

This trend analysis of 4,886 biomedical research abstracts from PubMed (2024–2026) reveals a research landscape dominated by China and the USA as leading contributors, with an overall open access rate of 65.1%. Publication volume peaks in early 2024, declining through the year consistent with indexing patterns. COVID-19 remains the dominant major topic, while emerging topics like Air Pollutants, Parkinson Disease, and Osteoarthritis show accelerating growth. The open access rate varies significantly by country, ranging from 57.5% (China) to 86.9% (Iran).

6. Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

1. Dominance of China and USA: Together, China (22.2%) and the USA (11.0%) account for over one-third of all publications. Funding strategists should monitor China's accelerating research output, particularly in environmental health and neuroscience domains.

2. Temporal Skew Requires Normalized Metrics: The 80.3% concentration in 2024 is an indexing artifact, not a real decline in 2025–2026 output. Year-over-year comparisons should use normalized rates; raw counts understate recent activity.

3. Open Access Varies Significantly by Geography: OA rates range from 57.5% (China) to 86.9% (Iran). Policy analysts tracking global knowledge diffusion should note that China—the largest contributor—has the lowest OA rate among top nations, suggesting potential barriers to accessibility.

4. Emerging Topics Signal Strategic Priorities: Air Pollutants, Parkinson Disease, and Osteoarthritis show accelerating growth in late 2024. Environmental health and neurodegenerative research appear to be gaining momentum, potentially reflecting post-pandemic research priorities.

5. Collaboration Intensity Remains High: With a median of 6 authors per publication, large collaborative teams are standard in biomedical research. Institutional strategies should account for team-based funding models.

6. COVID-19 Remains Dominant Despite Waning: COVID-19 is the #1 major topic (43 publications), but growth patterns suggest the pandemic research surge is normalizing. Surveillance and infectious disease research may need sustained funding even as attention shifts.

Data Limitation: This report is based on a 3.9% subset (4,886 of 126,832 records) of the source dataset. Monthly trends and emerging topic identification are subject to higher variance due to reduced sample size. All percentages and totals reflect the available subset.
