Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

  • The observed file contains 4,886 records, not the 126,832 described in the brief; all findings should be treated as an analysis of the supplied CSV extract until the full file is reconciled.
  • Temporal concentration is substantial: 80.3% of records are from 2024, while 2025 and early 2026 together account for 19.7%; raw year-over-year declines should not be interpreted as a contraction in science output.
  • Open access is the dominant access mode overall at 65.1%, but the monthly trend is volatile, including 23.5% in Mar 2026; policy interpretation should monitor sustained monthly rates rather than a single partial month.
  • Geographic output is concentrated but fragmented by country naming: the top 10 recorded country labels contribute 48.1%, led by China (952) and USA (415); harmonizing USA/United States and China/PR China would sharpen funding intelligence.
  • MeSH major-topic coverage is incomplete: 47.1% of records have a blank major topic. Emerging-topic signals are therefore useful for surveillance but should be interpreted as indexed-topic signals, not full-text topic prevalence.
  • Collaboration intensity is moderate, with a median of 6.0 authors per article; multicenter and clinical-trial publication types show higher medians where present, indicating larger-team research footprints.

Methods & Data Handling

All metrics were computed from /app/benchmark/data.csv. Monthly analyses use records where pub_month_num is 1–12; unknown-month records are retained in annual totals and disclosed separately. Research types are split on semicolons because the field can contain multiple PubMed publication types. Country labels are used as recorded, with no harmonization or geocoding. Major topics and MeSH keywords are parsed from major_topic and semicolon-delimited keywords.
