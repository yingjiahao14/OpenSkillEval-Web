Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

1. Temporal Skew Demands Normalized Interpretation

The dataset exhibits a steep temporal gradient: 80.3% of records are from 2024, 15.1% from 2025, and only 4.6% from 2026. This reflects real-world PubMed indexing lag, not a collapse in research output. Policy analysts should weight 2025–2026 data as early signals rather than annual totals and favor rate-based comparisons over raw counts.

2. Open Access Is the Dominant Publishing Model but Volatile

Overall 65.1% of indexed abstracts are open access, with 2025 showing the highest annual rate at 74.1%. However, monthly OA rates swing widely (23.5%–88.2%), suggesting that access policy varies significantly by journal and field. Funding bodies should monitor whether 2026's early-year volatility signals a policy shift or merely small-sample noise.

3. China Leads in Volume; Iran and the UK Lead in Open Access Intensity

China contributes 952 publications (19.5%)—more than double the USA's output in this sample. Yet China's OA rate (57.5%) lags behind Iran (86.9%), the UK (78.0%), and Japan (77.4%). Institutions negotiating transformative agreements should note these national disparities.

4. COVID-19 Remains the Single Largest Major Topic but Is Declining

COVID-19 is the top major MeSH topic (43 publications), yet its month-over-month trajectory is negative. Conversely, keywords such as "Algorithms," "Molecular Dynamics Simulation," and "Soil" show strong positive month-over-month growth, signaling a pivot toward computational biology, environmental health, and AI-driven methodologies.

5. Preprints Show the Highest Collaboration Intensity

Preprints carry a median of 9 authors versus 6 for standard journal articles, and they are 100% open access. This format attracts large consortia and early-sharing cultures. Funding strategies that incentivize preprint deposition may accelerate both collaboration and transparency.

Publication Volume & Temporal Distribution

The dataset spans January 2024 through March 2026. Because PubMed indexing accumulates retrospectively, 2024 dominates the record count. Of the 4,886 abstracts, 3,924 (80.3%) belong to 2024, 737 (15.1%) to 2025, and 225 (4.6%) to 2026.

Data Quality Note: 575 records in 2024 and 179 in 2025 have unknown publication months (pub_month_num = 0). These are excluded from monthly trend charts to avoid artificial spikes. The Jan-2024 peak of 1,665 publications likely reflects both genuine January submissions and retrospective batch indexing.

Monthly volumes decline sharply after Q1 2024, consistent with indexing lag. The 2026 data (January–March) already totals 225 records—suggesting that when fully indexed, 2026 may track closer to 2025's annualized pace than the raw count implies.
