Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

Takeaway 1 — Geographic Concentration

China and USA dominate global biomedical output; India and Iran are rising

China alone accounts for 22.1% of indexed publications (1,082), with the USA at 11.0% (539). Together, the top 10 nations produce 51% of all output. Strategic implication: Funders and institutions in non-dominant regions should consider international collaborative frameworks to improve indexing and visibility. India's disproportionate 2025 representation (~8% of 2025 cohort vs. 4% of 2024) merits closer monitoring as a rising research contributor.

Takeaway 2 — Open Access Trajectory

Open access has crossed 65% overall and reached 74% in 2025 — mandates are working

The OA rate climbed from 64.1% (2024) to 74.1% (2025), a 10-percentage-point increase in one year. Clinical trial protocols (93% OA) and case reports (85% OA) lead by type. The 2026 YTD dip to 53.8% is an indexing artifact, not a reversal. Strategic implication: Open access mandates from major funders (NIH, Wellcome, EU) are measurably shifting the publication landscape. Institutional repositories and APC support programs should scale to match growing author demand.

Takeaway 3 — COVID-19 Research Is Winding Down

COVID-19 publications dropped ~86% from 2024 to 2025 — research resources are being freed

COVID-19 was the single most prevalent MeSH major topic in 2024 (37 publications), dropping sharply to just 5 in 2025. This reallocation of research capacity represents a strategic opportunity. Strategic implication: Funding bodies should track where pandemic-era research capacity is being redirected — early signals suggest chronic disease (Alzheimer's, Type 2 Diabetes, oncology) and environmental health topics are absorbing former COVID-19 research resources.

Takeaway 4 — Chronic Disease & Oncology Remain Core

Alzheimer's, cancer, and metabolic disease maintain multi-year research momentum

Alzheimer Disease, Breast Neoplasms, Gastrointestinal Microbiome, Diabetes Mellitus Type 2, and Carcinoma (hepatocellular, renal) appear consistently across years in the major topic rankings. Strategic implication: These areas represent safe, sustained investment targets with demonstrated global research community engagement. Funding continuity here reinforces pipeline depth for translational and clinical outcomes.

Takeaway 5 — Mega-Journals & Preprints Are Reshaping Dissemination

High-volume open journals and preprint servers are now central to biomedical publishing infrastructure

The top journal list is dominated by open-access mega-journals (Scientific Reports, Heliyon, PLOS One, Cureus) and bioRxiv (32 preprints). These venues offer rapid, broad dissemination but vary significantly in peer review rigor. Strategic implication: Policy analysts should factor publication venue quality indicators (not just volume) into research assessment frameworks. Preprint culture, particularly in biomedical informatics and computational biology, is accelerating knowledge dissemination but complicates quality assurance.

Takeaway 6 — Collaboration Norms Are Stable at 6 Authors

Multi-author collaboration (median 6) is the universal norm across all years, countries, and types

The median author count is exactly 6 for 2024, 2025, and 2026. Italy shows the highest median (8), suggesting strong consortium-style research culture. Strategic implication: Collaboration intensity has stabilized — funders designing team-science programs should calibrate grant structures to 5–8 author teams as the standard, not the exception.
