6. Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

Synthesis of the most important findings into actionable insights for research policy, funding prioritization, and institutional strategy.

  1. 1
    Open Access has crossed the 65% threshold and is still rising. Policy

    The OA rate reached 65.1% overall, up from 64.1% in 2024 to 74.1% in 2025. This has direct implications for subscription budget modeling, repository infrastructure investment, and compliance with funder OA mandates. Institutions should plan for an OA-majority publishing environment by 2027.

  2. 2
    China leads global output but has the lowest OA rate among major producers — a strategic gap. Funding

    China produces 22.1% of indexed output (1,082 publications) but has the lowest OA rate among the top 15 countries at 55.5%. As international funders increasingly require OA, this gap represents both a challenge for Chinese researchers and a significant lever for publishers and policymakers.

  3. 3
    Environmental health and biodiversity research show the fastest normalized growth. Emerging

    Keywords related to air pollution, animal biodiversity, and environmental monitoring are growing rapidly in normalized rate. This aligns with the One Health framework and suggests funding agencies should anticipate increased grant applications at the intersection of environmental science and biomedicine.

  4. 4
    Pandemic-era research is normalizing — COVID-19 remains #1 but is decelerating. Funding

    COVID-19 is still the single largest major topic but its normalized publication rate dropped from 9.4/1K to 6.8/1K. Funding strategists should plan for the transition from emergency COVID funding toward sustained pandemic-preparedness and long-COVID research programs.

  5. 5
    Computational and signal-processing methods are diffusing across fields. Emerging

    Keywords such as "Signal Processing, Computer-Assisted" (+6.4/1K), "Molecular Dynamics Simulation", and "Algorithms" are among the fastest-growing. This signals that computational and AI/ML methods are becoming embedded across biomedical research, not siloed in bioinformatics. Investment in computational training and infrastructure should be a cross-cutting priority.

  6. 6
    Collaboration intensity is stable at 6 authors median, with geographic differences. Policy

    The median of 6 authors per paper has held steady year-over-year. However, country-level variation exists (range 5–8 across top 15). Preprints and Systematic Reviews involve systematically larger teams. This should be considered when benchmarking institutional collaboration KPIs.

  7. 7
    The publishing landscape is highly distributed — no single journal dominates. Policy

    The top 20 journals account for only 14.5% of output. This fragmentation has implications for literature surveillance, evidence synthesis, and collection development strategies. Tools and policies designed around a small set of "core journals" will miss the majority of relevant research.
