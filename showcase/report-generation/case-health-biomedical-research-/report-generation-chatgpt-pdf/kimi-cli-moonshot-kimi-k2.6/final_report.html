Executive Summary

This report analyzes 4,886 biomedical research abstracts indexed in PubMed from January 2024 through March 2026. The dataset spans 2,055 unique journals and 1,122 countries, offering a window into the velocity, geography, and accessibility of contemporary biomedical science.

Critical temporal caveat: Approximately 80% of records are from 2024, reflecting real-world PubMed indexing lag rather than a true decline in 2025–2026 output. All year-over-year comparisons should be interpreted as relative share shifts within the indexed corpus, not absolute volume trends.
Key Findings at a Glance
  • Volume skew: 2024 dominates with 3,924 abstracts (80%), while 2025 and 2026 account for 15% and 5% respectively — a natural artifact of indexing delay.
  • Open access is the majority norm: 65.1% of indexed abstracts are open access, with 2025 showing a higher OA rate (74.1%) than 2024 (64.1%). However, 2026 data shows volatility (53.8% OA), likely driven by small sample size and selective early indexing.
  • China leads in volume, the U.S. in OA intensity: China contributes 22.1% of all abstracts, followed by the United States at 10.7%. Yet the U.S. achieves a 76.4% OA rate versus China's 55.5%, suggesting different publishing channel preferences.
  • COVID-19 remains the top MeSH major topic (43 publications), but its monthly trajectory within 2024 shows a declining slope, consistent with the pandemic receding as a primary research driver.
  • Fully OA journals outnumber non-OA journals in this corpus (1,288 vs 692), though many high-volume non-OA titles (e.g., Food Chemistry, Journal of Hazardous Materials) remain influential.
  • Collaboration intensity varies by type and geography: Preprints show the highest median authorship (9 authors), while case reports are more solitary (median 5). Italy and China show the highest median team sizes among major producers.

1. Publication Volume & Temporal Distribution

The temporal distribution of this corpus is heavily weighted toward 2024, with 3,924 abstracts (80%), compared to 737 in 2025 (15%) and 225 in 2026 (5%). This imbalance is not a research-output decline but a well-documented PubMed indexing lag: older publications accumulate more complete metadata and MeSH terms over time, while very recent papers may lack full indexing.

Monthly Patterns

Among records with known months (85% of the corpus), January 2024 is a pronounced outlier with 1,665 abstracts — more than triple the next-highest month. This spike likely reflects a bulk indexing event or retrospective ingestion of early-2024 publications. Excluding January, 2024 shows a gradual decline from February (388) through August (59), followed by a modest year-end uptick.

2. Geographic Landscape of Biomedical Research

The corpus reflects a multipolar research landscape with strong Asian and North American representation. After normalizing country name variants (e.g., "PR China" → "China", "USA" → "United States"), the top 10 countries account for 52.6% of all abstracts.

Country Share Evolution

China's share drops from 23.8% in 2024 to 13.7% in 2025, then rebounds to 20.4% in 2026 (based on limited early-year data). The United States maintains a steadier profile (10.9% → 9.6% → 9.8%). This suggests China's 2024 dominance may be partially driven by bulk indexing of Chinese journals, while U.S. output is more evenly distributed across the indexing window.

3. Research Topic & MeSH Keyword Analysis

MeSH major topics and keywords provide a structured view of what the corpus is about. Note that 47% of records lack MeSH major topics, common for recently published articles that have not yet undergone full NLM indexing.

Figure 5. Most prevalent MeSH major topics among indexed abstracts with assigned topics (n=2,583).

Dominant Themes

COVID-19 leads with 43 publications, though its 2024 monthly trajectory shows a steady decline (slope = −0.93, R² = 0.44), consistent with the pandemic transitioning from acute crisis to endemic management. Diabetes Mellitus, Type 2 (24) and Breast Neoplasms (17) follow, representing persistent high-priority disease areas. Gastrointestinal Microbiome (16) and Alzheimer Disease (15) round out the top five, signaling sustained investment in microbiome science and neurodegeneration.

Keyword Growth Dynamics

Because major-topic volumes are too sparse for reliable 2024→2025 growth calculations, we analyzed MeSH keyword frequencies. Among keywords with at least 5 occurrences in 2024, Animal Structures showed the strongest relative growth (+57%), followed by stable performers such as Organ Size (0% change) and Body Size (−9%). Conversely, several molecular and clinical keywords — including RNA, Inflammasomes, and Reactive Oxygen Species — dropped to zero occurrences in 2025, likely reflecting both topic rotation and the smaller 2025 sample rather than a true disappearance from the literature.

Data limitation: With only 737 abstracts in 2025 and 225 in 2026, year-over-year keyword growth rates have wide confidence intervals. Policy analysts should treat 2025–2026 topic signals as directional indicators, not definitive forecasts.

4. Open Access Adoption & Journal Landscape

Open access (OA) — defined here as availability via PubMed Central — is the majority mode in this corpus, at 65.1%. However, the rate varies substantially by year, country, and journal.

Figure 6. Monthly open access rate for records with known months. Dashed line shows the overall corpus average.

Temporal OA Patterns

Monthly OA rates oscillate between roughly 35% and 88%, with no clear monotonic trend. The lowest rates occur in March–May 2024 (36%–37%), while the highest appear in January 2026 (88%) and December 2024 (86%). Quarterly smoothing shows 2024 Q4 at 77.2% and 2025 Q1 at 67.4%, suggesting that year-end and year-start indexing batches may favor OA journals.

Geographic OA Variation

Iran leads among major producers with an 86.9% OA rate, followed by Japan (77.4%) and the United States (76.4%). China, despite its volume dominance, has a lower OA rate of 55.5%, indicating a heavier reliance on subscription-based or hybrid Chinese journals in this sample.

Figure 7. Open access rate for the top 10 contributing countries. Dashed line indicates the overall corpus average.

Journal Landscape

The corpus spans 2,055 journals. Of these, 1,288 are fully OA (63%), 692 are non-OA (34%), and 75 are hybrid (4%). The fully OA set is numerically dominant but often lower in per-journal volume. The highest-volume fully OA titles are Scientific Reports (82), Heliyon (76), and Cureus (69). High-volume non-OA titles include Zootaxa (30), Food Chemistry (25), and Journal of Hazardous Materials (24) — the latter two reflecting strong environmental and food-science publishing pipelines that intersect with biomedical toxicology.

Author count serves as a proxy for collaboration intensity. Preprints show the highest median authorship (9), consistent with large consortia and data-intensive biology. RCTs also show elevated team sizes (median 7), reflecting multi-site trial infrastructure. 6. Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

For Research Policy Analysts & Funding Strategists
  • Temporal skew is structural, not cyclical. Any policy analysis using PubMed data must normalize for indexing lag. Raw 2024→2025 count declines are artifacts; use rates, proportions, and quarter-aligned comparisons instead.
  • Open access is maturing, not uniform. At ~65% OA, the biomedical literature in this corpus is predominantly open. Yet China's lower OA rate (55.5%) versus the U.S. (76.4%) and Iran (86.9%) suggests that national publishing infrastructure — not just author preference — drives accessibility. Funders targeting global equity should consider APC support or institutional agreements in lower-OA regions.
  • COVID-19 is declining as a major topic. The negative monthly slope within 2024 and the absence of COVID-19 from 2025–2026 top-topic lists signal a genuine pivot. Pandemic-preparedness funding should be reframed within broader infectious-disease or One Health portfolios rather than standalone COVID programs.
  • Chronic disease and microbiome research hold steady. Diabetes, breast cancer, Alzheimer's, and the gastrointestinal microbiome all maintain strong presence. These are safe bets for sustained funding and institutional investment.
  • Preprints represent a small but collaboration-intensive channel. With only 41 preprints (0.8% of corpus) but the highest median authorship, preprint servers serve large-team, rapid-disclosure science. Funding bodies evaluating impact should include preprint citations and altmetrics in assessment frameworks.
  • Journal diversification is high. 2,055 journals for 4,886 abstracts means most titles publish only 1–2 relevant papers in this window. Concentration in mega-journals (Scientific Reports, Heliyon, Cureus) is notable, but the long tail of specialist journals remains vital for field-specific dissemination.

Methodology & Data Notes

This report is based entirely on data.csv, which contains 4,886 cleaned biomedical abstracts sourced from PubMed and covering January 2024 through March 2026. All statistics, percentages, and trends were computed directly from this file. No external data or imputed values were used.

Known Limitations

  • Sample size vs. stated scope: The brief references 126,832 abstracts, but the provided data.csv contains 4,886 records. All figures in this report reflect the actual available data.
  • Temporal imbalance: ~80% of records are from 2024, 754 records have unknown months (15%), and 2,303 records lack MeSH major topics (47%). These gaps are inherent to PubMed indexing pipelines and were handled by restricting time-series analyses to known-month subsets and noting missing-data rates explicitly.
  • Country normalization: Variants such as "USA / United States" and "China / PR China" were consolidated. Some records carry non-standardized or missing country values (144 labeled "Unknown").
  • Research type simplification: PubMed allows multiple publication types per record. We assigned each record to a single simplified category using a priority hierarchy (Preprint > Systematic Review > Review > Case Report > RCT > Journal Article > Other).
