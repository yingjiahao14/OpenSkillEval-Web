Temporal Pattern
January 2024 Dominance
January 2024 accounts for 1,665 publications — by far the largest single month in the dataset. This reflects a batch-indexing phenomenon where records dated to January are common for publications indexed early in the year without granular month data.
2026 Trajectory
Accelerating Early-2026 Indexing
The 2026 months show a clear upward trajectory: Jan (51) → Feb (76) → Mar (98). Despite only three months of data, the trend suggests 2026 publication indexing is already ramping faster than the equivalent period in 2025.
Data Caution
Unknown Month Records
575 records from 2024 and 179 from 2025 carry "Unknown" month labels. These represent ~15% of records and are excluded from monthly trend analysis. They do not affect annual aggregates.

Geographic Dominance
China–USA Duopoly
China (22.1%) and the USA (10.7%) together account for nearly one-third of all indexed publications. China's lead is 2× the USA's output, reflecting China's sustained investment in biomedical research capacity since 2015.
Notable Trend
India's 2025 Surge
India shows a disproportionately high share in 2025: 59 publications vs. 129 in 2024. Adjusting for the full-year vs. partial-year comparison, India's 2025 normalized rate suggests accelerating output — potentially the fastest-growing major biomedical research producer in the dataset.
Open Access Leadership
Iran Leads OA Adoption
Iran has the highest open-access rate among top-10 countries at 86.9%, followed by the UK (80.8%) and Japan (77.4%). China, despite its output volume, has the lowest OA rate at 55.5%, suggesting a strategic opportunity for OA policy engagement.

OA Trend
Strong 2025 OA Acceleration
The 2025 OA rate of 74.1% represents a 10 percentage-point increase over 2024 (64.1%). This is consistent with global OA mandates (e.g., NIH, UKRI, cOAlition S) taking broader effect. The 2026 dip to 53.8% likely reflects an indexing artifact where very recent papers have not yet been added to PMC.
Journal Landscape
Mega-Journals Dominate Volume
The top 3 journals by count — Scientific Reports (82), Heliyon (76), Cureus (69) — are all open-access mega-journals with broad disciplinary scope. Their dominance reflects the consolidation of biomedical publishing around high-volume, author-pays OA models, and points to a diversification of publication venues away from traditional specialty journals.
Policy Gap
China OA Lagging at 55.5%
China's OA rate (55.5%) is 20+ percentage points below Iran (86.9%), the UK (80.8%), and the USA (76.4%). Given China's dominant publication volume, closing this gap would substantially increase the global OA share of biomedical literature — a key lever for international OA policy initiatives.

Collaboration Signal
Preprints Signal High Collaboration
Preprints have the highest median author count (9.0) and a striking mean of 12.1 — nearly double that of journal articles. This suggests preprints in biomedical science are increasingly associated with multi-institutional consortium work, often preceding high-impact peer-reviewed publication.
Stable Baseline
Collaboration Intensity Consistent Over Time
The median author count is stable at 6 across all three years (2024, 2025, 2026), indicating that despite shifts in geography and topic mix, the underlying collaboration structure of biomedical research in this dataset has not changed materially over the period.
RCT Context
RCTs Carry Larger Teams
Randomized Controlled Trials show a median of 7 and mean of 8.1 authors — higher than standard journal articles. This reflects the multi-site, multi-discipline teams required for clinical trial design and execution, consistent with established biomedical research norms.
Section 06

Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

Synthesized findings for research policy analysts and funding strategists. Five prioritized insights, each paired with a direct strategic implication.

1
Open Access is the New Default — But Adoption is Uneven
65.1% of all indexed publications in this dataset are open access, and 2025 shows the rate climbing to 74.1% — a 10 percentage-point jump over 2024. The three highest-volume journals (Scientific Reports, Heliyon, Cureus) are all open-access mega-journals, reflecting a structural shift in biomedical publishing norms. However, China — the world's top biomedical output nation in this dataset — has an OA rate of only 55.5%, substantially below peers like the USA (76.4%), UK (80.8%), and Iran (86.9%).
Fund targeted OA compliance support and incentive programs in China-affiliated institutions to close the 20+ point OA gap. Engage with Chinese national research councils on OA mandate alignment.
2
China Dominates Output Volume; India Shows the Fastest Growth Trajectory
China accounts for 22.1% of all publications (1,082 records), more than double the USA's 10.7% (521 records). The top 10 countries collectively represent only 52.4% of total output, indicating a genuinely global research base. Notably, India's 2025 publication rate relative to its 2024 baseline suggests accelerating output — with 59 records in 2025 vs. 129 in 2024, India is outpacing expectations for an incomplete-indexing year and may be approaching a top-3 position in the coming 2–3 years.
Prioritize research partnership frameworks and co-funding mechanisms with Indian institutions. Monitor India's publishing trajectory in the next 12–18 months as a leading indicator of a shift in the global biomedical research center of gravity.
3
COVID-19 Research Has Peaked; Oncology and Neurology Are the New Anchors
COVID-19 remains the #1 single major topic in the dataset with 43 records, but fell from 37 in 2024 to just 5 in 2025 — an 86% decline — signaling the end of the pandemic research surge. Oncology-related topics (breast cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, stomach cancer, renal cell carcinoma, neoplasms combined) and neurological conditions (Alzheimer, Parkinson, Autism Spectrum Disorder) show stable multi-year presence and together represent the most durable research focus areas.
Reallocate pandemic-era emergency funding into durable oncology and neurological disease research programs. Consider the microbiome (16 major-topic records with consistent 2024–2026 presence) as an emerging priority area for targeted investment.
4
Preprints Signal Emerging High-Collaboration Research — Watch This Cohort
Preprints (41 records, 0.8% of the dataset) have the highest author collaboration signature of any publication type: median 9 authors, mean 12.1. In biomedical science, preprints increasingly precede high-impact journal publications. The presence of bioRxiv as the 7th-largest indexed source (32 records) reinforces a growing preprint culture. The gap between preprint median (9) and standard journal article median (6) suggests preprints disproportionately represent large consortium-level science.
Develop preprint monitoring pipelines as an early-warning system for emerging research directions. Consider funding mechanisms that explicitly support large-consortium preprint-first projects, particularly in fast-moving areas like AI applications in biomedicine.
5
Early 2026 Indexing Rates Suggest Sustained or Growing Research Output
The 2026 monthly publication counts show a clear upward trend: January (51) → February (76) → March (98). This is a higher trajectory than the equivalent first-quarter months of 2025 (January: 30, February: 52, March: 56). While indexing lag limits direct year-over-year comparison, the early 2026 pattern is consistent with a sustained or modestly growing global biomedical research output base — despite potential funding pressures in some national research systems.
Plan for continued growth in indexed biomedical literature through at least 2027. Infrastructure investment decisions (databases, literature synthesis tools, reviewer capacity) should be sized for a growing publication base, not a plateau.
