1. Publication Volume & Temporal Distribution

Data Note: This dataset contains 4,886 records sampled from the full 126,832 PubMed abstract collection (2024–2026). The temporal distribution shows natural skew toward 2024 due to PubMed indexing lag—approximately 80% of indexed records are from 2024, with progressively fewer records for 2025 and 2026. This is a real-world artifact, not a data error.

The publication volume analysis reveals a significant concentration of indexed biomedical research in 2024, with 3,924 records representing 80.3% of the dataset. The years 2025 and 2026 show progressively lower counts (737 and 225 respectively), reflecting ongoing indexing processes rather than reduced research output.

Figure 1: Temporal distribution of publications showing (a) annual volume, (b) monthly breakdown by year, (c) cumulative trends, and (d) open access rate variation.

Key Observations

  • January 2024 dominance: 1,665 publications (34.1% of dataset) are concentrated in January 2024, suggesting batch indexing or database initialization effects.
  • Seasonal patterns: February and March 2024 show high volumes (388 and 380 respectively), with activity distributed across subsequent months.
  • Open access trajectory: Open access rate increased from 64.1% (2024) to 74.1% (2025), before dropping to 53.8% in 2026—the latter likely incomplete data for early 2026.

5. Research Type Distribution & Collaboration Indicators

Journal Articles dominate the dataset with 4,086 publications (83.6%), followed by Research Support publications (346) and Reviews (333). Case Reports, Systematic Reviews, and Preprints represent smaller but meaningful segments of the biomedical publication landscape.

Collaboration Patterns

The median author count of 6 authors per publication indicates substantial collaboration in biomedical research. The distribution shows:

  • Solo and small teams (1-2 authors): 388 publications (7.9%)
  • Medium collaboration (3-5 authors): 1,739 publications (35.6%)
  • Large collaborations (6-10 authors): 2,027 publications (41.5%)
  • Very large teams (11+ authors): 732 publications (15.0%)

The concentration in the 6-10 author range suggests that most biomedical research leverages moderate team sizes, with 85% of publications having 3 or more authors.

6. Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

Temporal Skew Awareness

Any temporal analysis must account for the ~80% concentration in 2024. Comparisons across years require normalized metrics. 2025-2026 data, while growing, remains partial due to indexing lag.

China's Research Dominance

China's 19.5% share (nearly double the USA's 8.5%) signals a major shift in global biomedical research output. Funding strategists should monitor this trajectory.

Open Access Momentum

65% overall OA rate with 74% in 2025 indicates strong compliance with OA mandates. Monitor 2026 data for trend confirmation as more records are indexed.

Collaboration Intensity

Median of 6 authors per publication reflects healthy multi-institutional collaboration. Large team publications (11+ authors) represent 15% of output—consistent with consortium-based research.

Persistent COVID-19 Focus

Despite the pandemic ending, COVID-19 remains the #1 topic by count (43 publications), indicating ongoing research into long-term effects and related health challenges.

Chronic Disease Priorities

Type 2 Diabetes, Breast Cancer, Alzheimer's, and HCC represent major chronic disease research clusters, suggesting alignment with global health burden priorities.

Recommendations for Policy & Strategy

  1. Resource Allocation: Given China's dominance, consider bilateral research partnerships and monitor competition dynamics in high-impact publication venues.
  2. Open Access Compliance: Continue supporting OA infrastructure—the 74% OA rate in 2025 shows progress but room remains for improvement.
  3. Emerging Topic Monitoring: Establish tracking for MRI and PTSD research growth, as these may signal new funding priorities or emerging health concerns.
  4. Collaboration Support: Multi-author research remains the norm; support infrastructure for large team coordination and international collaboration.
