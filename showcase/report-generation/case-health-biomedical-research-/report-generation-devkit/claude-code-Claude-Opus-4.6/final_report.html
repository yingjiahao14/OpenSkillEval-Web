Open Access Adoption & Journal Landscape

Open Access Rate Over Time

The overall OA rate rose from 64.1% in 2024 to 74.1% in 2025, a 10-percentage-point increase. The 2026 figure of 53.8% is based on only 225 records (Jan–Mar) and likely reflects incomplete PMC deposition — it should not be interpreted as a reversal of the OA trend. Monthly OA rates are volatile due to small sample sizes but the 2025 average clearly exceeds 2024.

Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

  1. Open Access is accelerating: The OA rate jumped from 64.1% (2024) to 74.1% (2025). Funding agencies pushing OA mandates appear to be having measurable impact. Institutions should ensure their publication infrastructure supports this trajectory.
  2. China dominates volume but the USA leads in OA: China produces 20.6% of publications but only 57.2% are open access — compared to 77.0% for the USA. This gap has implications for global knowledge accessibility and may influence international collaboration patterns.
  3. India is an emerging research powerhouse: India's normalized share surged from 3.3% to 8.0% in 2025. Funding strategists should consider increased engagement with Indian research institutions and their expanding output capacity.
  4. Post-pandemic topic shift: COVID-19 research is decelerating (0.72× normalized growth), while chronic disease areas — COPD, PTSD, metabolic conditions, and neurodegenerative disease — are accelerating. This signals a return to pre-pandemic research priorities and presents opportunities for targeted funding in these areas.
  5. Mega-journals dominate the publication landscape: The top journals are all high-volume, open-access venues (Scientific Reports, Heliyon, Cureus). Traditional subscription journals are less visible in terms of raw volume, though they may still lead in citations and impact.
  6. Collaboration patterns are stable: The median of 6 authors per paper is consistent across years. Preprints have notably higher collaboration intensity (median 9 authors), suggesting that preprint culture is driven by large collaborative teams rather than individual researchers.
  7. MeSH indexing lag is a significant limitation: Only 28.2% of 2025 and 20.4% of 2026 publications have MeSH terms assigned. Any topic-level analysis of recent trends will be incomplete until indexing catches up, typically 12–18 months post-publication.
