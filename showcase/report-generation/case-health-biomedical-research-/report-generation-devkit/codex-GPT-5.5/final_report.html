Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

1
Treat post-2024 volume as an indexing signal, not a demand collapse2024 accounts for 80.3% of the provided records (3,924 of 4,886), while 2025 and early-2026 are much smaller and should be interpreted with PubMed indexing lag in mind.
2
Open access is a material policy leverOverall open access is 65.1%. The rate rises to 74.1% in 2025 but falls to 53.8% in early 2026; March 2026 is especially low at 23.5%, likely reflecting early indexing composition.
3
Country concentration is moderate but source labels need normalizationThe top 10 raw country labels contribute 48.1% of all records. China is the largest single raw label with 952 publications; USA and United States appear separately, limiting direct country-comparative inference without standardization.
4
MeSH-based topic intelligence is useful but incompleteOnly 52.9% of records have a major_topic value. Within indexed records, COVID-19 leads with 43 major-topic records, followed by Type 2 Diabetes (24) and Breast Neoplasms (17).
5
Collaboration is typically mid-sized, with a long tailThe median article lists 6 authors, while the 95th percentile reaches 15; multicenter studies have a median of 12 authors in tokenized research-type records.

Strategic readout

China is the largest raw contributor with 952 records (19.5%). The top 10 raw country labels collectively account for 48.1%, indicating a concentrated but not fully dominant production base. Open-access rates among top raw contributors vary widely, from 25.0% to 86.9%, suggesting country- and journal-mix effects that funders may want to audit.

Policy implication: normalize affiliation geography before using this file for country benchmarking, but the raw data already signals where monitoring capacity and bilateral research intelligence may be most useful.
