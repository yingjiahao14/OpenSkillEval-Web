Executive Summary

This report examines 4,886 biomedical research abstracts indexed in PubMed from January 2024 through March 2026. The dataset reveals a heavily front-loaded temporal profile—approximately 80% of records originate from 2024, reflecting the natural indexing lag inherent to PubMed. Despite this skew, several clear strategic signals emerge for policy analysts and funding strategists.

China dominates the geographic landscape with nearly 23% of all publications, followed by the USA at 11%. However, India and Iran are gaining relative share in 2025, suggesting a gradual geographic diversification. Open access (OA) adoption stands at 65.1% overall, with 2025 showing a markedly higher OA rate (74.1%) than 2024 (64.1%)—a trend that bears watching as 2026 data matures. COVID-19 remains the single most prevalent major MeSH topic, though its concentration is declining as newer publications diversify into microbiome research, oncology, and neurodegeneration.

Headline insight: The combination of rising OA rates in 2025 and the emergence of India/Iran as growth markets suggests that funders should anticipate a more open, more geographically distributed research ecosystem—provided indexing lags do not obscure real-time signals.

Publication Volume & Temporal Distribution

The temporal distribution of the dataset is heavily skewed toward 2024, which accounts for 3,924 publications (80.3% of the total). This is not a data error but a well-documented artifact of PubMed indexing: older publications accumulate more complete metadata over time, while very recent papers may still be awaiting full indexing. 2025 contributes 737 records (15.1%) and 2026 contributes 225 (4.6%) through March.

Monthly patterns within 2024 show a pronounced January spike (1,665 records), followed by a steep decline through the spring and summer. This likely reflects batch indexing of backlogged 2023–2024 content rather than a true seasonal surge in publishing. By contrast, 2025 exhibits a more even monthly distribution from February onward, with a modest uptick in December (94 records). The first quarter of 2026 shows accelerating counts (51 → 76 → 98), suggesting that indexing is catching up with the publication pipeline.

Data limitation: 754 records (15.4%) lack precise month-level metadata and are recorded as "Unknown." These are concentrated in 2024 (575) and 2025 (179). Monthly trend analyses should be interpreted with this gap in mind.

Open Access Adoption & Journal Landscape

Overall, 65.1% of abstracts in the dataset are flagged as open access (available via PubMed Central). The year-over-year trajectory is non-linear: 64.1% in 2024, 74.1% in 2025, and 53.8% in 2026 (partial, through March). The 2026 dip is likely an indexing artifact—recent papers often enter PubMed before PMC archiving is complete.

Monthly OA rates fluctuate considerably, ranging from a low of 23.5% (March 2026) to a high of 88.2% (January 2026). The March 2026 outlier is driven by a single high-volume, low-OA journal batch and should not be over-interpreted. Excluding March 2026, the 2025–2026 monthly average sits at approximately 70%, reinforcing the impression of a structurally higher OA baseline than 2024.

OA Rate by Country

Open access penetration varies sharply by country. Iran leads the top 10 at 86.9%, followed by the UK (80.8%), Japan (77.4%), and the USA (77.0%). China trails at 54.7%, suggesting that Chinese publishing channels may have different OA policies or that Chinese authors favor subscription journals in the indexed sample.

OA by Research Type

Case reports show the highest OA rate (80.4%), possibly because they are shorter and cheaper to publish in OA venues. Journal articles and reviews cluster around 64–65%, while systematic reviews are lower at 57.1%—a counter-intuitive finding that may reflect the preference of high-impact subscription journals for synthesis papers.

Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

1. Indexing lag is the dominant temporal artifact—plan for it.
With 80% of records from 2024 and only 4.6% from 2026 (through March), any real-time policy analysis must apply significant discount factors to recent months. Funders should not interpret low 2026 volumes as a research slowdown.
2. Open access is trending upward, but not uniformly.
The 2025 OA rate (74.1%) is 10 percentage points higher than 2024 (64.1%). If this holds as 2025 data mature, it signals accelerating compliance with open-science mandates. However, China’s 54.7% OA rate lags the global average—funders partnering with Chinese institutions should verify repository compliance.
3. Geographic diversification is emerging.
India and Iran are gaining share in 2025, while China’s relative share declined. This may reflect differential indexing speeds, but it also aligns with broader trends of rising research investment in South Asia and the Middle East. Funding strategies should consider these markets for collaboration and capacity building.
4. Topic concentration is high but softening.
COVID-19, diabetes, and breast cancer dominate the major-topic rankings, yet new descriptors (extracellular vesicles, peripheral catheterization) are appearing in 2025–2026. Policy analysts should monitor whether these early signals mature into sustained research fronts.
5. Journal concentration creates leverage points.
Just six fully OA journals account for 328 records (6.7% of the dataset). If funders wish to influence OA penetration, targeted agreements or APC subsidies with these high-volume publishers could have outsized impact.
6. Collaboration intensity is stable.
A median of 6 authors per paper, with most work conducted by teams of 5–10, suggests that biomedical research remains moderately collaborative. There is no evidence of a shift toward either solo authorship or mega-consortia in this sample.

Methodology & Data Quality Notes

Dataset: 4,886 biomedical research abstracts sourced from PubMed (Kaggle: kanchana1990/biomedical-research-abstracts-20242026), covering January 2024 to March 2026. Columns include PMID, title, abstract, journal, publication date, author count, country, research type, MeSH keywords, major topic, and open-access flag.

Temporal skew: Approximately 80% of records are from 2024. This is a known PubMed indexing artifact, not a data error. Year-over-year comparisons use both raw counts and normalized shares where appropriate.

Missing data: 2,303 records (47.1%) lack MeSH keywords or major topics, and 754 (15.4%) lack month-level metadata. These gaps are concentrated in 2025–2026 and reflect incomplete indexing for recent publications.

Country cleaning: Obvious duplicates (e.g., "PR China" → "China", "United States" → "USA") were merged. Long or institution-like strings were retained as originally labeled; only exact duplicates were standardized.

Research type classification: Multi-value PubMed publication types were simplified to a single primary category using hierarchical rules: Case Report > Systematic Review > Review > Journal Article > Other.

Open access definition: Based on the boolean open_access field, which indicates PMC availability. This may undercount gold OA in journals not deposited to PMC.
