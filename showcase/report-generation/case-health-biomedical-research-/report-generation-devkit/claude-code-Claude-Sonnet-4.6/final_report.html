Section 06

Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

  • 📈

    Open Access is Accelerating Beyond Global Averages

    The OA rate jumped 10 percentage points year-over-year (64.1% in 2024 → 74.1% in 2025), driven disproportionately by mega-journal growth. Scientific Reports, Heliyon, PLoS ONE, and Cureus collectively account for a structurally significant share of output.

    Strategic Implication → Funders mandating OA compliance should expect easier enforcement going forward. Subscription-model publishers face accelerating disintermediation risk in the biomedical space.

  • 🌏

    China Leads in Volume but Trails in Open Access Adoption

    China accounts for 21.0% of indexed output — double the US share (10.7%) — but posts the lowest OA rate among top-10 producers (55.5%). All other top-10 countries maintain 68%–87% OA rates. Italy fields the largest teams (median 8 authors), signaling high multinational collaboration intensity.

    Strategic Implication → Bilateral or multilateral OA agreements with Chinese institutions represent the single highest-leverage policy lever for global OA coverage gains. Targeted infrastructure investment (institutional repositories, APC waivers) warrants consideration.

  • 🦠

    COVID-19 Research Is Maturing — Post-Pandemic Topic Shift Underway

    COVID-19 remains the top single MeSH major topic but its normalized rate declined from 2024 to 2025. Concurrently, neurological conditions (Alzheimer, Parkinson, EEG-linked research), oncology (Breast, Hepatocellular, Renal Cell neoplasms), and metabolic disease (Type 2 Diabetes) are consolidating as the dominant non-infectious clusters. Microbiome research (Gastrointestinal Microbiome: 16 records) signals growing fundamental science investment.

    Strategic Implication → Portfolio rebalancing away from acute-infectious disease infrastructure toward chronic disease, neurodegenerative, and microbiome-focused programs is already occurring. Funders should model a 3–5 year ramp-down of COVID-specific line items.

  • 🤝

    Collaboration Intensity is Stratified by Research Design

    Preprints (mean 12.1 authors) and RCTs (mean 8.2) represent the most collaborative output types, while Editorials (mean 2.9) and Case Reports (mean 5.2) are predominantly single-institution work. The overall median of 6 authors per article reflects a mature multi-author norm across biomedical research.

    Strategic Implication → Grant programs emphasizing large-team trial designs or consortium preprint output should expect and budget for higher author-overhead costs. Single-PI case-report pipelines can be supported with lighter infrastructure.

  • 🧬

    Emerging Acceleration Signals: AI/ML, PTSD, COPD, MicroRNAs

    Among topics with ≥ 3 appearances in 2024, Deep Learning and Machine Learning both show higher normalized rates in 2025 — consistent with the sector-wide AI integration trend. Stress Disorders (PTSD) and COPD also show accelerating normalized rates, potentially reflecting post-pandemic mental health and respiratory sequelae research. MicroRNA research continues expanding its footprint (9 in 2024 → 11 total).

    Strategic Implication → AI-in-biomedicine and digital health infrastructure funding should be treated as mission-critical rather than exploratory. PTSD/long-COVID respiratory research programs merit portfolio expansion ahead of anticipated volume growth.

  • ⚠️

    High-Impact Study Types Remain Underrepresented in OA

    Randomized Controlled Trials (43.1% OA) and Meta-Analyses (37.5% OA) — the two study designs most directly informing clinical practice — have the lowest open access rates of any research type. This creates a systematic gap between the highest-quality evidence and its accessibility to practitioners, especially in low-resource settings.

    Strategic Implication → Targeted OA mandates or APC subsidies specifically for RCTs and meta-analyses would yield outsized public health returns. Funders should consider differential OA requirements by study design rather than blanket policies.
