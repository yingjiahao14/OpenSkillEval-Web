Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

Indexing lag dominates temporal patterns. Approximately 80% of records are from 2024, with 2025 and 2026 representing progressively smaller slices. Any year-over-year comparison must be interpreted as a snapshot of indexed output, not absolute global production.
Open access is the default, but with volatility. The overall OA rate is 65.1%, rising to 74.1% in 2025 before dropping to 53.8% in early 2026 (likely due to indexing lag for PMC). Preprints are 100% OA, while systematic reviews lag at 56.5%.
China leads in volume, but Iran and the UK lead in openness. China contributes 19.5% of abstracts yet has a 57.5% OA rate—well below Iran (86.9%) and the UK (80.8%). Policy levers around mandates and APC funding may explain these gaps.
Emerging topic signals are environmental and computational. Keywords such as Air Pollutants, Environmental Monitoring, and Algorithms are gaining share in 2025–2026, while classical clinical study designs (retrospective studies, mice models) are declining in relative prominence.
Journal landscape is bifurcated. Mega-journals (Scientific Reports, Heliyon, PLOS ONE) are near-universally OA, whereas discipline-specific titles (Optics Express, Journal of Hazardous Materials, Zootaxa) remain largely closed.

Publication Volume & Temporal Distribution

The dataset spans 4,886 abstracts across January 2024–March 2026. The temporal distribution is heavily skewed toward 2024 (3,924 records, 80.3%), reflecting standard PubMed indexing lag rather than a collapse in 2025–2026 output.

Analyst note: The January 2024 spike (1,665 abstracts) is consistent with batch indexing of backlogged content. For trend analysis, focus on rates and shares rather than raw monthly counts.

Year-over-year, the OA rate shows an upward trajectory from 64.1% (2024) to 74.1% (2025). The 2026 dip to 53.8% should be treated cautiously: newer articles may not yet have completed PMC ingestion.

Journal Landscape

The most prolific journals in the dataset illustrate a clear split: broad-scope OA mega-journals dominate volume, while specialized subscription titles remain closed.

Policy insight: If funding agencies require OA publication, researchers in specialized fields (e.g., optics, environmental chemistry, taxonomy) face fewer compliant journal options, potentially creating equity concerns across disciplines.

Data Limitations & Methodology Notes

  • Temporal skew: ~80% of records are from 2024. This is a known PubMed indexing artifact, not a drop in research output. Comparisons use normalized shares where possible.
  • Unknown months: 754 records (15.4%) have unknown publication months and are excluded from monthly trend charts.
  • Missing MeSH data: 2,303 records (47.1%) lack keywords or major topics, predominantly newer articles awaiting indexing.
  • Country standardization: Some records list "PR China" separately from "China"; these were merged for geographic analysis. "Unknown" country entries (144 records) were retained.
  • Open access flag: Based on PMC availability. Articles in hybrid journals or with delayed OA may be undercounted.
