Publication Volume & Temporal Distribution

The dataset is temporally imbalanced: 2024 accounts for 3,924 of 4,886 records (80.3%). This skew is consistent with PubMed indexing lag (older publications have had more time to be indexed). As a result, raw counts in 2025–2026 should be interpreted as incomplete snapshots rather than true declines in underlying research activity.

Interpretation guardrail: treat 2025 and 2026 as “partial capture” periods. Use rates (e.g., open-access share) and within-period topic mix changes more than absolute volume when comparing across years.

Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

  1. Indexing lag dominates year-over-year volume. 2024 contains 80.3% of records (3,924/4,886). For planning, emphasize rates (OA share, topic mix) over raw publication counts in 2025–2026.
  2. Open access is majority-share in this dataset. Overall OA rate is 65.1% (3,183/4,886). Track the monthly OA rate trend as a policy KPI, but interpret late-window volatility with attention to smaller denominators.
  3. Geographic output is concentrated and labels need normalization. Top-10 countries contribute 48.1% of publications; country strings are non-standard (“USA” vs “United States”, “China” vs “PR China”), which should be harmonized before allocation decisions.
  4. MeSH-based topic acceleration is hard to measure reliably late in the window. Nearly half of records lack a major topic (47.1%), limiting robust “emerging topic” growth estimates and creating an inherent bias toward older, fully-indexed content.
  5. Collaboration intensity is mid-to-high by author count. The median is 6 authors/publication; distribution-based monitoring can detect growth in large-team research even when annual counts are incomplete.
