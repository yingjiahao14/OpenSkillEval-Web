1. Publication Volume & Temporal Distribution

The dataset captures 4,886 biomedical abstracts spanning January 2024 through March 2026. The pronounced concentration in 2024 (80.3% of records) reflects PubMed's indexing pipeline — older publications accumulate more indexed entries over time. This temporal skew is a known artifact rather than a data quality issue, and all year-over-year comparisons below use normalized rates rather than raw counts.

Monthly Publication Volume

Key Observation: January 2024 alone accounts for 1,665 publications (34% of the dataset), reflecting a heavy concentration of indexed records at the start of the observation window. From mid-2024 onward, monthly volumes stabilize at 30–100 records. The 2025 data shows a notably even distribution (26–94 per month), suggesting a more current indexing cadence. Early 2026 volumes (51–98 per month) show a rising trajectory consistent with ongoing PubMed processing.

Seasonality Patterns

Within 2024, a clear front-loading pattern is visible: Q1 accounts for 62% of that year's records. In 2025, the distribution is much flatter, with a modest uptick in December (94 records). These patterns reflect indexing timing rather than true publication seasonality, and should be interpreted accordingly when projecting future volumes.

Geographic Share Shifts (2024 → 2025)

India's Rising Share: India's contribution doubled from 3.3% of publications in 2024 to 8.0% in 2025, the most notable geographic shift in the dataset. Iran similarly rose from 2.0% to 3.7%. Meanwhile, China's share decreased from 22.6% to 13.2% in 2025 before rebounding to 20.4% in early 2026. The USA maintained a relatively stable share (10.9% → 9.6%).

Italy's share halved from 2.1% (2024) to 0.9% (2025), while the UK declined from 1.8% to 0.7%. These shifts may reflect differences in PubMed indexing timelines across journals with varying geographic footprints rather than true declines in research output.

Collaboration Intensity by Country

Italy stands out with the highest median author count (8.0), suggesting larger, more collaborative research teams. China follows with a median of 7.0 authors per paper. India, Iran, Canada, and South Korea tend toward smaller teams (median 5.0 authors), which may reflect different research funding structures and team composition norms.

6. Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

The following findings carry the most direct implications for research policy, funding prioritization, and institutional strategy.

1

Open Access Momentum Is Real — But Uneven

The OA rate rose from 64.1% (2024) to 74.1% (2025), a 10 percentage point jump. However, this varies sharply by geography: Iran (86.9%) and Japan (77.4%) lead, while China — the largest contributor — trails at 57.5%. Implication: Funding bodies targeting OA compliance should consider country-specific support mechanisms, particularly for Chinese-affiliated researchers.

2

China Dominates Volume; India Is the Fastest Riser

China accounts for 21.0% of publications, more than double the USA's share. India's contribution doubled from 3.3% to 8.0% between 2024 and 2025. Implication: Collaborative funding programs with Indian institutions may yield outsized returns as that research ecosystem scales rapidly.

3

COVID-19 Research Persists — But the Field Is Shifting

COVID-19 remains the single most-indexed major topic (43 records), but is joined by rising interest in the gut microbiome, Alzheimer disease, and microRNA biology. Implication: Strategic rebalancing of pandemic-era funding toward neurodegenerative disease and microbiome research aligns with observable publication momentum.

4

Mega-Journals and OA Platforms Are Reshaping Publishing

Scientific Reports, Heliyon, and Cureus top the journal rankings. The long tail is extreme: no single journal exceeds 1.7% of output. Implication: Institutional journal-level evaluation metrics are becoming less meaningful as publishing disperses across a broadening OA landscape.

5

Collaboration Size Is Stable and Substantial

The median of 6 authors per paper held constant across 2024–2026, with 83% of publications involving 4+ authors. Italy (median 8) and China (median 7) lead in team size. Implication: Funding models should continue to support multi-investigator grants; solo research is vanishingly rare in biomedical science (1.6%).

6

MeSH Indexing Lag Limits Real-Time Topic Intelligence

47.1% of records lack major topic assignments, concentrated in newer publications. This limits the utility of MeSH-based trend analysis for the most recent periods. Implication: Organizations relying on MeSH for strategic planning should supplement with NLP-based topic extraction on raw abstracts to capture emerging trends earlier.

Data Limitations: This analysis is based on a curated sample of 4,886 records from a larger universe of 126,832 abstracts. The temporal distribution is heavily skewed toward 2024 due to PubMed indexing lag. All percentages and trends should be understood within this context. Small sample sizes in 2025–2026 limit the statistical power of year-over-year comparisons. The Q1 2026 open-access dip is most likely an artifact of PMC embargo timing rather than a reversal in OA adoption.
