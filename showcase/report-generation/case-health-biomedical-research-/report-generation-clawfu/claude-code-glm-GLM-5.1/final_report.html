6. Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

Actionable insights for research policy, funding prioritization, and institutional strategy.

High Priority

Open Access Momentum Is Real — Sustain the Policy Push

OA rates climbed from 64.1% (2024) to 74.1% (2025), a 10-percentage-point gain in a single year. Funders should maintain or strengthen OA mandates; the data shows the research community is responding. The 2026 dip is an artifact of indexing lag, not a reversal.

High Priority

China's Dominance Is Structurally Expanding

China contributes 22.1% of all publications — more than double the USA's share (11.0%). India ranks third and is growing. Funding bodies in Western nations should assess whether current investment levels are sufficient to maintain competitive positioning, particularly in high-volume therapeutic areas.

Medium Priority

Post-COVID Research Pivot Is Underway

COVID-19 remains the #1 MeSH topic by volume, but its 2025–2026 share is -33.9% below the expected baseline. This signals a decisive shift away from pandemic-centric research. Funding for long-COVID and pandemic preparedness should be evaluated against this decline — the field is moving on, but strategic reserves may still be warranted.

Medium Priority

Emerging Topics: PTSD, Biosensing, and COPD Show Counter-Trend Growth

Stress Disorders (Post-Traumatic), Biosensing Techniques, and COPD research maintain or increase their share in 2025–2026 despite the overall volume decline. These represent genuine emerging interest and could merit targeted funding calls, especially as PTSD research intersects with post-pandemic mental health needs.

Monitoring

MeSH Indexing Gaps Limit Real-Time Intelligence

Only 52.9% of records have MeSH major topic assignments, with newer publications disproportionately missing. Policy analysts should factor this gap into topic-trend assessments and advocate for faster MeSH indexing to improve real-time research landscape monitoring.

Monitoring

Collaboration Intensity Varies Significantly by Region

Median author counts range from 5 to 8 across top-producing countries. Lower-collaboration regions may benefit from international partnership incentives. Systematic Reviews (median 6 authors) and Preprints (median 9) represent the most team-intensive research types.
