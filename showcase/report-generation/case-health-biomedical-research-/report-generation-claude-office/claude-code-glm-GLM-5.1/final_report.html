1 Publication Volume & Temporal Distribution

This section examines the monthly and yearly distribution of publications, revealing the natural skew toward 2024 due to PubMed indexing lag. As articles age, they accumulate more complete indexing entries, making 2024 appear dominant in raw count. Indexing Lag

Temporal skew is by design. Approximately 80.3% of records are from 2024, reflecting real-world PubMed indexing patterns where older publications accumulate more indexed entries over time. 2025 and 2026 have progressively fewer records. Year-over-year comparisons should use rates or normalized metrics rather than raw counts.

The January 2024 spike (1,665 publications) likely reflects batch-indexed backfill rather than a true publication surge. After February 2024, volumes stabilize to a more representative monthly baseline of 30–100 publications, gradually declining as indexing lag increases for more recent months. The uptick in early 2026 (51–98/month) may reflect active indexing catch-up cycles.

4 Open Access Adoption & Journal Landscape

Quantifying the proportion of open-access vs. closed-access publications, identifying leading journals by volume, and assessing whether open-access rates are changing over time or vary by country and research type.

The open access rate fluctuates monthly between ~35% and ~88%, with an overall rate of 65.1%. The 2025 yearly rate (74.1%) exceeds 2024 (64.1%), suggesting a trend toward greater OA adoption. The 2026 rate (53.8%) is lower but based on very few records and is not yet representative. The high variability in early months (especially the low rates in Feb–May 2024) may reflect indexing patterns where non-OA articles are indexed first.

Open Access Rate by Country (Top 10)

Iran leads with an 83.8% open access rate, followed by Japan (76.0%) and the USA (73.6%). China, despite being the largest contributor by volume, has the lowest OA rate among the top 10 at 54.3%. This gap may reflect differences in funder mandates, journal preferences, or institutional OA infrastructure.

Collaboration & Research Type Insights

Preprints have the largest teams. Preprints average a median of 9 authors, compared to 6 for journal articles. This may reflect the prevalence of preprints in large-scale collaborative research (e.g., genomics, clinical trials).
Preprints are 100% open access. All 41 preprint records in the dataset are open access, compared to 64.1% for journal articles and 65.4% for reviews. Case reports also show high OA rates at 83.2%.

6 Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

Synthesized findings with actionable insights for research policy, funding prioritization, and institutional strategy.

1. Open Access is Gaining Momentum

The 2025 OA rate of 74.1% marks a meaningful increase from 64.1% in 2024, suggesting funder mandates (e.g., NIH, Plan S) are having measurable impact. However, China — the largest contributor by volume — lags at 54.3% OA, indicating a significant untapped area for OA policy advocacy. Funders should prioritize OA infrastructure investments in high-output, low-OA countries.

2. China Dominates Output, But Watch India and Iran

China accounts for 25.6% of all publications, but India's share of 2025 publications (10.6%) exceeds its 2024 share (4.2%), and Iran shows a similar upward trend. These countries represent growing research capacity that funding strategists should monitor for collaboration opportunities and capacity-building investments.

3. COVID-19 Research is Declining; Oncology and Microbiome are Steady

COVID-19 remains the top MeSH topic by total count (43 publications), but it shows the steepest month-over-month decline (−16.9% MoM). Meanwhile, Diabetes Mellitus Type 2 (24), Breast Neoplasms (17), and Gastrointestinal Microbiome (16) maintain steady presence. Funders shifting away from pandemic-response portfolios should redirect toward chronic disease and microbiome research, which show sustained demand.

4. Emerging Topics Signal Future Investment Areas

Long Noncoding RNA (+33.3% MoM) and Non-Small-Cell Lung Carcinoma (+16.7% MoM) are the fastest-growing topics, albeit from small bases. These represent early-stage research waves likely to accelerate. Strategic early investment in these areas — particularly in related infrastructure (sequencing, clinical trial networks) — could yield high returns as these fields mature.

5. Collaboration Patterns Vary Significantly by Geography

Median author counts range from 5 (India, Iran, UK, South Korea) to 8 (Italy), indicating meaningful differences in collaboration culture. Institutions seeking international partnerships should consider that larger teams may indicate more multi-site studies, while smaller teams may suggest more independent research programs. Both models have value, but funding mechanisms should be tailored accordingly.

6. Temporal Data Requires Careful Interpretation

The 80.3% concentration of records in 2024 is an indexing artifact, not a research decline. Policy analysts should normalize by time period and account for indexing lag when assessing growth trends. The 2025 OA rate increase and India's share gain are promising signals, but both should be confirmed as the 2025–2026 indexing catches up.

Data Source: NCBI PubMed, via Kaggle (kanchana1990/biomedical-research-abstracts-20242026). Analysis covers 4,886 records from January 2024 through March 2026.

Methodology: Country names were cleaned and consolidated from raw affiliation fields (e.g., "USA", "United States", and US state abbreviations were merged). Research types were derived from PubMed publication-type tags. Open access status reflects PubMed Central (PMC) availability. Month-over-month growth rates were computed only for MeSH topics with at least 3 months of data.

Limitations: 1) Indexing lag causes temporal skew — 2024 is overrepresented. 2) Only 5.1% of records have populated major_topic fields, limiting topic analysis. 3) Country data reflects first-author affiliation only and had 456 unknown entries after cleaning. 4) The 2026 OA rate (53.8%) is based on only 225 records and is not yet representative.
