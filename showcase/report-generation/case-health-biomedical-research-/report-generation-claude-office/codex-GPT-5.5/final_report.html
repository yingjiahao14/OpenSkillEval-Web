Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

  1. Temporal interpretation must be rate-based, not volume-only. The actual file contains 4,886 records, with 80.3% in 2024, 15.1% in 2025, and 4.6% in 2026. This mirrors the documented PubMed indexing lag and means 2025–2026 should be read as partial, not as a collapse in biomedical output.
  2. Open access is policy-relevant but volatile month to month. Overall OA is 65.1%; by year it is 64.1% in 2024, 74.1% in 2025, and 53.8% in early 2026. The March 2026 monthly value is low in the file and should be treated cautiously because recent records are most exposed to indexing and PMC-linkage lag.
  3. Country concentration is substantial but fragmented by metadata aliases. After standardizing common labels, China contributes 1,082 records and the United States 524; the top 10 countries account for 52.9% of the sample. Funding analysts should normalize affiliation geographies before comparing institutional positions.
  4. MeSH-based emerging-topic signals are weak because indexing is incomplete. 2,303 records (47.1%) lack a major topic and 2,303 records (47.1%) lack keyword strings. The fastest-rising major topics listed in this report are therefore best treated as early watchlist items, not definitive growth markets.
  5. Collaboration intensity is moderate with higher tails in some outputs. The median article lists 6 authors; preprints in this file have a median of 9 authors and 100.0% OA, suggesting early dissemination channels are more team-intensive in this sample.

Data Integrity & Scope Note

Important discrepancy: the source brief describes 126,832 abstracts, but `/app/benchmark/data.csv` contains 4,886 records (3.9% of the briefed row count). All numbers, percentages, charts, and findings in this report are computed only from the available CSV.

Known-month monthly analysis excludes 754 records with `pub_month_num = 0`; yearly totals include them. MeSH topic analysis is limited by missing `major_topic` and `keywords` values, especially for newer publications.

Among records with a `major_topic`, COVID-19 is the largest single major topic with 43 records, followed by Diabetes Mellitus, Type 2 with 24. However, major topics are sparse: 47.1% of records have no major-topic value. The growth table uses a smoothed compound month-over-month rate from the first active topic window to the last active topic window; it is useful for scanning acceleration but should not be used as a sole funding trigger without a larger, fully MeSH-indexed extract.

Emerging-topic KPI definition: for recurrent major topics with at least four known-month articles and at least two active months, growth is calculated as `((last active window average + 0.5) / (first active window average + 0.5))^(1/months elapsed) - 1`. The 0.5 smoothing prevents zero-baseline inflation while preserving the direction of change.

Strategic Implications for Policy & Funding

Portfolio monitoring Track rates and shares instead of raw counts for 2025–2026 until indexing catches up. Month-level changes in recent records should be framed as provisional intelligence.

Topic intelligence Treat MeSH growth rankings as a watchlist layer. Pair them with text-mining of titles/abstracts or a refreshed PubMed pull before reallocating funds toward apparent accelerators.

Open science policy Use the OA time series and country/type slices to identify where PMC availability is lagging. Recent low OA values may reflect linkage delay, so repeated refreshes are essential.

Benchmarking Normalize country aliases and isolate Unknown values before institutional comparisons. The raw geography field otherwise splits major contributors and can understate national presence.
