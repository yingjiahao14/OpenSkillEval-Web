1. Publication Volume & Temporal Distribution

The dataset spans January 2024 through March 2026, with a pronounced temporal skew: 80.3% of records are from 2024, 15.1% from 2025, and 4.6% from 2026. This reflects real-world PubMed indexing lag, where older publications accumulate more complete metadata over time.

Data Quality Note: The 2024 dominance is an indexing artifact, not a signal of declining output. Any year-over-year comparison should use normalized rates or per-capita metrics rather than raw counts.

Monthly patterns reveal a massive January 2024 spike (1,665 publications) — likely a backlog of late-2023 publications indexed at the start of the calendar year. Excluding this anomaly, monthly volumes in 2024 trend downward from February (~388) through November (~36), before a modest December rebound. 2025 shows more even monthly distribution, with a notable December cluster (94) and early 2026 showing accelerating monthly counts (Jan 51 → Mar 98).

Strategic Insight: The January spike and declining 2024 trajectory suggest that analyses using this dataset should weight recent months more heavily or apply time-normalization when projecting forward.

2. Geographic Landscape of Biomedical Research

China leads decisively with 1,082 publications (22.1% of total), followed by the USA at 542 (11.1%). India, Japan, South Korea, Iran, Canada, the UK, and Germany round out the top tier. The top 10 countries collectively account for 53.6% of all indexed abstracts.

Year-over-year share shifts are modest given the small 2025–2026 sample sizes, but China maintains a ~24% share within each year, while the USA holds steady around 11–13%. India shows a slight uptick in proportional contribution in 2025 (8.0% vs 6.6% in 2024).

Strategic Insight: China’s dominance is structural and consistent across the time window. Funding strategies targeting high-output regions should prioritize Sino-US and Sino-European collaboration channels.

4. Open Access Adoption & Journal Landscape

The overall open-access rate across the dataset is 65.1%, but this masks significant temporal and structural variation. The 2024 OA rate stands at 64.1%, rising to 74.1% in 2025 before dropping sharply to 53.8% in 2026 — the latter likely an artifact of delayed PMC indexing for very recent publications.

Data Quality Note: The March 2026 OA rate of 23.5% is almost certainly an indexing lag effect. Recent publications take weeks to months to appear in PubMed Central. Do not interpret this as a reversal of OA adoption.

OA by Research Type

Preprints are 100% open access by definition in this dataset. Case Reports show the highest OA rate among peer-reviewed types (85.4%), while Systematic Reviews lag at 46.2% — possibly due to subscription-based specialist review journals.

Iran (86.9%) and the USA (76.8%) lead in national OA rates, while China (55.5%) trails despite its volume dominance — suggesting a large volume of subscription-based Chinese journal output.

Leading Journals

The most prolific journals in the dataset are Scientific Reports (82), Heliyon (76), and Cureus (69) — all broad-scope, high-volume outlets. Notably, Cureus, Heliyon, PLOS ONE, Frontiers in Immunology, and Nature Communications are 100% OA in this sample, while The Science of the Total Environment and Food Chemistry are predominantly closed-access.

5. Research Type Distribution & Collaboration Indicators

Journal Articles dominate the corpus at 93.9% (4,589 records). Case Reports (178), Systematic Reviews (39), Preprints (33), and Reviews (26) form the secondary tier.

Median author counts vary meaningfully by type: Randomized Controlled Trials and Preprints show the highest collaboration intensity (median 10 and 8 authors, respectively), while Editorials are typically solo or duo efforts (median 2). Systematic Reviews also show elevated collaboration (median 7), reflecting the labor-intensive nature of evidence synthesis.

Strategic Insight: China and Japan show the highest median author counts (7 and 6), suggesting larger research consortia. Funding instruments that support multi-institutional collaboration may be particularly effective in these ecosystems.

6. Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

  • 1. Temporal skew demands normalized analysis With 80% of records from 2024, raw counts favor earlier years. All trend comparisons must normalize by expected annual volume or use rate-based metrics. The Jan-2024 spike is an indexing backlog, not a true surge.
  • 2. China’s volume leadership is stable but OA lags China contributes over 1 in 5 abstracts yet has a 55.5% OA rate — well below Iran (86.9%) and the USA (76.8%). Funding bodies investing in Chinese partnerships should factor in access barriers and consider mandating OA compliance.
  • 3. Open access is accelerating — with caveats The 2024→2025 OA rate jump from 64.1% to 74.1% signals policy traction, but the 2026 drop is an indexing artifact. Expect true 2025–2026 OA rates to settle in the 70–80% range once PMC catches up.
  • 4. Emerging topics point to post-pandemic and environmental health shifts Air Pollution, Appendicitis, and Biosensing Techniques are the fastest-growing major topics on a normalized basis. Funding prioritization should anticipate continued growth in environmental health, surgical outcomes, and sensor-based diagnostics.
  • 5. Collaboration intensity varies by research type and geography RCTs and preprints show the largest teams; editorials the smallest. China and Japan lead in median authors per paper. Strategic grant design should calibrate collaboration expectations to research type and target country norms.
  • 6. The journal landscape favors mega-journals and OA-native outlets Scientific Reports, Heliyon, and Cureus dominate volume. These broad-scope OA platforms are reshaping dissemination patterns. Institutional library budgets and impact assessments should account for this shift away from traditional subscription specialty journals.
