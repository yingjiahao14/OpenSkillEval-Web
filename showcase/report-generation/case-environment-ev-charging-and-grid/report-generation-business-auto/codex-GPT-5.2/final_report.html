Optimization Insights & Recommendations

Recommendations synthesize queue performance, grid stress, renewables, pricing behavior, and the optimization reward signal. Actions below are phrased as interventions leaders can assign and measure.

1) Shift demand into green, lower-stress windows

Evidence: Off-Peak has materially higher renewable ratio (~0.393 vs ~0.290 Peak) and substantially lower station load (~35 kW vs ~84 kW Peak). Optimization reward is also significantly better Off-Peak (-7.06 vs -19.80).

Actions

  • Introduce explicit “Green Off-Peak” pricing incentives (discounts or rewards) to move flexible charging out of Peak.
  • Offer opt-in scheduled charging for fleets (Buses/Cars) with guaranteed start windows during Off-Peak.
  • Communicate sustainability: highlight higher renewable availability in Off-Peak windows to drive behavioral change.

2) Prioritize station-specific capacity and queue mitigation

Evidence: Stations with high mean demand/load (e.g., ST012, ST002, ST007, ST014, ST003) are likely to be bottlenecks. Separately, stations with the highest mean waiting time (ST004, ST009, ST011, ST012, ST014) create customer experience risk.

Actions

  • Deploy incremental capacity (additional stalls or higher-power chargers) first at ST012 and other high demand/load stations.
  • Implement queue management features (virtual queue, slot reservation) at high-wait stations starting with ST004 and ST009.
  • Track service levels: set operational targets for P90/P95 waiting time reduction (baseline P90=16, P95=18 minutes).

3) Use optimization reward as a control KPI

Evidence (correlations): optimization reward is strongly positively associated with renewable ratio, and strongly negatively associated with station load and charging demand. It is also negatively associated with waiting time.

Actions

  • Adopt mean optimization_reward as a weekly network KPI and tie it to operator incentives.
  • Use reward dips as early warnings for grid stress events, triggering load shedding or demand response workflows.
  • Re-parameterize dispatch: prioritize renewable-rich periods and penalize peak load in station-level control logic.

4) Fix pricing leverage (if the real network expects TOU behavior)

Evidence: Mean electricity price is nearly identical between Peak and Off-Peak in this dataset, and price has near-zero correlation with demand, renewables, and reward.

Actions

  • Introduce stronger time-of-use differentiation: raise Peak prices and/or discount Off-Peak to shape arrivals.
  • Couple incentives to renewables (e.g., additional discount when renewable ratio is above a threshold).
  • Measure impact with A/B tests: wait time tail (P90/P95), station load reduction at Peak, and reward improvement.
