2. Queue Length & Waiting Time Analysis

Queue lengths and waiting times by station, time slot, vehicle type, and traffic density — identifying bottleneck patterns and customer experience impact.

⏱️ Average wait time is 9.5 minutes across the entire network. The range across all segment combinations is narrow (9.06 – 10.35 min), indicating a systemic queue floor rather than isolated hot-spots.
💡 Traffic density is perfectly correlated with time slot: High traffic only occurs at Peak hours (load: 84.1 kW), Low/Medium only at Off-Peak. This confirms traffic is a downstream effect of time-of-day demand, not an independent variable.
🛣️ Highway Off-Peak shows the highest mean wait (9.63 min) while Urban Peak shows the lowest (9.31 min) — suggesting highway stations may benefit from additional charger capacity during off-peak periods when long-distance travelers arrive.
📅 Saturday carries the highest Off-Peak queue length (4.91 vehicles) — weekend leisure travel likely inflates highway station queues.

3. Energy Consumption & Grid Load Patterns

Energy consumption patterns, station load profiles across time slots and days of the week, and the relationship between charging power and grid stress.

⚡ Peak hours produce ~2.4× the grid load of Off-Peak (84.1 kW vs 34.6 kW), with minimal day-of-week variation within each slot. This points to time slot as the primary grid stress driver, not day of week.
💡 Urban and Highway stations carry nearly identical load profiles (Urban Peak: 84.1 kW, Highway Peak: 84.0 kW), indicating that infrastructure design is consistent and load balancing strategies can be applied uniformly across location types.
🔋 Average energy per session is highly consistent across vehicle types: Bus 39.5 kWh, Car 39.1 kWh, Two-Wheeler 38.7 kWh — suggesting charger power allocation, not vehicle type, is the binding constraint.
⏱️ Mean charging duration of ~179 minutes reflects relatively slow average charging speeds, meaning load-shifting incentives have a long window to act before sessions complete.

4. Renewable Energy Integration & Dynamic Pricing

Renewable energy ratio across conditions (time, weather, location) and dynamic electricity pricing correlations with demand, renewable availability, and optimization rewards.

🌱 Off-Peak hours consistently deliver ~36% higher renewable penetration than Peak hours (39.7% vs 29.1% under Clear skies). This is the clearest sustainability lever in the dataset — shifting demand to Off-Peak directly increases green charging.
💡 Weather has minimal impact on renewable ratio (Clear: 35.7%, Rainy: 35.4%, Cloudy: 35.1%). Grid mix is driven by demand timing, not weather conditions, suggesting a demand-side renewable grid.
🎯 Optimization reward strongly correlates with renewable ratio (r = +0.59) and inversely with station load (r = −0.58). Off-Peak sessions achieve mean reward of -7.1 vs -19.8 at Peak — a ~12.7 point improvement.
💰 Electricity pricing shows minimal time-slot variation (Off-Peak avg: $9.97, Peak avg: $9.94). This is a missed incentive opportunity — stronger peak/off-peak price differentiation would drive demand shifting.

6. Optimization Insights & Recommendations

Synthesized cross-section recommendations for load balancing, queue management, pricing strategy, and renewable maximization.

🎯 Key signal: Optimization reward correlates strongly with renewable ratio (+0.59) and inversely with station load (−0.58) and wait time (−0.52). Every intervention that reduces load or shifts sessions off-peak simultaneously improves sustainability and the composite grid score.

⚡ Load Shifting & Demand Management

  • Off-Peak station load is 2.4× lower (34.7 kW vs 84.1 kW at Peak). Target moving at least 15% of Peak sessions to Off-Peak to reduce grid stress materially.
  • Sessions average ~178 min — implementing time-of-use scheduling for non-urgent vehicles (especially Buses with predictable routes) can flatten the daily load curve.
  • Deploy dynamic queue reservation during Peak: vehicles arriving ≥30 min early can be deferred to Off-Peak slots with incentive credits.

🌱 Renewable Energy Maximization

  • Off-Peak renewable ratio is ~36% higher than Peak (39.7% vs 28.8%). Price incentives during Off-Peak directly increase green charging with no infrastructure cost.
  • Weather does not meaningfully affect renewable availability — focus renewable campaigns on time-of-day messaging, not weather-based triggers.
  • Set a network-level KPI target: Off-Peak renewable ratio ≥ 42% (current Off-Peak avg: 39.3%) via expanded off-peak capacity.

💰 Dynamic Pricing Strategy Redesign

  • Current pricing shows minimal Peak/Off-Peak differential ($9.94 vs $9.97). Introduce tiered pricing: 20–30% premium at Peak, 15% discount at Off-Peak.
  • Correlate pricing with real-time renewable ratio: offer "green rate" discounts when renewable ratio exceeds 45%, creating demand pull toward high-renewable windows.
  • Deploy price-transparency alerts in charging apps at time of arrival so customers can self-select into off-peak slots.

🛣️ Queue & Capacity Management

  • Wait times are systemically ~9.5 min with low variance — adding charger units at high-volume stations (ST013 Urban, ST017 Highway) will reduce queue length before it becomes a customer pain point.
  • Highway Off-Peak wait (9.63 min) slightly exceeds Urban Off-Peak (9.54 min) — prioritize highway charger expansion for long-distance travelers who have less flexibility.
  • Saturday Off-Peak shows peak queue length (4.91). Deploy weekend-specific overflow routing to direct vehicles to lower-utilization stations.
  • Leverage the strong priority system correlation: expanding High-priority lanes at busy stations improves both reward score and commercial EV throughput.
🏆 Composite Priority: The single highest-leverage intervention is Peak-to-Off-Peak demand shifting via tiered pricing. It simultaneously reduces grid load (−49.4 kW average), increases renewable ratio (+9 percentage points), and improves the optimization reward by ~12.7 points — all with near-zero capital expenditure.
🔧 Secondary Priority: Add chargers at ST013 Urban, ST017 Highway, and ST001 Highway (highest session volumes). A 10% capacity increase at these three stations would relieve ~70 sessions of queue pressure.
⚠️ Data Limitation: This dataset is simulated with no documented missing values. Real-world deployment should validate queue and wait patterns against actual infrastructure telemetry before acting on specific station recommendations.
