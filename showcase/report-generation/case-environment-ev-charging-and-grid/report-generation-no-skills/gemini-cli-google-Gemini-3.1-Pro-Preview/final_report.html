1. Station Utilization & Demand Analysis

This section outlines the distribution of charging demand across different location types and time periods.

Takeaway: Highway locations show different peak characteristics compared to Urban stations. Demand surges predictably during designated Peak hours, suggesting capacity constraints may be localized to specific time-windows rather than overall station limits.

2. Queue Length & Waiting Time Analysis

Analyzing customer wait times is critical for assessing the operational pain points and overall customer experience.

Takeaway: Peak time slots across Urban and Highway locations experience the highest wait times, correlating strongly with high traffic density. Addressing these specific bottlenecks is key to improving customer satisfaction.

3. Energy Consumption & Grid Load Patterns

Evaluating the electrical stress on the local grid infrastructure across different operational periods.

Takeaway: Station load closely tracks the time slots, with significant variation between Peak and Off-Peak times. The use of high-power chargers during peak periods disproportionately stresses the grid.

4. Renewable Energy Integration & Dynamic Pricing

Understanding how weather and time impact the availability of green energy and the resulting electricity pricing.

Takeaway: Clear weather conditions significantly boost the renewable energy ratio. There is an opportunity to leverage dynamic pricing to encourage charging when renewable availability is high, effectively lowering operational costs and carbon footprint.

5. Vehicle Charging Behavior & Session Profiles

Profiling the types of vehicles utilizing the network and their corresponding charging characteristics.

Takeaway: Different vehicle classes require vastly different energy transfers. Buses and high-capacity vehicles have long, energy-intensive sessions, suggesting a need for dedicated high-power bays or scheduled fleet charging to prevent bottlenecking passenger cars.

6. Optimization Insights & Recommendations

Based on the data analysis, the following actionable interventions are proposed to optimize grid stability, maximize renewable energy use, and reduce customer wait times.

Key Findings Recap

  • Queue Bottlenecks: Peak hour queues are severe, especially at Urban stations with high traffic density.
  • Grid Stress: High-power fast chargers operating during Peak grid-load times cause disproportionate strain and lower optimization rewards.
  • Green Charging: Renewable energy is underutilized during clear weather, off-peak periods when grid demand is low.

Strategic Interventions

  1. Dynamic Pricing for Load Shifting: Implement steeper pricing discounts during periods of high renewable energy availability (e.g., mid-day, clear weather) to shift demand away from evening Peak hours.
  2. Queue Management via Priority Scheduling: Introduce an app-based booking system for Urban and Highway stations to smooth out unexpected demand spikes and reduce average wait times.
  3. Smart Charger Throttling: Deploy dynamic power allocation that slightly throttles charge rates (e.g., from 50kW to 40kW) during maximum station load events. The data shows this will significantly improve the 'optimization reward' signal without severely impacting session duration.
  4. Dedicated Fleet Infrastructure: Given the high energy consumption of Buses and commercial vehicles, redirect fleet charging to dedicated hubs or mandate off-peak charging schedules to free up public chargers for transient users.
Executive Summary: The network is currently reactive to demand. Transitioning to a proactive model—using price signals and smart power throttling—will balance the grid, increase the renewable charging ratio, and substantially cut customer wait times.
