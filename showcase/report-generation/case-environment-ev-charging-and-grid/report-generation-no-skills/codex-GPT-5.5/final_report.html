Executive Summary

Operational performance is shaped primarily by time slot and location type. Across 8,354 sessions, average wait time is 9.5 minutes with an average queue length of 4.6 vehicles. The network consumes 39.1 kWh per session on average and runs at 35.4% renewable energy share.

Customer experience: The highest location/time wait segment is Highway Off-Peak at 9.6 minutes; the lowest is Urban Peak at 9.3 minutes.
Grid stress: The highest average station-load segment is Urban Peak at 84.1 kW.
Sustainability: The strongest weather/time renewable segment is Clear Off-Peak at 39.7%.

No Suburban rows are present in data.csv; location segmentation is therefore limited to Urban and Highway. No Mid-Peak rows are present in data.csv; time-slot segmentation is therefore limited to Off-Peak and Peak. These limitations are reported rather than filled with assumptions.

Optimization Insights & Recommendations

These actions synthesize the queue, utilization, grid-load, renewable, pricing, and reward findings into implementation-ready interventions.

1

Target queue relief at the worst location/time and station segments

Prioritize operational changes in Highway Off-Peak where average wait reaches 9.6 minutes. For the top-wait stations led by ST004 (10.4 minutes), add live overflow routing, display expected waits in-app, and reserve at least one charger for short-duration or high-priority sessions during constrained windows.

2

Shift flexible demand away from high-load windows

Use reservations, fleet scheduling, and tariffs to move flexible charging from the higher-load slot (Peak average 84.1 kW) toward the lower-load slot (Off-Peak average 34.7 kW). Set queue-aware caps when real-time load exceeds the observed P90 station load of 93.8 kW.

3

Align dynamic prices with renewable-rich periods

Offer renewable-window discounts and fleet charging commitments in the weather/time segment with the strongest renewable share: Clear Off-Peak at 39.7%. Avoid incentives in the weakest renewable segment, Rainy Peak at 28.8%, unless required for queue relief.

4

Use optimization reward as a dispatch guardrail

The best observed location/time reward segment is Urban Off-Peak (-6.9), while the weakest is Highway Peak (-20.0). Route non-urgent sessions toward the higher-reward segment when wait-time and travel-distance constraints are acceptable.

5

Segment expansion decisions by bottleneck type

At high-demand stations such as ST012, validate whether the constraint is physical charger count, feeder capacity, or dispatch sequencing. Add chargers where high wait and high demand coincide; deploy load control or on-site storage where high station load is the binding constraint.

Management decision: The strongest near-term ROI is likely from software-driven queue routing and pricing changes, followed by targeted capacity additions at the station list identified in this report. This avoids overbuilding in segments where waits are lower or where grid load, not charger count, is the primary constraint.

Data Integrity & Method Notes

  • All figures are calculated directly from /app/benchmark/data.csv; no values, trends, or categories were fabricated.
  • The file contains 8,354 rows and 27 original columns; the simple missing-value check found 0 blank cells.
  • Available location types in the file are Highway, Urban. No Suburban rows are present in data.csv; location segmentation is therefore limited to Urban and Highway.
  • Available time slots in the file are Off-Peak, Peak. No Mid-Peak rows are present in data.csv; time-slot segmentation is therefore limited to Off-Peak and Peak.
  • Correlations are Pearson correlations computed on row-level observations. They indicate association, not causality.
