Critical Finding: Priority Scheduling Ineffective

High-priority sessions wait 9.51 min — identical to Low-priority (9.51 min) and barely better than Medium (9.55 min). The priority system provides no meaningful queue advantage, directly undermining any SLA or emergency charging guarantees.

Queue Depth Note

Off-Peak periods show higher queue lengths (4.65 vs 4.50) and longer wait times (9.58 vs 9.43 min) than Peak, suggesting that Off-Peak sessions may have fewer active charger slots available, or that lower-urgency vehicles linger in queue longer.

6. Optimization Insights & Recommendations

Synthesized, actionable interventions for load balancing, queue management, pricing strategy, and renewable energy maximization — grounded in data findings across all prior sections.

  • 1

    Redesign Dynamic Pricing to Incentivize Off-Peak Demand Shift

    Finding: Off-Peak electricity price (9.97) is marginally higher than Peak (9.94), providing no financial incentive to shift demand. Meanwhile, Peak sessions yield optimization rewards 2.8× worse (−19.80 vs −7.06) and use 25% less renewable energy (29% vs 39%).
    Action: Implement a meaningful Off-Peak discount of 15–25% and a Peak surcharge. Target a price ratio of at least 1.20× between Peak and Off-Peak to materially shift EV charging behavior. Model shows potential to reduce Peak load from 84.07 kW toward 60 kW if 20–25% of Peak sessions are shifted.

  • 2

    Launch Renewable-Linked "Green Charging" Incentive Program

    Finding: Renewable ratio correlates positively with optimization reward (r = 0.59). Off-Peak periods have 34% higher renewable penetration (39% vs 29%). Clear weather sessions marginally outperform cloudy/rainy (−11.82 vs −12.01).
    Action: Introduce a real-time renewable energy indicator in charger UX and a "Green Charge" pricing tier active when renewable ratio exceeds 0.40. This capitalizes on the natural Off-Peak renewable surplus and improves the mean optimization reward toward the −7 range network-wide.

  • 3

    Fix the Charging Priority System — Current Implementation Is Inoperative

    Finding: High, Low, and Medium priority sessions all wait 9.51–9.55 minutes — a statistically negligible difference. Priority routing is providing no queue bypass benefit.
    Action: Audit the priority scheduling algorithm. Implement dedicated fast-queue lanes for High-priority sessions with a target wait time of ≤5 min. This is essential for commercial fleet customers (buses), emergency services, and premium service tiers. Without this fix, priority tiers cannot be marketed or monetized.

  • 4

    Investigate Off-Peak Queue Congestion — Counterintuitive Pattern Requires Root Cause

    Finding: Off-Peak periods show longer queues (4.65 vs 4.50 vehicles) and longer waits (9.58 vs 9.43 min) than Peak, despite lower station load (34.65 vs 84.07 kW). Sessions are 67% more frequent Off-Peak (5,222 vs 3,132).
    Action: Conduct charger availability audit during Off-Peak hours. Investigate whether reduced staffing, charger maintenance windows, or deliberate capacity throttling is creating artificial congestion. If charger count is restricted Off-Peak, restoring full capacity during these windows would directly improve both wait times and optimization rewards.

  • 5

    Manage Peak Load via Demand-Smoothing and Load Reservation

    Finding: Peak station load (84.07 kW) is 2.43× Off-Peak (34.65 kW) — a severe demand concentration. Sessions at Peak are fewer (3,132 vs 5,222) but far more grid-intensive per charger slot.
    Action: Implement arrival-time reservation for Peak periods to smooth intra-peak demand. Introduce 15-minute staggered booking windows per station to prevent simultaneous session starts. Additionally, deploy smart load balancing to distribute load across charger slots dynamically, targeting a Peak ceiling of 70 kW (−17% reduction).

  • 6

    Leverage Uniform Session Profile for Predictive Grid Pre-Positioning

    Finding: All vehicle types arrive at ~34–35% SoC and depart at ~98.5% SoC, consuming ~39 kWh over ~178 minutes with remarkable uniformity. This is a highly predictable demand signal.
    Action: Implement forward-looking grid pre-positioning: when arrival rates suggest an upcoming Peak window (e.g., >15 sessions/hour), pre-charge station battery buffers or negotiate demand-response contracts to secure renewable capacity. The uniform 39 kWh/session profile allows precise energy procurement forecasting with narrow confidence intervals.

Summary: Highest-Impact Interventions

Three interventions offer the highest combined ROI: (1) Dynamic pricing redesign to shift demand Off-Peak, (2) Priority queue repair to activate SLA-backed service tiers, and (3) Green Charging incentives tied to the proven renewable–reward correlation. Together, these address the network's three key failure modes: grid inefficiency at Peak, inoperative priority scheduling, and under-utilized renewable windows.
