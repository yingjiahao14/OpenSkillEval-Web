6 Optimization Insights & Recommendations

Synthesize findings into actionable recommendations for load balancing, queue management, pricing strategy, and renewable energy maximization.

Cross-Cutting Findings

  • Peak-to-Off-Peak grid load differential is 142.6% (84.07 kW vs. 34.65 kW), but wait times are flat across time slots — capacity exists to shift demand.
  • Off-Peak sessions have 35% higher renewable energy ratios than Peak (39.3% vs. 29.0%), and 30.1% of Off-Peak sessions exceed 50% renewable vs. just 8.8% during Peak.
  • Dynamic pricing is effectively flat (9.94–9.97 across time slots) and shows zero meaningful correlation with demand or renewable availability — a major policy gap.
  • Peak optimization reward is −19.80 vs. Off-Peak at −7.06, confirming Peak sessions generate nearly 3× worse grid outcomes.
  • Vehicle types and location types show minimal behavioral differences, enabling uniform policy interventions across the fleet.

Recommendations

1. Implement Time-of-Use Pricing
Introduce meaningful price differentials between Peak and Off-Peak windows. Current pricing is flat at ~9.96 across all slots. A 20–30% Off-Peak discount (targeting ~7.50–8.00) would create economic incentives aligned with the 142.6% load differential and 35% higher renewable availability during Off-Peak.
High Priority · Estimated impact: 15–25% Peak demand reduction
2. Green Charging Incentive Program
Offer a "Green Charging" discount when renewable ratios exceed 50%. Off-Peak already delivers this in 30.1% of sessions vs. 8.8% during Peak. Price at a premium when renewable ratio is below 25% (mostly Peak). This directly ties cost to sustainability outcomes.
High Priority · Estimated impact: 10–20% shift to high-renewable windows
3. Load-Shifting Campaigns for Off-Peak
62.5% of sessions already occur Off-Peak, but they carry only 34.65 kW average load vs. 84.07 kW during Peak. Target flexible chargers (Two-Wheelers with shorter sessions) through app notifications and scheduled charging features to fill Off-Peak capacity without increasing Peak demand.
Medium Priority · Estimated impact: 8–12% Peak load reduction
4. Smart Queue Prioritization
While wait times are currently manageable (~9.5 min average), implement priority queuing for sessions with high renewable energy availability. When renewable ratio >50%, fast-track vehicles to chargers. This maximizes green energy capture and improves the optimization reward metric from its current −11.83 baseline.
Medium Priority · Estimated impact: 5–10% improvement in optimization reward
5. Station-Level Capacity Rebalancing
Top stations (ST013, ST001, ST012) carry disproportionate demand. Evaluate adding charger capacity at these stations or redirecting traffic to lower-utilization stations. Current queue lengths (4.5–4.8 avg) suggest moderate congestion that could worsen with demand growth.
Lower Priority · Estimated impact: Preventive — future-proofing
6. Weather-Aware Scheduling
Clear-weather Off-Peak periods achieve the highest renewable ratios (39.7%). Integrate weather forecasts into the scheduling system to proactively offer "sunny day discounts" and encourage charging when renewable supply is forecasted to be strongest.
Lower Priority · Estimated impact: 3–5% renewable ratio improvement
Key Takeaway The network's primary optimization lever is temporal: shifting demand from Peak to Off-Peak simultaneously reduces grid stress by up to 142.6%, improves renewable energy capture by 35%, and nearly triples the optimization reward. The current flat pricing structure is the single biggest policy gap. Implementing time-of-use pricing with a green-charging incentive would align economic signals with grid and sustainability objectives, potentially moving 15–25% of Peak demand to Off-Peak windows.
