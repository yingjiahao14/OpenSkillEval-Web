Key Network Signal
The most critical finding across all KPIs: Peak time slots drive station load to 84.1 kW (2.4× off-peak) while simultaneously suppressing renewable energy availability to 29% (vs. 39% off-peak) — a compound problem where the grid is most stressed exactly when it is least green. Shifting sessions toward off-peak windows addresses both simultaneously.

Optimization Insights & Recommendations

Synthesis: Where Optimization Reward Is Won or Lost
Top-decile sessions (reward ≥ P90) average a renewable ratio of 59.4%, station load of 41.1 kW, and wait time of only 4.4 min — nearly all (96.5%) occur during Off-Peak. Bottom-decile sessions (reward ≤ P10) average a renewable ratio of 21.0%, station load of 88.1 kW, and wait time of 14.3 min — 97.4% occur during Peak. The intervention playbook follows directly from this contrast.

Specific Recommended Interventions

1

Deploy Off-Peak Load Shifting with Incentive Pricing HIGH PRIORITY

Peak sessions score 2.8× worse on the optimization reward metric (−19.8 vs −7.1) and carry 2.43× the station load (84 vs 35 kW). Introduce a time-of-use pricing discount of 20–30% during Off-Peak windows. Since price currently has zero correlation with demand (r = −0.01) or reward (r = 0.005), pricing reforms will create a new behavioral signal. Target: shift 10–15% of Peak sessions to Off-Peak, which could improve the mean network reward from −11.8 toward −9.5.

2

Create "Green Charging" Premium Off-Peak Slots with Renewable Incentive HIGH PRIORITY

Off-Peak windows offer 39.3% renewable ratio vs 29.0% during Peak — a 35% relative improvement in clean energy. Sessions charging when renewable ratio ≥ 50% average a reward of −3.16 (5.2× better than low-renewable sessions at −16.4). Introduce a "Green Charging" product tier that explicitly schedules sessions during high-renewable windows. Fleet operators (Buses: 2,760 sessions) are the highest-leverage target for bulk off-peak scheduling agreements.

3

Investigate Highway Off-Peak Queue Anomaly — Right-Size Capacity MEDIUM PRIORITY

Highway Off-Peak shows the highest queue lengths in the network (4.70 vehicles avg), higher than Peak (4.47). This is counterintuitive and suggests that while load is low, the number of simultaneously arriving vehicles exceeds stationed charger availability. Consider deploying 1–2 additional charger bays at high-utilization Highway stations (ST013, ST001) to absorb off-peak demand surges, which is the very window being targeted for load shift. Failure to expand capacity there would create a bottleneck that defeats the load-shifting strategy.

4

Couple Dynamic Pricing to Real-Time Renewable Ratio — Not Just Demand HIGH PRIORITY

Current pricing (range $5–$15, mean ~$9.96) is essentially random with respect to both grid load (r = −0.01) and renewable availability (r = −0.005). The optimization reward is strongly correlated with renewable ratio (r = 0.59) but not with price. Repricing electricity using a renewable-adjusted tariff — lower price when renewable ratio is high, higher when it is low — would align consumer incentives with the optimization objective. This single intervention addresses the most powerful lever identified in the data.

5

Reduce Long-Tail Wait Times via Smart Queue Prioritization MEDIUM PRIORITY

While median wait time is acceptable at 9 minutes, 5.5% of sessions wait 15+ minutes (up to 28 min max). Implement dynamic queue management that deprioritizes long-duration sessions (Two-Wheelers and Cars with high battery capacity) when queues exceed 6 vehicles, preferring lower-SOC sessions that need urgent charging. Target: reduce P90 wait time from 16 to under 12 minutes. This has outsized customer satisfaction impact for highway transit use cases where predictable wait times are critical.

6

Upgrade 7 kW and 11 kW Chargers at High-Utilization Peak Stations MEDIUM PRIORITY

The charger power fleet is evenly split across 7/11/22/50 kW tiers. Sessions on 7 kW chargers contribute disproportionately to session duration (energy is independent of power level), occupying a bay for much longer per kWh delivered. At the busiest stations (ST013: 459 sessions, ST001: 443 sessions), replacing 7 kW and 11 kW chargers with 22 kW units would improve bay throughput by 2–3× for the same energy delivered, directly reducing queue length and wait time without adding new bays.

7

Introduce Partial SOC Charging Incentives to Improve Throughput LOW PRIORITY

Average final SOC is 98.5%, with median at 100% — virtually every vehicle charges to full. While this serves the customer, it maximizes session duration and bay occupancy. Offer a pricing incentive (e.g., 5% discount) for sessions that exit at 80% SOC. Modelling suggests this could reduce average session duration by ~15–20%, directly expanding effective network capacity. This is especially relevant at congested Peak-hour stations where bay turnover is the limiting factor.
