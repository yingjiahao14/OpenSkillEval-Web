Station Utilization & Demand Analysis

Charging demand is heavily concentrated in Peak windows (07:00–10:00 and 17:00–21:00), where average demand reaches 84.1 kW versus 34.6 kW during Off-Peak. Highway and Urban stations show nearly identical demand profiles, indicating network-wide synchronization rather than localized spikes.

Queue Length & Waiting Time Analysis

Customer experience is under pressure: 11.3% of sessions exceed 15 minutes of wait time, and 16.0% arrive to a queue longer than 7 vehicles. While average wait times are similar across Peak (9.4 min) and Off-Peak (9.6 min), queue lengths compress slightly during Peak (4.5 vs 4.7), suggesting higher throughput but tighter margins.

Energy Consumption & Grid Load Patterns

Grid load doubles during Peak periods (84.1 kW vs 34.7 kW Off-Peak), yet energy consumed per session remains stable (~39.1 kWh). This indicates that Peak stress is driven by simultaneity — more concurrent sessions — rather than heavier individual charging. Two-Wheelers paradoxically show the highest average station load (54.5 kW), likely due to higher session concurrency.

Renewable Energy Integration & Dynamic Pricing

Renewable penetration averages 35.4% network-wide, but the distribution is bimodal: midday Off-Peak hours (10:00–16:00) achieve 60.1% renewable ratio, while night Off-Peak and all Peak hours hover around 25%. This creates a clear “green charging window” during late morning and early afternoon.

Vehicle Charging Behavior & Session Profiles

The fleet is evenly split across Cars (33.9%), Two-Wheelers (33.1%), and Buses (33.0%). Despite similar battery capacities (~61 kWh), session dynamics differ subtly: Buses have the longest waits (9.6 min) and highest SOC delta (63.9%), while Two-Wheelers show the highest station load (54.5 kW) — likely due to higher power-draw concurrency.

Optimization Insights & Recommendations

Synthesizing across station utilization, queue behavior, grid load, and renewable availability, five high-impact intervention areas emerge. Each recommendation is grounded in the dataset and quantified where possible.

1. Load-Shift 10–15% of Peak Demand to Midday Off-Peak

Peak periods account for 37.5% of total energy delivered but drive 84.1 kW average station load — 2.4× the Off-Peak baseline. Midday Off-Peak hours (10:00–16:00) offer a renewable ratio of 60.1% and much stronger optimization rewards (avg −3.3 vs −19.8 during Peak). Shifting even a modest share of Peak sessions to this window would reduce grid stress and raise sustainability simultaneously.

Action: Introduce time-of-use pricing with a 15–20% discount during 10:00–16:00 and a 10% surcharge during 07:00–10:00 and 17:00–21:00. Target commercial fleets (Buses) first, as they show predictable schedules.

2. Deploy Dynamic Queue Management at ST004, ST009, and ST012

ST004 and ST009 record the highest peak wait times (10.0 and 10.0 minutes), while ST012 tops demand (56.1 avg) yet posts the worst optimization reward (−12.7). These three stations represent the network’s primary customer-experience risk. High-load events (>90th percentile) at these stations average 5.2 vehicles in queue.

Action: Implement real-time queue alerts and a reservation system for Peak slots at these stations. Consider adding 1–2 overflow chargers or redirecting 10% of demand to neighboring ST005 and ST020, which have the best off-peak rewards and lower queue pressure.

3. Maximize Renewable Uptake During the Midday Green Window

Only 11.1% of Peak sessions achieve >40% renewable ratio, compared to 40.0% of Off-Peak sessions. The correlation between renewable ratio and optimization reward is strong (r = 0.59). Midday Off-Peak is the only window where renewable penetration consistently exceeds 50%.

Action: Launch a “Green Charging” incentive program offering loyalty credits or carbon-offset certificates for sessions booked between 10:00–16:00. Display real-time renewable share on station screens to nudge behavior.

4. Rebalance Charger Power Assignment by Vehicle Type

Two-Wheelers generate the highest average station load (54.5 kW) despite similar energy needs. This suggests they are frequently assigned to higher-power chargers, creating unnecessary grid stress. Lower-power chargers (7–11 kW) are already available in equal numbers and extend session duration without affecting energy delivery.

Action: Default-assign Two-Wheelers to 7–11 kW chargers and reserve 50 kW units for Buses and high-SOC Cars. This simple routing rule could reduce Two-Wheeler load contribution by an estimated 15–20%.

5. Align Pricing with Weather-Driven Renewable Forecasts

Electricity price varies weakly with renewable availability (r = −0.005), meaning the pricing signal is currently decoupled from green energy supply. Rainy Peak periods actually show the lowest prices (9.79) despite the lowest renewable share (0.288), creating a perverse incentive.

Action: Integrate weather-based renewable forecasting into dynamic pricing. Raise prices slightly during low-renewable Peak periods (Rainy/Cloudy evenings) and lower them during high-renewable midday windows. This would improve optimization rewards while maintaining revenue neutrality.
