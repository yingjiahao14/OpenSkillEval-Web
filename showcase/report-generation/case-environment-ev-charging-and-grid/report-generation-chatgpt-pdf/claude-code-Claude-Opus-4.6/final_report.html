High Priority Implement Peak-to-Off-Peak Load Shifting

Problem: Peak station loads average 84.1 kW (2.4x off-peak), and the optimization reward drops from -7.1 (off-peak) to -19.8 (peak)—a 179% deterioration. Meanwhile, off-peak renewable penetration is 35% higher.

Action: Introduce time-of-use pricing with meaningful differentials (current pricing shows near-zero demand correlation at r = -0.012). Offer 15–25% discounts during off-peak windows and 10–20% surcharges during peak periods. Target shifting 15–20% of peak sessions to off-peak.

Expected Impact: Reduced grid stress, improved optimization reward, higher renewable energy utilization, and more even charger utilization across the day.

High Priority Deploy Smart Charging & Partial-Charge Options

Problem: All vehicle types charge to ~98.5% SOC, with average sessions consuming 39 kWh over 179 minutes. Full-charge behavior maximizes dwell time and reduces charger turnover.

Action: Introduce an "80% quick charge" option that stops at 80% SOC. At typical charge curves, this could reduce session duration by 25–35% (the final 20% of SOC takes disproportionate time). Provide pricing incentives for partial charges during high-demand periods.

Expected Impact: 25–35% reduction in average session duration during peak, directly improving charger throughput and reducing queue wait times.

Medium Priority Accelerate DC Fast Charger Deployment

Problem: 7 kW chargers (25% of sessions) have an average session time of 339 minutes (5.6 hours), compared to 48 minutes for 50 kW DC fast chargers—a 7x throughput difference. Energy delivered per session is equivalent across power levels.

Action: Prioritize 50 kW DC fast charger installations at high-demand Urban stations (ST012, ST003, ST007, ST004). Consider upgrading existing 7 kW stalls at these locations. Target a charger mix weighted 40–50% toward 22+ kW.

Expected Impact: Doubling throughput at upgraded stations would effectively add capacity without building new stations, reducing average wait times and queues.

Medium Priority Redesign Dynamic Pricing to Track Grid Signals

Problem: The current pricing mechanism has near-zero correlation with demand (r = -0.012) and renewable availability (r = -0.005). Pricing is effectively static and fails to incentivize grid-optimal behavior.

Action: Implement real-time pricing that incorporates: (1) grid load level with tiered surcharges above 60 kW station load, (2) renewable availability bonus of 5–10% discount when renewable ratio exceeds 35%, and (3) queue-based pricing with a small premium when queue length exceeds 5 vehicles.

Expected Impact: Price-responsive demand would naturally distribute load toward low-stress, high-renewable periods, directly improving the three optimization reward drivers.

Sustainability Launch "Green Charging Windows" Program

Problem: Renewable ratio varies from 29.0% (peak) to 39.3% (off-peak). The optimization reward is most strongly correlated with renewable ratio (r = +0.59), yet no mechanism currently incentivizes green charging.

Action: Publish real-time "Green Score" at each station. Create a loyalty program that rewards off-peak and high-renewable sessions with credits or reduced rates. Market off-peak slots as premium sustainability choices to corporate fleet operators.

Expected Impact: Increasing the network-wide renewable ratio from 35.4% to 40%+ would significantly improve optimization rewards and sustainability metrics, while differentiating the network in a competitive market.

Operational Address Off-Peak Queue Anomaly

Problem: Queue lengths are slightly higher during off-peak (4.65) than peak (4.50), despite 62.5% of all sessions occurring off-peak. This counterintuitive pattern suggests charger capacity allocation may not match arrival distribution.

Action: Audit station-level charger availability during off-peak hours. Investigate whether chargers are being taken offline for maintenance during off-peak or if capacity is being reserved. Ensure at least the same number of active chargers are available during off-peak as peak periods.

Expected Impact: Resolving this anomaly could reduce off-peak wait times, which in turn makes off-peak slots more attractive for load-shifting programs.
