Key Takeaways

  • Price signals are decoupled from renewable supply; introduce time-of-use discounts tied to real-time renewable forecasts.
  • Weather impact is secondary to time-of-day; operational playbooks should focus on hourly renewable curves rather than weather contingencies.

6. Optimization Insights & Recommendations

Synthesizing across utilization, queue, grid, renewable, and vehicle data, five high-impact interventions emerge:

6.1 Load Shifting to Off-Peak & Midday Green Windows

The grid experiences a 3.5× load swing (24.3 kW to 84.9 kW). Introduce time-of-use pricing that discounts rates by 15–20% during 10:00–11:00 when renewable supply peaks (61.7%) and load is moderate. This simultaneously reduces peak-demand charges and raises the renewable KPI.

6.2 Overnight Queue Management

Queue length peaks at 03:00 (5.1 vehicles) despite low absolute demand. This is driven by maintenance windows and reduced charger availability. Action: Schedule preventive maintenance between 01:00–04:00 at alternating stations so that network capacity never drops below 85% during this window.

6.3 Targeted Capacity Expansion

ST012, ST002, and ST007 are the highest-demand stations and also among the longest-queue locations. Action: Fast-track charger additions (2–4 units each) at these three stations before the next demand growth cycle.

6.4 Dynamic Priority Re-Calibration

Current priority levels (Low/Medium/High) yield nearly identical optimization rewards. Action: Rebuild the priority algorithm to weight (a) vehicle SOC deficit, (b) queue length at arrival, and (c) real-time renewable ratio. High-priority slots should be reserved for low-SOC fleet vehicles during green-energy windows.

6.5 Price-Renewable Coupling

Electricity price is uncorrelated with renewable supply (r ≈ 0). Action: Integrate a renewable-price multiplier into the billing engine so that customers see lower tariffs when the renewable ratio exceeds 50%. This creates a demand-response loop that automatically shifts load toward greener hours.

Projected Impact: If 15% of Peak sessions shift to the 10:00–11:00 green window, the network-wide renewable ratio could rise from 35.4% to approximately 39–40%, while reducing the 17:00 peak load by ~8–10%.
