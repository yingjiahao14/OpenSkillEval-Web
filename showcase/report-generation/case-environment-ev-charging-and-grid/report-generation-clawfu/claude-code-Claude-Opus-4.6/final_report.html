Optimization Insights & Recommendations

Synthesized recommendations for load balancing, queue management, pricing strategy, and renewable energy maximization.

1. Implement Time-of-Use Pricing to Shift Demand Off-Peak

Current electricity pricing is flat (~$9.95) regardless of time slot, yet Peak grid load is 2.4x Off-Peak. Introducing a meaningful price differential (e.g., 20–30% premium during Peak) would incentivize drivers to charge during Off-Peak windows, reducing grid stress from 84 kW toward the 34.7 kW Off-Peak baseline. The optimization reward already shows a -19.8 penalty during Peak vs. -7.1 Off-Peak — pricing should reflect this.

2. Target Off-Peak Charging for Maximum Renewable Impact

Renewable energy ratio is 10 percentage points higher during Off-Peak (39.3% vs 29.0%). Coupling demand-shift pricing with a "green charging" marketing program during Off-Peak hours would simultaneously reduce grid stress and increase the network's sustainability KPI. Since weather has negligible impact on renewable ratio, this strategy is reliable year-round regardless of conditions.

3. Investigate Structural Queue Bottlenecks

Average wait time of 9.5 min (P90: 16 min) is constant regardless of time slot, location, traffic, or weather — an unusual pattern that suggests the bottleneck is charger availability or allocation logic rather than demand-driven congestion. Audit charger assignment algorithms and station capacity (currently ~4.6 vehicles in queue at any time) to identify whether adding charger units or improving scheduling can reduce structural wait times.

4. Prioritize Capacity Expansion at High-Demand Urban Stations

The top 4 stations by demand are all Urban (ST012, ST003, ST007, ST004), with ST004 showing the highest average wait at 10.1 min. Urban locations serve 50.1% of all sessions. Expanding charger capacity at these stations — particularly ST012 (demand 56.1, 6% above network mean) and ST004 (highest wait) — would yield the greatest customer experience improvement per unit of investment.

5. Leverage Optimization Reward as a Real-Time Scheduling Signal

The optimization reward metric (-11.83 mean) swings dramatically by time slot (-7.1 Off-Peak vs. -19.8 Peak) but is stable across locations and days. This makes it a strong candidate for real-time scheduling: use the reward signal to dynamically route vehicles to chargers or time slots with higher (less negative) scores, implementing a reinforcement-learning-based dispatch system that continuously improves grid outcomes.

6. Standardize Monitoring Across the Uniform Fleet

Given the remarkably uniform session profiles across vehicle types (identical ~39 kWh consumption, ~179 min duration, ~63.7 pp SOC gain), current infrastructure can be managed with a single capacity planning model rather than vehicle-differentiated strategies. If fleet diversity increases in the future, reassess with tiered charger allocation.

Bottom Line: The single highest-impact lever is introducing time-of-use pricing. Flat pricing during a 2.4x load swing is the root cause of both peak grid stress and sub-optimal renewable utilization. Pairing pricing reform with queue management improvements at the top urban stations would improve the optimization reward metric, reduce customer wait times, and increase the renewable energy share — all without new infrastructure investment.

Data Notes & Limitations

Dataset scope: 8,354 simulated sessions from January 2025 across 20 stations. Location types in the data are limited to Urban and Highway (no Suburban). Time slots are limited to Peak and Off-Peak (no Mid-Peak). Weather conditions include Clear, Cloudy, and Rainy (no Snowy). Vehicle types are Bus, Car, and Two-Wheeler only — the source schema mentions Sedan, SUV, and Truck but these do not appear in the data. The synthetic nature of the dataset produces unusually uniform distributions across vehicle types and locations, which may not reflect real-world operational variance. All figures are derived directly from the source data with no fabricated values.
