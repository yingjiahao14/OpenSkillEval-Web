4. Renewable Energy Integration & Dynamic Pricing

Sustainability Headline

The network averages 35.4% renewable energy ratio. Off-peak windows deliver 39.3% renewable penetration vs. 29.0% during peak — a 10.3 percentage-point green premium available to drivers who charge off-peak.

6. Optimization Insights & Recommendations

Synthesis Signal

The network's mean optimization reward is −11.83 with only 9.7% of sessions positive. The primary drivers of poor reward are peak-period grid stress (load 2.43× higher than off-peak) and low renewable penetration during peak windows (29% vs. 39% off-peak). The following recommendations address root causes directly.

Priority 1 — Load Shifting

Implement Differentiated Peak Pricing

  • Current pricing shows no meaningful peak/off-peak differential (9.92–9.99). Introduce a 30–50% peak surcharge to incentivize load shifting.
  • Pair with off-peak discounts (−20%) during Off-Peak windows where renewable ratio is highest (39.3%).
  • Target: shift 15–20% of peak sessions to off-peak, reducing peak load from 84 kW toward 70 kW mean.
  • Use charging priority (High/Medium/Low) as the tiering basis for pricing brackets.
Priority 2 — Renewable Maximization

Renewable-Linked Charging Incentives

  • Renewable ratio correlates strongly with optimization reward (r = 0.590) — the clearest single lever for score improvement.
  • Surface real-time renewable ratio to drivers via app; offer green-charging credits when ratio > 35%.
  • Schedule high-power DC fast chargers (50 kW) preferentially during off-peak windows where renewable mix is highest.
  • Target: raise network-wide renewable ratio from 35.4% to 42%+ through demand-side scheduling.
Priority 3 — Queue Management

Smart SoC Cutoff & Slot Reservation

  • All vehicles currently charge to ~98.5% SoC. An 80% smart cutoff option would reduce avg session duration by ~30–40 min, increasing station throughput by an estimated 15–20%.
  • Introduce pre-booking slots for highway stations, which show the highest off-peak queue length (4.70 vehicles).
  • Address the 11.3% of sessions with >15 min wait — target these via dynamic queue re-routing to lower-utilization stations.
Priority 4 — Highway Optimization

Highway Station Efficiency Uplift

  • Highway stations consistently show worse optimization rewards (−12.04 vs Urban −11.63) and higher off-peak queue lengths.
  • Deploy additional fast chargers at highest-load highway stations (ST003: 55.3 kW avg load, ST012: 56.4 kW).
  • Highway traffic patterns are less day-of-week sensitive — implement continuous load monitoring rather than time-of-day scheduling alone.
Priority 5 — Grid Balancing

Reinforcement Learning Reward Improvement

  • Only 9.7% of sessions achieve positive optimization reward. Increasing off-peak session share is the most direct path to reward improvement (off-peak reward −7.06 vs peak −19.80).
  • Integrate renewable ratio forecasting into charger dispatch logic — pre-position high-demand sessions ahead of high-renewable windows.
  • Evaluate V2G (Vehicle-to-Grid) capability for Bus fleet (39.45 kWh avg session) during peak hours to reduce net grid draw.
Priority 6 — Monitoring

Station-Level Performance Tracking

  • Establish per-station KPI dashboards tracking: wait time (alert threshold 15 min), station load (alert threshold 90 kW), and renewable ratio (target ≥35%).
  • ST003 and ST012 (highest avg loads: 55.3–56.4 kW) should be monitored for early capacity constraints.
  • ST018 Urban (avg wait 8.73 min — lowest in network) should be studied as a best-practice reference station.
