6 — Optimization Insights & Recommendations
Actionable interventions synthesized from all analytical dimensions, prioritized by impact and feasibility
1
Implement Differentiated Peak/Off-Peak Pricing
Current Peak vs Off-Peak electricity prices are virtually identical (9.94 vs 9.97 units). This eliminates the economic incentive for customers to shift charging to lower-demand periods. Introduce a meaningful price differential — Peak at ~1.4–1.6× Off-Peak — to actively redirect demand. Off-Peak sessions already achieve 35% higher renewable ratios and 2.8× better optimization rewards; pricing should amplify this natural advantage.
▲ Off-Peak renewable uplift: +35.6% | Opt. reward gap: −7.1 vs −19.8
2
Expand Capacity at ST004-Highway & ST009-Urban
ST004 (Highway) and ST009 (Urban) record the two highest average wait times in the network (10.35 and 10.00 minutes, respectively). Add at least one additional charger unit at each site — the near-balanced power tier distribution suggests fast-charging (50 kW) additions would serve mixed vehicle demand without disrupting the existing tier balance. Prioritize ST004-Highway given its outsized wait time and the generally higher charging demand at Highway locations.
▲ ST004-Highway wait: 10.35 min (+8.6% above network mean)
3
Deploy Off-Peak Demand Shifting Incentives
Peak sessions generate 84 kW average station load vs 34.7 kW Off-Peak — a 2.43× surge that drives negative optimization rewards. Introduce time-of-use discounts, loyalty credits, or reservation priority for Off-Peak charging across all location types. Buses and Cars represent the largest fleet segments and have the highest energy consumption per session; targeting fleet operators and EV car owners with Off-Peak scheduling contracts would yield the greatest load-shifting impact.
▲ Peak load reduction potential: target <60 kW avg via 15–20% demand shift
4
Launch Green Charging Windows (Off-Peak Renewable Programs)
Off-Peak periods achieve 39.3% renewable ratio, vs 29.0% during Peak. Clear-weather Highway sessions reach 35.8% renewable penetration — the network's cleanest combination. Market an explicit "Green Charging Window" product for Off-Peak sessions under Clear or Cloudy conditions, positioning it as a premium sustainability option. This serves both customer ESG preferences and grid operators seeking to align load with renewable generation curves.
▲ Max renewable ratio achievable: ~0.39 (Off-Peak, Urban)
5
Address Sunday Off-Peak Congestion
Sunday Off-Peak records the network's highest per-slot average wait (9.86 min) — higher than any weekday or Peak period. This suggests leisure-day users cluster in off-peak windows, compressing throughput paradoxically. Consider dynamic queue management on Sundays: activate standby chargers for mid-day Off-Peak windows, or implement Sunday-specific surge pricing to smooth demand across the full day.
▲ Sunday Off-Peak wait: 9.86 min vs network mean 9.53 min
6
Optimize High-Priority Queue Effectiveness
High-priority sessions average slightly better optimization rewards than Medium-priority sessions at both Urban and Highway locations, but the gaps are narrow (e.g., Highway: High −11.74 vs Medium −12.34). Review the priority assignment logic to ensure High designation is reserved for grid-friendly sessions (Off-Peak, high renewable availability) rather than being time-agnostic. Tying priority elevation to off-peak scheduling would amplify both queue efficiency and optimization scores.
▲ Highway High vs Medium reward delta: +0.60 units
7
Investigate Low-Traffic Paradox for Wait Time Reduction
Counter-intuitively, Low traffic density correlates with the longest wait times (Highway Low: 9.72 min) while High traffic yields shorter waits (Highway High: 9.55 min). This may indicate that stations in quieter corridors handle longer sessions or serve vehicles with lower arrival urgency, reducing throughput efficiency. Audit session duration patterns in Low-traffic periods and consider dynamic time limits or session scheduling in low-occupancy windows.
▲ Highway Low-traffic wait: 9.72 min vs High-traffic: 9.55 min
8
Pursue Renewable Energy Source Expansion
The network's 35.4% renewable ratio is a meaningful baseline, but leaves substantial decarbonization potential unrealized. Clear weather yields the best renewable performance; sites in high-solar-irradiance corridors (particularly Highway locations) are strongest candidates for on-site solar PPA agreements or battery storage integration. Coupling local storage with off-peak charging would allow excess renewable energy to be captured and dispatched during peak demand, directly improving both the renewable ratio and optimization rewards.
▲ Target: >50% renewable ratio via storage + solar integration
