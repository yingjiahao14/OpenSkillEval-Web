Long-Term Price Trajectory (1970–2026)

The full time series shows three broad regimes: a low and comparatively compressed pre-1973 base; a higher and more volatile post-1970s market with sharp reversals through the 1980s and 1990s; and a post-2000 period defined by much higher nominal prices, larger absolute moves, and recurring macro shocks.

Outlook & Conclusions

Recent observations in the 2020s remain elevated relative to the long-run full-sample median: the 2020s average is $71.75, compared with the full-sample median of $29.11. The last observation, Mar 2026, is $87.84, which is +201.72% versus the historical median and +120.68% versus the historical mean.

  • Long-run level shift: nominal prices are materially higher after 2000 than before 2000, even though the path is cyclical rather than monotonic.
  • Volatility is episodic: the largest shocks are clustered around abrupt macroeconomic, geopolitical, and demand-supply dislocations visible in the monthly series.
  • Decade context matters: average level and volatility can tell different stories; a high average decade is not necessarily the same as the highest-volatility decade.
  • Current context: the 2020s data should be interpreted as a partial decade and, per the source brief, later 2026-adjacent observations may include projected or estimated values.
Bottom line: the dataset depicts crude oil as a structurally repriced commodity over five decades, with post-2000 nominal prices occupying a higher regime and short-term risk dominated by discrete shock episodes rather than steady trend progression.
