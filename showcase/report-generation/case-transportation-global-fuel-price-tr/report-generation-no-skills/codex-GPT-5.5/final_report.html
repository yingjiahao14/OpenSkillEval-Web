Long-Term Price Trajectory (1970–2026)

The full time series is the backbone of the analysis. It identifies four broad regimes: a low-price pre-shock baseline through the early 1970s; a step-change higher in the 1970s and early 1980s; a lower and comparatively range-bound 1986–1999 period; and a post-2000 high-volatility era with repeated boom-bust episodes.

  • Scale shift: prices rose from $1.21 in Jan 1970 to $13.00 in Jan 1974, a +217.1% month-over-month jump and +525.0% year-over-year increase.
  • Peak: the maximum monthly price is $132.83 in Jul 2008, immediately before the sharp 2008 financial-crisis-era contraction visible in the series.
  • Latest reading: the final point, $87.84 in Mar 2026, is below the 2008 all-time high by $44.99, but above the full-sample mean by $48.04.

Outlook & Conclusions

The 2020s portion of the dataset presents a sequence of compression, rebound, spike, and moderation: annual average price moves from $41.26 in 2020 to $97.10 in 2022, then eases to $63.81 in 2025. The partial 2026 average is $70.78, based on 3 monthly observations through Mar 2026.

  • Historical context: current and recent readings sit in a high nominal-price regime relative to the full-sample median of $29.11, even though they remain below the 2008 maximum.
  • Volatility baseline: analysts should benchmark short-term moves against the full-sample monthly standard deviation of $30.61 and the historical single-month extremes of -26.96 USD/bbl and -39.6%.
  • Regime awareness: decade statistics show that price level and volatility are regime-dependent; the 1990s low-volatility average is a poor comparator for the 2000s–2020s environment.
