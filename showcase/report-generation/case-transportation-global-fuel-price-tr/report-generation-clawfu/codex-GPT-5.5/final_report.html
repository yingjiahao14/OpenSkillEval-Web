Interpretation

Early observations are nearly flat at $1.21, while the 1970s introduce step-change repricing. The 1980s and 1990s show lower nominal levels than the 2000s, but still include major dislocations. The 2000s produce the dataset peak before the financial-crisis collapse, and the 2020s combine an extreme demand shock with a rapid recovery and renewed high-price period.

The average price over the full dataset is $39.80, while the median is $29.11. The mean exceeds the median, indicating a right-skewed distribution driven by high-price episodes after 2000.

Outlook & Conclusions

The recent portion of the dataset places the 2020s in a high-volatility context: a pandemic-era shock, subsequent recovery, a 2022 high-price environment, and a latest observation of $87.84 in Mar 2026. Because the source documentation notes that later values approaching 2026 may include projected or estimated observations, conclusions about the latest months should be treated as dataset-specific rather than definitive market history.

  • Long-run trend: crude oil prices increased materially in nominal terms, but through step changes and shocks rather than a smooth growth path.
  • Structural shift: post-2000 observations account for the highest sustained nominal price levels and dominate the upper tail of the distribution.
  • Risk profile: extreme MoM and YoY moves cluster around geopolitical, macroeconomic, and demand-collapse episodes, making historical context essential for scenario analysis.
  • Analyst implication: decade-level averages are useful, but regime-aware analysis is more informative than extrapolating from the full-sample mean alone.
