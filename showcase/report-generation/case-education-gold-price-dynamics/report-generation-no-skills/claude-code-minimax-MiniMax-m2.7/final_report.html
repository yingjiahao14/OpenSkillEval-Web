Tail Risk Assessment

Gold exhibits negative skewness (-0.47), meaning downside moves tend to be larger than upside moves of equivalent magnitude. The excess kurtosis of 7.02 indicates fat tails — extreme events occur more frequently than a normal distribution would predict. The 99th percentile daily return (+2.82%) is smaller in absolute value than the 1st percentile (-3.10%), confirming the asymmetry. On 5% of trading days, investors can expect to lose more than 1.76% (VaR).

6. Key Takeaways & Forward-Looking Considerations

  1. 1
    Strong Long-Term Performance with Moderate Volatility: Gold delivered 12.38% CAGR with 17.69% annualized volatility over 25 years—a risk-adjusted return (Sharpe 0.70) that compares favorably to many equities. Maximum drawdown of -44.36% is significant but occurred during a specific multi-year correction.
  2. 2
    Volatility Is Regime-Dependent: Gold spends roughly equal time in low, medium, and high volatility regimes. Crisis events (2008, 2011-12, 2020, 2022-26) drive significant vol clustering. The current period (2025-2026) shows elevated volatility (~42%), suggesting an active risk-management posture is warranted.
  3. 3
    January Effect is Statistically Significant: Of all seasonal patterns, only January (+1.69%, p=0.007) and August (+1.07%, p=0.036) show statistically significant positive deviations from zero. June shows a slight negative tendency but is not significant. Seasonal trading strategies should focus on January positioning.
  4. 4
    Fat Tails Require Tail Risk Management: With excess kurtosis of 7.02 and negative skewness, gold's return distribution has heavier tails than normal. VaR (95%) of -1.76% per day implies meaningful daily loss potential. Position sizing should account for these tail characteristics rather than relying on normal-distribution assumptions.
  5. 5
    Current Regime Context (2025-2026): Volatility at ~42% is in crisis territory, above the 90th historical percentile. This elevated vol environment historically precedes continued strong returns (2009, 2010, 2020 all saw high vol + high returns) but also carries elevated drawdown risk. Momentum is strongly positive with prices at all-time highs.

Risk Considerations

The current bull market in gold (driven by geopolitical uncertainty, central bank buying, and tariff-related inflation concerns) could reverse if inflation moderates, real yields rise, or risk sentiment shifts. The -44.36% max drawdown historically demonstrates that gold is not downside-proof—even during its long-term uptrend. Diversification and position sizing remain essential.
