6. Key Takeaways & Forward-Looking Considerations

Summary of Findings

  • Structural Bull Market: Gold has delivered a 12.3% CAGR over 25 years with a cumulative return of 1,832%, significantly outpacing inflation and most real asset benchmarks.
  • Regime-Dependent Risk: Volatility is not constant. Gold transitions between low-vol trending regimes and high-vol crisis regimes, with crisis periods delivering meaningfully different return profiles. Risk models must account for regime-switching dynamics.
  • Fat-Tailed Distribution: The excess kurtosis of 7.0 and negative skew of -0.47 mean that Gaussian risk frameworks underestimate tail risk by a substantial margin. The 99% CVaR of -4.27% significantly exceeds the 99% VaR of -3.10%.
  • Drawdown Risk: The maximum drawdown of -44.4% — lasting from 2011 to 2015 — demonstrates that gold can suffer protracted, deep corrections even within a secular bull market. Recovery took 1680 days.
  • Limited Seasonality: While some months show higher average returns, the Kruskal-Wallis test does not find statistically robust seasonal differences at conventional significance levels. Calendar effects should be treated as marginal rather than primary drivers.
  • Momentum Works Tactically: The 50/200-day SMA crossover strategy captures major trend transitions with a 47% win rate, though with significant lag. It is better suited as a regime filter than a precise timing tool.

Implications for Portfolio Construction

  • Allocation: Gold's low-to-moderate correlation with equities and positive skew in crisis periods supports a strategic allocation of 5–10% in diversified portfolios as a tail-risk hedge.
  • Position Sizing: Given the fat-tailed return distribution, position sizing based on normal-distribution assumptions will understate required reserves. Use CVaR-based sizing or stress-test against observed tail events.
  • Entry Timing: Volatility regime awareness can improve entry/exit timing. Buying in low-volatility regimes (when gold is quietly trending) has historically yielded better risk-adjusted returns than chasing during crisis-driven spikes.
  • Risk Limits: The -44.4% historical maximum drawdown should anchor worst-case scenario planning. A 20–30% drawdown is a realistic expectation during regime transitions.
