6. Key Takeaways & Forward-Looking Considerations
Synthesize findings into actionable insights for portfolio construction and risk management.
  • Structural uptrend with compounding power. Gold has delivered a 12.4% CAGR over 25 years — a compelling long-term return driven by secular de-dollarization concerns, inflation hedging demand, and central-bank accumulation. This return profile is competitive with equities but with a different risk factor exposure.
  • Volatility clustering demands regime-aware positioning. Gold transitions between low-vol trending regimes and high-vol crisis regimes. Position sizing should adapt: reduce exposure in high-vol regimes (current 63-day vol threshold: 18.5% ann.) and lean in during low-vol trending environments. The current regime is High Volatility.
  • Maximum drawdown risk is material. The worst peak-to-trough decline was -44.4% (2011–2015). Position sizing for gold should assume 30–40% drawdowns are plausible during regime shifts, particularly around Fed tightening cycles.
  • Fat tails make normal-distribution risk models dangerous. Excess kurtosis of 7.0 means extreme moves are far more likely than Gaussian models predict. VaR and CVaR measures diverge significantly — tail hedging via options or reduced position sizes in volatile regimes is warranted.
  • Seasonality is weak but not zero. Statistical significance exists for some months, but effect sizes are small. Seasonal patterns should inform tactical tilts, not drive standalone strategies. The strongest seasonal tendency is the January–February positive bias.
  • Momentum works, but with whipsaw risk. The 50/200 MA crossover captures major trends but generates false signals during range-bound periods (e.g., 2013–2016). Trend-following approaches should be supplemented with volatility filters — only taking signals when volatility is below the 18.5% threshold.
  • Volume confirms, but only post-2003. Volume data before 2004 is unreliable. In the reliable period, volume-volatility correlation is positive (-0.02), and surges in volume often coincide with trend reversals or accelerations — a useful confirmation signal.
