Section 01

Market Overview & Long-Term Price Trend

Gold's 25-year trajectory through four distinct macro regimes: a bear-to-base transition (2000–2001), a structural bull run (2002–2011), a correction cycle (2012–2018), and a renewed bull market with accelerating momentum (2019–2026).

Bull Phase I (2002–2011)

Gold surged from ~$278 to a then-record $1,888.70 over nine years. This drove a near-uninterrupted bull run with only modest drawdowns, fueled by dollar weakness, post-9/11 geopolitical risk, the GFC, and quantitative easing.

Correction Cycle (2012–2018)

From the 2011 peak, gold shed 44.4% by December 2015 — the deepest drawdown in the dataset. The subsequent recovery was slow and choppy, with the 2011 high not eclipsed until 2020, a nine-year wait.

Bull Phase II (2019–2026)

Renewed monetary easing, COVID-19, geopolitical fragmentation, and record central bank buying drove gold to new all-time highs. The 2025 return of +62.7% is the single best calendar year in this dataset.

GFC Crisis (2008–2009)

The most intense volatility event: 159 consecutive crisis-regime days with peak 90-day annualized vol of 41.3%. Gold initially sold off with deleveraging then rallied sharply as a safe-haven, creating extreme two-directional vol.

Regime Persistence

Normal-regime days account for 77.2% of the sample, confirming gold is not inherently a high-volatility asset. Volatility is episodic and mean-reverting. Crisis regimes cluster, consistent with GARCH-family dynamics.

Low-Vol Scarcity

Only 3.1% of days registered sub-10% annualized vol. These periods occurred during quiet consolidation in 2005 and mid-2014, and may represent compressed volatility ahead of directional breaks.

January Effect — Strongest Signal

January shows the highest average daily return (+0.140%) with a t-stat of 2.73 — the only month with clear statistical significance at the 95% level. This aligns with the global "January effect" across asset classes and potentially demand from Asian gold-buying seasons.

August — Secondary Signal

August is the second-strongest month (t-stat 2.10, annualized +22.4%). This may reflect pre-Indian-festival-season accumulation (Diwali typically falls Oct–Nov) and historically elevated macro uncertainty in late summer.

Day-of-Week: Friday Outperforms

Friday's mean daily return of +0.086% (t-stat 2.60) is the strongest day-of-week signal. Monday is second at +0.059% (t-stat 1.85). Mid-week days show no distinguishable pattern. The Friday effect may reflect safe-haven positioning ahead of weekend geopolitical risk.

Tail Risk Warning: Excess kurtosis of 7.02 means the empirical 99th-percentile loss (3.10%) is substantially larger than the normal-distribution prediction (~2.59%). Standard VaR models using normality assumptions will underestimate gold's tail risk by a significant margin. Fat-tailed models (Student-t, stable distributions) are recommended.
Excess Kurtosis = 7.02

A kurtosis of 7.02 (excess) places gold firmly in "leptokurtic" territory. The 1% daily VaR is 3.10% — versus 2.59% predicted by a normal model. This 20% underestimation is material for risk capital calculations.

Negative Skew = −0.47

Mild negative skew means the distribution has a slightly longer left tail — large down moves are more common than large up moves of the same magnitude. This is relevant for downside protection strategies and options pricing (put-skew premium).

Median vs. Mean

The median daily return (≈+0.05%) is positive, consistent with the long-term uptrend. The mean (+0.047%) is similarly positive. The slight divergence and negative skew confirm that while gold drifts upward on average, tail events are negatively skewed.

MA Crossover Reliability

The MA-50/200 system generated 33 signals over 25 years — roughly 1.3 per year. Most major trend reversals were captured, though with varying lag. The 2001 Golden Cross and 2008 Death Cross were among the most consequential in terms of subsequent price movement.

Volume Structural Shift

Volume rose from near-zero in 2000–2003 to a peak average of 7,281 contracts/day in 2018, then structurally declined to ~4,000–5,000 range by 2022–2025. The 2008 GFC period saw a volume surge, consistent with crisis-driven safe-haven demand and liquidation.

Current Regime (as of Mar 2026)

The Nov 2023 Golden Cross remains intact, with a highly elevated price level (~$5,377). The 90-day volatility has re-entered crisis-regime territory (34.9%) in early 2026, suggesting the current rally is occurring under elevated risk conditions.

Scenario A: Continued Bull (Base)

If the Nov 2023 Golden Cross regime persists, historical analog suggests continuation toward new highs is consistent. The primary risk is the currently elevated 90-day vol (34.9%) — similar to prior crisis episodes that preceded sharp corrections.

Scenario B: Mean Reversion

The 2025 +62.7% return is the dataset's single best year. Historical precedent (2012–2015) shows that exceptional run-ups can be followed by multi-year correction cycles. A return to the ~$3,000–3,500 range would represent a drawdown comparable to 2012–2013 patterns.

Scenario C: Regime Shift Risk

A Death Cross (MA-50 below MA-200) would signal structural deterioration. This last occurred in 2018 at $1,265. The long lead time between Death Cross and the subsequent Golden Cross (Jan 2019 at $1,282) suggests regime shifts are slow to materialize but durable when they do.
