6. Key Takeaways & Forward-Looking Considerations

1. Gold Has Delivered Strong Risk-Adjusted Returns. A 12.4% CAGR with 17.7% annualized volatility yields a Sharpe-like ratio of 0.70. While drawdowns of 44% have occurred (2011–2015), gold's long-run compounding makes it a powerful diversifier.
2. Volatility Is Clustered and Regime-Dependent. Four distinct volatility regimes emerge from the data. Crisis episodes (2008, 2011, 2020, 2022) are brief but severe. The current environment shows elevated baseline volatility relative to the 2013–2019 calm period, with annualized rolling vol above 18.5% — the high-vol regime threshold.
3. Seasonal Patterns Exist but Are Weak. Monthly return differences yield an F-statistic of 0.79 (p ≈ 0.674). While some months (January, August) tend to deliver stronger returns and others (September, October) weaker, the effect is not robust enough to form the sole basis of a timing strategy. The seasonal signal should be used as a tilt, not a trade.
4. Tail Risk Is Real — Do Not Use Gaussian Models. Excess kurtosis of 7.0 means that 3- and 4-sigma moves occur substantially more often than a normal distribution predicts. The 99% CVaR of -4.34% per day translates to a ~68.9% annualized tail-loss estimate — relevant for risk budgeting and stop-loss placement.
5. Momentum Works in Trends, Fails in Ranges. The 50/200-day SMA crossover provides valuable directional context but should be supplemented with volatility-regime awareness. Momentum signals are most reliable during trending bull phases and least reliable during low-vol consolidations.
6. The Post-2020 Structural Shift Matters. Gold's price level, volatility profile, and volume characteristics are materially different after 2020. Central bank buying, de-dollarization trends, and elevated geopolitical risk have created a new regime that analysts should model separately from the pre-2020 historical sample.
