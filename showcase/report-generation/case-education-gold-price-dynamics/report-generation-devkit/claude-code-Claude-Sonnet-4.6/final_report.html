GFC — Peak Crisis Regime
2008 registered the dataset's highest annual realized volatility at 30.5%. Gold initially fell with the risk-off deleveraging (to ~$730) before recovering to close the year up +5.8%, illustrating its dual nature as both a risk asset and safe haven.
Low-Vol Bull Runs Reward Holders
Years 2017–2019 averaged only 10.8% annualized volatility while delivering cumulative price growth. Low-vol environments in gold tend to coincide with steady up-trends rather than correction phases.
2026 YTD — Extreme Regime
2026 (partial year) shows 42.6% annualized volatility — the highest in the dataset. The Jan 28–Feb 2 period saw a near-$900 intraday range, signaling a potential regime shift from sustained trend to turbulence.

Extreme Daily Moves — Top 10 Worst & Best Days
The asymmetry between left and right tails is evident: the single worst day (−12.07%) dwarfs the best day (+8.64%), consistent with the negative skewness reading.
Fat Tails: Normal Model Severely Underestimates Risk
Excess kurtosis of 7.01 means the probability of large moves is far higher than a Gaussian model implies. A ±3σ event should occur ~0.3% of the time under normality; in this dataset such moves are significantly more frequent. Risk models using normal distribution assumptions will chronically understate gold's tail risk.
Negative Skew: Crash Risk Exceeds Rally Magnitude
Skewness of −0.47 indicates the distribution is pulled left. The worst single-day loss (−12.07%) is 40% larger in magnitude than the best single-day gain (+8.64%). Options strategies should account for this asymmetry when pricing puts vs. calls.
Daily VaR Context for Portfolio Sizing
A 1% daily VaR of −3.11% implies that on approximately 64 trading days per year, a gold position would be expected to lose more than 3.11% in a single session. For a 10% portfolio allocation, this translates to a ~0.31% portfolio drawdown on those tail days.

Momentum Persistence: Bull Runs Cluster
Of 20 positive calendar years, 12 occurred in consecutive multi-year runs (2002–2007, 2009–2012, 2019–2020, 2023–2026). Gold exhibits significant trend autocorrelation — position sizing models that fade existing trends have historically underperformed trend-followers in this asset.
Volume Reliability Caveat
Volume data before 2004 contains many zero values reflecting data limitations rather than actual market inactivity. Volume-based signals (OBV, volume-price divergence) should be computed only from 2004 onward. Average daily volume rose from ~500 contracts/day in 2004 to 4,766/day by the full post-2004 period average.
Rate of Change: 2025 Stands Out
The 2025 calendar year return of +64.5% is the highest single-year gain in the dataset — approximately 2.4× the prior strongest year (2007: +31.4%). Combined with the high-vol reading (20.5%), this characterizes 2025 as a momentum/blow-off phase rather than a steady bull trend — a historically elevated risk environment.
Section 06

Key Takeaways & Forward-Looking Considerations

Synthesis of findings for macro-asset allocation and risk management. All forward context is framed around historical regime behavior — not price prediction.

1. Strong Long-Term Risk-Adjusted Return
A CAGR of 12.38% with annualized volatility of 17.69% yields a Sharpe-like ratio of 0.70 — comparable to or better than broad equity index risk-adjusted returns over the same period. Gold earns its place as a core macro allocation.
2. Drawdowns Are Deep & Extended
The 2011–2015 drawdown lasted over 4 years and reached −44.36%. Analysts sizing gold positions must have a time horizon and drawdown tolerance commensurate with this history. Stop-loss strategies based on shorter windows will trigger and cost return in extended bear phases.
3. Current Regime Is Elevated Risk
Gold is in or near a crisis-vol regime as of early 2026 (42.6% YTD ann. vol). Historically, such spikes have preceded either sharp corrections (2008, 2013) or volatile continuation (2011). Position sizing should reflect elevated volatility rather than normalizing to the full-period 17.69% baseline.
4. Seasonal Edges: Focus on Jan & Aug
Only January (t=3.17) and August (t=2.34) show statistically significant seasonal edges at the 95% confidence level. For tactical allocators, these months represent the highest-probability seasonal entry windows. All other months are not statistically distinguishable from random.
5. Trend-Following > Mean-Reversion
The 50/200 MA structure has spent most of the 2023–2026 period in a golden-cross regime. Combined with the momentum clustering finding (bull runs last 2–6 years), the data supports a trend-following rather than mean-reversion approach to gold in strong bull environments.
6. Tail Risk Requires Non-Normal Models
Excess kurtosis of 7.01 mandates the use of fat-tailed distributions (e.g., Student-t, EVT) for VaR and option pricing. Using Gaussian assumptions will underestimate tail loss probabilities by a factor of 3–5× at the 1% confidence level. All risk metrics in this report are empirically derived precisely to avoid this pitfall.
