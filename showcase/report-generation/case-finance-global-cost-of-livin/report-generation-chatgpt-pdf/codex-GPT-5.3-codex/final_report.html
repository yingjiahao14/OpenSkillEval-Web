Inflation and Oil Shock Exposure

  • Cities with High oil-shock exposure show much higher average inflation (20.4%) than Low (3.4%) and Medium (3.4%) groups.
  • The same high-exposure group also has the weakest average purchasing power (67.8), materially below low-exposure cities (128.6).
  • Tier-5 cities concentrate inflation stress (tier average 31.1%), indicating inflation is a central differentiator of acute crisis conditions.

Key Findings and Policy Implications

  1. Housing pressure is systemic: the global average rent-to-salary ratio is 61.5%, and all top-10 burden cities are near or above 69%.
  2. Tier severity is inflation-led: higher crisis tiers are associated with sharply elevated inflation and depressed purchasing power.
  3. Regional means can obscure local extremes: Singapore and Tel Aviv are substantial high-cost outliers within their regions.
  4. Urgent monitoring priority: Tier-5 cities (14 total), especially high oil-exposure cities with high inflation and low PPP.
  5. Policy triage framework: prioritize cities where high rent burden, low PPP, and high inflation overlap.
