Key Findings and Policy Implications

  • Housing affordability is the core stress channel: global average rent burden is 61.5%, with severe tails above 50% that require priority intervention.
  • High-cost is not identical to high-crisis: some expensive cities retain stronger purchasing power; intervention should target rent burden and wage adequacy, not price level alone.
  • Tier separation is policy-relevant: highest crisis-tier cities exhibit weaker purchasing power and heavier rent load, justifying differentiated support packages.
  • Regional strategy should be outlier-aware: city-level departures from regional averages indicate where national-level policy may miss local crises.
  • Shock resilience matters: inflation and oil exposure patterns suggest energy and transport policy can materially affect household affordability.
