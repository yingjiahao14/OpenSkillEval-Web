Inflation and Oil Shock Exposure

Cities with high oil-shock exposure and elevated rent burdens represent compounded vulnerability. These cities should be prioritized for resilience and affordability interventions.

Key Findings and Policy Implications

  • Housing costs are the primary pressure point: 80/80 cities exceed a 50% rent burden.
  • Crisis tiers are materially distinct: higher tiers show weaker purchasing power and higher burden/inflation.
  • Outliers matter: city-level deviations from regional averages are large enough to alter intervention prioritization.
  • Inflation and oil vulnerability interact: shock exposure can amplify already-stressed affordability conditions.
  • Policy targeting: prioritize combined indicators (rent burden, purchasing power, inflation, oil exposure) for watchlist-based action.
