Executive Overview

The global median cost-of-living benchmark is 65.5, while rent absorbs 61.5% of income on average. The purchasing-power spread reaches 148.7 points, signaling substantial inequality in household resilience.

14 cities fall in the highest crisis tier (Tier 5), indicating concentrated acute stress where inflation, rent burden, and weaker purchasing power compound affordability risks.

Key Findings and Policy Implications

  • Affordability stress is primarily housing-driven, with severe rent-to-income concentration in a subset of cities.
  • Tier-based prioritization is feasible: Tier 5 includes 14 acute-stress cities requiring immediate policy attention.
  • Regional means conceal critical city-level outliers; intervention planning should include explicit outlier targeting.
  • The 148.7-point purchasing-power gap underscores uneven shock absorption capacity across urban households.
  • Inflation and oil exposure indicators should be integrated into affordability surveillance for compound-risk detection.
