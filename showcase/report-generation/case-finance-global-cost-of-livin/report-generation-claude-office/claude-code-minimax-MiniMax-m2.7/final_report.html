Executive Overview

Affordability and Rent Burden Analysis

The rent-to-salary ratio is the single most actionable metric for housing policy intervention. Globally, the average rent-to-salary ratio stands at 61.5%—well above the 30% threshold considered affordable. Cities where rent exceeds 50% of salary represent severe affordability crises requiring urgent policy attention.

Critical Finding: Cities like Hong Kong (65.6%), Singapore (66.4%), and San Francisco (69.2%) demonstrate that even high-salary cities can face severe rent burdens. Meanwhile, Tier 4-5 cities face a dual crisis: low absolute salaries combined with rent ratios often exceeding 50-70%.

Regional Cost-of-Living Comparison

Regional analysis reveals stark disparities in economic conditions. Western European and North American cities generally exhibit high cost-of-living indices balanced by strong purchasing power, while cities in South Asia, West Africa, and Latin America face significantly lower costs but also dramatically reduced purchasing power.

Average economic indicators by region. NY Baseline (100) shown as gray dotted line. Western Europe and North America lead in cost of living and purchasing power; South Asia and West Africa show lowest indices but also lowest purchasing power.

Regional Highlights

  • Western Europe: Highest rent-to-salary ratios (66-71%) despite high salaries—housing affordability is structurally challenging even in affluent regions.
  • North America: High cost of living balanced by the highest average purchasing power (117.9) and relatively controlled inflation (3.1%).
  • South Asia: Lowest cost of living indices (27-33) but also lowest purchasing power (50-65). Dhaka and Karachi face combined rent burdens exceeding 50% alongside 10-28% inflation.
  • West Africa: Lagos (Tier 5) and Accra face hyperinflation (18-34%), severe purchasing power constraints, and rent burdens around 52-53%.
Outlier Alert: Doha (Qatar) stands out with an exceptionally low cost of living index (62.0) combined with high purchasing power (145.0) and the lowest rent-to-salary ratio among oil-rich nations. Its oil shock exposure is "High" but its diversification strategy insulates it from price volatility.

Key Findings and Policy Implications

Priority Cities Requiring Urgent Attention

  1. Beirut, Lebanon — 50% inflation, rent at 54.3% of salary, purchasing power index of 35 (lowest globally after Cairo)
  2. Buenos Aires, Argentina — 150% hyperinflation, Tier 5 classification
  3. Lagos, Nigeria — 34% inflation, only $380 avg monthly salary, rent-to-salary 52.4%
  4. Kyiv, Ukraine — War-driven crisis with 14.5% inflation and 53.1% rent-to-salary ratio
  5. Cairo, Egypt — 28% inflation, purchasing power index of 42, rent burden 53.1%

Structural Patterns Identified

  • Affordability Crisis is not limited to low-income cities. Hong Kong, Singapore, and San Francisco all show rent-to-salary ratios above 65%, indicating structural housing supply issues independent of income levels.
  • Western European rent burden is hidden by high salaries. Cities like Zurich and Oslo have 67-71% rent-to-salary ratios, but high wages mask the severity. Policy focus should be on supply expansion rather than wage subsidies.
  • Oil shock exposure alone does not determine crisis. Doha and Riyadh have "High" oil shock exposure but remain in Tiers 1-2 due to diversification and high wages. The key factor is economic diversification, not oil dependency per se.
  • Inflation clusters in conflict zones and low-purchasing-power regions. The 10 highest-inflation cities are all in Tier 4-5, confirming that inflation is both a cause and consequence of broader economic fragility.

Policy Recommendations

  1. Emergency intervention in Tier 5 cities: Humanitarian aid, price stabilization programs, and international coordination for conflict-affected cities (Beirut, Kyiv, Lagos).
  2. Housing supply reform in high-rent, high-salary cities: Policy focus on zoning reform and construction incentives for San Francisco, Hong Kong, Singapore, and Western European capitals.
  3. Inflation containment through monetary policy support in Tier 4 cities facing 10-20% inflation (Moscow, St. Petersburg, Istanbul, Nairobi, Bogota).
  4. Purchasing power protection for lowest-quintile cities through targeted subsidies and social transfer programs.
