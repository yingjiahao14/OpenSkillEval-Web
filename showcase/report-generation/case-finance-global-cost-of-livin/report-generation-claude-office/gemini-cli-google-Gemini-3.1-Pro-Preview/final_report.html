Executive Overview

This report examines cost-of-living pressures, affordability gaps, and economic stress indicators across 80 major cities worldwide in 2026. The analysis highlights severe disparities in housing affordability, purchasing power, and vulnerability to external economic shocks such as inflation and oil price volatility.

Key Findings and Policy Implications

  • Targeted Housing Interventions: Cities with a rent-to-salary ratio exceeding 50% require immediate policy interventions, such as zoning reform, rent stabilization, or subsidies to alleviate severe housing burdens.
  • Purchasing Power Inequality: The significant gap between high and low purchasing power cities highlights the need for localized wage adjustments and economic diversification to support lower-income households.
  • Vulnerability to Exogenous Shocks: Cities with high exposure to oil price volatility are currently experiencing the highest inflation rates. Policymakers must focus on energy transition and supply chain resilience.
  • Prioritizing High-Crisis Tiers: Resources should be disproportionately directed toward cities in the highest crisis tiers, where the compounded effects of high rent, inflation, and low purchasing power are causing acute economic stress.
