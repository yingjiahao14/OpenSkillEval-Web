Critical Finding — Discounts Are Not Working: Across all discount levels (10%, 15%, 20%), the average quantity purchased per order remains at exactly 3.0 units — identical to undiscounted orders. Profit margins also stay flat at 40% regardless of discount depth. This reveals that discounts are being absorbed structurally (likely in the cost structure or pricing model) and are not driving incremental volume. The 37.6% discount utilization rate is eroding per-order revenue ($27.15 → $21.44 at 20% off) without any offsetting volume benefit. Management should critically evaluate the ROI of the discounting program.

Customer Base Profile: The business is driven by broad, shallow reach rather than deep loyalty. 58% of customers (18,294) bought only once — a substantial one-time buyer pool that represents significant re-engagement opportunity. One-time and two-time buyers together account for 73.1% of revenue (~$932K), while just 4,039 regular customers contribute 26.5% of revenue each generating 3.3× more than a one-time buyer. The top 10% of customers by revenue account for 25.9% of total revenue — indicating moderate concentration. Investing in converting one-time buyers to 2+ purchases would have outsized revenue impact.
