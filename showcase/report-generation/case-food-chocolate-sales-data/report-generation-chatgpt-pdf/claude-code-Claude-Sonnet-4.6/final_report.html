The chocolate retail portfolio delivered $1.28M in total revenue and $511K in profit over the 2023–2024 period, maintaining a consistently strong 40% profit margin across all transaction types and discount levels. The business sustained healthy sales volumes of approximately 2,083 orders per month with minimal volatility, reflecting a stable consumer demand base.

Year-over-year growth was modest but positive: 2024 revenue of $643K outpaced 2023's $632K by 1.7%, and 2024 order volume (25,180) exceeded 2023 (24,820) by 1.5%. Importantly, the 40% profit margin held steady across both years — a signal that pricing and cost discipline is well-managed across the network. The top-performing store (S075) generated 30% more revenue than the lowest-performing store (S057), suggesting meaningful upside from operational benchmarking.

Discount utilization at 37.5% of orders is substantial. However, analysis reveals that discounting is not materially improving volume — discounted orders show similar average quantities (2.99 items) as non-discounted orders (3.00 items) while generating significantly lower average order values ($22.78 vs. $27.15). This is the primary strategic concern warranting management attention heading into the next fiscal year.

Strength 40% profit margin held consistently across both years, all price points, and all discount levels — demonstrating resilient cost and pricing discipline.
Key Risk Discount orders average 16% lower order values than full-price orders with no measurable quantity uplift — promotional ROI should be rigorously re-evaluated.
Opportunity 42% of customers make repeat purchases. Deepening loyalty engagement among this segment could drive significant revenue growth without additional acquisition spend.
Scale The top store outperforms the bottom store by $3,280 in revenue (29.8%). Closing even half this gap across the bottom quartile represents ~$40K in incremental annual revenue.

Discount Impact & Profitability Analysis

Discount analysis is one of the most strategically important sections of this report. Discounts are applied at four distinct rates: 0%, 10%, 15%, and 20%. In total, 18,774 orders (37.5%) carried a discount. The central question is whether this discount volume is generating incremental business or simply transferring revenue from full-price sales.

Average Order Value by Discount Tier
AOV falls from $27.15 at 0% discount to $21.44 at 20% — a 21% reduction in per-order revenue.
Order Count Distribution by Discount Tier
Discounted orders (10%, 15%, 20%) are nearly equally distributed at ~6,250 each, suggesting programmatic application rather than demand-driven promotion.
Discount Tier: Revenue Share vs. Order Share vs. Average Order Value
Although the three discount tiers represent 37.5% of all orders, they account for only 33.5% of total revenue — and maintain 40% margins entirely due to cost pass-through rather than pricing power recovery.

Critical Finding — Discount Effectiveness Average quantity per order is virtually identical across all discount tiers (2.98–3.00 items). Discounts are not driving customers to buy more units. Combined with the lower average order values at each tier, this indicates that discounts are being applied to transactions that would likely have occurred anyway — converting full-price orders into discounted ones without generating incremental volume.
Margin Stability Profit margins remain ~40% across all discount tiers, which suggests costs are being adjusted proportionally (or discounts are absorbed by the cost structure). This masks the true revenue cost of discounting.
Revenue Leakage 37.5% of orders carry discounts but generate only 33.5% of revenue. If those 18,774 discounted orders had been captured at full-price AOV ($27.15), total revenue would have been ~$509K higher — a 40% increase.
Strategic Recommendation: Conduct a controlled test removing or reducing the 20% discount tier for one quarter to measure true demand elasticity. Given that quantity per order is flat across all discount levels, there is strong evidence that at least part of the discounted volume represents demand that exists at full price. Reducing discount utilization by 10 percentage points could add $30K–$50K in annual revenue without volume loss.

Data Limitation Note Demographic segmentation by age, gender, and loyalty membership status requires joining data.csv with a customers.csv dimension table. That file was not available in the provided dataset. The source brief references these columns (age, gender, loyalty_member, join_date) but they cannot be analyzed without the supplementary dimension data. Management should ensure the full dimensional dataset is available for future analyses.
Loyalty Priority: The 13,237 repeat buyers represent 42% of the customer base but likely account for a disproportionately higher share of revenue (each additional transaction compounds). Building a structured loyalty program targeting this cohort — with personalized offers rather than blanket discounts — is the highest-ROI customer investment available based on current behavioral patterns.
