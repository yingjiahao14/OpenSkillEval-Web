Executive Summary

Over the 2023–2024 period, the business generated $1,275,308 in revenue and $510,548 in profit on 50,000 transactions, holding a healthy 40.0% profit margin. Revenue grew +1.7% year-over-year — modest momentum that warrants focus on growth levers in FY planning.

Headline Findings

  • Flat growth trajectory. Revenue rose +1.7% YoY and profit +1.6% — the business is stable but not expanding. Breaking out of flat requires either category-level promotion, underperforming-store intervention, or customer reactivation.
  • Margins are remarkably consistent. Across every discount tier, profit margin holds near 40.0%. This indicates pricing discipline but also suggests current discount depths are not driving incremental margin benefit.
  • Customer concentration is moderate. The top 10% of customers account for 25.9% of revenue, with average customer revenue at $40.45 across 1.59 orders.
  • Promotional volume lift is unclear. Average quantity per transaction is effectively identical (~3.0 units) across discounted and non-discounted orders — indicating discounts currently do not materially drive unit uplift.

Discount Impact & Profitability Analysis

Discounts were applied on 37.5% of transactions. The critical question for management: do discounts drive incremental value, or do they erode margin without producing offsetting volume?

Key insight — discounts are eroding AOV without lifting volume.
  • Profit margins are essentially flat at ~40% across every discount tier. This means unit-cost structure is being preserved — but it also means the current pricing ladder is calibrated so that discounts do not gain share of higher-margin basket.
  • Average order value drops from $27.15 (no discount) to $21.44 (16–20%+ discount) — a 21.0% AOV erosion for deeply discounted orders.
  • Average quantity per transaction is ~3.0 units across all tiers — discounts are not producing basket-expansion. Customers take the lower price on the same basket size.
  • Management recommendation: either (a) tighten discount governance to reserve deep discounts for genuine clearance/velocity problems, or (b) test "buy 3 get 1" / bundle mechanics designed explicitly to shift the quantity distribution upward, rather than flat percentage discounts that the data shows are non-incremental.

Strategic Recommendations for FY Planning

  1. Revisit the discount playbook. Current flat-% discounts erode AOV without lifting quantity. Pilot bundle/threshold mechanics in Q1 to measure true incremental volume.
  2. Close the store-performance gap. Pair bottom-quartile stores with top-quartile peers and audit assortment, staffing, and local promotion alignment.
  3. Reactivate one-time buyers. The single-order cohort (58.0% of customers) is the largest addressable reactivation pool — pilot a winback program measuring second-order conversion.
  4. Range-review bottom SKUs. The bottom-5 products deserve a "fix, replace, or delist" decision within the first quarter of the new fiscal year.
  5. Enrich the analytics foundation. Joining product, store, and customer dimension tables into the analytics fact would unlock brand/category/cocoa-% views, geographic comparisons, and loyalty-member ROI — all explicitly deferred in this report due to missing dimension data.
