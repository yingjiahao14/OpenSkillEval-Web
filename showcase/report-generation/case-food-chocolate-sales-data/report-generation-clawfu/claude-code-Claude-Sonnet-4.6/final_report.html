Headline Finding

The chocolate retail business delivered $1.275M in revenue and $510.5K in profit over the 2023–2024 period, maintaining an exceptional 40% gross margin with striking consistency across all stores, products, and discount levels. Revenue grew modestly by 1.72% in 2024, driven by order volume growth rather than price increases.

Strategic Highlights

Store performance is tightly clustered — the top-performing store (S075) outearns the #10 store by only ~4%. Similarly, top products are within 6% of each other by revenue. Discounts do not erode margins but do reduce absolute revenue per order by ~16%, suggesting promotional spend requires careful evaluation for volume uplift justification.

Revenue Erosion Without Upside

Discounted orders average $22–$24 vs. $27.15 for full price — a 16–18% revenue reduction with no measurable quantity uplift (qty stays at ~3.0 units across all tiers).

🔍

Margin Paradox

Despite lower per-order revenue, profit margins hold steady at ~40% across all discount levels. Costs appear to be discounted proportionally — pointing to a cost structure tied to unit price.

🎯

Discount Cap at 20%

No transactions exceed 20% discount in this dataset. Management may be enforcing a discount ceiling — a prudent guardrail given discounts don't drive quantity growth.

Loyalty Data Limitation

The core transaction dataset does not include loyalty membership or demographic flags. A join with the customers dimension table would be required to segment by loyalty, age, and gender — this analysis cannot be derived from the available data alone.

📆

Flat Day-of-Week Pattern

Sales volume and order value are remarkably stable throughout the week. Friday yields the highest AOV ($25.92) and Sunday generates the most orders (7,220) — both deviations are small and likely not significant.

🔑

Recommended Next Step

Merge customer loyalty and demographic fields to segment CLV (Customer Lifetime Value) by loyalty tier. This is the highest-priority data enrichment for the next reporting cycle.
