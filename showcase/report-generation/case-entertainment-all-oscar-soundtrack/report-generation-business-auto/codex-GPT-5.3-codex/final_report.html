Dataset Overview & Historical Context

This dataset covers 93 consecutive ceremonies from 1934 through 2026, with one winner recorded per year. It includes 92 unique winning films and 69 distinct credited composer entries.

The long-run signal is concentration with periodic renewal: repeat winners account for 25.8% of all ceremonies, yet nearly half of all ceremonies are won by composers making their first appearance in the winners list.

Era-Based Analysis of Winning Scores

Golden Age (1934–1959)

26 ceremonies, 19 unique winners. Leading winners: Max Steiner (3), Dimitri Tiomkin (3), Alfred Newman (2), Miklos Rozsa (2).

Silver Age (1960–1979)

20 ceremonies, 17 unique winners. Leading winners: Maurice Jarre (2), John Barry (2), John Williams (2), Ernest Gold (1).

Modern Era (1980–1999)

20 ceremonies, 16 unique winners. Leading winners: Alan Menken (3), John Williams (2), John Barry (2), Michael Gore (1).

Contemporary Era (2000–2026)

27 ceremonies, 21 unique winners. Leading winners: Ludwig Göransson (4), Howard Shore (2), Gustavo Santaolalla (2), Alexandre Desplat (2).

Across eras, the data indicates a shift from early-period repeat dominance toward broader rotation among winners in later decades, before partial reconsolidation among globally recognized franchise-era composers.
