Executive Summary

The active satellite catalog is overwhelmingly shaped by recent LEO constellation deployment. LEO accounts for 86.5% of active satellites, and Starlink alone contributes 10,083 spacecraft, or 67.8% of the entire catalog.

  • Launch acceleration is the headline: 13,461 currently active satellites were launched in 2020 or later, compared with 1,414 launched before 2020.
  • 2025 is the largest launch-year cohort: 4,329 active satellites in the catalog carry a 2025 launch designator.
  • Starlink is entirely LEO under the stated threshold: 10,083 Starlink satellites classify as LEO, representing 78.4% of all LEO objects in the active catalog.
  • Inclination is constellation-skewed: the median inclination is 53.2°, with dense shells near the mid-inclination Starlink bands and strong polar/sun-synchronous populations.

Data Integrity Notes

All counts, percentages, medians, and charts in this report are computed from /app/benchmark/data.csv. Launch year is extracted from the first four characters of OBJECT_ID. No external or invented statistics are used.

Primary orbital classification uses MEAN_MOTION thresholds only: LEO > 14 rev/day; MEO > 1.05 and ≤ 14; GEO 0.95–1.05; HEO / very high orbit < 0.95.

LEO dominates the operating environment: 12,860 active satellites classify as LEO, leaving 2,015 active satellites spread across MEO, GEO, and very high-orbit categories.

Starlink Effect

Starlink has 10,083 active satellites, exceeding the next largest parsed group, OneWeb (651) by a factor of 15.5.

  • Starlink accounts for 67.8% of the total active catalog.
  • Starlink accounts for 78.4% of all LEO-classified active satellites.
  • Excluding Starlink, the active catalog contains 4,792 satellites, of which 2,777 are LEO.
  • 10,066 Starlink satellites in the catalog were launched in 2020 or later.

Key Takeaways & Policy-Relevant Signals

  • LEO is the primary congestion domain: 12,860 active satellites classify as LEO, making LEO spectrum coordination, conjunction assessment, and deorbit compliance central policy concerns.
  • Single-constellation concentration is material: Starlink's 10,083 active satellites skew total counts, regime shares, and inclination distributions; analyses that aggregate all satellites should also show Starlink-excluded views.
  • Launch acceleration is recent and steep: 13,461 active satellites launched since 2020 dominate the catalog and show that licensing and coordination workloads increasingly track constellation deployment cadence.
  • GEO remains smaller by count but distinct by function: 595 GEO-threshold satellites represent 4.0% of active satellites, a much smaller population than LEO but still requiring dedicated orbital-slot and spectrum governance.
  • Eccentric and very high-orbit cases are a small tail: threshold-classified HEO / very high orbit contains 11 satellites, while eccentricity-based outliers are highlighted separately to avoid mixing mean-motion and shape criteria.
