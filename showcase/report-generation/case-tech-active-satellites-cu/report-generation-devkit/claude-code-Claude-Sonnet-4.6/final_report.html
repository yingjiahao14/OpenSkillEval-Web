3 Major Constellation & Operator Breakdown

Constellation membership is derived from OBJECT_NAME prefix parsing. Starlink's scale is so large that removing it changes virtually every population-level statistic — figures are presented both with and without Starlink where relevant.

Starlink dominates every metric: 10,083 of 14,875 satellites (67.8%) belong to a single commercial operator. The median inclination for the full catalog (53.2°) matches exactly the dominant Starlink shell at 53°. Remove Starlink and the non-Starlink median inclination rises to 87.9° — primarily reflecting polar/SSO missions.

4 Launch Year Trends

Launch year is extracted from the first four characters of OBJECT_ID (format YYYY-NNNXXX). The active catalog spans six decades of launches, but the growth story is almost entirely a post-2019 phenomenon. 90.5% of all active satellites were launched in 2020 or later — a reflection of both Starlink's exponential ramp and broader commercial smallsat proliferation.

Headline acceleration: From 1,414 total active satellites (end-2019) to 14,875 (March 2026) — a 10.5× increase in six years. The 2025 cohort alone added 4,329 satellites, exceeding the entire pre-2020 active fleet at the time.

6 Key Takeaways & Outlook

The following observations are drawn directly from catalog data and carry immediate implications for spectrum policy, orbital debris regulation, and constellation licensing frameworks.

LEO Congestion

12,860 satellites — 86.5% of the active fleet — occupy LEO. The 2025 cohort alone (4,329 objects) exceeded what was achievable in all prior history. At current launch rates, LEO object density is approaching levels that intensify conjunction risk and elevate collision probability for all operators.

Single-Operator Dominance

Starlink's 10,083 satellites (67.8%) mean that virtually every orbital-environment statistic is effectively a Starlink statistic. This concentration has significant implications for spectrum coordination — the Ku/Ka bands allocated to Starlink now represent the most congested slice of the radio spectrum in LEO.

New Entrant Pressure

Amazon Kuiper (210 satellites, rapid growth) and continued OneWeb operations (651) indicate the multi-constellation era is solidifying. Licensing frameworks that were designed for tens or hundreds of objects per operator require fundamental revision to accommodate systems authorised for thousands.

Orbit Circularity

92.1% of active satellites maintain near-circular orbits (eccentricity <0.001). This concentration in narrow altitude bands amplifies debris risk: a single collision event in a high-density shell could generate fragments at the exact orbital altitude of thousands of other operational satellites.

Recency of Active Fleet

90.5% of active satellites were launched since 2020. This recency reduces average on-orbit age, which is positive for reliability — but also reflects how rapidly operators must deorbit earlier generations to stay within ITU coordination agreements and avoid adding to the resident space object count.

Policy Implications

The 5-year growth from 1,414 to 14,875 active satellites requires that debris-mitigation rules (deorbit timelines, passivation requirements), spectrum coordination processes, and conjunction screening capacity scale proportionally. Current ITU and national regulatory frameworks were not designed for this velocity of deployment.
