Catalog Overview & Key Figures

The CelesTrak active-satellite catalog contains 14,875 operational spacecraft as of early 2026. The dataset spans launch years from 1964 to 2026, though the overwhelming majority of currently active assets were launched within the last six years.

Orbital parameters show a strong concentration in near-circular, low-inclination orbits typical of commercial mega-constellations. Median inclination sits at 53.2°, driven by the large Starlink population at ~53°. Median eccentricity is very low (≈0.0001), confirming that most operational satellites follow nearly circular orbits.

Data freshness: Epoch dates in the catalog are concentrated in March 2026, indicating the TLE sets are current. All statistics below are computed directly from the raw catalog; no extrapolation or modeling is applied.

Orbital Regime Classification

Satellites were classified using mean motion (revolutions per day) thresholds derived from standard orbital mechanics:

  • LEO — Mean motion > 14 rev/day (roughly 160–2,000 km altitude)
  • MEO — Mean motion between 1.0 and 14 rev/day
  • GEO — Mean motion ≈ 0.9–1.0 rev/day (geosynchronous altitude ~35,786 km)
  • HEO — Eccentricity > 0.5 (highly elliptical orbits, e.g., Molniya)

Launch Year Trends

The growth curve of active satellites is the headline story of this catalog. Before 2020, the active fleet grew gradually; since 2020 it has exploded. Of the 14,875 active satellites, 13,461 (90.5%) were launched in 2020 or later, while only 1,414 pre-date that threshold.

The peak single-year contribution comes from 2025, with 4,329 currently active satellites. The post-2020 surge is almost entirely attributable to Starlink batch launches and, more recently, Amazon's Kuiper initial deployment.

Orbital Parameter Deep Dive

Beyond population counts, the catalog's orbital parameters reveal how operators are using physical space.

Inclination

Inclination distribution is bimodal: a massive peak near 53° (Starlink shells) and a secondary cluster near 97–98° (sun-synchronous orbits used by imaging and weather satellites). The median across all satellites is 53.2°.

Eccentricity

Nearly all active satellites have eccentricities below 0.1, confirming operational preference for circular orbits that simplify station-keeping and payload pointing. A small tail above 0.5 represents HEO assets (e.g., Molniya orbits).

Key Takeaways & Outlook

  • LEO is the new normal. With 86.5% of active satellites in LEO, spectrum allocation and debris-mitigation policy must prioritize low-altitude regimes.
  • Starlink reshapes every aggregate. At 10,083 satellites, SpaceX's constellation is larger than the rest of the active fleet combined. Analysts should always present segmented and un-segmented views.
  • Post-2020 growth is unprecedented. 13,461 of 14,875 active satellites launched in the last six years. Licensing regimes and space-traffic-management systems designed for a slower era are now under strain.
  • GEO is stable but shrinking in share. GEO remains vital for broadcast and weather, yet its share of the active fleet has fallen to 0.1%. Spectrum re-farming and orbital-slot coordination in GEO still matter, but the center of gravity has shifted to LEO.
  • Orbital shells are highly structured. The tight clustering of inclination and near-zero eccentricity shows that operators are deploying into discrete, pre-planned shells. This predictability aids collision-avoidance planning but also concentrates risk.
