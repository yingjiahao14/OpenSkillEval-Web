Interpretive summary

The median mean motion of 15.27 rev/day places the central active satellite in a low-altitude orbital population rather than a legacy GEO-centered fleet.

The median inclination of 53.2° reflects dense use of inclined LEO shells. Starlink materially affects this central tendency: its median inclination is 53.2°, compared with 87.9° for all other active satellites.

Orbital Regime Classification

Orbit regimes are classified from MEAN_MOTION rather than altitude fields. Cutoffs used in this report are: LEO when MEAN_MOTION > 14 rev/day; GEO when 0.95–1.05 rev/day and ECCENTRICITY < 0.05; HEO when ECCENTRICITY ≥ 0.25 or an eccentric high-inclination non-LEO orbit; MEO for remaining non-LEO, non-GEO, non-HEO satellites.

Key Takeaways & Outlook

LEO is the dominant operating environment. Using the MEAN_MOTION threshold, 86.5% of active satellites are in LEO, making near-Earth spectrum coordination, conjunction screening, and end-of-life compliance central policy concerns.
Starlink reshapes every aggregate statistic. Starlink alone accounts for 67.8% of all active satellites and 78.4% of LEO satellites in the catalog.
The active fleet is heavily recent. 13,461 active satellites were launched since 2020, and 2025 is the largest active launch-year cohort, evidence of a step-change in constellation deployment cadence.
Orbital-regime licensing should be segmented. MEO, GEO, and HEO populations remain numerically smaller than LEO but have distinct coordination requirements; MEAN_MOTION-based classification keeps these regimes transparent and reproducible.
