Orbital Regime Classification

Satellites are classified using MEAN_MOTION thresholds: LEO (>14 rev/day, ~160–2,000 km), MEO (2.0–14.0 rev/day, ~2,000–35,000 km), GEO (0.9–1.1 rev/day, ~35,786 km), and HEO (eccentricity >0.1 with mean motion <2.0). Sub-GEO objects with near-circular orbits (<0.9 rev/day) are classified as Super-GEO/Other.

Major Constellation & Operator Breakdown

Constellation identification was performed by parsing OBJECT_NAME prefixes and substrings. Starlink's 10,083 satellites represent 67.8% of the entire catalog — a concentration unprecedented in space history. The remaining 4,792 satellites span dozens of operators, with OneWeb (651) and Kuiper (210) forming the only other multi-hundred satellite broadband constellations currently active.

Statistical distortion warning: Any fleet-wide statistic — median inclination, LEO share, launch-year distribution — is substantially shaped by Starlink. Analysts should segment by operator or orbital regime to avoid drawing conclusions that simply reflect SpaceX's deployment cadence.

Launch Year Trends — The Mega-Constellation Era

Headline finding: 13,461 of 14,875 currently active satellites (90.5%) were launched in 2020 or later. Annual launch counts grew from ~150 in 2019 to 4,329 in 2025 — a 29× increase in six years. This is not organic industry growth; it is the signature of sequential Starlink and OneWeb mega-constellation deployment phases.

Key Takeaways & Outlook

LEO Congestion Is Starlink-Driven

86.5% of active satellites are in LEO, but Starlink alone comprises 78.4% of that LEO population. Policy frameworks that treat LEO as a generic commons are inadequate — congestion risk is concentrated in specific orbital shells (primarily 15.0–15.5 rev/day, inclination 53°). Spectrum and conjunction analysis should be shell-specific.

The Fleet is Overwhelmingly New

90.5% of active satellites launched since 2020. This implies most active spacecraft are operating within their designed service lifetimes, but also that end-of-life deorbit obligations for thousands of satellites will converge within the next 5–7 years — creating a coordinated deorbit compliance challenge unprecedented in scale.

2025 Acceleration Warrants Scrutiny

The 4,329 satellites launched in 2025 represent a 75% year-over-year surge and the highest single-year count in the catalog. If 2026 maintains the pace implied by its partial-year count (962 through March 30), annual launches may exceed 3,800. Regulators must assess whether orbital coordination and debris-mitigation frameworks can scale at this rate.

GEO Remains Strategically Significant

596 satellites occupy GEO (4.0% of the total), providing continuous coverage for communications, broadcasting, and weather. Despite its small numerical share, GEO remains the highest-value orbital regime — each slot is scarce, non-renewable, and contested under ITU coordination procedures. Slots should not be evaluated purely by satellite count.

Competitive MEO Tier Emerging

1,336 satellites occupy MEO (8.98%), primarily navigation constellations (GPS/NAVSTAR, GLONASS, Galileo, BeiDou). This regime has historically been stable, but growing broadband use of MEO by operators like Telesat could introduce new coordination requirements between navigation and commercial broadband services.

Kuiper's Entry Changes the Calculus

Amazon's Kuiper constellation has 210 active satellites in the catalog as of March 2026 — still early-stage, but authorized for thousands more. A second megaconstellation operator at Starlink's scale would roughly double today's LEO congestion risk. Conjunction frequency and spectrum coexistence planning should model this scenario now.

For policy advisors: The 2026 orbital environment is defined by a single private operator's deployment program. Any framework — debris mitigation, spectrum allocation, orbital slot licensing — that does not explicitly account for megaconstellation dynamics at scale will be structurally inadequate within the decade. The data points are unambiguous: the pace of orbital population growth in 2020–2026 has no historical precedent.
