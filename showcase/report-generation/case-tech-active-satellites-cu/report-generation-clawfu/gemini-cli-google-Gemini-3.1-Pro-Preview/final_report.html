Launch Year Trends

The most defining characteristic of the current space era is the exponential acceleration in launch activity since 2020. Over 90% of currently active satellites were launched in the last 6 years.

Orbital Regime Classification

Using Mean Motion thresholds (>14 rev/day for LEO, ~1.0 for GEO), it is clear that LEO dominates the active satellite population, driven by broadband constellations.

Major Constellation Breakdown

SpaceX's Starlink dominates the active satellite catalog, making up roughly two-thirds of the total population. This single operator significantly skews overall orbital distributions.

Orbital Parameter Deep Dive

Analyzing the inclination of active satellites reveals distinct orbital "shells." The median inclination is driven heavily by Starlink's primary operational inclination of ~53 degrees.

Key Takeaways & Outlook

1. Mega-Constellation Dominance: Starlink is by far the largest single factor in the current orbital environment, representing 10,083 satellites. Any regulatory or spectrum policy must account for the outsized impact of a single operator's architecture.

2. The LEO Congestion: With 86.5% of all satellites residing in Low Earth Orbit, space traffic management and debris mitigation in the LEO regime are critical priorities. GEO and MEO represent a much smaller, largely static, strategic asset base.

3. Unprecedented Growth Rate: The fact that 13,461 active satellites were launched since 2020 demonstrates a paradigm shift in space access. The transition from large, exquisite satellites to proliferated small-satellite architectures is complete.
