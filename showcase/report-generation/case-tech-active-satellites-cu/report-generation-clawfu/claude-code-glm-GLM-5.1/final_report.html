01Catalog Overview & Key Figures

This catalog captures 14,875 active satellites tracked by NORAD and published via CelesTrak. Orbital elements are current as of the epoch range 2026-02-26 through 2026-03-30. Starlink alone accounts for 10,083 satellites (67.8% of the total), fundamentally reshaping the statistical profile of the orbital environment. Of all active satellites, 13,461 (90.5%) were launched in 2020 or later.

Key Finding: The median inclination of 53.2° reflects the dominance of near-polar sun-synchronous and mid-inclination LEO shells, heavily influenced by Starlink's ~53° and ~97° orbital planes.

06Key Takeaways & Outlook

Synthesis of findings for space-sustainability policy and constellation licensing decisions.

1. Mega-constellation dominance: Starlink's 10,083 satellites represent 67.8% of all active spacecraft. Any statistical analysis of the orbital environment must segment Starlink from the rest of the fleet to avoid distorted conclusions.

2. LEO congestion is accelerating: LEO holds 12,860 satellites (86.5% of total), with 78.4% of those belonging to Starlink. The post-2020 surge is almost entirely a LEO phenomenon driven by commercial broadband constellations.

3. Rapid fleet turnover: 90.5% of the active fleet was launched since 2020. This implies a young population with short design lifetimes, increasing replacement launch rates and end-of-life disposal demands.

4. Orbital regime balance is shifting: GEO — long the primary orbit for communications — now holds only 4.4% of active satellites. Policy and spectrum frameworks designed for a GEO-centric era may need updating for the LEO-dominated reality.

5. Concentration risk: A single operator controlling over 67.8% of all active satellites creates systemic dependencies for space situational awareness, collision avoidance, and debris mitigation. Regulatory frameworks should consider operator-level concentration limits.
