Launch Year Trends

Launch year is extracted from the first four characters of OBJECT_ID. This analysis covers currently active satellites only — deorbited or failed spacecraft are excluded from the catalog.

Key Finding: The active satellite population has experienced explosive growth since 2020. Annual launches of still-active satellites surged from 151 in 2019 to 4,329 in 2025 — a 28.7x increase in just six years. The cumulative curve shifts from near-linear to exponential around 2020, coinciding with the acceleration of mega-constellation deployments.

Major Constellation & Operator Breakdown

This single-operator concentration has profound implications for spectrum management, debris risk, and orbital slot availability.

Orbital Regime Classification

Classification Method (MEAN_MOTION thresholds):
LEO (Low Earth Orbit): MEAN_MOTION > 11.25 rev/day and eccentricity ≤ 0.25 — corresponds to altitudes ~160–2,000 km
GEO (Geostationary/Geosynchronous): 0.99 ≤ MEAN_MOTION ≤ 1.01 rev/day and eccentricity ≤ 0.25 — ~35,786 km altitude
MEO (Medium Earth Orbit): 1.01 < MEAN_MOTION ≤ 11.25 rev/day and eccentricity ≤ 0.25 — ~2,000–35,000 km
HEO (Highly Elliptical Orbit): eccentricity > 0.25 — includes Molniya, Tundra, and transfer orbits

Orbital Parameter Deep Dive

Examining the distributions of inclination, eccentricity, and mean motion reveals the dominant orbital shells and highlights how constellation design choices shape the aggregate population.

Inclination Distribution (5° bins)

The distribution is heavily multimodal. The dominant peak at 50–55° (5,324 satellites) corresponds to Starlink's main shell. The secondary peak at 40–45° (3,744) reflects Starlink's v2 Mini shells. The 95–100° cluster (2,803) captures sun-synchronous orbits used by Earth-observation missions. Near-equatorial orbits (0–5°, 523) predominantly house GEO communications satellites.

Eccentricity Profile

99.0% of satellites maintain near-circular orbits (eccentricity < 0.01), reflecting the operational preference for uniform ground coverage. Only 36 satellites occupy highly elliptical orbits.

Mean Motion Distribution

The mean motion histogram shows a sharp peak at ~15 rev/day (typical LEO altitude ~550 km), with a secondary cluster near 1.0 rev/day (GEO). The median of 15.27 rev/day confirms that the "typical" active satellite today is a LEO broadband spacecraft.

Key Takeaways & Outlook

1. Unprecedented Concentration
A single operator (SpaceX) controls 67.8% of all active satellites. This concentration raises questions for spectrum regulators and orbital debris policymakers about systemic risk from a single point of failure.
2. LEO is the New Default
94.6% of active spacecraft orbit in LEO. The ratio will likely increase as Amazon Kuiper (216 satellites deployed) and other constellations scale up, further concentrating traffic in the 500–600 km shell.
3. Exponential Growth Trajectory
90.5% of the active fleet launched after 2020. With 2026 Q1 already at 962 satellites, the annual launch rate shows no signs of plateauing. Long-term sustainability of the LEO environment depends on reliable deorbit compliance.
4. Orbital Shell Crowding
Inclination and mean-motion data reveal that most satellites cluster in narrow orbital shells (~53° inclination, ~550 km altitude). This concentration increases conjunction frequency and demands robust space traffic management.
Policy Implications: The data supports three actionable priorities: (1) spectrum allocation frameworks must account for single-operator dominance and the associated interference risks at scale; (2) orbital debris mitigation rules should be proportional to constellation size, with enhanced monitoring for the most populated shells; (3) constellation licensing should incorporate capacity-based spacing requirements to preserve long-term orbital access for new entrants.
