Key Takeaways & Outlook

  • Launch-year acceleration is visible in the active fleet: the post‑2020 distribution peaks in 2025 with 4,329 active satellites from that year in this catalog snapshot.
  • LEO dominates by mean-motion classification: 12,860 satellites (86.45%) are in LEO under the stated thresholds.
  • Constellation concentration is extreme: Starlink accounts for 67.78% of all active satellites here, heavily shaping regime and inclination aggregates.
  • Orbital shells are apparent in inclination clustering: the catalog shows strong modes near ~53° and ~43° (visible in the inclination histogram), consistent with heavily populated LEO shells in the data.
  • Most orbits are near-circular: ECCENTRICITY is typically small (p95 ≈ 0.001311), with a limited high-eccentricity tail (41 satellites with ECCENTRICITY > 0.1).

Policy relevance: spectrum and licensing discussions should treat constellation concentration as a first-order effect; aggregated “global” statistics in this catalog are strongly driven by a single operator group.
