1. Catalog Overview & Key Figures

This catalog contains 14,875 active satellites tracked by NORAD and published via CelesTrak. The orbital element sets span from 2026-02-26 to 2026-03-30, reflecting recent positional data for all operational spacecraft. The dataset includes civil, commercial, and military satellites across every orbital regime — from the International Space Station in low Earth orbit to geostationary communications platforms at 35,786 km altitude.

Key structural insight: 10,083 of 14,875 active satellites (67.8%) belong to SpaceX's Starlink constellation. A single commercial operator thus controls more than half the active orbital population, profoundly shaping aggregate statistics for orbital regime, inclination, and launch cadence.

2. Orbital Regime Classification

Satellites are classified into four regimes using MEAN_MOTION thresholds supplemented by eccentricity checks for highly elliptical orbits:

  • LEO — MEAN_MOTION > 14 rev/day (approximately 160–2,000 km altitude)
  • MEO — MEAN_MOTION 1.8–14 rev/day (approximately 2,000–35,786 km, excluding HEO)
  • GEO — MEAN_MOTION 0.9–1.8 rev/day (approximately 35,786 km altitude)
  • HEO — ECCENTRICITY > 0.1 with MEAN_MOTION < 12, or MEAN_MOTION < 0.9

Key Observations

  • Sun-synchronous cluster: The 97–98° inclination bin contains 2,809 satellites, reflecting the popularity of sun-synchronous orbits (SSO) for Earth observation and weather missions.
  • Starlink shell at ~53°: The 50–70° bin is dominated by Starlink's nominal 53° and 53.2° orbital shells, contributing thousands of satellites in a narrow inclination band.
  • Low eccentricity norm: 14,651 satellites (98.5%) have eccentricities below 0.005, indicating nearly circular orbits — typical of station-kept operational spacecraft.
  • HEO outliers: 41 satellites exhibit eccentricities above 0.1, including Molniya and GTO-class orbits.
  • GEO concentration: A distinct peak near MEAN_MOTION ≈ 1.0 rev/day corresponds to the geostationary ring, hosting 624 active satellites.

6. Key Takeaways & Outlook

  • LEO is now the overwhelmingly dominant regime — 86.5% of all active satellites orbit below ~2,000 km. This share is set to grow as Starlink, OneWeb, and planned constellations continue launching.
  • A single operator reshapes the statistics. Starlink's 10,083 satellites (67.8% of the catalog) skew inclination distributions toward 53°, inflate LEO counts, and account for the majority of post-2020 launch activity. Any regulatory or policy analysis must segment by operator to avoid misleading aggregates.
  • Post-2020 launch surge is unprecedented. 13,461 satellites (90.5%) were launched since 2020. This represents a step-change in orbital population growth, with implications for conjunction assessment, collision risk, and debris generation rates.
  • Orbital congestion is concentrated. The combination of high satellite density in the 500–600 km altitude band (Starlink) and the 97–98° sun-synchronous corridor creates localized congestion. Spectrum coordination and right-of-way norms will face increasing pressure.
  • GEO remains strategically vital despite its small share. With 624 active satellites (4.2%), GEO hosts the bulk of communications, weather, and early-warning missions that require persistent regional coverage.
  • Space sustainability requires action. The rapid growth of the LEO population, combined with planned mega-constellation filings (tens of thousands of additional satellites), demands proactive debris mitigation, active removal capabilities, and internationally coordinated traffic management to preserve the orbital environment for future use.
