Orbital Regime Classification

Satellites are classified into four orbital regimes using MEAN_MOTION thresholds (orbits per day):

  • LEO (Low Earth Orbit): MEAN_MOTION > 14 rev/day (~160–2,000 km altitude)
  • MEO (Medium Earth Orbit): MEAN_MOTION 2.0–14 rev/day (~2,000–35,786 km)
  • GEO (Geostationary Earth Orbit): MEAN_MOTION ~1.0 rev/day (~35,786 km)
  • HEO (Highly Elliptical Orbit): MEAN_MOTION < 2.0 rev/day with high eccentricity

Orbital Parameter Deep Dive

Inclination Distribution

Inclination (orbital tilt relative to the equator) varies from 0.0° to 149.6°, with a median of 53.2°. The distribution reflects two dominant populations: near-equatorial constellations serving communications hubs and mid-inclination shells optimized for regional coverage.

Mean Motion (Altitude Proxy)

Mean motion, measured in revolutions per day, serves as a proxy for orbital altitude. The bimodal distribution shows clear peaks at LEO (~14–15 rev/day) and GEO (~1.0 rev/day), with a sparse MEO bridge connecting them.

Eccentricity Distribution

Eccentricity measures orbit shape, from circular (e = 0) to highly elliptical (e → 1). Most satellites operate in near-circular orbits (median eccentricity 0.00016), with a long tail of highly eccentric HEO orbits including Molniya-type spacecraft.

Key Takeaways & Outlook

  1. Starlink Dominance: SpaceX operates 10,083 satellites — 67.8% of all active spacecraft. Any orbital congestion, debris, or spectrum policy discussion must center on LEO megaconstellation management.
  2. LEO Congestion: 86.5% of all active satellites orbit in LEO. The 14,875-satellite active population is not uniformly distributed — it is heavily concentrated in a handful of orbital shells at specific inclinations.
  3. Unprecedented Launch Rate: 90% of the active fleet was launched since 2020. The cadence of new deployments has no historical parallel, outpacing regulatory frameworks, debris mitigation norms, and conjunction monitoring capacity.
  4. Operator Concentration: Beyond Starlink, the next largest operators (OneWeb, Iridium, Cosmos) hold orders of magnitude fewer spacecraft. This asymmetry complicates multilateral space governance.
  5. Policy Implications: Current orbital debris guidelines and ITU spectrum allocation processes were not designed for megaconstellation-scale deployments. The data in this report provides the baseline against which policy interventions should be measured.
