Active Satellites in Earth Orbit

2026 Catalog Analysis

Executive Summary

This report presents a comprehensive analysis of the 14,875 active satellites currently orbiting Earth, derived from NORAD Two-Line Element (TLE) tracking data. It evaluates orbital regime distributions, operator dominance, and the accelerating rate of satellite launches.

1. Catalog Overview & Key Figures

The analyzed dataset contains accurate orbital elements with an EPOCH range extending from 2026-02-26 to 2026-03-30, representing near real-time operational status. Across the catalog, high-level distributions highlight a heavily populated near-Earth environment. The fleet exhibits an average mean motion of 14.33 rev/day, an average inclination of 60.0°, and a very low average eccentricity of 0.0023, underscoring the prevalence of highly circular LEO orbits.

2. Orbital Regime Classification

Satellites are classified into Low Earth Orbit (LEO), Medium Earth Orbit (MEO), Geosynchronous Equatorial Orbit (GEO), and Highly Elliptical Orbit (HEO) using the following thresholds:

  • LEO: Mean Motion > 14 rev/day
  • MEO: Mean Motion between 1.1 and 14 rev/day
  • GEO: Mean Motion between 0.9 and 1.1 rev/day
  • HEO: Eccentricity > 0.25

4. Launch Year Trends

A profound acceleration in launch rates is visible from 2020 onward, correlating directly with the commercial deployment of LEO broadband constellations like Starlink and OneWeb. The historical growth curve shows that the majority of humanity's active satellite fleet is only a few years old.

6. Key Takeaways & Outlook

  • The rapid commercialization of space has resulted in 13,461 active satellites launched since 2020, representing 90.5% of the total operational fleet.
  • Constellation dominance is stark: Starlink's 10,083 satellites constitute 67.8% of all active satellites, fundamentally shaping LEO utilization.
  • Orbital congestion is highly concentrated: With 86.5% of satellites placed in Low Earth Orbit, and specific inclination shells (like 53°) densely populated, collision risks are compounding rapidly.
  • To ensure long-term space sustainability, operators and regulators must prioritize automated traffic management, stricter de-orbit compliance, and active debris removal protocols in these crowded orbital regimes.
