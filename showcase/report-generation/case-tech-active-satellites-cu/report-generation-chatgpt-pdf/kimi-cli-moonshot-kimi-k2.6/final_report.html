1. Catalog Overview & Key Figures

The catalog contains 14,875 active satellites with orbital element sets (TLEs) generated between 2026-02-26 and 2026-03-30. The data spans launch years from 1964 to 2026, with the vast majority of active spacecraft launched in the last five years.

Data Freshness: All element sets are from February–March 2026, confirming the catalog reflects the current operational fleet rather than historical debris or inactive objects.

The mean motion distribution is heavily concentrated around 15.27 rev/day (median), consistent with large LEO constellations. Mean eccentricity is extremely low (0.000156 median), indicating predominantly circular orbits across the fleet.

2. Orbital Regime Classification

Satellites were classified using MEAN_MOTION thresholds derived from Keplerian orbit mechanics:

  • LEO — Mean motion > 11.25 rev/day (altitude < ~2,000 km)
  • GEO — Mean motion 0.90–1.10 rev/day (~35,786 km altitude)
  • MEO — Mean motion 1.10–11.25 rev/day with eccentricity ≤ 0.1
  • HEO — Eccentricity > 0.1 (highly elliptical orbits, e.g., Molniya, Tundra)

4. Launch Year Trends

The growth curve of currently active satellites is the defining story of this catalog. 13,461 satellites (90.5%) were launched in 2020 or later. The five-year period 2020–2024 delivered 12.2× as many active spacecraft as 2015–2019.

2025 was the peak year: 4,329 satellites launched, of which 3,160 were Starlink and 1,169 were non-Starlink. Even excluding Starlink, 2025 set a record for non-constellation launch activity.

Starlink's deployment began in earnest in 2019 (17 satellites) and accelerated dramatically: 298 in 2020, 1,486 in 2022, 1,812 in 2023, 1,874 in 2024, and 3,160 in 2025. This single program now outnumbers the entire non-Starlink active fleet.

5. Orbital Parameter Deep Dive

Inclination

The median inclination across all active satellites is 53.2°, driven overwhelmingly by Starlink's ~53° shells. Without Starlink, the median jumps to 87.9°, reflecting the large population of polar and sun-synchronous orbits used by Earth-observation and weather satellites.

Nearly all active satellites (92.1%) have eccentricity below 0.001, indicating near-circular orbits. Only 41 satellites exceed 0.1; these are primarily scientific and military missions in highly elliptical orbits (HEO), such as the Cluster II spacecraft, THEMIS probes, and Russian Meridian/Arktika-M communications satellites.

6. Key Takeaways & Outlook

Orbital congestion is now a LEO phenomenon. With 94.6% of active satellites in Low Earth Orbit and 10,083 Starlink spacecraft alone, collision-avoidance maneuvering, spectrum coordination, and debris-mitigation rules must be designed around mega-constellation densities rather than the traditional GEO-slot scarcity model.

Constellation dominance reshapes every statistic. Starlink's 67.8% share of the total fleet and 71.6% share of LEO means that any aggregate metric—median inclination, mean motion, launch rate—is effectively a Starlink metric unless explicitly segmented. Policy analysis must treat "constellation" and "non-constellation" populations as distinct datasets.

Launch acceleration shows no sign of slowing. The jump from 2,477 launches in 2024 to 4,329 in 2025—plus 1,169 non-Starlink satellites, the highest non-SpaceX total on record—suggests the entire industry is scaling, not just SpaceX. Amazon Kuiper, Chinese LEO constellations, and European sovereign programs are all adding to the count.

Implications for policy:

  • Spectrum allocation: With 10,083 co-orbiting transmitters in similar altitude shells, interference management and ITU coordination filing backlogs will intensify.
  • Debris policy: The 14,073 LEO satellites represent an unprecedented target density. Post-mission disposal reliability and active debris removal priorities should focus on the 500–600 km altitude band where Starlink and OneWeb shells overlap.
  • Licensing: National regulators may need constellation-specific environmental assessments rather than per-satellite licenses, given that a single operator now controls two-thirds of the active fleet.
