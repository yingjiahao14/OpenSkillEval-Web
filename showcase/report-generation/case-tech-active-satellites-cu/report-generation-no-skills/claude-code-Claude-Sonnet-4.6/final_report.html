Insights

Key Takeaways

Summary findings derived from the full statistical analysis of the 14,875-satellite catalog.

LEO Dominance

94.6% of all active satellites (14,073) orbit in Low Earth Orbit (mean motion >11.25 rev/day). This concentration is driven almost entirely by Starlink and other broadband mega-constellations deployed since 2019.

Starlink Scale

With 10,083 satellites, Starlink alone accounts for 67.8% of the entire active catalog. This single constellation has fundamentally reshaped the orbital environment in just a few years of deployment.

Post-2020 Surge

13,461 satellites — 90.5% of the total — have been launched since 2020. This unprecedented pace of deployment represents a structural shift in space access driven by reusable launch vehicles.

Inclination Clustering

The median inclination of 53.2° reflects strong concentration near 53° (Starlink Phase 1), ~70° (polar broadband), and 90° (sun-synchronous Earth observation). GEO belt satellites contribute a cluster near 0°.

GEO Belt Stability

596 GEO satellites (4.0%) maintain near-stationary positions over the equator. Despite LEO growth, GEO remains commercially significant for broadcast and high-throughput satellite services.

MEO Navigation Fleets

195 MEO satellites (1.3%) represent primarily GNSS navigation constellations: GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, and BeiDou. This regime serves as the backbone of global positioning infrastructure.
