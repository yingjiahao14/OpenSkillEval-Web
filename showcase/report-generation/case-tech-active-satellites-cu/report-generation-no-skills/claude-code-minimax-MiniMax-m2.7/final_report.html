6. Key Takeaways & Outlook

1. Starlink dominance reshapes orbital environment: At 10,083 satellites (67.8% of total), SpaceX's Starlink is not merely the largest constellation — it is the orbital environment. Any debris, spectrum, or collision-risk policy discussion is effectively a Starlink discussion.
2. LEO is overwhelmingly dominant: 86.5% of active satellites occupy LEO (MEAN_MOTION > 14 rev/day), driven almost entirely by Starlink and OneWeb. MEO (9.5%) is populated primarily by navigation constellations (GPS, Galileo, GLONASS, BeiDou). GEO (3.9%) hosts traditional comsats but is the smallest regime by count.
3. Launch acceleration is unprecedented: With 13,461 of 14,875 satellites (90.5%) launched since 2020, the current active fleet is overwhelmingly recent. 2025 alone added 4,329 satellites — more than the entire pre-2020 operational fleet. Licensing and debris mitigation frameworks face severe stress-test timelines.
4. Inclination clusters at ~53° median: The median inclination of 53.16° reflects Starlink's orbital shell design (mostly 53° inclination). However, the distribution shows significant spread across 40–60° and a secondary cluster at 90–100° (polar orbits), indicating heterogeneous traffic patterns even within LEO.
