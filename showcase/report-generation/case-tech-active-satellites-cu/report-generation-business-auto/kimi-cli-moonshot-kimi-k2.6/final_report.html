Catalog Overview & Key Figures

This report analyzes the full catalog of 14,875 active satellites sourced from the CelesTrak space-tracking platform (via NORAD TLE data). The dataset spans launch years from 1964 to 2026, with orbital element epochs ranging from 2026-02-26 to 2026-03-30, confirming high data freshness.

Key orbital parameters show a highly concentrated population:

  • Mean Motion: The vast majority of satellites orbit at ≥15 rev/day (n = 11,093), consistent with large LEO constellations. Only 32 objects have mean motion below 1 rev/day.
  • Inclination: The median inclination is 53.16°, driven by Starlink shells near 53° and polar orbits near 90°. The distribution peaks in the 50–70° band (5,748 satellites) and the 85–100° polar band (3,739 satellites).
  • Eccentricity: 14,721 satellites (99.0%) have near-circular orbits (eccentricity <0.01). Only 32 objects exhibit highly eccentric trajectories (>0.5).

Orbital Regime Classification

Satellites were classified using MEAN_MOTION thresholds applied to the catalog data:

  • LEO (Low Earth Orbit): >14 rev/day (~160–2,000 km altitude)
  • MEO (Medium Earth Orbit): 1.5–14 rev/day
  • GEO (Geostationary Orbit): 0.9–1.5 rev/day
  • HEO (Highly Elliptical Orbit): Eccentricity >0.25 regardless of mean motion

Launch Year Trends

Launch year was extracted from the first four characters of OBJECT_ID. The resulting time series reveals a dramatic acceleration in the active satellite population, especially after 2019.

Of the 14,875 currently active satellites, 13,461 (90.5%) were launched in 2020 or later. This reflects the rapid deployment of Starlink and competing broadband constellations. Prior to 2019, annual additions to the active fleet rarely exceeded a few hundred spacecraft; since 2020, yearly launch counts have risen into the thousands.

Headline: The post-2020 launch surge has added more active satellites in six years than the previous five decades combined. For spectrum regulators and debris-mitigation planners, this is the defining trend of the current orbital environment.

Orbital Parameter Deep Dive

Inclination

Inclination determines a satellite’s ground-track coverage and launch-site latitude requirements. The catalog shows two dominant clusters:

  • ~53° inclination: The Starlink shell population drives the 50–70° band (5,748 satellites).
  • Near-polar (~90°): Earth-observation and some communications missions populate the 85–100° band (3,739 satellites).

Starlink’s own inclination statistics: mean = 54.51°, median = 53.16°, std = 15.02°. The tight interquartile range (25th–75th percentile: 43.0°–53.22°) confirms disciplined shell deployment.

Eccentricity

Almost the entire active fleet (14,721 of 14,875) maintains near-circular orbits (eccentricity <0.01). High-eccentricity outliers (32 satellites) are predominantly legacy scientific or communications satellites in Molniya-like orbits.

Key Takeaways & Outlook

  • Orbital congestion is now a LEO story. With 86.5% of active satellites in Low Earth Orbit, spectrum coordination and conjunction-assessment workloads are concentrated in altitudes below 2,000 km.
  • One operator reshapes global totals. Starlink’s 10,083 satellites represent 67.8% of the catalog. Any policy framework for space sustainability must account for the statistical dominance of a single constellation.
  • Growth is historically unprecedented. 13,461 satellites — 90.5% of the active fleet — were launched in the last six years. Licensing regimes and debris-mitigation guidelines designed for slower eras will face pressure to adapt.
  • Most orbits are predictable. Near-circular paths and tightly clustered inclinations simplify tracking and collision-risk modeling, but the sheer volume of objects raises the aggregate probability of close approaches.
