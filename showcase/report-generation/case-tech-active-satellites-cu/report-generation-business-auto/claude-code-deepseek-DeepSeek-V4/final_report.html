01 Catalog Overview & Key Figures

This report analyzes the 14,875 active satellites tracked in the NORAD / CelesTrak TLE catalog as of late March 2026. The dataset captures the operational state of Earth's orbital population across all regimes — from the International Space Station in Low Earth Orbit to geostationary communications platforms at 35,786 km altitude.

Headline: Only 1,414 satellites (9.5%) currently active were launched before 2020. The other 13,461 (90.5%) were deployed in the past six years — a growth curve without precedent in the history of spaceflight.

04 Launch Year Trends

Launch year is extracted from the first four characters of OBJECT_ID (e.g., "2024-001A" → 2024). This represents the year the satellite was placed in orbit, not the year of catalog entry. Because the dataset includes only currently active satellites, the chart below reflects survival-weighted trends — older launches are underrepresented due to natural decay and deorbiting.

The acceleration is extraordinary. Nearly half of all active satellites — 4,329 (29.1%) — were launched in 2025 alone. More satellites were launched and kept active in 2025 than existed in the entire active catalog as recently as 2022 (then ~4,800).

06 Key Takeaways & Outlook

1. The Starlink Effect Is Total — and Growing. One operator fields 67.8% of all active satellites. Starlink's 10,083 spacecraft skew every aggregate statistic: LEO share, median inclination, mean eccentricity, and the launch growth curve. Any analysis of the orbital environment that does not segment by operator risks being a Starlink analysis in disguise.
2. The Megaconstellation Era Has Reshaped Space Access. With 90.5% of active satellites launched since 2020, the orbital population has turned over almost completely in six years. The active catalog in early 2020 (~1,900 satellites) has been multiplied 7.8× by sustained high-cadence launches, primarily from SpaceX but increasingly from OneWeb, Amazon (Project Kuiper), and Chinese state-backed constellations.
3. LEO Congestion Is Concentrated at Specific Altitudes. The Mid-LEO band (MM 15–16, ~450–600 km) hosts 11,014 satellites — 74.1% of the entire catalog. This altitude range is the preferred deployment zone for broadband megaconstellations. Conjunction risk, debris mitigation, and spectrum coordination are all concentrated in this narrow shell.
4. The Non-Starlink Population Tells a Different Story. Excluding Starlink, the remaining 4,792 satellites distribute more evenly: 57.9% LEO, 28.9% MEO, 12.4% GEO. The median inclination rises to 87.9°, reflecting the dominance of sun-synchronous polar orbits in Earth observation, weather, and government missions. Multiple non-Starlink constellations (OneWeb, Kuiper, China's Hulianwang Digui/Qianfan) are in active deployment, suggesting the operator-concentration problem may ease — even as total population continues to climb.
5. Orbital Sustainability Is Now a First-Order Concern. The current 14,875 active satellites represent a ~7.8× increase over 2020 levels. With multiple operators planning constellations of 3,000–13,000+ satellites, the active catalog could exceed 30,000–50,000 objects within this decade. Collision avoidance maneuvers, post-mission disposal compliance, and debris mitigation enforcement will define whether this growth trajectory is sustainable or carries systemic risk.
