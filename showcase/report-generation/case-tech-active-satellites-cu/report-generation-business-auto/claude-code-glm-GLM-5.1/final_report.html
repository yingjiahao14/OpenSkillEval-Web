2. Orbital Regime Classification

Satellites are classified by MEAN_MOTION thresholds:

  • LEO — MEAN_MOTION > 14.0 rev/day (approx. 160–2,000 km altitude)
  • MEO — 2.0 < MEAN_MOTION ≤ 14.0 rev/day
  • GEO — 0.9 ≤ MEAN_MOTION ≤ 1.1 rev/day (~35,786 km)
  • HEO — Eccentricity > 0.1 (highly elliptical, e.g. Molniya)

5. Orbital Parameter Deep Dive

Inclination Distribution

The inclination histogram reveals distinct clustering: the ~53° shell (Starlink), the ~97° Sun-synchronous band (Earth observation), and the ~0–28° equatorial belt (GEO comsats, ISS at 51.6°). The Starlink peak at 53° dominates the distribution, underscoring how a single operator reshapes the statistical landscape.

Mean Motion Distribution

The LEO cluster (MM > 14) is overwhelmingly the largest, with the GEO peak at ~1 rev/day forming a distant second. The MEO band (2–14 rev/day) is sparsely populated, occupied primarily by navigation constellations (GPS, GLONASS, Galileo).

Eccentricity Distribution

The vast majority of active satellites operate in near-circular orbits (e < 0.01). The long tail toward higher eccentricities corresponds to HEO/Molniya-type orbits and transfer orbits. Only 41 satellites (0.3%) exceed e = 0.1, the HEO threshold used in this analysis.

6. Key Takeaways & Outlook

Orbital Congestion Is Accelerating

The active satellite population has grown from a steady-state of ~2,000–3,000 pre-2015 to 14,875 today. With 90% of the fleet launched since 2020, LEO is experiencing unprecedented density. This has direct implications for:

  • Collision risk: Conjunction events in LEO are rising proportionally to the square of object density.
  • Spectrum allocation: Downlink interference between large constellations requires ongoing ITU coordination.
  • Debris policy: Post-mission disposal compliance rates must improve as the population grows.

Constellation Dominance Skews Statistics

Starlink's 10,083 satellites (68% of the total) dominate LEO and shift aggregate metrics. Any analysis of orbital trends that does not segment by operator will primarily reflect a single constellation's deployment schedule. Policy decisions on constellation licensing must account for this concentration of orbital assets under one operator.

The LEO Share Is Unprecedented

86% of all active satellites occupy LEO. This concentration means that the most commercially and scientifically valuable low-altitude shells are filling rapidly. Future licensing frameworks may need to consider altitude-based density caps.

GEO and MEO Remain Stable

With 596 GEO and 1,336 MEO satellites, these regimes show the characteristics of mature, slowly evolving infrastructure. Navigation constellations (GPS, GLONASS, Galileo) and geostationary communications represent long-lived, replacement-driven populations rather than growth-driven ones.

Outlook

The current growth trajectory is unlikely to plateau soon. SpaceX continues Starlink expansion, Amazon's Kuiper constellation is in early deployment, and multiple national operators have announced LEO constellation plans. The 2026 catalog will likely show further acceleration, making space sustainability frameworks increasingly urgent.
