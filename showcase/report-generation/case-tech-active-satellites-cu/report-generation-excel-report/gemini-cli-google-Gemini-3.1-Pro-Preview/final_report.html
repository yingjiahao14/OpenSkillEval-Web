1. Catalog Overview & Key Figures

This analytical report presents a comprehensive overview of the 14,875 active satellites currently in orbit around Earth. Based on recent TLE data, the analysis underscores a rapidly changing orbital environment characterized by exponential growth in low-Earth orbit (LEO). With an overall median inclination of 53.2°, the data highlights the prevalence of polar and near-polar orbits designed for global coverage.

2. Orbital Regime Classification

Satellites were classified based on Mean Motion (revolutions per day) and Eccentricity:

  • LEO (> 11.25 rev/day): Low Earth Orbit.
  • MEO (1.1 - 11.25 rev/day): Medium Earth Orbit.
  • GEO (0.9 - 1.1 rev/day): Geosynchronous / Geostationary Orbit.
  • HEO (Eccentricity > 0.25): Highly Elliptical Orbit.

The vast majority of active satellites reside in LEO, accounting for 86.5% of the population (using the >14 rev/day threshold). GEO remains a stable, but proportionally small, segment dedicated to telecommunications and weather monitoring.

3. Major Constellation & Operator Breakdown

The rise of mega-constellations has fundamentally reshaped the satellite catalog. SpaceX's Starlink constellation alone comprises 10,083 satellites, representing roughly 67.8% of all active satellites.

4. Launch Year Trends

An analysis of the OBJECT_ID international designators reveals an exponential increase in satellite deployments. A staggering 13,461 active satellites (90.5% of the total) have been launched since 2020.

5. Orbital Parameter Deep Dive

The distribution of orbital parameters illustrates distinct operational "shells". The concentration of satellites around specific Mean Motion values (~15 rev/day) and Inclinations (e.g., 53°, 97°) aligns directly with the targeted orbital planes of major communications and earth observation constellations.

6. Key Takeaways & Outlook

  • Unprecedented Congestion: The rapid injection of satellites since 2020 marks an era of high congestion, predominantly in LEO.
  • Constellation Dominance: Single-operator mega-constellations, most notably Starlink, overwhelmingly drive the statistical averages of the current orbital environment.
  • Policy Implications: The dramatic shift towards densely populated orbital shells necessitates stringent space traffic management, updated spectrum allocation policies, and proactive debris mitigation strategies to ensure long-term space sustainability.
