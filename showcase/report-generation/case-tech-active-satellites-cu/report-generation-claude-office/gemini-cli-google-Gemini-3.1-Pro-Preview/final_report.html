Catalog Overview & Key Figures

This report analyzes the current operational satellite catalog based on TLE data from the CelesTrak platform. The dataset covers 14,875 active objects. The median orbital inclination across all satellites is 53.2°, reflecting the prevalence of mid-to-high inclination orbits suited for global coverage. The catalog demonstrates a staggering acceleration in launch activity, with 13,461 satellites (roughly 90.5%) having been launched in or after 2020.

Launch Year Trends

The headline story in the satellite industry is the dramatic inflection point in launch activity over the past half-decade. Driven by the deployment of mega-constellations and a reduction in launch costs, the number of currently active satellites placed in orbit annually has skyrocketed.

Orbital Regime Classification

The orbital environment is overwhelmingly dominated by Low Earth Orbit (LEO) systems. By classifying objects based on Mean Motion (revolutions per day) and Eccentricity, we find that LEO systems constitute 94.6% of the active catalog.

  • LEO (Low Earth Orbit): >11.25 rev/day
  • MEO (Medium Earth Orbit): Between LEO and GEO
  • GEO (Geosynchronous Orbit): ~1.0 rev/day
  • HEO (Highly Elliptical Orbit): Eccentricity > 0.25

Major Constellation & Operator Breakdown

SpaceX's Starlink constellation reshapes the entire statistical picture of the orbital environment. With 10,083 operational satellites, Starlink accounts for nearly 67.8% of all active satellites. Other major contributors include OneWeb, Planet's Flock, and Spire's Lemur systems.

Orbital Parameter Deep Dive

The distribution of Mean Motion versus Inclination highlights dense "orbital shells." Starlink and other mega-constellations form distinct horizontal bands (indicating consistent altitude/mean motion) across a few key inclinations (e.g., 53°, 70°, 97° SSO). Geostationary satellites form a cluster near 0° inclination and 1.0 rev/day mean motion.

Key Takeaways & Outlook

1. Unprecedented Growth: The exponential growth curve in launch volume over the past five years indicates a structural shift in how space is utilized, moving from bespoke, large-scale satellites to mass-produced LEO constellations.

2. The LEO Congestion: With over 94.6% of active objects in LEO, the lower orbital regimes are becoming increasingly dense. This necessitates advanced space traffic management and stricter orbital debris mitigation protocols.

3. Monopolization of Orbit: A single operator, SpaceX, dominates the catalog. This skew heavily influences global statistics and underscores the regulatory and spectrum allocation challenges facing policy advisors in the near future.

4. Orbital Shell Density: Specific orbital parameters—namely, mid-inclination LEO bands and Sun-Synchronous Orbits (SSO) around 97-98 degrees—are highly sought after and represent the most congested "highways" in space.
