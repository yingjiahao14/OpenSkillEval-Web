Classification Methodology

Orbital regime is determined by the following decision rules applied in order:

HEOEccentricity > 0.25 (highly elliptical, e.g. Molniya, Tundra, GTO)
LEOMean motion > 11.25 rev/day (orbital period < ~128 min, altitude < ~2,000 km)
MEOMean motion > 1.8 and ≤ 11.25 rev/day (altitude ~2,000–35,000 km; includes GPS, GLONASS, Galileo)
GEOMean motion ≥ 0.85 and ≤ 1.8 rev/day (geostationary / geosynchronous belt)
HEO (residual)Mean motion < 0.85 rev/day (very high orbits, e.g. Chandra, INTEGRAL)

Key Takeaways & Outlook Section 6

Mega-Constellation Dominance

Starlink alone accounts for 67.8% of all active satellites. This single-operator concentration is without historical precedent and fundamentally reshapes orbital regime statistics, inclination distributions, and launch cadence metrics. Any policy analysis must segment Starlink-driven effects from broader industry trends.

LEO Congestion Accelerating

LEO hosts 94.6% of all active spacecraft — over 14,073 satellites in a finite volume below 2,000 km altitude. The collision probability environment is increasingly driven by constellation density rather than legacy debris alone, demanding updated conjunction assessment frameworks.

Fleet Recency

90.5% of the entire active fleet was launched in 2020 or later. This recency means most spacecraft are early in their operational life, implying sustained orbital occupancy and a future deorbit surge when design lifetimes expire — a factor for long-term debris modeling.

Near-Circular Orbit Preference

99.0% of active satellites maintain near-circular orbits (eccentricity < 0.01), reflecting the requirements of both LEO constellations and GEO communications payloads. HEO missions remain a small but strategically significant niche.

Spectrum & Coordination Pressure

With 624 active GEO satellites and 142 MEO spacecraft (largely navigation constellations), the pressure on orbital slots, spectrum allocation, and coordination filings continues to intensify across all regimes — not just LEO.

Policy Implications

The data supports urgent attention to: (1) constellation licensing frameworks that account for fleet-scale deployment, (2) orbital debris mitigation requirements scaled to LEO density, and (3) international coordination mechanisms for the ~50 launch-year cohorts now simultaneously active in orbit.
