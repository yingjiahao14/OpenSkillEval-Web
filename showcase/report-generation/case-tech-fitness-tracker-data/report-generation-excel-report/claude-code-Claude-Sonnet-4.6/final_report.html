6Key Insights & Recommendations
Data-driven synthesis mapped to concrete product and engagement actions. Figures reference directly computed values from the dataset.

Key Findings

83.1% workout participation
High baseline — but with volatile swings
Participation ranged from 77.5% (2018 trough) to 86.6% (2019 peak) — a 9.1 pp swing. Both steps and participation hit minimums in 2018, indicating a cohort-wide disengagement event. 2025 shows re-acceleration (85.6%), but the pattern demonstrates the engagement floor is not stable.
+9.2% steps: Dec vs Jan
December & March lead; January & June lag
December (11,113 steps) and March (10,976) are the peak activity months. January (10,176) and June (10,267) are the annual lows — a holiday-surge/new-year-slump and summer-plateau pattern. Monthly workout participation mirrors this: August peaks at 86.5%, October bottoms at 80.1%.
5.64 avg stress in 2025 — 11-year high
Stress is trending upward; mood aligns
Stress climbed from 5.33 (2015) to 5.64 (2025). Running (5.36) and Yoga (5.42) users report the lowest stress; Cycling the highest (5.55). Mood confirms: Tired users average 5.58 stress vs Happy at 5.33 — a 0.25pt gap across all 4,000 records.
Thursday: 10,317 steps — weekly low
Mid-week dip: Tuesday peaks, Thursday dead-zones
Tuesday (10,799) is the best step day; Thursday (10,317) the worst — a 482-step (4.5%) intra-week gap. Weekend activity (Sat 10,537, Sun 10,445) is moderate, suggesting a weekday-dominant exerciser cohort where mid-week momentum losses are recoverable.
18–24 (10,853 steps) and 45–59 (10,656) lead
25–44 is the engagement gap segment
The 18–24 cohort records the highest avg daily steps; the 45–59 group also outperforms average. The 25–44 band (10,392–10,449) is the only segment below the global mean (10,574). This mid-career segment likely faces time constraints that the product does not adequately address.
608–622 kcal: tight calorie burn across all workout types
Workout type does not drive calorie differentiation
Despite Gym sessions being the longest (55.6 min avg), they do not produce proportionally higher calorie burn (615.7 kcal) vs Running's 55.0 min / 622.0 kcal. Duration effects are minimal — intensity, not session length, is the primary calorie driver in this cohort.

Product & Engagement Recommendations

R1
Deploy January re-engagement campaign
January records the year's lowest steps (10,176) and below-average participation (81.2%). Activate a new-year streak challenge and goal-reset wizard in the first week of January. Segment the push by users who were inactive in December — the ones who missed the holiday surge are highest churn risk.
R2
Thursday nudge feature
Thursday is the consistent weekly step floor (10,317 — 4.5% below Tuesday). A mid-week push notification ("Thursday Challenge") or a streak-break warning could recover 200–400 steps/user/week at scale. Prioritize Gym workout users, who show the sharpest mid-week dip.
R3
Stress & mood tracking integration
With stress at a dataset high in 2025 (5.64), proactively surface Yoga and Running recommendations to high-stress users — these workout types correlate with the lowest reported stress (5.42, 5.36). Linking in-app stress scores to workout suggestions would create a measurable feedback loop.
R4
Re-engage the 25–44 age segment
The 25–44 band (10,392–10,449 steps/day) is the only demographic below the global mean. A career-stage-aware onboarding flow — micro-workouts, desk-break reminders, 20-minute workout badges — would address this group's time constraints without requiring schedule restructuring.
R5
August premium upsell window
August has the highest workout participation rate (86.5%) in the full 11-year dataset. This is the optimal window for premium feature launches, annual subscription promotions, and social challenges. Users are at peak engagement and habit-formation receptivity — capitalize before the October participation dip (80.1%).
R6
Build a 2018-pattern early-warning dashboard
The 2018 cohort-wide disengagement (steps −9.2% vs 2016 peak; participation −9.1 pp) was the largest single-period decline. Monitor both metrics jointly on a quarterly basis: if either drops ≥4% YoY AND the other drops ≥2%, trigger a retention intervention. The co-movement signal is more reliable than either metric alone.
