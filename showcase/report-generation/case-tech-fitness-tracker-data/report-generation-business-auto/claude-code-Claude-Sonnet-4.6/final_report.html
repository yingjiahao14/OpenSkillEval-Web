Section 06

Key Insights & Recommendations

Steps Drive Calories — Not Session Length

Steps and calories burned are nearly perfectly correlated (r = 0.97). Workout duration has negligible correlation (r = 0.02). This means raw movement volume — not gym session length — determines energy expenditure.

Product: Prioritize step-count nudges and walking challenges over duration-based workout prompts. Daily movement streaks will move the calorie needle more than session tracking.

Workout Participation: 83.1% is High but Volatile

The cohort-wide participation rate is strong, but year-level variation ranges from 77.5% (2018) to 86.6% (2019) — a 9.1 pp swing. Low participation years (2018, 2020, 2021) should be studied for external drivers (schedule disruption, product gaps).

Engagement: Implement year-over-year participation tracking in dashboards. Flag cohorts dropping below 80% for re-engagement campaigns.

December & March are Peak Activity Months

December averages 11,113 steps/day — the annual high — and March is second (10,976). June is the lowest (10,267), a 846-step gap vs December. Workout rate peaks in August (86.5%) and March (85.9%).

Product Roadmap: Schedule major feature launches and challenges in March (strong momentum) or July (pre-August peak). Invest in June re-engagement mechanics to prevent the mid-year dip.

Stress is Trending Up in 2025

Average stress reached 5.64 in 2025, the highest in the entire 2015–2025 series. August is the most stressful month (5.79) while September offers the sharpest relief (5.11). The sleep–stress correlation is near-zero (r = -0.008), suggesting stress is not primarily driven by sleep in this dataset.

Feature: Launch a stress management module (breathing exercises, mood journaling) ahead of August. August-to-September relief period is a natural in-app moment to surface wellness content.

Gym is the #1 Workout But Running Drives Most Steps

Gym is the most common workout type (17.2% of records, 689 days), but Running users average the highest daily steps (10,721) and highest calories (622.0 kcal). Walking users have the lowest steps (10,353) despite a near-identical session length (53.6 min).

Personalization: Segment push notifications by workout type. Running users respond to step milestones; Gym users to calorie or PR targets. Walking users may benefit from route challenges to increase step density.

Young Users (18–29) are Most Active but Not Most Engaged

The 18–29 age group leads in avg steps (10,709) and calories (622.1), but their workout participation rate (82.9%) matches other cohorts exactly. The 40–49 group actually posts the highest workout rate (83.8%).

Targeting: Don't assume young users need activity prompts — they're already moving. Focus retention and habit-formation features on the 40–49 cohort, which shows the strongest workout consistency.

"Tired" Users Log More Steps — Counterintuitive Signal

Users reporting "Tired" mood average 10,749 steps — the highest of any mood category, outpacing "Happy" users by 391 steps. Tired users also carry the highest avg stress (5.58) and burn the most calories (622.3 kcal).

Insight: Tired may reflect post-exertion fatigue, not disengagement. Consider a recovery-focused notification variant for high-activity days ("Great work today — here's a stretching routine") rather than rest prompts.

Gender Parity Across All Key Metrics

Male and female users show virtually identical metrics: steps (10,580 vs 10,568), calories (617.6 vs 615.8), BMI (24.55 vs 24.62). Workout participation differs by only 0.9 pp (83.6% M vs 82.7% F). Male users report marginally higher stress (5.53 vs 5.37).

Product: Gender-segmented features are not justified by the data. Focus engineering effort on workout-type and mood-based personalization, which shows more differentiation.
