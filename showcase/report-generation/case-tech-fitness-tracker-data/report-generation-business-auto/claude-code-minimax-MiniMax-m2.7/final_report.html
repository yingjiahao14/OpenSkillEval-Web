6. Key Insights & Recommendations

Summary of Principal Findings

1. Workout Participation is High but Plateauing

83.1% of records include a workout. This is a strong baseline engagement metric. However, year-over-year rates hover between 77–87% with no clear upward trajectory. Implication: Focus on converting the 17% non-participants rather than pushing current participants to work out more.

2. Activity Levels are Stable but Sensitive to External Shocks

Average daily steps (10,574) have remained within a narrow band (~10,000–11,000) across all 11 years. However, 2018 (-5.4%) and 2020 (-5.3%) show notable drops correlating with potential macro events. Implication: Build push notification campaigns for "break the routine" challenges to prevent engagement decay during low-activity periods.

3. Running is the Most Calorie-Efficient Workout

Running yields 622 kcal burned per day (vs. 608 for Walking) despite similar durations (~55 min). Users who run accumulate more steps and burn more calories. Implication: Promote running programs for users seeking weight management outcomes. Offer cadence-based guided runs for Gym/Yoga users to increase calorie burn.

4. Sleep is Largely Independent of Activity

Correlation between steps and sleep is near zero (r = 0.022). Users with high activity don't necessarily sleep more or less. Implication: Sleep and activity should be marketed as separate health outcomes. A user who walks 15,000 steps may still need reminders to get 8 hours of sleep.

5. December Spike Presents Engagement Opportunity

December shows 8.2% higher steps than the annual average (11,113 vs 10,274 baseline). This suggests users are naturally more active during the holiday season. Implication: Capitalize on this momentum with year-end fitness challenges, activity competitions, or "holiday streak" features.

6. Mood and Stress Show No Meaningful Correlation with Activity

Stress levels are uniformly distributed and essentially uncorrelated with steps, calories, or workout type. Happy, Neutral, and Tired moods all show ~83% workout participation. Implication: Don't rely on mood-based segmentation for workout recommendations. Instead, focus on behavioral triggers (time of day, weather, previous workout type).

7. Gender Parity in Activity and Participation

Male (83.6%) and Female (82.7%) workout rates are nearly identical, with equivalent average steps. Implication: Gender-specific marketing or features are unlikely to yield significant engagement lift based on this data.

Recommended Next Steps

  1. Investigate the 2018 dip — Understanding what caused the 5.4% drop could inform crisis response features.
  2. Design December engagement campaign — Ride the natural activity spike with targeted features.
  3. Develop "first workout" conversion funnel — Target the 17% who never log a workout.
  4. Test running promotion — A/B test running recommendations vs. other workout types for calorie-focused users.
