Section 5 — HIGH PRIORITY

Temporal Trends & Seasonal Patterns

Year-over-year shifts and monthly/seasonal cycles in activity, wellness, and engagement metrics. This section drives roadmap timing decisions.

Note: Each year contains approximately 365 records (one per day). Yearly aggregates are daily averages — not cumulative totals. The dataset spans 2015-01-01 to 2025-12-13 (11 years, partial 2025).

Annual Average Daily Steps (2015–2025)

Steps peaked at 11,105 in 2016, dipped to 10,003 in 2018, and show a 2025 decline to 10,271 — the lowest since 2020. No sustained upward trend.

Annual Steps by Gender (2015–2025)

Male and Female steps cross frequently; neither gender consistently outpaces the other

Annual Workout Participation Rate (2015–2025)

2018 lowest at 77.5%; 2019 highest at 86.6%. 2025 on a rebound at 85.6%.

Annual Average Stress Level (2015–2025)

Stress shows a modest upward creep — from 5.33 in 2015 to 5.64 in 2025 (+0.31, ~6% increase)

Annual Average Calories Burned (2015–2025)

Calorie burn tracks steps (r=0.973); 2016 peak (639.7 kcal), 2018 trough (598.3 kcal)

Monthly Seasonality — Avg Daily Steps (All Years Combined)

December highest (11,113) and March high (10,976) vs June trough (10,267) and January low (10,176). Mild inverse-summer pattern with Q4 surge.

Monthly Workout Participation Rate

August (86.5%) and March (85.9%) are highest; October (80.1%) and January (81.2%) are lowest

Monthly Average Stress Level

August peaks at 5.79; September lowest at 5.11. Mid-summer stress spike is notable.

Day-of-Week Patterns — Steps & Stress

Tuesday highest steps (10,799); Thursday lowest (10,317). Weekend step counts are below weekday averages, suggesting weekday-driven activity culture.

Section 6

Key Insights & Recommendations

Data-driven findings synthesized into actionable product and engagement strategies.

🚶

Step Count is the Master Engagement Metric

Steps and calories burned have a near-perfect correlation (r = 0.973). Step count explains far more variance in calorie burn than workout type or session duration. Product implication: double down on step-count nudges, streaks, and daily step goals as the primary engagement lever — they directly proxy calorie outcomes better than any workout segmentation.

High Impact
🏋

83.1% Workout Rate — With Wide Seasonal Swing

Annual workout participation ranges from 77.5% (2018) to 86.6% (2019) — a 9.1 pp band. Monthly variation adds another 6.4 pp (Jan 81.2% vs Aug 86.5%). Recommendation: Time re-engagement campaigns to January and October lows; deploy seasonal challenge features in Q3 (Aug) to sustain momentum heading into historically weak Q4 start.

Engagement Timing
💡

Stress Rising — 2025 Hits 10-Year High of 5.64

Stress level has drifted upward from 5.33 (2015) to 5.64 (2025), a +6% increase over the decade. August shows a peak at 5.79. Recommendation: Launch or prominently surface stress-management features (guided breathing, mindfulness check-ins) in summer. Running users show the lowest stress (5.36) — a messaging opportunity to convert sedentary users toward aerobic activity.

Feature Opportunity
🌞

December & March Are Peak Activity Months

December (11,113 avg steps) and March (10,976) are the two highest step months. June (10,267) and January (10,176) are the lowest. Recommendation: Run New Year step challenges in January to capitalize on intent while lifting the lowest-performing month. Leverage December's naturally high activity with "year in review" social sharing to boost retention.

Seasonal Strategy
📈

Workout Type Mix is Flat — No Dominant Preference

All 5 workout types are used within a 1.3 pp band (16.0%–17.2%). This shows neither consolidation around a preferred mode nor meaningful segmentation. Recommendation: Rather than over-investing in any one workout vertical, deploy a "variety score" feature rewarding cross-type participation — users who diversify may serve as the highest-value cohort for subscription upgrade campaigns.

Product Design
💤

Sleep Is Flat — But "Tired" Users Are More Active

Tired-mood users log the highest steps (10,749 avg) and participate in workouts at 82.6% — only 1.3 pp below Happy. Sleep difference between moods is negligible (Happy 7.02 h vs Tired 6.95 h). Recommendation: Avoid assuming "tired" users are disengaged; they may be high-effort users needing recovery-focused content (sleep tracking, rest day scheduling) rather than motivation nudges.

Segmentation
👥

Demographics Are Uniform — Minimal Targeting Levers

Age, gender, height, and weight show minimal differential in steps (max gap across age groups: 488 steps, <5%) and workout participation (max gap: 1.0 pp). BMI is identical across genders at 24.6. Implication: Demographic segmentation is a weak targeting axis for this cohort. Behavioral dimensions (workout type, mood, stress level) offer stronger differentiation and should be prioritized in personalization models.

Personalization
✅

Tuesday Engagement Peak — Target Mid-Week

Tuesday has the highest avg steps (10,799) while Thursday is the lowest (10,317), a 482-step difference. Weekends (Sat 10,537, Sun 10,445) underperform weekday averages. Recommendation: Schedule push notifications and challenge prompts for Tuesday to reinforce the natural peak; introduce "weekend warrior" challenges to lift the lower weekend baseline.
