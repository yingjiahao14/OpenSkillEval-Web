Temporal Trends & Seasonal Patterns HIGH PRIORITY

Year-over-year shifts, monthly seasonality, and day-of-week effects across all key KPIs (2015–2025)

Average Daily Steps by Year (2015–2025)

Key observation: Steps peaked in 2016 at 11,105/day (+4.8% vs baseline) and hit a trough in 2018 at 10,003/day (−8.7%). A partial recovery in 2019 (10,909) was followed by COVID-era softness in 2020–2022 (10,335–10,425). 2025 (partial year, through Dec 13) is tracking at 10,271 — the second-lowest annual average.

Monthly Seasonality — Avg Steps & Workout Participation Rate

Seasonal pattern: December leads all months at 11,113 steps/day, followed by March (10,976) and July (10,829). January is the weakest month at 10,176 steps. Workout participation peaks in August (86.5%) and March (85.9%), and dips lowest in October (80.1%) and January (81.0%) — suggesting a Q4 fatigue / new-year lag dynamic.

Product & Engagement Recommendations

📅
Engagement · Seasonal

Deploy Jan/Oct Re-engagement Campaigns Tied to Step Drop

January and October are the two lowest-participation months (81.0% and 80.1% workout rate; Jan steps avg only 10,176). Pre-schedule push notification campaigns, streak-save nudges, and "get back on track" challenges to launch the first week of these months. Data shows March and August are natural recovery peaks — use them as campaign anchors for "Spring Fitness Push" and "Summer Strong" features.

⚡
Feature · Activity Monitoring

Build a "Low-Activity Alert" for the 27% Sub-7,500 Step Segment

27.1% of daily records fall below 7,500 steps (292 below 2.5K, 546 in 2.5–5K band). A feature that detects 3+ consecutive sub-7,500-step days and surfaces a personalized "light walking challenge" would directly address this long tail. The Tuesday–Thursday step trough (10,317 avg on Thu vs 10,799 on Tue) is a reliable weekly trigger window for mid-week activity prompts.

😩
Feature · Wellbeing

Introduce Overtraining / Recovery Warnings for High-Step + "Tired" Users

The "Tired" mood segment averages 391 more steps than "Happy" users while sleeping 0.07 h less and reporting 0.25 higher stress — a consistent overtraining pattern. A wellness score that combines mood, sleep hours, and rolling 7-day step load could surface proactive rest-day recommendations. Yoga practitioners show the lowest "Tired" prevalence (31.3%) and should be spotlighted as a recovery modality.

🏋️
Feature · Workout Diversity

Promote Running and Yoga for Stress Reduction

Running is associated with the lowest mean stress (5.36) while Yoga has the lowest "Tired" mood prevalence (31.3%) and the highest "Neutral/Happy" combined rate (68.7%). Both are already popular (641 and 675 records respectively). Feature cross-recommendations — e.g., "you've been cycling a lot; try a 30-min yoga session to balance your recovery" — could improve retention and engagement breadth.

📈
Product · Roadmap Timing

Prioritize Q4 / December Feature Launches — Not January

December is the peak activity month (11,113 steps, 635.7 kcal, 83% workout rate) — not January. New features released in late November/early December have the highest probability of organic adoption. January new-year resolution campaigns align with a structural dip in actual activity, making them better suited to re-engagement messaging than new feature discovery.

💧
Data Quality · Analytics

Investigate 2018 Workout Participation Anomaly (77.5% — 5.6 pp Below Mean)

2018 is a statistical outlier with the lowest workout participation rate (77.5% vs 83.1% mean) and lowest step average (10,003). This dip does not correspond to changes in sleep, heart rate, or stress — pointing to a data collection or instrumentation issue rather than a true behavioral shift. Validate whether device firmware updates, sensor changes, or cohort churn in 2018 explains this anomaly before relying on 2018 data for longitudinal benchmarks.
