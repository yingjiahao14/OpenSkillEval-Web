Investor takeaway: AXP’s long-run record is equity-like and strongly positive, but risk is not theoretical. The stock has experienced multi-year capital impairment, with the worst observed drawdown taking from 2007-07-19 to 2009-03-06 and not recovering the prior adjusted close until 2012-04-27.

Long-Term Price Trend Analysis

The multi-decade price series is best read on a logarithmic scale because AXP moved from low single-digit adjusted prices in the 1970s to triple-digit prices in the 2020s. The full-period CAGR of 10.81% masks sharply different regimes: negative performance in the inflationary 1970s, exceptional compounding in the 1980s and 1990s, a flat 2000s decade due to the financial crisis, and renewed growth after 2010.

Prices are adjusted values as provided in data.csv. Early OHLC and volume records may be sparse per the source documentation; close is the primary trend field.

Major bull and bear phases

  • 1972–1974 bear market: the local window fell -74.7% from the January 1973 local peak to the October 1974 trough.
  • 1980s–1990s expansion: adjusted close rose from $0.73 at the start of 1980 to $34.10 at the end of 1999.
  • 2000s lost decade: the 2000s returned -0.3%, with the global financial crisis dominating the decade’s risk profile.
  • Post-GFC compounding: from March 2009 to year-end 2019, AXP returned 1,294.2% at a 27.60% CAGR.
  • COVID trough to latest: from the March 2020 trough window to the latest record, AXP returned 379.8% at a 30.07% CAGR.

Methodology & Data Integrity Notes

  • All numerical values are computed only from /app/benchmark/data.csv.
  • CAGR uses calendar elapsed years between first and last trading dates in each period.
  • Annualized volatility equals the standard deviation of daily close-to-close returns multiplied by √252.
  • Maximum drawdown is calculated from adjusted Close versus the running prior high.
  • Dividend totals are simple sums of the Dividends column. No external dividend history, reinvestment assumptions, inflation adjustment, benchmark comparison, or fundamental data were added.
  • Early 1970s volume and OHLC fields may be sparse as documented in the source brief; close-price analysis remains the core trend metric.
