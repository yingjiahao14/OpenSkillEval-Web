Executive Overview

American Express has delivered one of the most compelling long-term equity track records in U.S. financial services. A single dollar invested in AXP on its first recorded trading day in June 1972 grew to $249.95 by March 2026 on price appreciation alone — a 24,895% cumulative return at a 10.81% compounded annual growth rate.


That trajectory, however, was punctuated by severe drawdowns: the 1973–74 oil crisis drove the adjusted price down 74.7% from its early 1973 peak; the 2008 Global Financial Crisis produced a peak-to-trough decline of 83.9%; and the COVID-19 shock erased nearly half the stock's value in weeks. Each episode was ultimately followed by full recovery and new all-time highs.


The stock closed at $305.99 on 11 March 2026, near its all-time adjusted high of $384.04 set on 11 December 2025. The 2020s have been AXP's strongest decade since the 1990s tech boom, with a CAGR of ~19.6% over the 2020–2026 window.

Trading Volume Analysis

Average Daily Trading Volume by Year (shares, millions)
Volume Structural Observations
  • 1972–1972: Volume data unavailable (recorded as 0). Earliest valid data begins 1973.
  • 1973–1987: Gradual rise from ~1.2M to ~7M daily avg. Reflects growing institutional participation.
  • 1987–1990: Sharp post-Black Monday volume decline as market confidence fell.
  • 2008–2009: Volume surged to 14.8M and 19.2M daily average — the highest in the dataset — driven by GFC panic selling and forced deleveraging.
  • 2010–2026: Secular decline in average daily volume from ~10.9M to ~2.8M, reflecting algorithmic and ETF fragmentation of order flow.
