Executive Overview

American Express delivered a substantial long-run capital appreciation record over the dataset: the closing price rose from $1.22 on Jun 1, 1972 to $305.99 on Mar 11, 2026. That equates to a 24,894.9% cumulative price return and a 10.81% compounded annual growth rate across 53.8 years.

Long-Term Price Trend Analysis

The long-term price chart is best read on a logarithmic scale because the stock moved from low single-digit adjusted prices in the 1970s to above $300 by 2026. The record shows several distinct market regimes: early-1970s collapse, 1980s and 1990s compounding, a lost 2000s decade shaped by the dot-com/9-11 and financial-crisis cycles, and renewed compounding from 2010 onward.

Major Bull and Bear Phases

Expansion phases. The 1980s and 1990s were the foundational compounding era: AXP gained 568.5% in the 1980s and 597.4% in the 1990s, with CAGRs of 20.9% and 21.5%. After the 2009 trough, the 2009–2026 period delivered 1,964.6% total return and a 19.3% CAGR.

Contraction phases. The most severe peak-to-trough loss was the Global Financial Crisis drawdown from $49.30 on Jul 19, 2007 to $7.93 on Mar 6, 2009. The prior high was not recovered until Apr 27, 2012, 1,744 calendar days after the peak.

Methodology & Data Integrity Notes

  • All calculations use only /app/benchmark/data.csv; no outside prices, benchmark data, inflation data, or macroeconomic figures are added.
  • Cumulative return is calculated as latest close divided by first close minus one; CAGR uses calendar days between those two observations divided by 365.25.
  • Maximum drawdown uses the running maximum daily close and the subsequent lowest close before recovery.
  • Annualized volatility uses close-to-close daily returns and a 252-trading-day annualization convention.
  • Dividend yield proxy equals annual recorded dividends divided by that year’s final available close; if a year is partial, the figure reflects only data present in the file.
  • The source documentation notes early OHLC and volume sparsity; volume comparisons involving the 1970s should therefore be read cautiously.
