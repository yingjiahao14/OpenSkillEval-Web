1. Executive Overview

Over more than five decades of daily trading data, American Express (AXP) has delivered a cumulative return of 24,895%, transforming a $1.00 investment in June 1972 into approximately $249.95 by March 2026 on a split-adjusted basis. This translates to a compounded annual growth rate (CAGR) of 10.8%.

The stock reached an all-time high of $384.04 on December 11, 2025, and its adjusted all-time low was $0.38 on October 03, 1974. The journey between these extremes has been marked by significant volatility — annualized at 34.7% — and a maximum drawdown of -83.9%, experienced during the 2008–2009 financial crisis.

AXP has consistently returned capital to shareholders through dividends, paying a cumulative $34.27 per share (split-adjusted) across 6 stock splits and 197 dividend distributions. The stock's long-term trajectory reflects both the resilience of the American Express franchise and the financial services sector's sensitivity to macroeconomic cycles.

Key Investor Takeaway: AXP has been a strong long-term compounder with a 10.8% CAGR, but investors must be prepared for severe drawdowns — the stock lost -83.9% peak-to-trough in the 2008 crisis. The 2010s and 2020s delivered the strongest performance, with the stock recently trading near all-time highs.

4. Trading Volume Analysis

Early data (pre-1980s) lacks reliable volume figures (recorded as zero). From the 1980s onward, AXP shows a clear secular increase in trading volume, reflecting broader market evolution — electronic trading, index fund inclusion, and growing institutional interest.
