Executive Overview

American Express (NYSE: AXP) has been a publicly traded financial services company since well before 1972, and this dataset captures its entire modern trading history. From an adjusted opening price of $1.22 in June 1972 to $305.99 as of March 2026, the stock has delivered one of the most compelling long-term wealth-creation stories in the U.S. financial sector.

The journey, however, was far from smooth. AXP experienced severe drawdowns during the 1973–74 oil crisis (−74.7%), the 1987 Black Monday crash (−45.9%), the dot-com bust (−59.1%), and most catastrophically the 2008 Global Financial Crisis (−83.9%). Each time, the stock recovered — often reaching new highs within 3–7 years — underscoring the resilience of its business model and brand.

Dividend income has been a meaningful component of total return, with $34.27 in cumulative per-share distributions. Yields were highest in the 1980s (~7.9% average annual yield) and have compressed to roughly 1.0–1.5% in recent decades as price appreciation outpaced payout growth.
