Key Takeaway

American Express has delivered exceptional long-term returns, turning a $1,000 investment at the start of 1972 into approximately $249,000 by March 2026—representing a 24,895% cumulative return. The stock's 10.81% CAGR over 53+ years significantly outpaces broad market benchmarks, though this performance came with substantial volatility (34.69% annualized) and episodic drawdowns exceeding 80% during major financial crises.

The "Lost Decade" (2000s)

The 2000s stands out as the weakest decade, delivering essentially flat returns (-0.03% CAGR) despite two major bull markets. The combination of the dot-com bust and the catastrophic 2008 Financial Crisis offset gains, resulting in a lost decade for buy-and-hold investors.

The Recovery Decades (2010s & 2020s)

The 2010s saw a strong recovery with 13.47% CAGR and significantly lower volatility (22.72%). The 2020s have continued this trend with 16.91% CAGR, though the elevated volatility (37.39%) reflects the COVID shock and subsequent recovery.
