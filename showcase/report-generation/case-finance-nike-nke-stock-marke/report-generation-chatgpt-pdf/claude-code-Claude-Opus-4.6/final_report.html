Executive Summary

Over the 26-year period from January 2000 to January 2026, Nike Inc. (NKE) delivered a cumulative return of 1,282.3%, translating to a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 10.6%. An investor who purchased $10,000 of NKE stock at the start of 2000 would have seen that investment grow to approximately $138,235 by the end of January 2026 — a powerful demonstration of long-term compounding despite multiple periods of significant drawdown.

The stock reached an all-time adjusted high of $166.25 on November 05, 2021, representing a roughly 37x appreciation from the start of the dataset. The deepest drawdown was -68.5%, occurring between November 2021 and April 2025, a decline from $166.25 to $52.31.

Of the 27 calendar years analyzed, 18 delivered positive returns while 9 were negative — a 67% win rate on an annual basis. The best year was 2013 (+53.8%) and the worst was 2022 (-28.2%). Annualized volatility stood at 31.2%, moderate for a large-cap single stock, with pronounced spikes during the 2008 financial crisis and the 2020 COVID-19 sell-off.

Long-Term Price Trend Analysis

The chart below traces Nike's adjusted closing price from January 2000 through January 2026. The trajectory reveals a long-term compounder with several distinct structural phases.

Structural Phases

2000 – Feb 2000
Post-Bubble Decline. NKE fell from $4.47 to an adjusted low of $2.47 (-44.8%), mirroring the broader market's post-dot-com correction. Despite being a consumer brand rather than a tech stock, Nike was not immune to the risk-off environment.
Feb 2000 – Dec 2007
Recovery and Re-Rating. From the early-decade lows, NKE rallied to $13.11 — a 431% gain. Strong international expansion and improving brand momentum drove a sustained re-rating of the stock.
Dec 2007 – Mar 2009
Global Financial Crisis. The 2008 crisis pulled NKE down to $7.73, a decline of -41.1% from the preceding high. The sell-off was sharp but relatively short-lived compared to many financials.
Mar 2009 – Nov 2021
The Great Bull Run. This was Nike's defining growth era. From $7.73 to an all-time high of $166.25, the stock delivered a remarkable 2051% gain. The rise was underpinned by digital transformation, direct-to-consumer strategy, and consistent earnings growth through the longest bull market in history.
Nov 2021 – Present
Post-Peak Correction. After reaching record highs, NKE has pulled back to $61.81 (-62.8% from the peak), reflecting a period of multiple compression amid macro headwinds, inventory challenges, and competitive pressures. The stock trades at its lowest level in several years.

Key Takeaways and Investment Implications

Long-Term Compounding Power: A 1,282.3% cumulative return over 26 years (10.6% CAGR) confirms Nike as a strong long-term compounder. Patient investors were rewarded through multiple market cycles.
Drawdown Discipline Required: The maximum drawdown of -68.5% underscores that even blue-chip compounders can inflict significant pain. Investors who sold during the worst decline would have missed the subsequent recovery.
Current Positioning: NKE is trading -62.8% below its all-time high of $166.25. For value-oriented investors, this may represent an opportunity — though the depth and duration of the current correction warrant careful analysis of fundamentals before commitment.
