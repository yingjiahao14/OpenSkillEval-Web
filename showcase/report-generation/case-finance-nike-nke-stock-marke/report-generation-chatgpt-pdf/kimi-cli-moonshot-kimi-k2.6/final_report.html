Executive Summary

From January 2000 through January 2026, Nike (NKE) delivered a +1282.3% cumulative return, equivalent to a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 10.6%. A $10,000 investment at the start of the period would have grown to approximately $138,235 by early 2026—before dividends.

However, the journey was far from smooth. The stock experienced a maximum drawdown of -68.5%, with the deepest trough occurring in April 2025 amid post-pandemic margin compression and competitive headwinds. Annualized volatility of 31.2% places NKE in the higher-volatility tier of large-cap consumer discretionary names, meaning investors were compensated with strong long-term returns but had to endure significant interim losses.

Key Milestone: NKE reached its all-time high of $166.25 on November 05, 2021, representing a ~37× multiple from the 2000 starting price. The subsequent decline to ~$61.81 by early 2026 underscores the cyclicality embedded in even the highest-quality consumer franchises.

Volatility and Risk Assessment

NKE's rolling 30-day annualized volatility shows clear regime shifts: elevated readings during the dot-com bust (2000–2002), the 2008 financial crisis, the COVID-19 crash (March 2020), and the 2022–2025 earnings-driven selloff. Outside of these stress periods, volatility typically compressed into the 20–25% range.

Figure 3. Rolling 30-day annualized volatility. Shaded areas denote periods above the long-term mean.

The daily price range (High − Low) averaged $0.88 over the full period, but spiked during crises. For example, the average daily range in March 2020 was $5.51—more than 3× the long-run average—reflecting extreme intraday uncertainty.

Risk Insight: While NKE's long-term CAGR of 10.6% is attractive, the 31.2% annualized volatility implies that a "typical" year's return could reasonably fall within a ±31.2% band around the mean. Investors must size positions accordingly and be prepared for multi-year drawdowns.

Key Takeaways and Investment Implications

  1. Long-term compounding worked, but patience was essential. A 10.6% CAGR over 26 years is respectable, yet investors who entered near the 2021 peak are underwater. Time horizon and entry valuation matter enormously.
  2. Drawdown risk is real and persistent. A 68.5% maximum drawdown is not a tail-risk scenario—it actually occurred. Investors must assess whether they can withstand multi-year losses of this magnitude without forced liquidation.
  3. Volatility clusters around crises and earnings shocks. The rolling volatility chart shows that risk is not evenly distributed. Tactical investors may use elevated volatility as a signal for position sizing; long-term holders should ignore short-term noise.
  4. Volume validates price dislocations. The largest volume days consistently accompanied extreme price moves. For fundamentally oriented investors, these spikes often marked capitulation or euphoria points worth monitoring.
  5. The 2022–2026 period is a structural reset, not a blip. With the stock down ~63% from its ATH and the 2020s decade CAGR negative, NKE is undergoing a repricing of growth expectations. Value-oriented investors may find opportunity; momentum investors should remain cautious.
Bottom Line: Nike has been a strong long-term compounder, but its risk profile is more aggressive than many assume. The combination of 31% annualized volatility and 68% peak-to-trough drawdowns demands disciplined position sizing, a multi-year holding horizon, and a willingness to add during severe dislocations rather than chase highs.
