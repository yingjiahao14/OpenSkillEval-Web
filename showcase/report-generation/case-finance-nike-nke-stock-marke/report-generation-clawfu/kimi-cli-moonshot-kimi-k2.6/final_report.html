Executive Summary

Over the 26-year period from January 2000 to January 2026, Nike (NKE) delivered a +1282% cumulative return, equivalent to a 10.6% compound annual growth rate (CAGR). This places Nike among the stronger large-cap compounders of the 21st century. However, the journey was far from smooth: the stock experienced a maximum drawdown of 68.5% from its November 2021 peak to its April 2025 trough, and annual volatility of 31.2%—well above the S&P 500 average.

The stock's trajectory can be divided into six distinct regimes: the dot-com bust and recovery, a mid-2000s growth phase, the Global Financial Crisis, a long post-crisis bull market, pandemic-era acceleration to an all-time high of $166.25, and the ongoing deep drawdown that has erased much of those gains by early 2026. For long-term investors, the key lesson is that NKE rewarded patience across multi-year horizons, but required tolerance for severe and sometimes prolonged declines.

Structural Bull & Bear Phases

Dot-Com Bust (2000–2003): NKE fell to an all-time low of $2.47 in February 2000 amid the broad tech collapse. The stock took nearly three years to establish a durable bottom, testing investor resolve early in the dataset.

Mid-2000s Growth (2003–2007): A powerful multi-year rally driven by international expansion and margin improvement. The stock climbed from roughly $3 to over $15 by late 2007.

Global Financial Crisis (2007–2009): NKE declined approximately 45% peak-to-trough—painful, but materially less severe than the broader market. The recovery began in March 2009.

Post-Crisis Compounding (2009–2015): A steady, multi-year advance characterized by consistent earnings growth, China momentum, and the early stages of digital transformation. CAGR through this period exceeded 20%.

Pandemic Acceleration (2015–2021): NKE surged to its all-time high of $166.25 in November 2021, fueled by direct-to-consumer strategy, e-commerce tailwinds, and robust athletic wear demand.

Post-Peak Drawdown (2021–2026): From the ATH, NKE has fallen into a drawdown of -68.5% as of early 2026, driven by margin compression, inventory challenges, and macro headwinds. This is the deepest and longest drawdown in the dataset.

Drawdown Profile

The maximum drawdown of 68.5% occurred between 2021-11-05 (ATH) and 2025-04-08 (trough). Prior to this episode, the worst peak-to-trough decline was roughly -55% during the GFC. The current drawdown is therefore exceptional not only in depth but in persistence: as of early 2026, NKE remains more than 60% below its 2021 high with no clear recovery base formed.

The highest single-day volume in the dataset occurred on June 28, 2024 (~130M shares), coinciding with a -20% single-day decline—likely an earnings-driven gap down. Other volume spikes include:

  • Feb 27, 2001 (~98M shares): Dot-com panic selling; -20% daily return.

  • Dec 19, 2025 (~109M shares): Sharp -10.5% drop amid ongoing drawdown.

A clear pattern emerges: the largest volume days are overwhelmingly negative price days, suggesting that panic and forced selling drive the most intense liquidity events.

Secular Volume Trends

Average daily volume has trended higher over time, rising from ~6M shares/day in the early 2000s to ~12–15M in the 2020s. This reflects:

  • Market microstructure evolution (decimalization, HFT).
  • Increased institutional ownership and index inclusion.
  • Higher retail participation via zero-commission platforms.

Notably, volume remained elevated throughout the 2022–2025 decline, indicating sustained distribution rather than a quiet bottoming process.

Key Takeaways and Investment Implications

For Long-Term Holders

  • Compounding worked: A 10.6% CAGR over 26 years turned $10,000 into roughly $138,000—despite multiple 30–50% drawdowns.
  • Time horizon matters: Returns were strongly positive over 5+ year windows for most of the dataset, but the 2021–2026 period shows that even multi-year horizons can deliver severe losses.
  • Volatility is the price: 31.2% annualized volatility means investors must size positions with the expectation of large interim losses.

Risk Considerations

  • Current drawdown is unprecedented: At -68.5%, it exceeds the GFC decline by ~20 percentage points and has lasted over 3 years without recovery.
  • Consecutive down years: 2022–2025 is the first 4-year losing streak in the dataset, breaking the historical pattern of quick rebounds.
  • Volume confirms stress: Elevated volume on down days and throughout the decline suggests institutional repositioning, not capitulation.
Bottom line: Nike has been a powerful long-term compounder, but its risk profile is closer to a growth stock than a defensive consumer staple. Investors should expect 30–50% drawdowns as a normal feature of ownership, size accordingly, and treat the current 2021–2026 episode as a reminder that even dominant brands are not immune to prolonged valuation resets.
