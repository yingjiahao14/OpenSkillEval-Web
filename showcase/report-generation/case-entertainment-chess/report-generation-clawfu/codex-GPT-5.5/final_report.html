Section 2 · Centerpiece

White vs. Black: The First-Move Advantage

Across all decisive games, White won 10,001 and Black won 9,107. That gives White a decisive-game win rate of 52.34%, a +2.34 pp edge over parity. The Wilson 95% interval is 51.63%–53.05%; a binomial z-test against 50% gives z=6.47 and p=9.97e-11.

Section 6

Key Findings and Strategic Takeaways

  • White has a real but modest edge: White won 52.34% of decisive games, with a 95% interval of 51.63%–53.05%. The result is statistically distinguishable from 50%, but the edge is small enough that rating gaps often matter more.
  • Rating differential is the most actionable KPI: the higher-rated player won 65.29% of decisive non-tie-rating games overall, rising to 86.2% once the absolute rating gap is at least 400 points.
  • Opening conclusions require frequency discipline: among the top 15 openings, Black outperformed White in selected lines such as Van't Kruijs Opening (64.2% black decisive), but these are descriptive outcomes influenced by player pool and time control.
  • Game endings are dominated by human decision pressure: resignations account for 55.6% of all games, checkmates for 31.5%, and timeouts for 8.4%. Coaches should analyze resignation timing separately from tactical checkmate patterns.
  • Time control changes the meaning of performance: timeout prevalence peaks in Bullet (<3m), so outcome analysis should not pool bullet, blitz, rapid, and classical without stratification.
  • For platform analysts: the most robust dashboard cuts are color × rating tier, signed rating gap bins, top opening names with minimum sample thresholds, and time-family outcome mix. These preserve interpretability while avoiding overfitting to sparse ECO/opening combinations.

Limitations: this report uses only columns present in data.csv. It does not infer engine accuracy, clock times per move, player identities beyond IDs, or causal effects of openings. Time-family labels are derived from the base minutes in increment_code and are approximate groupings for analysis.
