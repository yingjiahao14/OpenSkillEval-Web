1. Dataset Overview & Game Distribution

This report analyzes a dataset of 20,058 chess games played on Lichess. Of these, 80.5% were rated games. The analysis below breaks down how these games concluded and who emerged victorious.

2. White vs. Black: The First-Move Advantage

The "First-Move Advantage" is a cornerstone of chess theory. In our dataset, White won 52.3% of all decisive games (excluding draws). Below we explore how this advantage scales with player strength and time control.

3. Rating Dynamics and Their Impact on Outcomes

Elo ratings provide a strong predictor of match outcomes. The analysis below charts the win probability of the higher-rated player against the rating differential, confirming a strong monotonic relationship. Additionally, we observe that average game length tends to increase slightly at higher rating brackets, reflecting fewer early blunders and more resilient defensive play.

4. Opening Analysis: Popular Openings and Their Success Rates

We analyzed the most frequently played openings to determine their effectiveness. The stacked bar chart illustrates the outcome distributions for the top 15 openings. Notably, some openings yield heavily skewed results, providing actionable insights for opening repertoire construction.

5. Time Control Patterns and Game Characteristics

Time controls profoundly impact game dynamics. Speed chess (e.g., Bullet and Blitz) often leads to a higher proportion of games ending via timeout compared to longer rapid or classical time controls, where resignation and checkmate are far more prevalent.

6. Key Findings and Strategic Takeaways

  • The White Advantage is Real but Nuanced: White holds an overall 52.3% win rate in decisive games. This advantage remains relatively stable across rating tiers but shows minor fluctuations depending on the time control played.
  • Rating Differentials Strongly Predict Outcomes: A rating advantage of just 100 points translates to a win probability of roughly 58.5%. As the gap widens past 200 points, upsets become exceptionally rare, validating the Elo system's accuracy on the platform.
  • Opening Choices Matter: Among the top openings, performance varies significantly. Players looking to maximize win rates with Black should review the performance of common defenses against 1.e4 and 1.d4, as certain popular variations underperform compared to the statistical average.
  • Time Scarcity Changes Game Endings: In shorter time controls, the "out of time" victory status surges, shifting the strategic focus from pure positional/tactical superiority to time management and practical chances. In contrast, higher-rated games and longer time controls end more frequently in resignation, indicating a deeper understanding of lost positions.
  • Game Length Increases with Skill: The average number of turns per game correlates positively with the players' rating bracket, underscoring that stronger players mount stiffer defenses and carry games deeper into the endgame.
