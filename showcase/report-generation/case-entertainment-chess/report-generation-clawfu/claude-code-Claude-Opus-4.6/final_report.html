1. Dataset Overview & Game Distribution

The dataset comprises 20,058 chess games collected from the Lichess platform. Of these, 16,155 (80.6%) are rated games affecting player Elo, while 3,903 (19.4%) are casual. Player ratings span from 784 to 2,723 Elo, representing a broad cross-section of online chess skill levels.

The majority of games end in resignation (55.6%), followed by checkmate (31.5%). Timeout and draws each account for a smaller share. The relatively high checkmate rate (compared to professional chess) reflects the amateur-to-intermediate skill level of most players in the dataset.

2. White vs. Black: The First-Move Advantage

The perennial chess question: does going first matter? In this dataset, white wins 52.34% of all decisive games (excluding draws). While this is a meaningful edge, it's more modest than the ~55% often cited at grandmaster level — likely because at amateur ratings, tactical errors dominate over positional nuances.

Openings Where Black Outperforms White

Four openings in the top 15 produce better results for black than white:

Sicilian Defense (2,573 games) — black wins 52.0% of decisive games, the strongest black performance of any common opening. This aligns with the Sicilian's reputation as black's most aggressive answer to 1.e4.

Queen's Pawn Game (1,059 games) — black edges out white 50.8% to 49.2%. The broad "Queen's Pawn" classification here often includes non-standard or passive white setups.

King's Pawn Game (917 games) — nearly even at 50.1% for black, these are typically early deviations from standard 1.e4 lines where white doesn't develop aggressively enough.

Van't Kruijs Opening (368 games) — black wins a remarkable 64.2% of decisive games. This 1.e3 opening is considered passive and concedes the initiative to black immediately.

Opening Depth and Game Outcomes

Games with deeper opening theory (higher opening_ply) tend to be longer and slightly more drawn. Openings with 13+ half-moves of theory average 69.4 turns and a 6.9% draw rate, versus 58.3 turns and 4.6% draws for 1–3 ply openings.

5. Time Control Patterns and Game Characteristics

The dataset is dominated by Rapid games (10–29 min base time), which account for 73.3% of all games. Blitz (3–9 min) contributes 19.6%, Classical (30+ min) 6.4%, and Bullet (<3 min) a mere 0.7%. The single most popular time control is 10+0 (10 minutes, no increment) at 38.5% of all games.

6. Key Findings and Strategic Takeaways

Below are the most actionable insights synthesized from this analysis of 20,058 Lichess games.

  • Color White's first-move advantage is real but modest: 52.34% in decisive games (+4.68 pp). This edge narrows to just 50.6% at 2000+ Elo and inverts entirely in Bullet time controls (45.7% white WR). The advantage is most pronounced in Classical games (53.4%) and among 1200–1399 rated players (53.8%).
  • Rating Rating differential is the dominant predictor of outcome. A 200-point gap gives the favorite ~64% win probability; at 500+ points, the favorite wins 84.8%. Coaches and matchmakers should treat every 100-point gap as approximately a 7–8 percentage point shift in expected outcome.
  • Rating Higher-rated players play longer games. Average game length rises 36% from sub-1200 (50.0 turns) to 2000+ (68.1 turns). This reflects deeper calculation, more complex positions, and fewer early blunders.
  • Opening The Sicilian Defense is black's best weapon. In 2,573 games (the most popular opening), black wins 52.0% of decisive games — one of only four openings where black outperforms white. Its asymmetric, combative nature gives black genuine winning chances.
  • Opening The Bishop's Opening and English Opening are white's best-performing popular openings at 60.8% and 57.2% white WR respectively. The Ruy Lopez (55.7%) and Zukertort Opening (58.1%) also give white above-average results.
  • Opening Avoid the Van't Kruijs Opening (1.e3). With only a 35.8% white win rate across 368 games, this passive opening concedes all of white's first-move advantage and then some. It has the worst performance of any opening in the top 15.
  • Opening Medium-depth opening theory (4–12 ply) maximizes white's edge. White scores 53–55% in these ranges vs 50% or below in shallow (1–3 ply) or very deep (13+) openings. Investing in opening preparation up to about 6 moves deep yields the highest returns for white.
  • Time 55.6% of games end by resignation; only 31.5% by checkmate. The draw rate is just 4.7%, far lower than professional chess (~30–50%), reflecting the amateur-level play where decisive mistakes are common.
  • Time Time controls shape how games end more than how long they last. Classical games have the highest resignation rate (61.8%) and draw rate (7.8%), while Bullet has the highest timeout rate (15.3%). Surprisingly, Classical games are shorter in moves (54.2 avg turns) than Rapid (61.1) due to earlier recognition of lost positions.
  • Time Rapid (10–29 min) dominates the Lichess ecosystem. 73.3% of all games are Rapid, with 10+0 alone accounting for 38.5%. Platform designers and analysts should weight their analyses accordingly.
