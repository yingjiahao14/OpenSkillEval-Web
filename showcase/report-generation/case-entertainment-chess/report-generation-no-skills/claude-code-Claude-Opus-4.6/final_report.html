White wins 49.9% of all games, black wins 45.4%, and 4.7% end in draws. The most common way for a game to end is by resignation (11,147 games, 55.6%), followed by checkmate (6,325, 31.5%), timeout (1,680, 8.4%), and draw (950, 4.7%).

2. White vs. Black: The First-Move Advantage

Headline: Among decisive games (excluding draws), white wins 52.3% vs. black's 47.7% — a 4.7 percentage-point advantage for the first mover.

The first-move advantage is one of chess's most debated asymmetries. In this dataset of 20,058 games, white's edge is clear and consistent. Across all 19,108 decisive games, white converts 52.3% into wins. This aligns closely with historical norms (white typically wins 52–56% of decisive games in large databases).

2.2 White Advantage by Rating Bracket

White's advantage persists across all rating brackets, but the magnitude varies. The data shows that the first-move advantage is not simply a beginner phenomenon — it endures even at higher rating levels, though the exact magnitude fluctuates.

2.3 White Advantage by Time Control

Time control has a modest but visible effect on white's edge. In faster formats (bullet), the advantage tends to be slightly compressed, likely because tactical blunders and time pressure introduce more variance. In longer time controls, where players can exploit positional edges more carefully, the advantage can be more stable.

Both white and black rating distributions are approximately bell-shaped and nearly identical (mean white: 1597, mean black: 1589), confirming that Lichess's matchmaking pairs players of similar strength. 5.2 Victory Status by Time Control

Time control has a profound effect on how games end:

  • Bullet games see the highest timeout rate — with very little time on the clock, running out of time is a common decider.
  • Blitz remains resignation-heavy but with a meaningful share of timeouts.
  • Rapid and Classical formats see more resignations and checkmates, reflecting more deliberate play with fewer time-pressure losses.

5.3 Game Length by Time Control

Longer time controls produce longer games, as players have the luxury of deeper calculation. Bullet games average 44.7 half-moves, while classical games average 54.2 — a significant difference reflecting the quality and depth of play.

6. Key Findings and Strategic Takeaways

  1. COLOR White's first-move advantage is real and persistent: 52.3% win rate among decisive games. This ~2-point edge holds across all rating brackets and time controls. Coaches should emphasize opening preparation for both colors, but particularly help students build robust black repertoires.
  2. RATING Rating differential is the strongest predictor of outcome: A 200-point rating edge translates to roughly 70%+ win probability. The correlation between rating difference and outcome is 0.361. Matchmaking quality is high (mean |diff| = 173), but even small rating edges have outsized impact.
  3. OPENINGS Opening choice matters, but not equally: The most popular opening (Bishop's Opening) favors white at 60.8%, while Van't Kruijs Opening is black's best bet at 64.2%. Some openings partially neutralize white's advantage — Sicilian Defense, Queen's Pawn Game, King's Pawn Game, Van't Kruijs Opening.
  4. TIME Time control shapes the game's character: Bullet games are shorter (45 avg half-moves), more likely to end on time, and show a slightly compressed color advantage. Classical games are longer (54 avg half-moves), more decisive by resignation or checkmate.
  5. ENDGAME Resignation dominates, checkmate is rare: Only 31.5% of games end in checkmate; 55.6% end in resignation. Higher-rated players resign earlier and more accurately, leading to shorter decisive games at the top.
  6. HIGHER-RATED The better player usually wins: The higher-rated player wins 62.2% of games where ratings differ. While not surprising, this confirms that Lichess ratings are a reliable signal of strength — and that upsets, while possible, remain the exception.
Bottom Line for Analysts: Color, rating, and time control form a three-axis framework for predicting game outcomes. White + higher-rated + longer time control is the highest-probability winning profile. Opening choice modulates the color axis but rarely overrides the rating axis. For platform analytics, rating differential is the single most useful feature for outcome modeling.
