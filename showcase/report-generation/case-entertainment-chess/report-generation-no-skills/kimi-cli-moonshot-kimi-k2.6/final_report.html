1. Dataset Overview & Game Distribution

The dataset comprises 20,058 completed Lichess games spanning casual and rated play. Of these, 80.5% are rated, leaving 19.5% as unrated casual games. The average game lasts 60.5 half-moves (median 55.0), with white players averaging 1597 Elo and black players 1589 Elo — a near-even matchmaking profile.

Decisive games dominate: white wins 10,001 games, black wins 9,107, and draws occur in only 950 contests (4.7%).

Resignation is the most common termination method (11,147 games, 55.6%), followed by checkmate (6,325, 31.5%), timeout (1,680, 8.4%), and drawn games (906).

The rating distribution is roughly bell-shaped and centered in the 1400–1799 band, typical of a broad online player pool. Both colors show nearly identical distributions, confirming Lichess's balanced pairing algorithm.

6. Key Findings and Strategic Takeaways

  • First-move advantage is real but modest. White wins 52.34% of decisive games — a 4.7-point edge that is stable across rating brackets and time controls. The advantage is largest at intermediate levels (1200–1399) and compresses toward parity at 2000+.
  • Rating differential is the dominant outcome predictor. Below 50 points, the higher-rated player wins barely more than half the time. Above 200 points, the win rate exceeds 75%, and above 500 points it nears 88%. A 150-point gap is the practical inflection point for predictive reliability.
  • Resignation, not checkmate, ends most games. 55.6% of all games end in resignation versus 31.5% by checkmate. Coaches should emphasize middle-game evaluation and resignation discipline rather than purely tactical checkmate patterns.
  • Opening choice matters at the margin. The top 15 ECO codes cover the majority of games. While most favor white, a few openings yield near-parity or slight black overperformance. Deep opening lines (≥12 plies) are rare and show high variance.
  • Time control shapes how games end, not who wins. White's edge persists across all popular controls, but faster formats inflate timeout rates and slightly amplify white's practical advantage. Slower formats increase checkmate frequency and average game length.
  • Higher-rated games are longer and more decisive by mate. Average turns increase with rating, suggesting stronger players defend longer and convert more precisely. Pairing and coaching tools should account for rating-tier-specific game-length expectations.
Bottom line for analysts: Color advantage is a constant baseline (~52%), but rating differential is the lever that moves outcomes. Focus predictive models on the 150+ point gap threshold, segment by time control for termination-type forecasts, and treat opening ECO as a secondary feature with high variance in tail codes.
