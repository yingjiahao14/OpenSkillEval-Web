Win Probability vs. Rating Differential (White − Black)

Win probability follows a near-sigmoid curve. Even at zero differential, white wins slightly more often (51%), reflecting the first-move advantage. Each 100-point advantage shifts win probability by ~8–12 percentage points.

Average Game Length by Rating Tier

Rating Differential Distribution

Rating differentials cluster near zero — most matchups are reasonably even. The distribution is symmetric, reflecting Lichess's matchmaking system.

Section 06

Key Findings and Strategic Takeaways

Synthesised insights for coaches and platform analysts. Read this section alone for the full picture.

Finding 01 — Color Advantage

White's first-move advantage is real, persistent, and rating-sensitive

White wins 52.34% of decisive games — a 4.68 pp gap over black's 47.66%. This holds across every rating bracket and most time controls. Crucially, the gap narrows as skill rises: at 2000+ Elo, white's edge shrinks to just 0.6 pp above 50%, suggesting stronger players are able to neutralise the inherent imbalance. Coaches should note: beginner-level black players face a measurably larger structural disadvantage.

Finding 02 — Rating Differential

Rating is by far the strongest predictor of outcome — stronger than colour

The higher-rated player wins 64.6% of decisive games. When one player is 200+ points stronger, their win rate reaches 76–84%. A 100-point advantage is worth roughly 8–12 pp in win probability. This dwarfs the colour advantage (~4 pp). For any matchmaking or seeding decision, ELO differential is the dominant variable to control.

Finding 03 — Resign Culture

Lichess players overwhelmingly resign rather than play to checkmate

55.6% of games end in resignation vs. 31.5% in checkmate. Resignations peak in longer time controls (classical: 71% at 30+30) where positional clarity is higher. Checkmates are relatively more common in bullet. This has implications for endgame training: the data suggests most games are decided before the endgame phase begins.

Finding 04 — Opening Strategy

The Sicilian Defense is the most-played opening and the one most favourable to black

At 2,573 games (12.8% of dataset), the Sicilian Defense is the dominant opening — and uniquely, it is the only high-volume opening where black outperforms white (52.0% black win rate). Ruy Lopez, English Opening, Bishop's Opening, and Zukertort Opening all tilt 55–61% toward white. Players seeking black-side advantage at the club level should consider the Sicilian; white players should note that 1.e4 against a Sicilian statistically disadvantages them.

Finding 05 — Time Control & Parity

Faster time controls erode white's advantage and shift outcomes toward checkmate and timeout

Bullet games (≤3 min) show white win rates of 44–47% — below parity — and timeout losses peak at 21%. Classical games (≥60 min) show the highest white win rate (54.6%) and draw rate (9.6%). This suggests that time pressure is a great equaliser for black, while more deliberate play increasingly favours white's structural opening edge.

Finding 06 — Game Length & Skill

Expert players play longer, more complex games regardless of outcome

Average game length increases monotonically from 48 turns (sub-1000) to 68 turns (2000+). Draw games are the longest at 83.8 turns, while resignations end earliest at 53.9. Coaches should calibrate endgame training to skill level: beginner games rarely reach the endgame, while high-rated games frequently do.

Finding 07 — Van't Kruijs Anomaly

The Van't Kruijs Opening (1.e3) is a significant white-side trap — avoid it

With 368 games and a black win rate of 64.2%, the Van't Kruijs Opening (A00) dramatically underperforms for white. This is the largest negative delta for white of any high-volume opening in the dataset. Platforms offering opening recommendations should flag this as a suboptimal choice for white players.
