White vs. Black: The First-Move Advantage

Centerpiece result: excluding draws, White won 10,001 of 19,108 decisive games (52.34%) versus Black's 47.66%. That is a 4.68 percentage-point decisive edge. A binomial test against a 50/50 decisive baseline gives z=6.47 and p=9.97e-11; the 95% Wilson interval for White's decisive win rate is 51.63%–53.05%.

On all games including draws, White's record is 10,001 wins (49.86%), Black's is 9,107 wins (45.40%), and draws account for 950 games (4.74%).

Rating Dynamics and Their Impact on Outcomes

The most predictive KPI in the dataset is rating differential. The point-biserial correlation between white_rating − black_rating and White winning a decisive game is 0.367. Separately, the correlation between absolute rating gap and the higher-rated player winning is 0.229, with the higher-rated player winning 65.29% of decisive non-equal-rating games.

Key Findings and Strategic Takeaways

1. White has a real but modest edge. White wins 52.34% of decisive games, a +4.68 pp edge over Black, statistically distinguishable from 50/50 in this sample.
2. Rating gaps dominate color effects. Near-equal games give White 51.51%, but a 201–400 point White advantage lifts White to 78.70%; a similarly large Black advantage drops White to 24.53%.
3. Stronger games last longer and draw more. Average length rises from 50.0 plies below 1200 to 68.1 at 2000+, while draw rate rises from 5.13% to 8.01%.
4. Popular openings are not automatically good for White. 9 of the 15 most frequent exact openings favor Black in decisive results, including Van't Kruijs Opening and several Sicilian/Indian structures.
5. Resignation is the platform's main terminal event. Resignations represent 55.57% of all games, far ahead of mate at 31.53% and timeout at 8.38%.
6. Coaching implication: opening choice should be filtered by rating and color. For this dataset, players should treat first-move advantage as secondary to rating-matched preparation and avoid overgeneralizing from high-frequency opening popularity.
