Lichess Chess Game Analytics

Patterns of Victory, Openings, and Player Dynamics

This report provides a comprehensive, data-driven analysis of over 20,000 chess games played on Lichess. By examining patterns related to color advantage, player ratings, opening choices, and time controls, we aim to uncover the statistical realities of online chess and offer actionable insights for analysts, coaches, and players.

Dataset Overview & Game Distribution

The analysis is based on a dataset of 20,058 completed chess games, of which 80.5% were officially rated. The games span a broad spectrum of skill levels, time controls, and outcomes.

White vs. Black: The First-Move Advantage

The first-move advantage is one of the most studied phenomena in chess. In this dataset, across 19,108 decisive games (excluding draws), White achieved a win rate of 52.34%. This confirms a distinct, statistically significant advantage.

Notice that White's advantage remains relatively stable across rating brackets but peaks slightly in the middle tiers (1500-2099). Below 1200, the first-move advantage is less pronounced, likely due to a higher frequency of tactical blunders neutralizing the opening edge.

Rating Dynamics and Their Impact on Outcomes

We analyze how rating disparities influence game predictability and how player strength affects the length of the game.

As demonstrated in the probability curve, a rating advantage of 150-200 points pushes the expected win rate past 70%, and beyond 300 points, the upset probability drops well below 10%. Additionally, higher-rated players consistently play longer games, suggesting better defensive resilience and a tendency to reach endgames rather than capitulating to early tactical traps.

Shorter games (e.g., blitz or bullet without increment) logically feature a much higher proportion of 'out of time' finishes. Longer formats allow players to reliably calculate to a forced mate or a clear resignation state.

Key Findings and Strategic Takeaways

  • Persistent First-Move Edge: White secures a 52.3% win rate in decisive matches. This advantage is consistent but can be neutralized by deep opening preparation.
  • Rating Gravity: Outcomes are heavily deterministic at high rating differentials. A 150-point rating advantage yields a ~70% win probability, confirming the Elo system's predictive validity within this pool.
  • The Resilience of Stronger Players: Games in the 2100+ bracket average significantly more plies than sub-1200 games. Higher-rated matches are battles of attrition, whereas lower-rated games are frequently decided by early tactical blunders.
  • Black's Counter-Punch: Certain high-volume defenses statistically invert the first-move advantage. Players seeking to maximize Black win rates should explore variations that historically out-perform White in their specific rating bracket.
  • Time Dictates the Endgame: The prevalence of 'out of time' victories correlates perfectly with tighter time controls. Time management is an equally critical skill to positional understanding in fast increment formats.
