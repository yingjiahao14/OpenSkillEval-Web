1. Dataset Overview & Game Distribution

This analysis draws on 20,058 completed chess games collected from Lichess.org. The dataset provides a rich cross-section of online chess play, with games spanning the full spectrum of player skill levels, time controls, and opening repertoires.

Key summary: Of 20,058 games, 16,155 (80.5%) were rated contests affecting player Elo. 19,108 games (95.3%) produced a decisive result, while 950 (4.7%) ended in draws. Among decisive outcomes, the most common termination method was resignation (55.6% of all games), followed by checkmate (31.5%) and timeout (8.4%).

2. White vs. Black: The First-Move Advantage

The "white advantage" — the statistical edge conferred by moving first — is one of the most studied phenomena in chess. In this dataset, white won 52.3% of all decisive games, compared to 47.7% for black. This represents a +4.7 percentage-point advantage for white — a statistically robust confirmation of the first-move advantage in online play.

Headline finding: White wins 52.3% of decisive games versus black's 47.7% — a ratio of 1.10:1 in white's favor. This is consistent with the long-established ~52-56% white win rate observed across competitive chess history, confirming the first-move advantage persists robustly in online amateur play.

Key observation: Faster time controls (bullet, blitz) have significantly higher timeout rates. This is intuitive — with less time per move, the probability of flagging increases. 5.3 Game Length and Time Control

Longer time controls produce longer games in terms of moves, as players have more time to calculate and plan. Classical games average 50 turns, while bullet games average just 48 turns. This 2×+ difference reflects both deeper calculation in longer games and the fact that players in fast time controls often reach tactical conclusions more rapidly.

6. Key Findings and Strategic Takeaways

This section synthesizes the most important patterns discovered across all analyses into actionable insights. A coach, platform analyst, or competitive player should be able to read just this section and walk away informed.

Finding 1
White's Advantage is Real but Modest
Across 19,108 decisive games, white won 52.3% versus black's 47.7% — a +4.7 pp advantage. This confirmes the first-move edge but also shows that for the average online player, color matters far less than rating and tactical execution. White's edge is most pronounced in faster time controls and in games ending by resignation.
Finding 2
Rating is the Dominant Predictor
The higher-rated player wins 65.3% of the time when a rating gap exists. A 200+ point Elo advantage translates to a ~80.5% win probability. Rating differentials under 50 points produce near-even odds, confirming that the Lichess Elo system is well-calibrated for matchmaking.
Finding 3
Resignation Dominates, Draws are Rare
55.6% of all games end by resignation, compared to 31.5% by checkmate and 8.4% by timeout. The draw rate of 4.7% is far below elite over-the-board rates (~30%), reflecting the decisive nature of amateur online play where mistakes are frequent enough to produce winners in the vast majority of games.
Finding 4
Opening Choice Matters at the Margins
Among the 15 most popular openings, white win rates range from 35.8% to 65.5%. Notably, Van't Kruijs Opening is one of the few openings where black actually outperforms white (64.2% vs 35.8%), suggesting it may be an effective weapon against unprepared opponents.
Finding 5
Time Controls Shape Game Character
Faster time controls lead to shorter games and higher timeout rates. Bullet games average 48 turns with a 12.8% timeout rate, while classical games average 50 turns with only 4.3% timeouts. Players seeking to improve their tactical vision should favor faster controls; those working on strategic depth benefit from longer ones.
Finding 6
Higher-Rated Players Play Longer Games
Master-level games (2000+) average 68.1 turns compared to 51.7 turns at the beginner level. Stronger players fight longer in worse positions, convert advantages more methodically, and more frequently reach complex endgames rather than falling to early tactical traps.

Strategic Recommendations

  • For players: If you are rated below 1500, focus on tactics and blunder reduction — the rating differential data shows that avoiding one-move blunders is more impactful than subtle opening choices at this level.
  • For coaches: Use the opening analysis to identify which openings produce the highest win rates at your students' rating levels. Prioritize teaching openings where win rates are balanced, as these tend to be the most theoretically sound.
  • For the platform: The 52.3% vs 47.7% white-black win split is within the normal range. No matchmaking adjustment is needed, but color-alternation enforcement (ensuring players get equal games with white and black) remains a best practice.
  • For analysts: The relationship between rating differential and win probability (Figure 3.1) provides a benchmark for evaluating any rating system's predictive accuracy. Deviations from this curve could flag issues with rating inflation or deflation.
