White vs. Black: The First-Move Advantage

Across decisive games, white won 10,001 of 19,108 results, a 52.34% share. That is a 2.34% lift over parity and a 4.68% spread over black's decisive share.

Statistical signal: the 95% Wilson confidence interval for white's decisive share is 51.63% to 53.05%. A normal-approximation parity test gives p=9.97e-11, so the observed edge is unlikely to be random sampling noise in this dataset.

Rating Dynamics and Their Impact on Outcomes

Ratings are one of the clearest outcome drivers. In decisive games where players had unequal ratings, the higher-rated player won 65.3% of the time. The point-biserial correlation between absolute rating gap and higher-rated decisive win is 0.229; the signed correlation between white's rating edge and a white decisive win is 0.367.

White ratings average 1,596.6; black ratings average 1,588.8.

The longest games occur in the 2000+ average-rating tier at 68.1 turns.

Key Findings and Strategic Takeaways

White's first-move edge is real but moderate. White wins 52.3% of decisive games, with a 95% CI of 51.6%-53.0%. The edge is strongest around the 1200-1399 average-rating tier and fades toward parity in 2000+ games.
Rating gap is the most operationally useful predictor in this report. Higher-rated players win 65.3% of unequal-rating decisive games, and their all-game win share rises from 48.9% at 1-49 points to 86.5% at 600+ points.
Openings should be evaluated by observed player pool, not reputation alone. In the top 15 exact openings, 9 openings show black raw win rates above white. The largest black share among them is 61.4% in A00 - Van't Kruijs Opening.
Time controls change how games end. Common exact controls vary meaningfully in timeout rate, from 3.2% to 17.9% among the top 12. Longer base-time families show lower timeout rates and higher draw rates.
For coaching, prioritize rating-matched opening review before color narratives. Color matters, but the rating-gap curve is much steeper. A 200-399 point gap produces a 74.1% higher-rated all-game win rate, dwarfing the overall white parity lift of 2.3%.

Limitations: this is an observational sample from the supplied Lichess dataset. The report does not include engine evaluation, player history, move accuracy, or causal controls. All statistics above are computed from /app/benchmark/data.csv.
