6. Key Findings and Strategic Takeaways

The First-Move Advantage is Real and Consistent

White wins 52.3% of decisive games, a 4.7-point edge over black. This advantage holds across every rating bracket and time control analyzed, though it narrows slightly at the highest rating tiers where players are better equipped to neutralize white's initiative.

Rating Differentials Are Predictive

The relationship between rating differential and higher-rated player wins (r = 0.239 for absolute gap) confirms that even modest rating gaps translate into measurable win-probability shifts. Within 50 Elo points, games are near coin-flips (49.7%); a 100-point gap lifts the stronger player to ~64%; at 200+ points, win probability exceeds 75%; and at 400+ points, upsets become rare (<16%). Coaches should note that upsets are the exception, not the norm.

Resignation Dominates, but Checkmate Rates Vary by Time Control

Overall, 55.6% of games end by resignation vs. 31.5% by checkmate. However, in bullet/ultra-bullet formats, timeouts surge and checkmate rates drop, while longer time controls see checkmate climb above the overall average — players with more time convert advantages to mate rather than accepting resignation.

Opening Choice Matters — Some Defenses Favor Black

While white holds the aggregate edge, specific openings reverse the trend. Among openings with 50+ games, several ECO codes show black winning more often than white, suggesting that theoretically sound defenses can neutralize or even reverse white's first-move advantage when played by prepared opponents.

Game Depth Correlates with Player Strength

Expert-tier games (1800+ average rating) average noticeably more turns than beginner games, indicating that stronger players navigate deeper into endgames rather than falling to early tactical blows. Opening depth (number of book moves) also correlates with game length and outcome distributions.

Time Control Shapes Game Character

Bullet games are chaotic — shorter, timeout-heavy, with a slightly different white-advantage profile. Classical games are more "chess-like": longer, more draws, higher checkmate rates, and a more pronounced rating effect. Players choosing time controls should understand these structural differences when interpreting their own statistics.
