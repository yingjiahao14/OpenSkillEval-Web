6. Key Findings and Strategic Takeaways

1. White's First-Move Advantage Is Real but Modest

White wins 52.34% of decisive games — an edge of 4.68 percentage points. This advantage is equivalent to roughly 30–50 Elo points and is consistent across resignation (52.4%) and checkmate (52.9%) outcomes. It diminishes at higher ratings (50.5% at 2000–2200) and nearly vanishes in timeout games (49.7%), where clock management dominates positional factors.

2. Rating Difference Is the Strongest Outcome Predictor

A 200+ Elo advantage translates to a 64–84% win probability for the higher-rated player. In near-equal matchups (|diff| < 50), white's structural advantage produces a ~3pp edge. The asymmetry between equivalent positive and negative rating gaps suggests white's first-move advantage adds ~30–50 effective Elo.

3. Opening Choice Can Erase or Amplify White's Edge

The Sicilian Defense family (2,573 games) consistently favors black (49.5% vs 45.6%). At the ECO level, Van't Kruijs (A00) and Sicilian B20 see black win >56% of games. Conversely, the Philidor (C41) and Nimzowitsch (B00) massively favor white (>59%). For competitive players, opening selection is a lever: the English Opening (54.9% white WR) and Ruy Lopez (52.6%) are white's strongest weapons; the Sicilian is black's best counterargument.

4. Resignation Is the Dominant Game Termination

55.6% of games end in resignation, followed by checkmate (31.5%) and timeout (8.4%). Draws are rare at 4.7% — far below professional norms (~15%). Coaches should note: online players resign too early or too late, and improving evaluation of hopeless positions would boost both rating and game quality.

5. Time Control Shapes the Game Profoundly

No-increment formats (8+0, 10+0) produce the highest timeout rates (10–18%), while generous increments (30+30) push resignation to 71%. Higher time controls increase draw rates (4.2% Rapid → 6.5% Classical) and resignation rates, while reducing checkmates and timeouts. Platform designers should note that increment formats produce more "complete" games with fewer time-related terminations.

6. Stronger Players Play Longer, More Drawn Games

Mean game length rises from 48 turns (<1000 rating) to 77 turns (2200+). Draw rates climb from 2.2% to 12.9% across the same range. This reflects both deeper positional understanding and superior defensive technique at higher levels.
