1. Dataset Overview & Game Distribution

The dataset comprises 20,058 chess games collected from Lichess.org. Of these, 16,155 (80.5%) were rated games and 3,903 (19.5%) were casual/unrated. Draws accounted for 950 games (4.7%), leaving 19,108 decisive games for win-rate analysis.

Key takeaway: Resignation is the dominant endgame mechanism (55.6%), reflecting Lichess's culture of players conceding lost positions rather than playing to mate. Only 31.5% of games reach actual checkmate. With draws at just 4.7%, the dataset skews toward decisive outcomes — far lower draw rates than elite classical chess (~30–40%).

2. White vs. Black: The First-Move Advantage

Of the 19,108 decisive games, white won 10,001 (52.3%) and black won 9,107 (47.7%). This 4.7 percentage-point gap confirms a statistically meaningful first-move advantage — white wins approximately 1.10 games for every 1 game black wins.

2.3 White Win Rate by Time Control

Across the 10 most common time controls, white's win rate clusters tightly around 51–53%, with one notable outlier: 15+10 (55.3%) and 8+0 (53.1%) show the strongest white advantages. Time controls with increment (the "+N" component) tend to favor white slightly more, as increment benefits the player with the initiative who can generate threats without flag concerns.

4.4 Opening Depth and Game Outcomes

White's win rate increases from 45.7% at minimal opening depth (0–2 plies) to a peak of 55.6% at 5–6 plies, then gradually declines. Draw rates rise with opening depth (from 3.8% to 7.4%), and game length increases — deeper opening knowledge correlates with longer, more technically fought games. This suggests that players who follow established theory further into the game (5+ plies) reach more balanced, complex middlegame positions.

6. Key Findings and Strategic Takeaways

Synthesis of the data across all 20,058 games:

1. White's advantage is real, but not monolithic

At 52.3% across all decisive games, white's first-move advantage is statistically meaningful but modest. The advantage is conditional — it barely exists below 1200 Elo (35.7%, actually negative) and becomes dominant above 2100 Elo (72.3%). This gradient is the single most powerful finding in the dataset. For coaches: teaching beginners how to use the initiative is more impactful than telling them white has an advantage.

2. Rating differential is the strongest single predictor of outcome

A 100-point rating gap translates to roughly a 64% win probability for the higher-rated player; at 200 points, it rises to 75%; at 400+, it reaches 86%. This holds regardless of color, meaning a 100-point rating advantage overwhelms white's first-move edge. The practical implication for platform matchmaking: rating parity within ±50 points produces the most competitive games (near 50-50).

3. Opening choice can erase or amplify white's advantage

Several popular openings — particularly the Van't Kruijs (A00), Sicilian variations (B20/B27), and the Indian Game (A45) — systematically produce black-favorable outcomes with win rates 55–64% for black. Players seeking to neutralize white's edge can target these counter-attacking systems. Conversely, the Philidor Defense #3 and Scandinavian: Mieses-Kotroc are white-dominant (65%+), suggesting defensive weaknesses white can exploit.

4. Time pressure equalizes rating advantages

In Bullet, white's edge evaporates (45.1%) and timeout rates surge to 12.8%. Time trouble is the great equalizer — faster controls compress the skill gap, making outcomes more volatile. This is most visible in 8+0, where the 17.9% timeout rate (vs. a 4–10% baseline) shows that removing increment disproportionately punishes players who lag on clock management.

5. Longer games favor the stronger and the prepared

Average game length rises from 50.6 turns (sub-1200) to 65.9 turns (1800–2100). Classical time controls see draw rates climb to 9.8% (vs. 2.1% in Bullet). Deeper opening theory knowledge (5–6 plies) correlates with the highest white win rates (55.6%). For improvement: investing in opening preparation and endgame technique pays dividends in longer time controls.

6. Resignation culture dominates Lichess

At 55.6% of all game endings, resignation — not checkmate — is the modal outcome. This reflects a platform culture of efficiency: players concede lost positions rather than playing out forced mates. Combined with the low 4.7% draw rate (vs. 30–40% in elite OTB chess), Lichess games are decisively outcome-oriented. Timeouts (8.4%) are a meaningful but secondary factor.

7. The "rating improvement formula"

Synthesizing the data: to maximize win probability on Lichess, a player should (a) play rated games in the 10–15 minute range with increment; (b) target equal-or-slightly-stronger opponents (avoiding 200+ rating gaps against); (c) learn one white-favorable opening deeply (e.g., Scotch Game, King's Knight Opening, or C40 systems) and one black counter-attacking system (Sicilian or Indian Game family); (d) practice clock management, especially in no-increment formats where timeout risk is highest.
