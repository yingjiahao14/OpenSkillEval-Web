Key Findings and Strategic Takeaways

  1. Across 20,058 games, White wins 52.3% of decisive results (95% Wilson CI: 51.6%–53.0%).
  2. White’s edge is modest overall, but it varies meaningfully by time control and rating bracket (see the heatmap); small-sample cells are flagged by low game counts in the accompanying table.
  3. Rating gaps strongly shape outcomes: higher-rated players win more often as |Δrating| increases, with point-biserial correlation r=0.229 between |Δrating| and higher-rated win (decisive, non-equal ratings).
  4. Most games end by resignation; checkmates account for 31.5% of all games, and timeouts are concentrated in faster controls.
  5. Among the most common openings, several show Black outperforming White on decisive results—use this as a prompt to examine player-pool and selection effects (opening choice is not random).
