6. Key Findings and Strategic Takeaways

  1. White's first-move advantage is real but modest. White wins 52.3% of decisive games — a 4.7 percentage-point edge. This advantage is consistent across rating brackets and time controls, though it narrows slightly at higher ratings where draw rates increase.
  2. Rating differential is the strongest predictor of outcome. When white is rated 200+ points higher, white's win rate jumps to ~64%; when black holds that advantage, white's rate drops to ~34%. The correlation between rating gap and higher-rated player winning is 0.030.
  3. Resignation is the most common game-ending mechanism at 55.6% of games, followed by checkmate (31.5%) and timeout (8.4%). Only 4.5% of games end in draws — far below elite-level chess, reflecting the amateur composition of this dataset.
  4. Faster time controls amplify decisive outcomes. Bullet games see the highest timeout rates, while classical games favor resignation. Checkmate rates are fairly stable across formats.
  5. Higher-rated games are longer. Average game length increases from 50 turns at <1200 to 68 turns at 2000+, reflecting deeper calculation and more resilient defense at higher levels.
  6. The most popular opening is Van't Kruijs Opening (368 games). A few openings show black outperforming white — potential equalizing choices for black players.
  7. Opening depth correlates modestly with draws. Drawn games average 5.1 opening ply vs 4.9 for white wins and 4.7 for black wins, suggesting that deeper theoretical preparation may lead to more balanced positions.
