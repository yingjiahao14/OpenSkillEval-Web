White vs. Black: First-Move Advantage

Excluding draws, White won 10,001 of 19,108 decisive games, a decisive win rate of 52.34%. That is a 2.34 percentage-point edge over parity; a normal approximation to the binomial test gives z=6.47 and p=0.0000, with a 95% confidence interval of 51.63%–53.05%.

Key Findings and Strategic Takeaways

  • White has a real but limited first-move edge: White wins 52.34% of decisive games, a +2.34 pp lift over parity. Coaches should treat color as a small structural advantage, not a substitute for rating or clock management.
  • Rating gaps dominate outcome expectation: the higher-rated player wins 65.3% of decisive, non-tied-rating games, and the binned curve rises to 86.2% for 400+-point gaps.
  • Rapid games define the sample: Rapid accounts for 73.3% of all games, so platform-level conclusions are most representative of 10–29 minute base controls.
  • Resignation is the dominant endpoint: 55.6% of games end by resignation, implying many outcomes are decided before checkmate and are better modeled as advantage conversion rather than final-move tactics.
  • Opening popularity is not the same as opening performance: the most common named opening, Van't Kruijs Opening, appears 368 times, but the best top-15 White result is Philidor Defense #3 at 65.5%.
  • Longer recognized openings correlate with longer games: openings classified through 9+ plies average 64.1 turns, versus 56.7 turns for 1–2 plies.
Analyst note: all percentages and counts are computed directly from /app/benchmark/data.csv. Draws are excluded only where the KPI explicitly requires decisive-game win rate; otherwise they remain in the denominator and are labeled as such.

Data Notes and Limitations

The report uses the provided Lichess/Kaggle game file as-is. The created_at and last_move_at timestamps are present, but many exported values are coarse enough that elapsed duration is not used as a headline metric. Time-control analysis therefore relies on increment_code, and game length relies on turns.

Rating brackets use the relevant player rating for color-advantage tables and average player rating for game-length tables. Opening win rates are descriptive and not causal: player strength, transpositions, and selection effects influence opening outcomes.
