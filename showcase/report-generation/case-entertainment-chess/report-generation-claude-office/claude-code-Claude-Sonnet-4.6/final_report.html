Dataset Overview & Game Distribution

The dataset comprises 20,058 completed Lichess games collected via the Lichess API, with each row representing a unique rated or casual game. A strong majority—16,155 games (80.5%)—were rated, meaning outcomes affected Elo ratings. The remaining 3,903 (19.5%) were casual.

Winner Distribution

Resignation dominates: 55.6% of games end in resignation, compared to 31.5% by checkmate, 8.4% by timeout, and just 4.5% drawn. The low draw rate (4.7%) reflects the amateur-to-intermediate player distribution of this dataset—lower-rated games rarely reach theoretical endgame complexity.

White vs. Black: The First-Move Advantage

The first-move advantage is a foundational concept in competitive chess. Across the full dataset, white won 10,001 games (49.9% of all games) versus black's 9,107 (45.4%). Excluding the 950 draws, white wins 52.3% of decisive games and black wins 47.7%—a 4.6 percentage-point edge for white.

Rating Dynamics and Their Impact on Outcomes

Player ratings follow a bell-curve distribution centered around 1,597 Elo (white) and 1,589 Elo (black), with a standard deviation of 291 for both colors. The slight discrepancy in means reflects normal sampling variation rather than systematic bias.

Rating Distribution (White & Black)

Higher-Rated Player Win % by Rating Gap

Rating differentials are the single strongest predictor of outcomes. When two players are within 50 Elo of each other, the nominally higher-rated player wins only 48.9%—essentially a coin flip. At a 200-point gap the favorite wins 64%, at 300–500 points the figure rises to 79.2%, and beyond 500 points it reaches 84.9%. This near-linear progression confirms Elo's predictive validity at scale.

Time Control Patterns and Game Characteristics

The dataset is dominated by Blitz (6–10 min) games, which account for 57.2% of all games (11,478). The single most popular time control is 10+0 with 7,721 games (38.5% of all). Rapid formats (11–15 min) are second at 19.2%.

Games by Time Control Category

Victory Status Mix by Time Control

Timeout risk drops sharply with longer time controls. In Bullet (<3 min), 15.3% of games end in timeout. This falls to 9.4% in Blitz (3–5 min), 9.7% in Blitz (6–10 min), 6.3% in Rapid (11–15 min), and only 4.5% in Rapid/Classical (16+ min). Conversely, resignation rates are highest in longer formats (58.9% in Rapid/Classical), where players have more time to assess hopeless positions.

Draw rates rise with time: Rapid/Classical has a 6.6% draw rate—more than double the Blitz draw rate of ~4.0%. This mirrors the pattern seen at professional levels, where more time allows both players to navigate to balanced endgames rather than blundering into decisive errors.

Average game length peaks in Blitz (6–10 min) at 62.3 turns, then somewhat paradoxically dips in longer formats. This may reflect faster resignations in classical formats once a material disadvantage becomes clear, compared to blitz where players often play on hoping the opponent flags.

Key Findings and Strategic Takeaways

♟
White's First-Move Advantage is Real but Erodes at High Ratings
White wins 52.3% of decisive games overall, a consistent 4.6pp edge. However, this advantage is concentrated in the 1,200–1,800 Elo range (53–54%). At 2,000+ Elo the gap narrows to just 50.6%—within statistical noise for the sample size. This confirms that opening preparation, which neutralizes white's initiative, is a key skill separator at higher ratings. Platform design interventions (e.g., opening training tools) targeting players in the 1,400–1,800 range would have the most measurable impact on evening color-based outcomes.
📊
Rating Differential is the Dominant Outcome Predictor
The higher-rated player's win probability scales almost linearly from 48.9% (0–50 point gap) to 84.9% (500+ point gap). A 200-point gap already confers a 64% win probability. This validates Elo as a strong predictor in this dataset and confirms that matchmaking quality—not opening choice—drives most outcome variance. For coaches: players at a significant rating disadvantage should focus on time-management and practical fighting chances rather than theoretical preparation.
📖
Opening Choice Matters: Several Popular Choices Are Strategically Unsound for White
The Van't Kruijs Opening (1.e3) is the most counterproductive choice for white among popular openings—black wins 61.4% of these games, a 27.2pp deficit for white. Similarly, the Sicilian Defense (black wins 54.2%) and Indian Game (55.2%) heavily favor black. Conversely, Philidor Defense #3 and Scandinavian: Mieses-Kotroc Variation produce exceptional white results (64–65% win rates), though the underlying cause may be that these openings are played most often when white has a significant rating advantage over the opponent.
⏱
Bullet Chess Has Unique Dynamics; Timeout Is a Major Risk Factor
Bullet games (<3 min) show 15.3% timeout rate—nearly 3× the rapid rate—and black actually wins more often than white in this format (unusual reversal). Blitz (6–10 min) is the clear modal format at 57.2% of all games. Players who want to practice opening theory in time-pressure conditions are best served by 5+5 or 10+0 formats, where flag-related noise is minimized but games remain tactical enough to generate actionable feedback.
🎯
Resignation Dominates; Checkmates Require ~20% More Moves
55.6% of games end by resignation, and only 31.5% by checkmate. Resigned games average 53.9 turns, while checkmate games require 65.4 turns—a 21% premium. Drawn games stretch to 83.8 turns. This means the ability to press and convert a won endgame (rather than accept resignation) is a meaningful skill differentiator above ~1,800 Elo. Game length also increases strongly with skill level (+41% from <1,000 to 2,000+ Elo), confirming that mistake-reduction—not pattern complexity—is the key factor in game duration.
