The existence of a 'first-move advantage' is a long-standing topic in chess. In this dataset, excluding drawn games, White won 52.34% of games, while Black won 47.66%. Key Findings and Strategic Takeaways

1. The First-Move Advantage is Real but Marginal Across over 20,000 games, White holds a 52.3% win rate when excluding draws. This advantage appears slightly more pronounced in lower rating brackets, where opening principles play an outsized role before blunders occur.
2. Rating Differential is Destiny Above 200 Points The probability of the higher-rated player winning scales linearly up to a 150-point difference. However, once the rating gap exceeds 200 points, the favored player wins over 72% of the time, making upsets statistically rare.
3. Game Durability Increases with Skill Players rated over 2000 average roughly 10-15 more turns per game compared to players under 1200. Higher-level games are less likely to end in early mating nets or rapid blunders, pushing the action into deeper endgames.
4. Black Can Fight Back in the Opening Phase While White generally leads in win rates, Black actually outperforms White in specific popular setups like Van't Kruijs Opening, Sicilian Defense, showing that solid preparation as Black can neutralize the initial disadvantage.
5. Time Control Dictates the Endgame Type Blitz time controls without increment (e.g., 10+0, 5+0) exhibit a massive spike in 'out of time' finishes. Conversely, increment time controls (like 15+2 or 10+5) afford players the time to either find a checkmate or realize their position is lost and resign.
