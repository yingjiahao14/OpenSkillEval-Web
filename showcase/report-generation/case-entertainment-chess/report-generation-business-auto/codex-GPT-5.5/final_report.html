Dataset Overview & Game Distribution

The dataset contains 20,058 completed Lichess games, with 80.5% rated play and a broad player-rating range from 784 to 2,723. The central tendency is mid-club level: the combined player-rating median is 1564.

Outcome distribution is decisive-heavy: 19,108 games (95.3%) ended with a white or black winner, while 950 (4.7%) were draws. Resignation dominates the end-state mix at 55.6%, followed by checkmate at 31.5% and timeout at 8.4%.

Centerpiece analysis

White vs. Black: The First-Move Advantage

White wins 52.34% of decisive games versus black's 47.66%, a 4.68 percentage-point decisive-game gap. A binomial test against a 50/50 decisive baseline gives z = 6.47 and p < 0.001; the 95% confidence interval for white's decisive win rate is 51.63%–53.05%.

Interpretation: the first-move edge is statistically meaningful in this sample, but not uniform. It narrows at the highest rating tier and reverses in the small ultra/hyperbullet slice.

By rating tier, white's decisive win rate moves from 52.7% in games averaging below 1200 to 50.6% above 2000. That pattern suggests the raw first-move signal is strongest in lower-to-mid pools and attenuates among stronger players. By time control, white is strongest in classical games at 53.9%, while the 127 decisive ultra/hyperbullet games show white at only 45.7%; because that cell is small, it is best treated as directional rather than definitive.

White's edge appears similarly in checkmates and resignations: white accounts for 52.9% of mates and 52.4% of resignations. Timeout games are closer to balanced, with white at 48.4% and black at 49.0%.

Rating Dynamics and Their Impact on Outcomes

Rating differential is the strongest practical driver in the report. The higher-rated player wins 62.2% of non-equal-rating games, and the relationship strengthens as gaps widen: from 48.9% at gaps below 50 points to 92.5% at 800+ points.

The signed-rating curve is steep: when white is ahead by 400+ points, white wins 88.1% of decisive games; when black is ahead by 400+ points, white's win probability falls to 15.9%. The point-biserial correlation between signed white rating advantage and white winning is 0.367; the correlation between absolute rating gap and higher-rated-player success is 0.225, reflecting a nonlinear but clearly monotonic effect.

Executive synthesis

Key Findings and Strategic Takeaways

White's advantage is real but modest. White wins 52.3% of decisive games, a 4.7 percentage-point gap over black, with strong statistical support from 19,108 decisive games.
Rating gap overwhelms color. A 400+ point advantage pushes decisive win probability above 82.1% for the higher-rated player, compared with only 52.3% for white overall.
The white edge fades among stronger pools. White scores 50.6% above 2000 average rating, compared with 53.8% in the 1200–1399 bracket.
Opening popularity can conceal risk. The most frequent opening, Van't Kruijs Opening, is black-favorable in this sample, while Philidor Defense #3 is the strongest white scorer among the top 15.
Game endings reflect skill and clock pressure. Checkmate rates fall sharply with rating, while ultra/hyperbullet games show the highest timeout rate at 15.3%.
For coaching and platform analytics, segment first. The same aggregate KPI changes materially by rating bracket, time control, and rating gap; conclusions based only on overall win rates risk masking the main drivers.
