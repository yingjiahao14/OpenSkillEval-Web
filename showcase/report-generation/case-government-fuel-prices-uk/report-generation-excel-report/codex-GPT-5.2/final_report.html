Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Median prices vary substantially by fuel type: E10 is lowest (median 144.90 ppl) while HVO is highest (median 189.90 ppl), a median gap of 45.00 ppl.
  2. Within-fuel dispersion is material: B10 has the widest typical price band (IQR 48.80 ppl). The widest overall max–min range is in E5 (1889.00 ppl), indicating large headline disparities driven by outliers and/or data quality issues.
  3. Reported price freshness is generally high at the centre of the distribution (median lag 0.048 hours ≈ 2.9 minutes), but a non-trivial tail exists: 10.53% of records lag by >24h and 3.60% lag by >7 days.
  4. Potential timestamp consistency issues appear in 1,011 records where `source_updated_at` is later than `recorded_at` (negative lag up to ~1 hour); freshness KPIs clip these to 0 to avoid nonsensical negative values.
  5. Fuel availability is uneven: standard fuels dominate coverage (unique stations: B7_STANDARD 6,494, E10 6,244) while alternatives are rare (HVO 54, B10 34), limiting like-for-like consumer comparison for niche fuels.
Suggested regulatory focus: (1) use IQR-based dispersion as a stable competitiveness indicator; (2) treat max–min spreads as a screening metric requiring outlier validation; (3) monitor tail-lag shares and station-level stale clusters as potential compliance risks.
Methodological notes: Prices are analysed in pence per litre as provided. Update lag is computed as (`recorded_at` − `source_updated_at`) in hours; negative values are clipped to 0 for freshness KPIs to avoid nonsensical negative lags while keeping the records visible for data-quality monitoring. This report is computed strictly from `/app/benchmark/data.csv` with no imputation or fabricated values.
