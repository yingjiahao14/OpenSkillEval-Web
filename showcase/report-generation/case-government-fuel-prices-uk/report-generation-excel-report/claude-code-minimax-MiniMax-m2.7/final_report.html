6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

1. B7_STANDARD dominates market reporting with widest station coverage

B7_STANDARD (standard diesel) has the highest station coverage (6,494 stations) and highest record volume, making it the most reliable fuel type for market analysis. Policy analysts should note this fuel type provides the most robust dataset for ongoing monitoring.

2. Significant price spread exists across all fuel types

Price ranges vary from 383.0 ppl (E10) to 1889.0 ppl (B7_PREMIUM). This spread represents meaningful potential savings for consumers who can choose between stations. The IQR for B7_PREMIUM (30.0 ppl) indicates typical consumer savings opportunities of several pounds per refill.

3. Premium fuels (B7_PREMIUM, B10) show highest price variability

Both B7_PREMIUM (mean: 186.16 ppl, std: 28.73) and B10 (mean: 163.40 ppl, std: 114.65) exhibit higher price variability than standard fuels. This may reflect less competitive market dynamics or greater retailer discretion in premium fuel pricing.

4. Median update lag of 0.05 hours indicates generally prompt reporting

Most retailers update their prices within 3 minutes of changes. However, the 95th percentile lag of 106.4 hours and maximum lag of 1263.6 hours (52.6 days) raise compliance concerns for mandated real-time reporting.

5. E10 offers lowest mean price (145.90 ppl) with tightest price clustering

E10 (standard unleaded) is both the cheapest fuel type on average and shows the smallest IQR (16.00 ppl), indicating more uniform pricing across stations. This may reflect a more competitive retail market for the most common petrol grade.

6. Station fuel type diversity is limited — most stations report only 2-3 fuel types

4,666 stations (63.3%) report only 2-3 fuel types. This limits consumer choice and may indicate gaps in the reporting mandate's coverage of premium or alternative fuels.
