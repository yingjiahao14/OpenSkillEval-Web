Data integrity notes

Prices are stored in pence per litre. The dataset contains 129 records (0.258%) outside a plausible retail band of 80–250 PPL (including 0–1 PPL and ~1000+ PPL values). These are treated as likely data-entry/unit issues and are excluded from the price distribution charts and the “clean” summary table below, but retained in the raw summary for transparency.

1,011 records (2.02%) show a negative update lag (recorded before source_updated_at), up to ~60 minutes. This is consistent with clock skew, ingestion ordering, or timestamp granularity differences; these records are included but flagged in the freshness analysis.

Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Price levels differ materially by fuel type. Within the plausible retail band (80–250 PPL), the mean is lowest for E10 (145.20 PPL) and highest for HVO (187.44 PPL). Implication: consumer-facing communications and comparisons should remain fuel-type specific; pooling fuel types obscures the true competitive picture.
  2. Competitiveness signals vary by fuel type. Among common grades, IQR spans from roughly 16 PPL (E10) to 33 PPL (B7_STANDARD). Implication: diesel markets appear to exhibit a wider “typical” consumer price band than E10, warranting monitoring for local market power and transparency effectiveness.
  3. Extreme raw-price outliers exist and should be monitored as data-quality risks. 129 records (0.258%) fall outside 80–250 PPL, including near-zero values and ~1000+ PPL values. Implication: compliance/QA checks (e.g., plausibility filters) are important for maintaining trust in open reporting and avoiding consumer-facing misinformation.
  4. Retailer updates are typically very fresh, but the tail is policy-relevant. The median lag is 0.048 hours (~2.9 minutes), yet 1,802 records (3.60%) show >7 days lag. Implication: enforcement and consumer protection may need to focus on persistent laggards rather than broad-based interventions.
  5. Station-level staleness is concentrated. Using median lag per station, 342 stations exceed 24 hours and 133 exceed 7 days. Implication: targeted outreach and compliance checks could be prioritized on these stations to improve overall data freshness.
