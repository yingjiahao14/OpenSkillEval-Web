Stale data: 3,035 records (6.1%) have a reporting lag exceeding 72 hours, affecting 2,143 unique stations. Additionally, 1,011 records (2.0%) show negative lag (source updated after recording), which may indicate pre-fetched timestamps or clock synchronisation issues.

Compliance Concern: Stations with persistently stale data may not be meeting their reporting obligations under the UK open data mandate. A lag exceeding 72 hours likely means the displayed price is no longer accurate for consumer decision-making.

6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. E10 and diesel (B7) dominate the market, accounting for the majority of records and station coverage. These are the fuels most relevant to consumer protection policy, as they are purchased by the vast majority of motorists.
  2. Super unleaded (E5) carries a significant premium — averaging 162.7p/litre compared to 145.9p for E10. The price spread for E5 is also wider, suggesting less competitive pressure in the premium fuel segment.
  3. The IQR for mainstream fuels is relatively tight (16.0p for E10, 33.0p for diesel), indicating that most consumers encounter broadly similar prices. However, the full max-min range is substantially larger, pointing to a tail of outlier stations.
  4. Reporting freshness is a concern. The median update lag of 0.0 hours is acceptable, but 6.1% of records have lags exceeding 72 hours. This undermines the value of the open data mandate for real-time consumer price comparison.
  5. Station coverage varies by fuel type. Not all stations report all fuel types, which creates blind spots in the data. Policy-makers should consider whether reporting requirements should be tightened to ensure comprehensive fuel type coverage.
  6. Data integrity is generally strong, with no missing values detected. However, the presence of negative lag values warrants investigation into timestamp handling in the data collection pipeline.
Recommendations for Policy: (1) Investigate and engage stations with persistently stale reporting. (2) Consider mandating maximum acceptable reporting lag thresholds. (3) Monitor the outlier tail of fuel prices to identify potential anti-competitive pricing. (4) Expand data schema to include station brand and location for geographic analysis.
