UK Retail Fuel Price Trend Analysis — February 2026

A comprehensive analysis of retail fuel pricing patterns across UK forecourts, examining price distributions by fuel type and temporal update behaviour based on government-mandated open data.

Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Standard unleaded was the lowest-priced major fuel by median price. Among fuels with at least 1,000 February records, E10 had a median of 132.0p/litre, while B7_PREMIUM was highest at 163.9p/litre. This confirms material grade-level price differentiation that consumer-facing tools should make prominent.
  2. Typical price dispersion is policy-relevant even before considering extreme outliers. The widest IQR among major fuels was E5 at 12.1p/litre, indicating that consumers commonly faced a meaningful price band within the same fuel category.
  3. Extreme max–min ranges are dominated by apparent anomalous price records. February contains 91 records below 100p or above 250p per litre; these are retained in the statistics because no values were fabricated or removed, but they should be investigated before enforcement conclusions are drawn from maximum or minimum prices.
  4. Reported-price freshness is uneven. Median lag between retailer source update and dataset recording was 27.36 hours; 4,173 records (50.3%) exceeded 24 hours and 1,764 (21.3%) exceeded seven days, which is relevant to transparency and compliance monitoring.
  5. Fuel coverage differs sharply by product. B7_STANDARD was reported by 2,096 stations, while B10 appeared at only 16; niche products should not be interpreted as market-wide indicators.

Price Spread & Market Competitiveness

The IQR is the most reliable indicator of the typical consumer-facing price band because it is less affected by erroneous or highly unusual values. The max–min range is still shown because it captures the full extent of reported disparities and potential data-quality or compliance issues.

Major-fuel IQRs range from 5.1p/litre to 12.1p/litre. E5 has the widest typical band, while max–min ranges are much larger because February includes apparent anomalous records such as prices at or near 0–1p/litre and prices above £10/litre. For policy analysis, the IQR should be prioritised for competitiveness assessment and the extremes should trigger data validation.

Methodology & Limitations

All statistics are derived directly from /app/benchmark/data.csv. Prices are reported in pence per litre. Records were filtered to February 2026 using UTC recorded_at timestamps. No price values were imputed, winsorised, or removed; apparent outliers are disclosed rather than corrected.

Key limitations are the absence of station geography, retailer brand, and forecourt address fields, plus the use of a 50,000-record extract rather than the full dataset referenced by the documentation. These constraints prevent area-level price comparisons and brand-level competitiveness conclusions.
