6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Significant price variation exists within each fuel type, creating a consumer penalty for uninformed purchasing.
    E10 prices span 1358.0 ppl across forecourts, equating to a potential difference of £746.90 on a standard 55-litre fill. For B7 Standard Diesel, the range is 1878.0 ppl (£1032.90 per fill). This disparity underscores the value of price comparison tools and the need for accurate, timely data to support consumer choice.
  2. Premium fuel carries a substantial markup that may not always be transparent to consumers.
    B7 Premium Diesel averages 186.2 ppl — 18.8 ppl more than B7 Standard. E5 Super Unleaded averages 162.7 ppl, 16.8 ppl above E10. Without clear signage and comparison tools, consumers may inadvertently select premium grades at significantly higher cost.
  3. Update lag is a material concern for data quality and consumer trust.
    The median update lag is 0.0 hours, but 7.4% of records are more than 48 hours stale. This means consumers using price comparison platforms may be acting on outdated information. Retailers with persistent reporting delays should be flagged and potentially subject to compliance enforcement.
  4. The IQR is relatively narrow, suggesting most stations price within a competitive band — but outliers are common.
    The IQR for E10 is 16.0 ppl (11.0% of mean), indicating that the central 50% of stations price within a 16.0 ppl band. However, the long tails visible in the distribution plots reveal stations charging well above the competitive norm, potentially exploiting local market power or captive audiences (e.g., motorway services, remote areas).
  5. Fuel type coverage is uneven across forecourts, with specialty fuels under-reported.
    While E10 and B7 Standard are reported by the vast majority of stations, premium and biodiesel variants have substantially lower coverage. This may reflect genuine limited availability, but could also indicate gaps in retailer reporting compliance that merit regulatory attention.
  6. Policy recommendation: Strengthen enforcement of timely reporting and expand mandatory fuel grade coverage.
    The data supports two key regulatory actions: (a) enforce stricter timeliness requirements for price updates, with penalties for consistently stale reporting, and (b) extend mandatory reporting to all fuel grades sold at a forecourt, not just the most common ones. These measures would enhance the reliability and completeness of the open data that underpins consumer price transparency.
