Dataset Overview & Coverage Summary

This report analyses fuel price records sourced from the UK Government's official Fuel Finder API, aggregated by fuelcosts.co.uk. The dataset encompasses 50,000 individual price observations across 7,373 distinct forecourts. The reporting period spans from 2026-02-07 to 2026-04-16. The dataset covers 6 distinct fuel types: B7_STANDARD, E5, B7_PREMIUM, B10, E10, HVO.

Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Price Disparities: There is a measurable spread between maximum and minimum prices across all fuel types, suggesting that geography or station type (e.g., motorway vs supermarket) plays a significant role in retail pricing. Policy analysts should investigate local monopolies or specific regions experiencing structurally higher prices.
  2. Market Consistency: The Interquartile Range (IQR) for standard fuels (E10, B7_STANDARD) is relatively tight compared to the absolute spread. This indicates that while extreme outliers exist, the majority of consumers are facing a somewhat consistent pricing band.
  3. Reporting Compliance: The median reporting lag is 0.0 hours. This suggests that the open data mandate is generally succeeding in providing near real-time pricing to the market. However, the long tail shown in Figure 2 highlights that some retailers are slow to update, which could distort consumer comparison tools.
  4. Fuel Availability: Standard diesel (B7_STANDARD) and standard unleaded (E10) are the most widely available fuels, followed by premium grades (E5, B7_PREMIUM). Any policy interventions regarding pricing transparency should primarily target E10 and B7_STANDARD, as these impact the vast majority of motorists.
