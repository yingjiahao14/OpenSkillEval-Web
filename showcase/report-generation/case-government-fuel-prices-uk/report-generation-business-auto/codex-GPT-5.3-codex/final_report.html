Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Non-trivial price dispersion persists within each fuel type. IQR and max-min ranges indicate meaningful within-fuel price heterogeneity, supporting continued consumer-facing transparency tools and price-comparison access.
  2. Fuel-type stratification is clear. Average prices differ materially by fuel type, so policy monitoring should avoid pooled metrics and retain fuel-specific benchmarks.
  3. Freshness risk is concentrated in a tail of stale updates. While central lag metrics are moderate, records with long lags (24h+) suggest compliance or system latency pockets warranting targeted oversight.
  4. Coverage breadth differs by fuel type. Lower station coverage for some fuels can reduce competitive pressure and consumer optionality, especially where only limited grades are available locally.
  5. Regulatory reporting quality can be tracked with lag thresholds. Simple KPIs (median lag, P90 lag, share over 24/48/72h) are suitable for ongoing compliance dashboards and enforcement triage.
Method note: All metrics in this report are computed directly from /app/benchmark/data.csv with no imputation or synthetic augmentation. Datetimes are parsed as UTC ISO-8601 values; price is treated as integer pence per litre.
