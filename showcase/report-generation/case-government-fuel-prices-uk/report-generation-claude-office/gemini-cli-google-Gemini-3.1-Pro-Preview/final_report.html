Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Price Variation: There are significant variances in fuel pricing across the market, especially observed in the maximum vs. minimum ranges. Policy makers should investigate whether these extreme spreads are geographically localized or correlated with specific retailer brands.
  2. Data Freshness: The median update lag is 0.05 hours. If this lag is excessively high, it indicates non-compliance or technical inefficiencies in the open data mandate, which could mislead consumers relying on real-time price finders.
  3. Market Competitiveness: The Interquartile Range (IQR) for standard fuels like E10 and B7_STANDARD reveals the typical price disparity. A narrow IQR suggests a generally competitive mainstream market, even if outliers exist.
  4. Fuel Availability: The data shows variations in how many fuel types stations report, which may point to either physical unavailability of certain fuels (like premium variants) at some forecourts or selective reporting behaviour.
