⚠
Anomaly — Negative update lags: 1,011 records (2.0%) show source_updated_at after recorded_at, which is logically impossible (prices cannot be updated after they are recorded). These suggest either clock synchronisation issues between the retailer's system and the government feed, or data processing artefacts. Maximum negative lag is −0.999 hours. Regulators should request timestamp audit trails from affected retailers.
⚠
Stale data concern: 1,802 records (3.6%) have a lag exceeding 7 days, with the maximum lag reaching 1,263 hours (52.6 days). These represent cases where the retailer's government-feed price has not been updated for over a week at time of collection, calling into question the accuracy of those forecourt prices for consumers using comparison tools.

Section 06

Key Findings & Policy Implications

Evidence-based insights for fuel pricing policy, consumer protection, and market transparency

1
Price Levels

E10 petrol is the cheapest fuel at 145.2p/litre; B7_PREMIUM and HVO are 28% more expensive at ~187p/litre

The six-fuel price hierarchy is clear and consistent: E10 (145.2p) → E5 (161.3p) → B7_STANDARD (167.0p) → B7_PREMIUM (185.8p) ≈ HVO (187.4p). The standard diesel premium over standard petrol is 21.8p/litre — a meaningful ongoing cost burden for diesel vehicle owners and freight operators.

2
Consumer Price Disparity

B7_STANDARD diesel shows the widest price spread of main fuels: 33p IQR, meaning consumers risk paying up to £16.50 more per fill depending on forecourt choice

The 33p interquartile range for diesel is more than double E10's 16p IQR. For a 50-litre diesel fill, selecting a Q3-priced station over a Q1-priced station costs £16.50 more. This scale of disparity suggests reduced competition or geographic pricing segmentation in the diesel market, warranting further investigation under competition policy frameworks.

3
Market Transparency

The majority of retailers (80.4%) update prices within 1 hour — the government open data scheme is broadly working as intended

With a system-wide median update lag of just ~3 minutes and 80.4% of records updated within an hour, the UK Fuel Finder open data scheme is delivering near real-time price transparency at the majority of forecourts. This is a positive baseline for consumer-facing comparison tools.

4
Compliance Concern

3.6% of records (1,802) have update lags exceeding 7 days; one record is 52 days stale — indicating a tail of non-compliant or malfunctioning reporters

While most retailers perform well, a persistent tail of stale reports undermines data reliability and consumer trust. Stations with lags exceeding 7 days should be identified and audited. The maximum observed lag of 1,263 hours (52.6 days) is particularly concerning and suggests systematic non-compliance at a small number of sites.

5
Data Integrity

1,011 records (2.0%) carry impossible negative update lags — a systemic data quality issue requiring investigation with the government feed operator

Records where source_updated_at post-dates recorded_at indicate either retailer-side clock errors, timestamp normalisation bugs in the data pipeline, or issues in the government Fuel Finder API. While the magnitude (maximum −0.999 hrs) is modest, the pattern affects 2% of the dataset and should be escalated as a data quality defect to fuelcosts.co.uk and the API operator.

6
Alternative Fuels

HVO and B10 have critically limited infrastructure: 52 and 33 stations respectively, with poorer reporting compliance than mainstream fuels

HVO (Hydrotreated Vegetable Oil) and B10 biodiesel are positioned as lower-carbon alternatives, but near-zero station coverage and higher reporting lag rates (HVO median lag: 1.02 hrs, only 49.5% within-1-hr vs. ~82% for mainstream fuels) make them impractical for most consumers. Policy action to expand infrastructure and improve reporting compliance could accelerate decarbonisation in the road transport sector.

Data Notes & Methodology

  • Dataset: 50,000 rows sampled from the UK Fuel Finder price history dataset (Kaggle: jamesb7/fuel-prices-uk), compiled by fuelcosts.co.uk under CC BY 4.0.
  • Outlier exclusion: 127 records with price_pence <100p or >300p removed from price distribution analyses as likely data-entry errors; all records used for lag analysis.
  • Station identifiers are anonymised SHA-256 hashes; brand and geographic analysis is not possible from this dataset alone.
  • All timestamps parsed as UTC (ISO 8601 with Z suffix). Lag = recorded_at − source_updated_at.
  • B10 and HVO statistics should be treated as indicative only due to small sample sizes (54 and 89 records respectively).
  • This sample represents approximately 12.6% of the full 396,502-row upstream dataset; findings may not fully represent the complete market.
