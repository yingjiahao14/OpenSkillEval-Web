6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Price Transparency is Strong for Standard Fuels: E10 and B7_STANDARD show tight price distributions (IQR: 16p and 33p respectively), indicating a competitive and transparent market for the most common fuel types.
  2. Premium Fuels Show Greater Price Dispersion: B7_PREMIUM and specialty fuels like B10 and HVO exhibit wider IQRs, suggesting less price consistency. Consumers choosing premium fuels may benefit from greater price comparison.
  3. Most Retailers Update Prices Promptly: The median update lag of approximately 3 minutes indicates most retailers comply with the real-time reporting requirement. However, a small subset of stations (5,266 records) show lags exceeding 24 hours, warranting compliance review.
  4. Market Coverage is Comprehensive: With 7,373 unique forecourts tracked across 6 fuel types, the dataset provides robust national coverage enabling meaningful market analysis.
  5. Data Quality Requires Ongoing Monitoring: 2,270 records contained price values outside expected ranges, likely representing data entry errors or non-standard transactions. Implementing validation rules at data ingestion would improve dataset reliability.

Policy Recommendations

  • Consider implementing automated validation checks for price outliers (e.g., flag prices <£0.10 or >£5.00 per litre).
  • Investigate stations with update lags exceeding 48 hours for potential compliance issues.
  • Expand monitoring of premium fuel markets where price dispersion is highest.
