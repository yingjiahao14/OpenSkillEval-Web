Dataset Overview & Coverage Summary

This analysis uses 50,000 fuel price records from 7,373 distinct stations (anonymised node_id values). The dataset contains 6 fuel types.

Recorded-at window: 2026-02-07 to 2026-04-16 (UTC). Retailer-reported update timestamps span 2025-12-17 to 2026-04-16.

Data integrity note: A small share of records (123, 0.246%) contain prices outside 50–300 p/L (e.g., 1.00 or 999.90). These are treated as likely data/entry artefacts and excluded from distribution/spread statistics; all exclusions are quantified and the raw data remain unchanged.
Lag integrity note: Update lag is computed as recorded_at − source_updated_at (hours). Most observations are near-real-time (median 0.048h ≈ 2.9 minutes), but the tail is heavy: 10.53% of records exceed 24 hours and 3.60% exceed 7 days. A small fraction (1,011, 2.02%) have negative lag (source_updated_at after recorded_at), consistent with clock skew or ingestion ordering and should be monitored as a data-quality indicator.

Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Fuel-type price levels differ materially: median HVO exceeds median E10 by 45.00 p/L (valid-price subset).
  2. Within-fuel dispersion is non-trivial: the widest typical band (IQR) is for B10 at 47.82 p/L, implying meaningful consumer price differences even without location metadata.
  3. Extreme spreads remain present even after excluding implausible prices: B7_PREMIUM shows a max–min span of 215.40 p/L within the 50–300 p/L validity band.
  4. Reporting freshness is strong in the centre but weak in the tail: median lag is 2.9 minutes, yet 10.53% of records are >24h and 3.60% are >7d — a compliance and consumer-trust concern.
  5. Coverage is uneven for alternative fuels: only 34–6,494 stations report each fuel type (e.g., B10 at 34 stations), limiting comparability and consumer choice visibility for niche fuels.
Policy relevance: The combination of sizable within-fuel dispersion (IQR) and heavy-tailed reporting lag supports targeted monitoring: (i) identify stations consistently outside typical price bands (potential local market power), and (ii) prioritise enforcement or nudges for stations with persistently stale updates that undermine transparency.
