3. Price Spread & Market Competitiveness

Price spread — the gap between the cheapest and most expensive observations — directly measures consumer exposure to pricing disparities. The interquartile range (IQR) provides a more robust measure of the typical price band, excluding outlier stations.

Policy Implications

  • For the three main consumer fuel types (E10, B7_STANDARD, E5), the IQR ranges from 16.0p to 33.0p, indicating moderate within-market price variation.
  • The max-min spread is considerably larger — driven by outlier stations at the extremes. This suggests that while most consumers encounter a relatively narrow price band, a minority face significantly higher or lower prices.
  • Premium fuels (B7_PREMIUM, E5) show wider absolute spreads, consistent with less competitive pricing in the premium segment where fewer stations compete.

6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Standard fuels are competitively priced, but meaningful disparities exist. E10 and B7_STANDARD show IQRs of 16.0p and 33.0p respectively. While the central price band is relatively tight, the total spread (1,358.0p for E10, 1,878.0p for B7_STANDARD) indicates that some consumers face notably higher prices — potentially due to geographic isolation or local market dominance. Competitiveness
  2. Premium fuel segments exhibit wider pricing variation. B7_PREMIUM and E5 show larger IQRs and absolute spreads, suggesting weaker competitive pressure where fewer retailers offer these products. This may warrant targeted attention if premium fuels grow in market share. Market Structure
  3. A material share of reported prices are stale. With 10.5% of records lagging more than 24 hours and 3.6% exceeding a week, the open data feed's usefulness for real-time consumer decisions is compromised. Policy enforcement of timely reporting should be considered. Data Quality
  4. Station coverage for standard fuels is strong; niche fuels are underrepresented. E10 and B7_STANDARD are reported at the vast majority of stations, providing robust data for analysis. However, HVO (54 stations) and B10 (34 stations) have minimal representation, making any statistical conclusions about these fuels unreliable. Coverage
  5. Median prices reflect expected market dynamics. Diesel (B7_STANDARD) is priced above standard petrol (E10), and premium grades command expected premia. This pricing hierarchy is consistent with input costs and market positioning, suggesting no structural anomalies at the aggregate level. Market Health
