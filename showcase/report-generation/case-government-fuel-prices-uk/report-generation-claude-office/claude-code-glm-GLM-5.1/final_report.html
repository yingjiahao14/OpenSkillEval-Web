Stale Data Flag

2,143 stations (29.1%) have at least one record with an update lag exceeding 72 hours (3 days). These stations may not be promptly updating their reported prices, potentially misleading consumers who rely on the Fuel Finder service.

The bimodal pattern visible in the lag histogram suggests two populations of retailers: those updating frequently (sub-hourly or daily) and those updating infrequently (weekly or less). This disparity affects the real-time reliability of the open data scheme and warrants further regulatory attention.

7. Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Significant price disparities exist for common fuels. The price range for E5 spans 1889.0p/l, meaning a consumer filling a 55L tank could overpay by up to £1038.95. Policy implication: Consumer education and price comparison tools remain essential for market transparency.
  2. The interquartile range is relatively narrow for E10 and B7_STANDARD (16.0p and 33.0p respectively), indicating that most stations price within a tight band. However, outliers on both ends create real savings or cost penalties for consumers at the margins. Policy implication: Investigate whether outlier-high prices reflect geographic isolation (captive markets) or other structural factors.
  3. Update lag is a material concern. 10.5% of records have a source-to-recorded lag exceeding 24 hours, and 29.1% of stations have at least one record older than 72 hours. Policy implication: Consider mandating more frequent price updates, particularly for retailers showing chronically stale data, to improve the reliability of the Fuel Finder service.
  4. Premium fuel coverage is limited. While E10 and B7_STANDARD are reported by most stations, premium fuels like B7_PREMIUM and E5 are available at far fewer locations. Policy implication: Expanding mandatory reporting to cover all sold fuel types at every station would improve consumer choice transparency.
  5. Specialty fuels (B10, SDV) have very sparse data with few reporting stations and wide price variance, making it difficult for consumers to compare. Policy implication: As alternative fuels grow in market share, ensuring robust reporting coverage will be important for emerging fuel markets.
  6. The dataset covers 7,373 unique forecourts over a 120-day period with 50,000 total records, providing a statistically robust foundation for pricing analysis. No data quality issues (nulls, malformed timestamps) were detected.
