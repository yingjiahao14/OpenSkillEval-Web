Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. 1. Standard petrol E10 has the lowest median among widely available fuels at 144.9ppl, while B7_PREMIUM has the highest widely available median at 187.9ppl; this confirms clear price tiering by product grade.
  2. 2. Typical price dispersion is policy-material: IQRs range from 16.0ppl (E10) to 48.8ppl (B10), meaning consumers often face non-trivial savings from comparing forecourts.
  3. 3. Market-wide max-min ranges are dominated by anomalous reports, reaching 1889.0ppl for E5; regulators should treat extremes as data-quality or exceptional-pricing flags rather than typical consumer outcomes.
  4. 4. Reported prices are usually fresh: the median lag is 2.9 min and 73.4% of records are captured within five minutes of the retailer source update.
  5. 5. Freshness is uneven at the tail: 5,266 records (10.5%) lag the source update by more than 24 hours, and the latest station-fuel observations include 1,120 stale pairs across 1,014 stations.
  6. 6. Coverage is broad for standard fuels but thin for alternatives: B7_STANDARD appears at 6,494 stations and E10 at 6,244, while HVO and B10 appear at only 54 and 34 stations respectively.

Price Spread & Market Competitiveness

The IQR captures the price band faced by the middle half of reported observations and is therefore more informative for market competitiveness than isolated extremes. The widest IQR is 48.8ppl for B10, while the largest max-min range is 1889.0ppl for E5.

Policy reading: IQRs of 16–33ppl for the high-coverage standard and premium fuel categories indicate that consumers can face meaningful forecourt-level price differences. Very large max-min ranges should trigger reporting-quality checks before being interpreted as normal competitive variation.

Methodology Notes

All prices are reported in pence per litre. Distribution statistics are calculated directly from row-level observations, so stations with multiple observations contribute multiple records. Station coverage counts use distinct node_id values. Update lag is measured in UTC using parsed ISO timestamps. No records were imputed, removed, winsorised, or otherwise altered for the reported statistics.
