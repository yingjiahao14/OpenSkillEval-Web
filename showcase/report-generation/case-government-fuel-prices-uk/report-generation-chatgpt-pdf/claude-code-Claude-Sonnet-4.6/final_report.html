01 Dataset Overview & Coverage Summary

This analysis draws on 50,000 price observations across 7,373 unique UK forecourts reported under the government-mandated Fuel Finder open data scheme. Data compiled by fuelcosts.co.uk covers prices recorded between 07 Feb 2026 and 16 Apr 2026, with retailer price updates spanning 17 Dec 2025 to 16 Apr 2026. Six fuel types are present; three (E10, B7 Standard, E5) account for the substantial majority of records.

Data cleaning note: 908 records (1.8%) were identified as extreme price outliers (values outside P1–P99 per fuel type, e.g. £18.79/L for diesel) and excluded from distributional statistics. These appear to be data entry errors in the source feed. Record counts and station figures use the full raw dataset.

03 Price Spread & Market Competitiveness

Two metrics characterise market competitiveness: the full price spread (max minus min, after P1/P99 clipping) measures the widest consumer disparity in the market; the IQR (P25–P75 range) describes the price band that the majority of consumers actually encounter. The IQR is the more policy-relevant metric as it is robust to outliers.

04 Retailer Reporting Freshness Analysis

Update lag is defined as recorded_at − source_updated_at: the time between when a retailer last updated their price in the government feed and when that record was captured by the aggregation system. A short lag confirms near-real-time data; a long lag indicates that the displayed price may not reflect what consumers actually pay at the pump.

ℹ️
Negative lag artefacts: 1,011 records (2.0%) show a negative lag (source_updated_at later than recorded_at), likely caused by minor clock-skew between the government API and the collection system, or retroactive price corrections. These records are excluded from all lag statistics and charts below.

06 Key Findings & Policy Implications
  • Most retailers update prices in near real-time — but a stale tail undermines data integrityData Quality
    The median update lag of 0.0 hours is impressive, with 88.1% of records updated within 6 hours. However, 3.7% of records are older than 7 days and 0.2% exceed 30 days. These stations present stale prices on consumer comparison platforms, eroding user trust and market efficiency. A mandatory maximum update interval (e.g. 24 hours) with automated compliance reporting is recommended.
  • Diesel commands a persistent 21.9p/L premium over standard unleadedMarket
    B7 Standard Diesel averages 167.0p/L against 145.1p/L for E10 — a gap of 21.9p/L (15.1% above E10). This is policy-relevant given the prevalence of diesel among commercial vehicles and higher-mileage private motorists, who are disproportionately exposed to fuel cost volatility.
  • Inter-forecourt price disparity is significant for diesel and E5Policy
    B7 Diesel's IQR of 33.0p/L means that choosing a 25th-percentile over a 75th-percentile station saves £16.50 on a 50-litre fill. For E10 the equivalent saving is £8.00. These disparities are large enough to warrant consumer protection scrutiny and underline the value of maintaining an open, real-time price register.
  • Extreme price outliers signal data quality failures in the reporting feedData Quality
    The raw dataset contains prices as high as 1359.0p/L for E10 and 1879.0p/L for diesel — clearly erroneous values likely caused by unit confusion (e.g. price in £ entered as pence) or system errors. A total of 908 records (1.8%) fell outside the P1–P99 range. The Fuel Finder API should implement server-side validation to reject prices outside a plausible range (e.g. 100–300p/L).
  • Emerging fuel types (HVO, B10) lack the scale for robust market analysisMarket
    HVO (Hydrotreated Vegetable Oil) and B10 (bio-diesel blend) together account for just 155 records across fewer than 88 stations. As the UK transitions toward lower-carbon fuels, regulators should ensure these types are mandated within the open data scheme before their market share grows, to enable competitive oversight from the outset.
  • Geographic and brand-level analysis is currently impossible — a structural gapPolicy
    Station identifiers are anonymised hashes. Without address, postcode, or retailer brand, it is impossible to identify rural price premiums, supermarket vs. motorway pricing, or regional competition gaps — all of which are central to effective consumer protection policy. The data sharing framework should be enhanced to include NUTS2-level geographic identifiers or, at minimum, a rurality/urban classification.
✅
Recommended Actions: (1) Mandate maximum 24-hour price refresh intervals and publish station-level compliance metrics publicly. (2) Implement server-side price validation in the Fuel Finder API to reject implausible values (<100p or >300p/L). (3) Require stations to supply NUTS2-level regional identifiers to enable geographic competition analysis. (4) Extend mandatory reporting to HVO and B10 fuel types given their growing market presence. (5) Add a "data freshness" indicator on consumer-facing fuel comparison platforms to distinguish real-time from stale prices.
A Appendix: Methodology & Data Notes

Data Source

UK Government Fuel Finder API, aggregated by fuelcosts.co.uk (CC BY 4.0). Dataset: jamesb7/fuel-prices-uk via Kaggle. Analytical sample: 50,000 rows.

Price Units & Outlier Treatment

All prices are in pence per litre (p/L). Raw values include both integer (e.g. 145p) and decimal (e.g. 139.9p) formats. Distributional statistics exclude P1–P99 outliers per fuel type (908 records, 1.8%); raw min/max are reported for transparency. No imputation was performed.

Lag Calculation

Update lag = recorded_at − source_updated_at in hours. Records with negative lag (1,011, 2.0%) are excluded from lag statistics — these likely reflect clock-skew or retroactive updates in the source API.

Date Range

Despite the report's February 2026 framing, the dataset's recorded_at timestamps span 07 Feb 2026 to 16 Apr 2026, and source_updated_at dates range from 17 Dec 2025 to 16 Apr 2026. February 2026 represents the core collection period; some records extend into March–April 2026.

Statistical Definitions

IQR: interquartile range (P75 − P25). Spread: max − min within P1–P99 bounds. Box plot whiskers: extend to 1.5× IQR beyond Q1/Q3; values beyond this are plotted individually. All percentiles computed via linear interpolation.
