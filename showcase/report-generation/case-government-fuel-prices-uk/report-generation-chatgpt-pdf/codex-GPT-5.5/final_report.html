Key Findings & Policy Implications

These findings are derived directly from the supplied CSV. They prioritise price distribution, market spread and reporting freshness because these are most relevant to consumer protection and market transparency.

  1. 1. Standard petrol (E10) is the lowest-priced high-coverage fuel on a median basis at 144.9 ppl, while premium diesel (B7_PREMIUM) has the highest median among widely reported fuels at 187.9 ppl. Policy implication: headline comparisons should separate standard and premium fuels rather than use a single market average.
  2. 2. Typical price dispersion is material: B10 has the widest IQR at 48.8 ppl, and B7_STANDARD’s middle-50% band spans 149.9–182.9 ppl. Policy implication: consumers can face meaningful differences even before considering extreme outliers.
  3. 3. The full max–min ranges are dominated by anomalous reported prices, including E5 at 0.0–1889.0 ppl and B7_STANDARD at 1.0–1879.0 ppl. Policy implication: transparency systems need validation rules and exception monitoring, not only publication mandates.
  4. 4. Reporting freshness is generally strong at the median (2.9 minutes), but 5,266 records (10.5%) lag by more than 24 hours and 1,014 stations have at least one latest node-fuel observation over 24 hours stale. Policy implication: compliance monitoring should target stale pockets rather than assume uniform freshness.
  5. 5. Coverage is broad for B7_STANDARD (6,494 stations) and E10 (6,244), but narrow for B10 (34) and HVO (54). Policy implication: alternative-fuel insights are less statistically robust and should not be weighted equally with mainstream fuels.

Methodology & Data Integrity

All statistics are computed directly from /app/benchmark/data.csv. No station geography, retailer brand, address or coordinate fields are present, so the report does not make geographic or brand-level claims. Fuel prices are analysed in pence per litre and timestamps are parsed as UTC.

  • Price KPIs: mean, median, standard deviation, min, max, IQR and full range are calculated by fuel type.
  • Competitiveness KPIs: IQR is used for the typical spread; max–min range is shown separately because raw outliers are substantial.
  • Freshness KPI: lag equals recorded_at − source_updated_at; records above 24 hours are flagged as stale for transparency monitoring.
  • Coverage KPI: unique station counts use distinct node_id values; station fuel availability is based on distinct node_id–fuel_type pairs.
