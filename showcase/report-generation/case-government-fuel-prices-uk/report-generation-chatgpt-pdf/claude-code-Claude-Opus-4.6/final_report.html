1. Dataset Overview & Coverage Summary

This analysis examines 50,000 fuel price observations collected from 7,373 unique forecourts across the UK. The data spans from 07 February 2026 to 16 April 2026, sourced from the UK Government's Fuel Finder scheme via fuelcosts.co.uk.

The dataset contains six fuel types, with B7_STANDARD (standard diesel) being the most frequently reported (17,854 records), followed by E10 (standard unleaded, 15,082 records) and E5 (super unleaded, 9,335 records). Premium diesel (B7_PREMIUM) accounts for 7,574 records, while B10 and HVO represent niche fuel types with 64 and 91 records respectively.

No missing values were detected in price or timestamp columns. All prices are recorded as pence per litre (PPL).

6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

1. Significant price variation exists across UK forecourts. E10 prices range from 1.0p to 1359.0p per litre — a spread of 1358.0p. While the IQR of 16.0p suggests most consumers face moderate variation, the tails indicate that some motorists pay meaningfully more. Policy implication: Price comparison tools and transparency mandates remain essential for consumer protection.
2. Premium fuel segments exhibit weaker price competition. B7_PREMIUM diesel shows a range of 1398.0p and IQR of 30.0p — notably wider than mainstream fuels. This suggests less competitive pressure in premium segments. Policy implication: Regulators may wish to examine whether premium fuel pricing lacks sufficient competitive discipline.
3. Diesel is consistently priced above unleaded petrol. B7_STANDARD diesel averages 167.4p versus 145.9p for E10 — a premium of 21.5p per litre. This diesel premium persists across the distribution. Policy implication: The sustained diesel premium may warrant monitoring given diesel's importance to commercial transport and logistics costs.
4. Most retailers report prices promptly, but a notable minority are slow. The median update lag of 0.0 hours is encouraging, but 10.5% of records are stale by more than 24 hours. Policy implication: Enforcement of reporting timeliness requirements may need strengthening to ensure the open data feed remains reliable for consumers.
5. Niche fuel types have very limited coverage. HVO and B10 are available at only 54 and 34 stations respectively, limiting consumers' access to alternative fuels. Policy implication: If the government wishes to promote alternative fuels, increasing forecourt availability is a prerequisite.
