Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Significant price disparity exists across retailers. For B7_STANDARD diesel, the IQR of 33p means typical consumers see a 33p band between the 25th and 75th percentile. The full range reaches 1,878p — indicating some consumers may face prices nearly £19/litre higher than others for standard diesel. Policy implication: Price transparency tools must account for this spread; consumer guidance should help drivers find the lowest prices in their area.
  2. E10 is the most affordable fuel type, but its savings may not be fully passed through. With a mean price of 145.90p/litre versus 167.41p/litre for B7_STANDARD, E10 offers clear savings at the pump. However, the high standard deviation (31.42p) suggests inconsistent pricing strategies across retailers. Policy implication: Promoting E10 adoption and ensuring its pricing is transparent could benefit cost-conscious consumers.
  3. Most retailers update prices promptly, but outliers present data quality risks. The median lag of just 3 minutes (0.05 hours) demonstrates excellent real-time reporting for the majority. However, maximum lags exceeding 1,000 hours (40+ days) for B7_STANDARD, E10, and E5 indicate some stations may be operating with stale price data. Policy implication: Automated alerting for stations with data older than 24-48 hours would improve dataset reliability; compliance enforcement for infrequent reporters should be considered.
  4. Premium and niche fuels have limited station coverage. B10 (only 34 stations) and HVO (only 54 stations) represent less than 1% of the market each. While these are specialty fuels, limited availability may restrict consumer switching behaviour that would otherwise improve market competitiveness. Policy implication: If government policy aims to support HVO adoption for heavy goods vehicles (HGVs), incentivising broader reporting of HVO prices would improve market visibility.
  5. Station consolidation of fuel types is the norm. Most stations (2745 of 7,373, or 37.2%) report only 1-2 fuel types. This limits the ability of consumers to compare across fuel categories at a single location. Policy implication: Requiring stations to report all available fuel types would improve data completeness and consumer comparison capability.
