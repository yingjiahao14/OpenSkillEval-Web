Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Typical prices differ materially by fuel type: HVO has the highest median price (189.9 PPL) while E10 has the lowest (144.9 PPL), supporting fuel-type-specific consumer impacts.
  2. Within-fuel dispersion is non-trivial: IQRs range from 16.0 to 48.8 PPL across fuel types, indicating consumers can face meaningfully different prices even when choosing the same fuel grade.
  3. Extreme price ranges (max–min) are very large for several fuels, driven by rare outliers; this suggests a need for monitoring and validation rules in the reporting pipeline to avoid misleading signals in transparency tooling.
  4. Reporting freshness is generally high for the bulk of observations (median lag 0.05 hours), but a long tail remains (95th percentile 106.4 hours), which can undermine consumer trust if stale prices persist in the open feed.
  5. At the station level, 342 stations have a median update lag exceeding 24 hours, and 133 exceed 7 days—potential candidates for compliance follow-up or automated nudging.
  6. Data integrity flags: 129 records (0.26%) fall outside a broad 80–250 PPL band; policy dashboards should treat such values as potential errors unless corroborated.

Method note: This analysis uses the provided extract (50,000 records). Geographic or brand-level competitiveness cannot be assessed because station metadata is not present in the CSV.
