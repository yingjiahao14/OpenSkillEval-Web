A comprehensive analysis of retail fuel pricing patterns across UK forecourts, examining price distributions by fuel type and temporal update behaviour based on government-mandated open data.

Generated: 2026-05-09 20:34 UTC Primary source: /app/benchmark/data.csv Audience: government policy analysts and energy economists

Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Robust price comparisons show clear fuel-type segmentation. E10 has the lowest mean price at 145.9ppl, while HVO has the highest mean at 188.9ppl. This supports fuel-specific monitoring rather than a single all-fuel benchmark.
  2. Consumers face meaningful typical price variation within fuel types. Among the major fuels, IQRs run from 16.0ppl to 33.0ppl; B10 has the widest IQR at 48.8ppl. IQR is the strongest competitiveness indicator here because raw ranges are distorted by extreme reported values.
  3. Raw ranges reveal data-quality and market-monitoring risks. E5 has the largest raw max-min range at 1,889.0ppl. Several minima and maxima are implausible for normal retail prices, so policy dashboards should include automated anomaly flags before using raw extremes in consumer-facing tools.
  4. Most records are fresh, but a material tail is stale. The median source-to-recorded lag is 0.048 hours, yet 5,266 observations (10.5%) exceed 24 hours and 1,802 (3.6%) exceed 7 days. Compliance monitoring should focus on the long tail, not only the median.
  5. Forecourt coverage is broad for standard fuels and thin for niche fuels. B7_STANDARD appears at 6,494 stations (88.1%), while B10 appears at 34 stations (0.5%). Availability analysis should therefore separate mass-market fuels from specialty fuels.
  6. Geographic price conclusions cannot be drawn from this file alone. The station identifier is anonymised and no location, brand, postcode, or road-type metadata is provided. Area-level consumer detriment analysis would require linking station metadata to these node IDs.

Price Spread & Market Competitiveness

The interquartile range (Q3 minus Q1) captures the typical price band consumers encounter within a fuel type. The raw max-min range measures the full reported disparity, but it is heavily affected by anomalous reports such as zero, one-pence, or four-digit pence-per-litre values.

B10 has the largest typical spread at 48.8ppl, signalling the strongest within-fuel price dispersion among reported categories. E5 has the largest raw range at 1,889.0ppl; because raw ranges include extreme outliers, they should be treated as a trigger for validation rather than a direct estimate of normal consumer choice.

Policy implication: consumer-facing transparency tools should display robust local comparisons where possible and flag stale or implausible prices. Without location metadata, this file can establish market-wide dispersion but cannot identify high-price localities.

Method Notes & Data Integrity

  • All statistics are calculated directly from /app/benchmark/data.csv; no external data, modelled values, or manual adjustments are introduced.
  • Prices are stated in pence per litre. Means, medians, quartiles, ranges, and standard deviations use the full reported price field.
  • Freshness lag is calculated as recorded_at - source_updated_at after parsing both fields as UTC datetimes.
  • Station-level analysis groups by anonymised node_id. No geographic, brand, postcode, or ownership conclusions are possible from this dataset alone.
  • Charts are embedded inline as SVG data URIs, making this HTML file self-contained and browser-openable without network access.
