Data Quality Alert: Extreme outlier values (prices of 0, 1, 900+, and 1,800+ ppl) are present across all fuel types and inflate the reported range statistics significantly. These are likely data entry errors, sensor failures, or test values in the source data. The IQR (interquartile range) is the more reliable measure of typical price spread as it is robust to these outliers.

Policypoint

Among the major fuel types with sufficient data (E10, E5, B7_STANDARD, B7_PREMIUM), diesel fuels show a higher IQR (30–33 ppl) than petrol fuels (16–18 ppl), suggesting greater price dispersion in the diesel market. Consumers purchasing diesel may face more significant regional or retailer-based price disparities than petrol buyers.

Key Observations

For the vast majority of records, the median update lag is approximately 3 minutes (0.05 hours), indicating that the UK Fuel Finder API provides near real-time price updates. However, some records show lags exceeding 1,000 hours (~44 days), which may indicate stations that have ceased trading, inactive feeds, or data pipeline issues requiring investigation.

Key Findings & Policy Implications

  • 1

    Petrol is notably cheaper than diesel at the UK pump

    E10 standard petrol has a median price of 144.90 ppl compared to 167.90 ppl for B7_STANDARD diesel — a 23 ppl (~16%) premium for diesel. This is consistent with broader energy market trends and has direct implications for consumer cost-of-living analysis.

  • 2

    Diesel markets show greater price dispersion than petrol markets

    B7_STANDARD diesel has an IQR of 33 ppl versus E10 petrol's IQR of 16 ppl. This means diesel buyers experience a wider typical price band across stations, which may reflect weaker price competition or greater regional cost variation in diesel supply chains.

  • 3

    Extreme price outliers require data quality investigation

    Every fuel type contains unrealistic values (0–1 ppl or 900–1,889 ppl). While the IQR and median are robust, the reported ranges and means are significantly distorted. The source data pipeline should be audited to identify and filter erroneous records before use in policy decisions.

  • 4

    Retailer reporting is near real-time for most stations

    The median update lag is ~3 minutes across all fuel types, indicating the UK Fuel Finder API provides highly current price data. However, select stations show lags exceeding 1,000 hours, suggesting inactive or defunct feeds that may require regulatory follow-up to ensure compliance.

  • 5

    Alternative fuels (HVO, B10) lack sufficient market coverage for reliable benchmarking

    With only 44 and 22 stations reporting HVO and B10 respectively, these fuel types do not meet the threshold for statistically reliable market analysis. Policy analysts should note this data gap when assessing the transition away from fossil fuels.
