6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

The following findings synthesise the analytical results into actionable insights for fuel pricing policy, consumer protection, and market transparency assessment.

  1. Finding 1: Substantial price variation exists across all fuel types.
    HVO commands the highest median price at 189.9p/litre, while E10 is the most affordable at 144.9p/litre — a gap of 45.0p. Within individual fuel types, full price ranges of up to 1889p (E5) indicate extreme outliers in the market.
    Policy Implication: The wide price ranges suggest that some consumers may face significantly higher costs depending on their choice of forecourt. Enhanced price comparison tools and consumer awareness initiatives could help mitigate this disparity.
  2. Finding 2: Core market pricing is more competitive than headline ranges suggest.
    The IQR for most fuel types is considerably narrower than the full range, indicating that the majority of stations price within a relatively tight band. E10 shows the tightest core pricing with an IQR of just 16.0p.
    Policy Implication: While outlier pricing warrants scrutiny, the competitive core market suggests that the open data scheme is supporting price transparency for most consumers. Regulatory focus may be best directed at understanding why outlier stations deviate significantly.
  3. Finding 3: The median update lag is 0.0 hours, but a tail of stale data persists.
    80.4% of records have a lag under 1 hour, and 89.5% are within 24 hours. However, 7.4% of records show lags exceeding 48 hours, and 1377 stations have data stale by more than 7 days.
    Policy Implication: Most retailers report promptly, but the long tail of stale data undermines the reliability of the open data scheme. A compliance threshold (e.g., mandatory updates within 24 hours of any price change) could improve data freshness and consumer trust.
  4. Finding 4: Station coverage varies significantly by fuel type.
    B7_STANDARD has the broadest station coverage (6,494 stations), while B10 has the narrowest (34 stations). This reflects genuine market availability differences — not all stations sell premium or specialty fuels.
    Policy Implication: Price statistics for fuel types with narrow station coverage should be interpreted with caution in policy analysis. The reporting mandate should ensure that all fuel types sold at a forecourt are reported, not just the most common ones.
  5. Finding 5: The dataset of 50,000 records across 7,373 stations provides a robust basis for market monitoring.
    With complete data (no null values), multiple fuel types, and both standard and premium grades represented, the UK open data scheme is generating a valuable resource for market surveillance.
    Policy Implication: This dataset supports ongoing programmatic monitoring of fuel market competitiveness. Regular automated reporting — extending this analysis with time-series trend detection and anomaly flagging — could form the basis of a proactive regulatory dashboard.
