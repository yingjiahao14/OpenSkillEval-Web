6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

The following findings synthesise the main insights from this analysis, with direct implications for fuel pricing policy, consumer protection, and market transparency.

  1. Significant Price Hierarchies Exist Across Fuel Types

    B7_PREMIUM commands the highest median price at 187.9p/litre, followed by B7_STANDARD (167.9p), E5 (160.9p), and E10 (144.9p). At stations offering both grades, diesel costs a median of 22.0p/litre more than standard petrol, while super unleaded (E5) carries a 16.0p/litre premium over E10. These differentials appear consistent with quality/grade positioning rather than reflecting cost-pass-through variations.

    Consumer Protection

  2. Diesel Markets Show the Widest Price Dispersion

    B7_STANDARD exhibits the largest interquartile range (33.0p) and total range (124.0p) among mainstream fuels. A consumer filling a 50-litre tank at the 75th percentile station pays £16.50 more than at the 25th percentile — the largest differential of any fuel type. This suggests diesel pricing is less competitive or more geographically segmented than petrol markets.

    Market Competitiveness

  3. All Fuel Types Experienced Sharp Price Increases in Q1 2026

    Between early February and mid-April 2026, B7_STANDARD prices rose by 51.1p/litre (36.2%), B7_PREMIUM by 47.7p/litre (29.4%), E10 by 26.8p/litre (20.2%), and E5 by 25.2p/litre (16.7%). Diesel grades saw substantially larger increases than petrol, widening the diesel-petrol gap. A modest 0.5–1.2% decline in the most recent week may indicate prices have peaked.

    Price Monitoring

  4. Data Quality Issues Affect Nearly One in Five Stations

    18.4% of forecourts (1,358 stations) have at least one price record with a lag exceeding 7 days, indicating stale data. Additionally, 2.03% of all records show negative lag (recorded before the stated update time), suggesting system synchronisation issues. While the median lag is under 3 minutes, the long tail of stale data undermines real-time price transparency.

    Data Transparency

  5. Premium and Alternative Fuels Have Limited Availability

    While standard fuels (B7_STANDARD, E10) are available at over 84% of stations, premium variants and alternative fuels remain niche. B7_PREMIUM is offered at 44.0% of forecourts, E5 at 60.3%, and alternative fuels (HVO, B10) at under 1%. This limits consumer choice for lower-carbon alternatives and may constrain competition in premium segments.

    Market Structure

  6. E10 Shows the Most Uniform Pricing, Suggesting Stronger Competition

    Standard unleaded petrol (E10) has the lowest standard deviation (10.5p) and smallest IQR (16.0p) of all mainstream fuels. This pricing uniformity, combined with its near-universal availability (84.7% of stations), suggests a more competitive and transparent market segment — potentially serving as a benchmark for other fuel categories.

    Competition Policy

Recommendations for Policymakers

1. Strengthen stale data monitoring: With 18.4% of stations reporting stale prices, consider automated alerts or compliance thresholds for maximum acceptable update lag.

2. Investigate diesel price dispersion: The 33.0p IQR for B7_STANDARD warrants deeper analysis into geographic and brand-level drivers of diesel price variation.

3. Monitor post-peak trends: The recent 0.5–1.2% price decline after weeks of sharp increases should be tracked to determine if the market has stabilised.

4. Address negative lag records: The 2.03% of records with negative timestamps indicates data pipeline issues that should be resolved to maintain trust in the open data scheme.
