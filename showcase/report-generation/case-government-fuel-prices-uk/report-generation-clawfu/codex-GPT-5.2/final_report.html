UK Retail Fuel Price Trend Analysis — February 2026

Retail fuel pricing patterns across UK forecourts, with emphasis on price distributions by fuel type, price spreads as a proxy for competitiveness, and reporting freshness (lag between retailer updates and dataset capture).

Audience: Government policy analysts & energy economists Dataset: benchmark/data.csv (50,000 rows) Generated: 2026-05-06 (UTC)

Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Price dispersion is material within major fuels: within this sample, the interquartile range (IQR) is 16–33 pence/litre across the three high-coverage fuels (E10, E5, B7_STANDARD). This implies a wide “typical” price band faced by consumers and is consistent with meaningful local market power and/or limited price transparency for some consumers.
  2. Premium products exhibit higher typical prices and similar or higher spread: B7_PREMIUM averages 186.16 pence/litre with a 30.00 pence/litre IQR (sample), suggesting that premium pricing is not only higher but also widely dispersed across retailers.
  3. Extremely large max–min ranges indicate outliers or heterogeneous products: several fuels show max–min ranges exceeding 1,000 pence/litre in this extract (notably B7_STANDARD and E5). This is a red flag for outliers, data entry issues, or atypical observations and should be reviewed in regulatory monitoring pipelines rather than assumed to reflect genuine consumer prices.
  4. Reporting freshness is usually high, but a long tail of stale updates exists: the median lag between source_updated_at and recorded_at is ~0.05 hours (~3 minutes) for the major fuels, but 162 stations have a latest-observation lag > 24 hours and 74 stations exceed 7 days. This tail is relevant for compliance and for consumer-facing accuracy.
  5. Small timestamp inconsistencies exist: ~2.0% of records have source_updated_at later than recorded_at by up to ~1 hour, suggesting clock skew or ingestion ordering effects. Freshness KPIs should clamp negative lags to zero (as done below) and track the rate of negative-lag records as a data quality metric.

Method notes
  • All statistics are computed directly from benchmark/data.csv (50,000 rows). No imputation or fabricated values.
  • Prices are treated as pence per litre. Reported min/max indicate the presence of outliers; percentiles/IQR are emphasized for policy interpretation.
  • Freshness lag is calculated in hours as recorded_at − source_updated_at (UTC). Negative lags are clamped to zero for KPI tables and histograms and should be tracked as a quality indicator.
