7. Key Findings & Policy Implications

Synthesised insights for fuel pricing policy, consumer protection, and market transparency.

  1. Price Hierarchy Aligned with Market Structure. Premium fuels (E5, B7_PREMIUM) command £1.50 – £1.62 per litre on average, while standard grades (E10, B7_STANDARD) average £1.33 – £1.42. This differential is consistent with the higher production costs of premium grades and does not, in itself, indicate a competition problem.
  2. Significant Price Dispersion Raises Consumer Protection Questions. The E10 price spread of 179.9p/litre across stations translates to a £98.95 difference per 55-litre fill — a material amount for consumers. The IQR of 23.7p indicates that even the typical consumer faces non-trivial price variation. Policy-makers should consider whether this dispersion reflects genuine competitive differentiation or information asymmetry.
  3. Update Freshness Is Uneven and a Compliance Concern. The median update lag varies from 10.9h (B7_STANDARD) to 67.2h (B10). 20.8% of records are more than one week stale. For the Fuel Finder scheme to serve its consumer transparency purpose, mandating a maximum reporting interval (e.g., daily or every 48 hours) could significantly improve data quality.
  4. Station Coverage Is Incomplete for Niche Fuels. While E10 and B7_STANDARD are reported by the vast majority of the 4,324 stations, niche fuels like B10 (15 stations) and HVO (15 stations) have very limited coverage. This limits the scheme's utility for consumers of alternative fuels.
  5. Market Stability Observed. No significant daily price volatility was detected during February 2026. Prices were broadly stable, suggesting a market in equilibrium over the observation period. This is consistent with a commodity-driven pricing model where retailers respond to wholesale costs that change gradually.
