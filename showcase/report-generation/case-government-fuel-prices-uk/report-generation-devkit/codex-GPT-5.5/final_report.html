UK Retail Fuel Price Trend Analysis — February 2026

A comprehensive analysis of retail fuel pricing patterns across UK forecourts, examining price distributions by fuel type and temporal update behaviour based on government-mandated open data.

Audience: policy analysts and energy economists Generated: 07 May 2026 15:52 UTC Primary source: /app/benchmark/data.csv

Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Price levels differ materially by fuel type. HVO renewable diesel has the highest mean price at 188.9p/litre, while E10 petrol has the lowest mean at 145.9p/litre. This supports fuel-specific monitoring rather than a single aggregate retail fuel benchmark.
  2. Consumers face meaningful price dispersion in mainstream fuels. The central 50% price band is 33.0p for B7 standard diesel, 30.0p for B7 premium diesel, 18.0p for E5 and 16.0p for E10. These IQRs indicate non-trivial local search value for consumers.
  3. The full max−min spreads are dominated by likely reporting anomalies. Reported ranges reach 1889p/litre for E5 super unleaded; the file also contains 1 zero-price record, 76 records below 50p and 47 records above 300p. Regulatory analytics should retain raw submissions for auditability but use validation flags before public comparison tools rank stations.
  4. Most records are operationally fresh, but a tail of stale reporting remains. The median update lag is 0.05 hours (2.9 minutes), yet 5,266 records (10.5%) are more than 24 hours old and 1,802 (3.6%) exceed seven days.
  5. Coverage is broad for core petrol and diesel but sparse for alternative fuels. B7 standard diesel is reported by 6,494 stations (88.1% of stations), while B10 diesel appears at only 34 stations (0.5%). Policy assessments of alternative-fuel competition should treat the HVO and B10 samples as narrow coverage indicators rather than market-wide estimates.

Methodology & Limitations

All calculations use the supplied columns node_id, fuel_type, price_pence, recorded_at and source_updated_at. Prices are treated as pence per litre. Timestamp fields are parsed as UTC ISO 8601 values. No values are imputed, removed, winsorised or corrected.

Because the CSV does not include retailer brand, postcode, address, coordinates or region, the report cannot make geographic or brand-level claims. The observed price outliers and negative update lags should be investigated as data-quality issues before being used in consumer-facing rankings or enforcement thresholds.

Data source: UK Government Fuel Finder scheme data compiled by fuelcosts.co.uk, provided in /app/benchmark/data.csv. Report generated from raw supplied data without fabricated or imputed values.
