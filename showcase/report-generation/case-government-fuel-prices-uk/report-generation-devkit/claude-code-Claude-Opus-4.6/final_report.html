6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

The following findings synthesise the most policy-relevant insights from this analysis, targeted at informing consumer protection, market regulation, and data quality improvement efforts.

  1. Significant upward price trend across all fuel types All major fuel types experienced substantial price increases between February and April 2026. E10 unleaded rose from approximately 132p to 159p/litre (+20%), while B7 diesel climbed from 141p to 192p/litre (+36%). These sharp increases over a ten-week period warrant monitoring to assess whether they reflect wholesale cost pass-through or widening retail margins.
  2. Diesel price dispersion is meaningfully wider than petrol B7 Standard Diesel has an IQR of 33p compared to E10's 16p — roughly double the typical price band. This suggests the diesel market may be less competitive, potentially due to fewer alternative suppliers or greater geographic pricing variation. A 50-litre diesel fill could cost consumers up to £16.50 more at a 75th-percentile station versus a 25th-percentile one.
  3. The majority of price reports are highly timely 78.4% of all records show a reporting lag under one hour, with a median lag of just 2.9 minutes. This indicates the government's open data pipeline is working well for the vast majority of participating stations.
  4. A persistent tail of stale data undermines coverage quality 214 stations (2.9%) consistently show reporting lags exceeding 3 days, and 133 exceed 7 days. Consumers relying on these prices for comparison may be seeing outdated information. Policy action could include automated flagging of stale entries or compliance thresholds for reporting frequency.
  5. Specialty fuel coverage is extremely limited HVO and B10 are each reported by fewer than 60 stations (under 1% of all forecourts). While these fuels serve niche markets, their pricing transparency is effectively negligible. As demand for alternative fuels grows, extending mandated reporting requirements to these fuel types may become increasingly important.
  6. Data quality is high but not flawless The dataset contains zero null values, but 123 records (0.25%) contain implausible prices (below 50p or above 300p/litre), and 1,011 records show negative reporting lags. These anomalies suggest room for input validation at the data ingestion stage to prevent erroneous prices from entering the public feed.
