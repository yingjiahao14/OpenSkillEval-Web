6) Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Clear price stratification by fuel type: Mean prices differ substantially by grade, with premium fuels structurally above standard grades. Policy use: benchmark expected grade premiums when monitoring outliers.
  2. Material within-grade dispersion: Max-min and IQR metrics indicate that consumers can face meaningful same-fuel price differences across stations, consistent with search-cost and local-competition frictions.
  3. Freshness is mostly timely but with a long tail: Median lag is low, yet non-trivial tails (>24h and >48h) suggest pockets of stale reporting that can reduce consumer trust in mandated transparency feeds.
  4. Coverage asymmetry across fuel types: Some grades are reported by far fewer stations, limiting comparability and potentially biasing perceptions of market-wide pricing for those fuels.
  5. Compliance monitoring opportunity: Station-level median lag flags a manageable subset of forecourts for targeted regulator follow-up and data-quality enforcement.

Interpretation constraints: this file has no station geography, branding, or ownership metadata; therefore, geographic or firm-level competition conclusions cannot be drawn directly from this dataset alone.
