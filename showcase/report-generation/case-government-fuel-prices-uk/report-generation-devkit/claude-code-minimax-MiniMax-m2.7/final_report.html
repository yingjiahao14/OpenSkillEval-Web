1. Dataset Overview & Coverage Summary

This analysis is based on official UK government fuel price reporting data collected from the Fuel Finder API, aggregated by fuelcosts.co.uk. The dataset provides retail fuel prices at individual forecourt level across England, Scotland, and Wales.

Key Observations

E10 shows the lowest price spread (185.89p range) with the tightest IQR (16p), indicating a more standardised market. In contrast, E5 and HVO show the highest spreads (324.40p and 383.00p respectively), suggesting consumers face meaningful price disparities for premium fuels. Standard diesel (B7_STANDARD) also shows substantial variation with a 233.90p spread.

Compliance Assessment

While the median update lag of just 0.05 hours indicates most retailers update their prices promptly, the long tail of stale data is concerning from a regulatory perspective. 1,740 records (3.5%) show lags exceeding 7 days, with the maximum reaching 1263.6 hours. This suggests a subset of retailers may not be meeting their reporting obligations under the open data scheme.

6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

  • 1. E10 is the cheapest fuel type; diesel variants are significantly more expensive. Mean E10 price is 145.21p/litre versus 167.01p for standard diesel — a 15% premium. Policy should consider whether diesel consumers face structural cost disadvantages.
  • 2. Price spreads for premium fuels indicate market segmentation opportunities. E5 (super unleaded) shows a 324p spread between cheapest and most expensive stations. This variability suggests competitive markets but also potential for consumer harm if price transparency is inadequate.
  • 3. Update lag compliance is mixed — most retailers are compliant, but a tail of non-compliance exists. While 96.5% of records update within 7 days, the 1,740 records with excessive lag suggest enforcement mechanisms may be needed. Real-time price reporting is essential for consumer trust in the Fuel Finder scheme.
  • 4. Standard diesel (B7_STANDARD) has the broadest station coverage, but price variability is high. The 233.90p spread and 33p IQR for B7_STANDARD indicate that diesel consumers have strong access but face meaningful geographic price disparities worth monitoring for anti-competitive behaviour.
  • 5. Limited data for alternative fuels (HVO, B10) constrains policy analysis. With fewer than 100 records each, HVO and B10 analysis is statistically limited. As alternative fuel adoption grows, expanding data collection for these categories should be considered.
