6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Clear Price Segmentation: There is a distinct pricing hierarchy among fuel types. HVO is the most expensive on average, while E10 serves as the baseline, representing the most affordable option. This structural difference requires continued monitoring to ensure margins remain proportionate.
  2. Market Disparities: Significant price spreads exist, particularly for B7_PREMIUM (144.4p difference between highest and lowest). Such disparities suggest uneven local competitiveness and highlight the necessity of transparent consumer-facing price discovery tools.
  3. Data Freshness & Transparency: With a median update lag of 0.05 hours, the aggregate open data ecosystem performs reasonably well. However, 6.1% of records fall into the '> 3 days' lag category. Regulators should investigate whether this reflects stagnant pricing or technical non-compliance in the reporting feed.
  4. Data Integrity Constraints: While the broader dataset is robust, approximately 0.26% of inputs contain extreme anomalies (e.g., below 100p or above 250p per litre). Policy makers should ensure data providers implement stronger input validation mechanisms at the source to prevent rogue submissions.
  5. Coverage Gaps: The availability of certain fuel types, such as B10, is highly fragmented (0.5% coverage). Policy should differentiate expectations between ubiquitous staple fuels and niche grades when evaluating market accessibility.
