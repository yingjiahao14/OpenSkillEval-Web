Dataset Overview & Coverage Summary

The dataset contains 50,000 price observations for 7,373 distinct forecourts (node_id) across 6 fuel types: B10, B7_PREMIUM, B7_STANDARD, E10, E5, HVO.

All required analysis dimensions are present (fuel_type, price_pence, source_updated_at, recorded_at, node_id), with no missing values in the provided file.

Data integrity note (prices): 129 records (0.26%) fall outside a conservative plausibility band of 80–250 pence per litre. These include near-zero values (0–1 p/L) and extreme highs (e.g., >£10/L equivalents). To avoid distortion of distribution and spread metrics, the report’s core price distribution and competitiveness statistics are computed on the 80–250 p/L filtered subset; counts are reported transparently by fuel type.

Data integrity note (timestamps): 1,011 records show source_updated_at later than recorded_at (minimum lag -0.999 hours). For KPI reporting and charts we clip negative lags to 0.

Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Premium petrol E5 is priced about 16.0 p/L higher than standard petrol E10 at the median.
    This median uplift provides a benchmark for grade segmentation and consumer switching incentives.
  2. Standard diesel B7_STANDARD is about 23.0 p/L higher than E10 at the median.
    Diesel premia matter for distributional impacts and may warrant targeted transparency messaging.
  3. Within-fuel dispersion is material: B7_STANDARD shows an interquartile range (IQR) of 33.0 p/L.
    IQR captures the typical band consumers face; sustained wide IQRs can indicate uneven local competition or pass-through.
  4. Reported prices are usually fresh: the median record-level update lag is 0.05 hours (~3 minutes).
    This supports near-real-time transparency goals, though monitoring should focus on the long tail.
  5. Tail staleness is policy-relevant: in the latest observation per station, 162 stations (2.2%) are more than 24 hours out of date.
    This provides a concrete compliance metric for escalation (e.g., warnings or audits for repeat non-updaters).
  6. A small but important quality issue remains: 129 records (0.26%) contain implausible prices and would inflate naive max−min metrics.
    Regulatory dashboards should enforce plausibility checks and surface anomalies to data providers.
