Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Central-market competition appears stronger than tail competition. Core IQR bands are much tighter than full spreads, suggesting isolated high/low pricing outside typical consumer exposure.
  2. Specialty fuels show weaker comparability. Lower station coverage for premium/alternative products can amplify local price dispersion and reduce consumer switching options.
  3. Median reporting latency is very low. Sub-hour median lag indicates strong baseline timeliness in retailer-to-platform transmission.
  4. Stale-data risk remains policy-relevant. 10.53% of records are older than 24 hours, potentially affecting live price transparency for a meaningful minority of listings.
  5. Coverage asymmetry should shape regulatory monitoring. Near-ubiquitous standard fuels support robust benchmarking, while lower-coverage fuels may require targeted oversight.
