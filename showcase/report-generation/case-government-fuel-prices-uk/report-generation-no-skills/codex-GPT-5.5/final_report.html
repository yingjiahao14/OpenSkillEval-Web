Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Standard fuels show materially different price levels. Median E10 petrol is 144.9 ppl, compared with 167.9 ppl for standard diesel and 160.9 ppl for E5 super unleaded. This confirms that fuel-type mix should be controlled for in consumer-price monitoring.
  2. Price dispersion is large enough to matter for consumers. The widest IQR is B10 at 48.8 ppl; the largest full max–min range is E5 at 1,889 ppl. Full ranges are inflated by apparent data-quality outliers, but IQRs still indicate meaningful within-fuel price bands.
  3. Reported prices are usually fresh, but stale records exist. 39,192 records (78.4%) have a source-to-recorded lag below one hour, while 1,802 records (3.6%) exceed seven days. On the latest station-fuel records, 393 of 20,514 pairs (1.9%) remain over seven days stale.
  4. Timestamp anomalies warrant governance checks. 1,011 rows (2.0%) have source_updated_at later than recorded_at, which is not logically expected if both fields are captured consistently in UTC.
  5. Coverage is broad for main fuels and sparse for specialty products. B7_STANDARD appears at 6,494 stations (88.1%), while B10 appears at 34 stations (0.5%). Policy analysis of B10/HVO should be treated as niche-market evidence rather than whole-market evidence.

The supplied file contains 50,000 records across 7,373 anonymised forecourt identifiers and 6 fuel types: B10, B7_PREMIUM, B7_STANDARD, E10, E5, HVO. No missing values were found in the columns analysed (id, node_id, fuel_type, price_pence, recorded_at, source_updated_at).

Scope limitation: the dataset does not include brand, address, local-authority, region, or coordinates. As a result, this report cannot credibly identify geographic price differences or retailer-brand behaviour from the supplied data alone.
Data integrity note: 130 rows have prices below 100 ppl or above 250 ppl. These are retained in all reported statistics because they are present in data.csv, but charts use percentile whiskers where needed so the central distribution remains interpretable.

Methodology & Reproducibility Notes

  • All statistics are calculated directly from /app/benchmark/data.csv; no values are imputed or fabricated.
  • Prices are reported in pence per litre. Decimal values are preserved where present in the CSV.
  • Quartiles use linear interpolation over sorted observed values. IQR equals Q3 minus Q1; price spread equals max minus min.
  • Update lag is calculated in UTC as recorded_at - source_updated_at, expressed in hours.
  • Charts are embedded inline as SVG, making this HTML file self-contained and openable directly in a browser.
Data source described in /app/benchmark/source_brief.md: UK Government Fuel Finder data aggregated by fuelcosts.co.uk via Kaggle dataset jamesb7/fuel-prices-uk. Station identifiers are anonymised node_id hashes.
