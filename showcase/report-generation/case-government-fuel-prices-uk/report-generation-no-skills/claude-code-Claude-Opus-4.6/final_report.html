6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

1. Significant price variation exists across fuel types. The most expensive fuel (HVO) has a median price of 189.9p, while the cheapest (E10) sits at 144.9p — a spread of 45.0p per litre. This reflects the expected premium structure but warrants monitoring to ensure premiums remain proportionate to actual cost differences.
Policy relevance: Consumer protection agencies should monitor premium fuel margins to ensure they reflect genuine cost differentials.
2. Price disparity within fuel types signals competitive imbalances. E5 shows the widest max–min range (1889.0p), indicating some consumers may be paying substantially more than others for the same product. Even the IQR varies meaningfully: B10 has an IQR of 48.8p, while E10 achieves 16.0p.
Policy relevance: Wide intra-fuel price ranges suggest localized market power or information asymmetry. Targeted transparency interventions in high-spread areas could benefit consumers.
3. Update freshness is generally acceptable but with a long tail of stale data. The median update lag of 0.0 hours is reasonable, but 10.7% of records exceed a 24-hour lag, and 294 stations (4.0%) have a median lag exceeding 48 hours.
Policy relevance: Mandatory reporting timeliness thresholds (e.g., 24-hour maximum) should be considered. Stations with persistently stale data undermine the purpose of the open data scheme.
4. Data quality anomalies require attention. 1,011 records show negative update lag, and the maximum lag reaches 52.6 days. These anomalies may reflect clock synchronisation issues or, in extreme cases, retailers submitting outdated prices.
Policy relevance: The data collection infrastructure should implement validation checks to flag and quarantine anomalous timestamps.
5. Not all stations report all fuel types. Station coverage varies significantly across fuel types: B7_STANDARD is reported by the most stations, while B10 has the narrowest coverage. This is structurally expected but means aggregate statistics for niche fuels should be interpreted with caution.
Policy relevance: When assessing market-wide pricing, analysts should weight or segment by fuel type availability to avoid bias from niche fuel data.
6. The dataset provides a robust foundation for ongoing monitoring. With 50,000 records across 7,373 stations and 6 fuel types, this dataset offers sufficient breadth for systematic market surveillance. Combining it with geographic and brand metadata would significantly enhance its analytical value.
Policy relevance: Consider mandating station metadata (brand, location) as part of the open data scheme to enable geographic and competitive analysis.
