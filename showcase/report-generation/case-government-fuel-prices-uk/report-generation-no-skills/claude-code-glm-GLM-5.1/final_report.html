6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

1. Price Levels Vary Significantly by Fuel Type. The average price per litre ranges from 145.9p (E10) to 188.9p (HVO). The diesel–petrol premium (B7_STANDARD vs E10) stands at approximately 21.5p per litre. Implication: Consumers relying on diesel face proportionally higher fuel costs; policy interventions targeting diesel pricing transparency may disproportionately benefit commercial and logistics operators.
2. Substantial Price Disparities Exist Within Fuel Types. The E5 fuel type shows a 1,889.0ppl gap between the cheapest and most expensive stations. Even within the interquartile range, consumers face price bands of 16.0ppl (E10) and 33.0ppl (B7_STANDARD). Implication: The wide price spread suggests the market is not fully competitive at the retail level. Price transparency tools and real-time comparison services are critical for enabling consumers to make informed choices.
3. A Meaningful Share of Reported Prices May Be Stale. The median update lag is 0.0 hours, but 7.4% of records show lags exceeding 48 hours, and 2,523 stations have at least one stale record. Implication: If consumers are making decisions based on the Fuel Finder data, a non-trivial portion of displayed prices may not reflect current forecourt pricing. Strengthening compliance requirements for timely reporting could improve consumer trust and market efficiency.
4. Premium Fuels Have Limited Reporting Coverage. While E10 and B7_STANDARD are reported by the majority of stations, premium fuels such as E5, B7_PREMIUM, and B10 are reported by far fewer. Implication: Limited coverage of premium fuel types reduces the utility of the open data scheme for consumers of these products. Policymakers should consider whether mandated reporting should be extended or whether voluntary reporting incentives are sufficient.
5. Data Quality Concerns in Timestamp Reporting. 1,011 records (2.0%) exhibit negative update lag (source_updated_at after recorded_at), suggesting possible clock synchronisation issues or data pipeline artefacts. Implication: For regulatory monitoring purposes, timestamp data quality should be audited and standardised to ensure reliable freshness metrics.
