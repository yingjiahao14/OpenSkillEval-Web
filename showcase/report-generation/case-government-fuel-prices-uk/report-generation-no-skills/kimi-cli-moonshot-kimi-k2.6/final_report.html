3. Price Spread & Market Competitiveness

A competitive market should show narrow price bands for identical products. The interquartile range (IQR) captures the band within which the middle 50% of stations price their fuel, while the full range (max − min) reveals the extreme disparity consumers may encounter.

B7_STANDARD exhibits the widest IQR (33.0 PPL), meaning a typical diesel consumer could face a £0.33 per litre difference depending on which station they visit. For a 50-litre fill-up, this equates to roughly £16.50 — a material sum for household budgets. B7_PREMIUM also shows a wide IQR (30.0 PPL) and the largest overall range (193.9 PPL), though this is inflated by a small number of anomalous readings.

By contrast, E10 and E5 display tighter IQRs (16.0 and 18.0 PPL respectively), suggesting more uniform pricing in the petrol segment — possibly due to higher consumer price sensitivity and greater media attention.

Policy implication: The substantial IQR for standard diesel (B7_STANDARD) indicates that consumers are not benefiting from uniform competitive pressure across all stations. Interventions that increase price transparency — such as real-time app-based comparison tools — may help narrow this spread by empowering consumers to switch.

5. Station Coverage & Fuel Type Availability

Not every forecourt stocks every fuel grade. Understanding coverage helps assess whether consumers have genuine choice, particularly for alternative or premium fuels.

Figure 9. Distribution of how many fuel types each station reports.

B7_STANDARD (standard diesel) and E10 (standard unleaded) enjoy near-universal coverage, appearing at 6,494 and 6,244 stations respectively. E5 (super unleaded) is available at 4,447 stations — roughly two-thirds of the network. B7_PREMIUM reaches 3,241 stations, while alternative fuels HVO (54 stations) and B10 (34 stations) remain niche offerings.

Most stations (2,717, or 36.9%) report exactly three fuel types, typically E10, B7_STANDARD and either E5 or B7_PREMIUM. Only two stations in the dataset report all six fuel types, and 796 stations (10.8%) report just one — usually either E10 or B7_STANDARD.

Consumer choice insight: While standard petrol and diesel are universally available, consumers seeking premium or low-carbon alternatives face significantly restricted choice. Policy measures to incentivise broader availability of HVO and B10 could support decarbonisation goals, but current coverage suggests these markets are in early stages.

6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Clear price hierarchy exists across fuel grades. E10 averages 145.2 PPL, E5 161.3 PPL, B7_STANDARD 167.0 PPL, and premium grades (B7_PREMIUM / HVO) approach 186–188 PPL. This gradient is broadly consistent with production and distribution costs, but the wide spreads within each grade suggest room for consumer savings through better information.
  2. Diesel consumers face the greatest price dispersion. B7_STANDARD’s IQR of 33.0 PPL is more than double that of E10 (16.0 PPL). For a typical 50-litre purchase, a diesel driver could save or lose roughly £16.50 depending on forecourt choice. Targeted consumer-awareness campaigns or enhanced real-time price apps could help compress this spread.
  3. Data freshness is generally excellent but uneven. The median capture lag is only ~3 minutes, yet roughly 10–13% of standard-fuel records and up to 28% of B10 records are stale by more than 24 hours. Regulators should consider minimum update-frequency requirements, particularly for niche fuels where delayed reporting may mask true market prices.
  4. Extreme outliers warrant data-quality safeguards. One hundred and twenty-six records (0.25%) contained prices below 50 PPL or above 250 PPL — including multiple instances of exactly 1.00 PPL and 999.9 PPL. Automated range-validation rules at the point of data ingestion would prevent these errors from propagating into public-facing price tools.
  5. Alternative-fuel availability is highly limited. HVO and B10 are offered at fewer than 60 stations each. If the government wishes to accelerate uptake of lower-carbon fuels, supply-side incentives or infrastructure grants may be needed to expand forecourt coverage beyond early-adopter locations.
  6. Negative timestamp lags indicate system-level synchronisation issues. 1,011 records show source_updated_at later than recorded_at. While small in proportion (~2%), this undermines confidence in audit trails. A standardised UTC clock-sync protocol across the reporting pipeline would improve data integrity.
