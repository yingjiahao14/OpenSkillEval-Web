6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

1. Diesel consumers face materially higher price dispersion than petrol consumers. B7_STANDARD has an IQR of 33.00p versus E10's 16.00p — more than double. While median diesel (167.90p) is ~23p more expensive than median petrol (144.90p), the spread in diesel prices is the more significant consumer protection concern. Consumer ProtectionPrice Transparency
2. E10 is the most affordable mainstream fuel; E5 premium petrol carries a ~16p price premium over E10. This is consistent with the octane rating premium for super unleaded. Fuel Affordability
3. B7_STANDARD and E10 together account for 68.3% of all records — the market is well-covered for these two fuels. However, B10, HVO, and premium diesel variants have insufficient data for robust statistical inference. Expanding reporting mandates for alternative fuels could improve market transparency. Market CoverageAlternative Fuels
4. Most retailers report prices with a lag of under 5 minutes, but 10.7% of records show lags exceeding 24 hours. This suggests a two-tier compliance landscape: most major retailers maintain near-real-time feeds, while a minority may be failing to update promptly. Reporting ComplianceData Quality
5. Wide max–min spread for B7_PREMIUM (100p) and E10 (95.79p) indicates significant price disparities at the extremes. The IQR provides a more robust signal for typical consumer experience, but outlier prices at both ends could reflect pricing errors or market anomalies that merit monitoring. Retailer Monitoring
6. Station-level fuel type diversity is moderate: 37.6% of stations report 3 fuel types and 27.5% report 2 fuel types. Only 1 station reports all 6 fuel types. This reflects commercial reality — most forecourts do not stock every fuel variant — but consolidated multi-fuel reporting improves market transparency. Station Coverage
