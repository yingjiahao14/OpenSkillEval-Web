Five Critical Takeaways for Executive Leadership

FINDING 01
Automation risk has nearly doubled over 20 years
Average AI risk score rose from 0.30 in 2015 to a projected 0.51 by 2035 — a 70% increase. By 2035, nearly 1 in 6 roles is classified as at-risk (survival class 0), up from roughly 1 in 11 in 2015. The acceleration is continuous with no plateau in sight.
FINDING 02
Risk is role-specific, not sector-wide — with a 5× spread
Business Analysts (avg risk: 0.75) and Data Analysts (0.65) face acute exposure, while AI Researchers (0.15), Cybersecurity Analysts (0.25), and ML Engineers (0.25) remain well-insulated. Blanket risk mitigation will miss the mark — targeted intervention by role is essential.
FINDING 03
High automation risk carries a steep salary penalty
By 2035, median salary in low-risk roles reaches $49K vs. $24K in high-risk roles — a 2× gap that has widened from 1.5× in 2015. Compensation is a leading indicator: roles where pay is stagnating are likely also facing displacement risk.
FINDING 04
Experience is the strongest protection — seniority nearly eliminates at-risk classification
Only 5.5% of Senior-level workers fall in survival class 0, versus 16.1% of Entry-level workers. Conversely, 62.8% of Senior workers hold the strongest survival class (2). Reskilling programs must target early-career professionals before displacement occurs.
FINDING 05
Deep technical skills are the survival differentiator
Workers in survival class 2 (strong) average a skill demand score of 82.9, versus 74.2 for at-risk workers — an 11.7% gap. AI Researchers, ML Engineers, and Cybersecurity Analysts — all powered by Deep Learning, Python, and Security skills — show near-zero at-risk rates. Investment in these competencies has measurable survival ROI.

💰
High-Risk Roles Earn Half the Salary of Low-Risk Roles by 2035
In 2015, low-risk roles earned a 49% salary premium over high-risk roles ($26.8K vs. $18.0K). By 2035, this gap expands to a 103% premium ($49.0K vs. $24.1K). Compensation trajectories are a lagging but reliable signal of AI displacement pressure.
🌏
India's Salary Gap Creates Workforce Planning Complexity
India's median salary ($12.4K) sits at 29% of the USA median ($43.4K). When building global teams, risk exposure profiles and compensation structures require country-specific treatment — global averages will obscure material differences.
🎓
Education Level Produces Minimal Salary Differentiation
Bachelor ($31.6K), Master ($31.4K), and PhD ($31.7K) holders earn nearly identical median salaries. Role selection and skill depth — not academic credentials alone — drive compensation outcomes in this dataset.

Total Job Market Remains Robust Despite Automation
Annual job openings hover near 14–16 million across all years with no sustained decline. Automation is displacing specific roles — not shrinking total labor demand. The composition, not the volume, is shifting.
📉
India Faces Acute Survival Pressure
India reports the highest at-risk rate (21.4% class 0) and the lowest strong-survival rate (8.8% class 2) of all six countries. This likely reflects the concentration of lower-experience, higher-volume roles in the dataset for India and suggests geographic-specific reskilling urgency.
🏆
Best-Positioned Profile: Senior + AI/Cloud Role + USA/Australia
Combining senior experience (62.8% class 2 rate), an AI- or cloud-adjacent role (AI Researcher: 88.9% class 2, ML Engineer: 71.2%), and a high-income geography (USA: 58.1% class 2) produces the strongest possible survival profile in this dataset.
Section 6

Strategic Recommendations

Each recommendation is grounded in a specific data finding. Act on these in priority order.

1
Launch Role-Targeted Reskilling — Not Generic Programs
Business Analysts (avg risk 0.75, 57% at-risk class) and Data Analysts (avg risk 0.65, 43.5% at-risk) are the most urgent reskilling priorities. Redirect skill development investment from broad digital literacy to Python, Deep Learning, and Security for these populations. Generic programs will dilute ROI.
Data basis: 5× risk spread across roles (0.15–0.75); skill demand gap of 8.7 points between survival class 2 and class 0 workers.
Prioritize Entry-Level Reskilling Before Mid-Career
2
Entry-level workers carry a 16.1% at-risk rate — nearly 3× the 5.5% rate of senior workers. Early-career intervention has the highest leverage: reshaping skill trajectories before automation exposure compounds over years of experience in vulnerable roles.
Data basis: Survival class 0 rates — Entry: 16.1%, Mid: 11.4%, Senior: 5.5%; Class 2 rates — Entry: 22.5%, Senior: 62.8%.
Treat Stagnating Salaries as an Early Automation Warning Signal
3
Use compensation data as a leading indicator when prioritizing workforce planning decisions. Roles in the High-Risk category earn 103% less than Low-Risk roles by 2035. Annual salary reviews that reveal widening role-level compensation gaps should trigger proactive risk assessment and redeployment planning — before headcount decisions are forced.
Data basis: Median salary gap widened from 1.49× (2015) to 2.03× (2035) between Low Risk and High Risk roles.
Increase Headcount in AI-Adjacent and Cybersecurity Roles
4
AI Researcher, ML Engineer, and Cybersecurity Analyst roles show 0% at-risk classification, 70–89% strong-survival rates, and the highest salary ceilings. Job opening data shows consistent annual volumes of 1.1–1.9M for these roles. These are talent markets where under-investment has compounding costs — both in capability gap and in competitive disadvantage.
Data basis: AI Researcher class 2 rate: 88.9%; ML Engineer: 71.2%; Cybersecurity Analyst: 53.2%; median salaries $39K–$43K across all years.
Deploy a Geographic-Sensitive Risk Management Framework
5
India's 21.4% at-risk rate and 8.8% strong-survival rate are materially worse than the USA (6.2% at-risk, 58.1% class 2). Global workforce strategies must account for geographic variation — what constitutes an acceptable risk level for talent in the USA may represent an acute risk concentration for India-based teams doing comparable roles.
Data basis: Country survival class 0 rates — India: 21.4%, Germany: 10.1%, UK: 9.7%, Canada: 9.0%, Australia: 9.9%, USA: 6.2%.
Do Not Over-Index on Education Level Alone for Hiring or Development Decisions
6
Bachelor, Master, and PhD holders show near-identical median salaries ($31.4K–$31.7K) and statistically similar survival class distributions. Role assignment and skill development deliver more measurable returns than credential screening. Redirect hiring criteria toward demonstrated skill depth — particularly Python, Deep Learning, and Security — over degree requirements.
Data basis: Median salary spread across education levels — $31.4K (Master) to $31.7K (PhD), a difference of under 1%.
