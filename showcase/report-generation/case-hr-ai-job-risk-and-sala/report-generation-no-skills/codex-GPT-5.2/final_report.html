Executive Overview

What moved most (2015→2035)

  • Automation exposure rises materially: average AI risk score increases from 0.295 (2015) to 0.508 (2035).
  • Compensation rises while survivability declines: global median salary grows from $24,247 to $36,763, while survival rate falls from 91.1% to 83.7%.
  • Openings expand overall: total job openings increase from 14,024,622 to 14,838,117.
  • Pay tends to be lower where risk is higher: salary-risk correlation is negative (r = -0.245 overall), and the High Risk group has a lower median salary than other risk groups.

Interpretation note: This dataset is synthetic and includes projected years (2025–2035). Use trends for strategic direction; validate with internal labor data before committing capital.

Strategic Recommendations

Each recommendation below is explicitly grounded in the computed findings from data.csv (no external numbers).

Prioritize reskilling in high-risk business/process roles

Evidence from the dataset: The roles with the highest average AI risk are concentrated in business analysis and reporting (e.g., top average risk roles include Business Analyst and Data Analyst). In the dataset, the High Risk category also shows materially lower job survival outcomes.

Action: Shift these populations toward adjacent, lower-risk work by pairing domain expertise with automation-era skills (e.g., requirements engineering, AI product ops, data quality governance, and decision intelligence).

Use compensation as an early-warning signal for automation exposure

Evidence from the dataset: Salary and AI risk are negatively correlated in this dataset (Pearson r = -0.245 overall; also negative in sampled years such as 2015 and 2035). High-risk roles tend to sit at lower median salaries than medium/low risk groups.

Action: Overlay internal pay bands with task-automation assessments: when pay is stagnant while AI capability rises, treat it as a trigger to redesign roles (human-in-the-loop), not only to reduce headcount.

Protect hiring pipelines in roles with expanding openings

Evidence from the dataset: Total job openings rise from 14,024,622 (2015) to 14,838,117 (2035) in the dataset, and several roles show strong growth in openings by 2035.

Action: For roles with sustained openings growth, build (1) early-career pipelines, (2) internal mobility paths, and (3) AI-augmented productivity playbooks to scale output without scaling cost linearly.

Anchor future-proofing on rising skills demand

Evidence from the dataset: The fastest-growing primary skills by average demand between 2015 and 2035 include Java, Python, AWS, SQL, Excel.

Action: Align learning investments and role architectures to these skills; define proficiency ladders and validate through project-based credentialing (internal gigs).

Plan for declining survival rates even as salaries rise

Evidence from the dataset: The job survival rate (class ≥ 1) declines from 91.1% (2015) to 83.7% (2035), while global median salary increases from $24,247 to $36,763.

Action: Budget for continuous redesign: allocate capacity to (a) workflow automation deployment, (b) controls & governance, and (c) redeployment of affected workers into growing demand areas.
