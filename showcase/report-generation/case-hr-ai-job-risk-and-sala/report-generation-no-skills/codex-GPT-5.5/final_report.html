Strategic Recommendations

Each recommendation below ties directly to a quantified finding in this dataset and is intended for workforce strategy, talent investment, and reskilling portfolio decisions.

Prioritize automation-aware redesign for high-risk roles.
Business Analyst carries the highest 2035 average AI risk among roles at 0.85; redesign work around human judgment, governance, customer context, and AI-supervised workflows before displacement pressure peaks.
Protect and scale resilient technical capability.
Data Scientist has the strongest 2035 survival rate among roles at 100.0%, while Python leads 2035 skill demand at 82.9; expand hiring and internal academies around these durable capability pools.
Use compensation as a risk signal, not only a retention signal.
Salary and AI risk show a dataset-wide Pearson correlation of -0.24; median pay is $21,683 in High Risk records versus $34,105 in Low Risk records, so workforce planning should treat lower-paid, high-risk job families as priority reskilling populations while still monitoring costly roles with rising risk.
Allocate reskilling by trend velocity.
Average AI risk rises from 0.30 in 2015 to 0.51 in 2035, while skill demand rises from 78.9 to 80.2; fund annual skill refresh cycles rather than one-time reskilling programs.
Plan geography-specific workforce mixes.
By 2035, India has the highest average country risk at 0.52, while UK is lowest at 0.49; tailor location strategy to local risk, salary, and openings rather than applying a uniform global policy.
