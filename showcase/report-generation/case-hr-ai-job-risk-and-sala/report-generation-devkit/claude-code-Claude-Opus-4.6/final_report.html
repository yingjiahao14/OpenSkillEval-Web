Key Takeaways

  1. Automation risk is climbing steadily. The average AI risk score has risen from 0.30 in 2015 to 0.51 in 2035 (+72.0%), with Business Analyst facing the highest average risk (0.75) and AI Researcher the lowest (0.15).
  2. Salaries continue to grow, but unevenly. Global median salary grew +51.6% from $24,247 to $36,763, with USA leading at a median of $43,422. However, high-risk roles command lower median pay than low-risk roles, indicating a salary–risk divergence.
  3. Job survival varies sharply by role. Overall, 89.0% of records are classified as surviving (class ≥ 1), but survival rates range from 43% to 100% across job titles, making role selection a decisive factor in career resilience.
  4. Skill demand is polarizing. Java shows the largest demand growth (+2.1 points from early to late period), while certain skills are plateauing, creating a widening gap between future-proof and declining competencies.
  5. Job openings are growing. Total openings changed by +5.8% from 2015 to 2035, with AI-adjacent roles dominating growth while high-risk categories see relative stagnation.

6. Strategic Recommendations

The following recommendations are each grounded in specific findings from the preceding analysis. They are designed for executive leadership and workforce strategists making decisions about talent investment, reskilling programs, and organizational restructuring.

Recommendation 1

Prioritize Reskilling for High-Risk Roles

Business Analyst roles carry the highest average automation risk (0.75) and a survival rate of just 43%. Invest in structured transition pathways that redirect talent in these roles toward lower-risk, higher-demand positions — particularly those requiring Deep Learning skills, which show the highest job survival rate (100%).

Data basis: Section 2 (Risk by Job Title) and Section 4 (Skill Survival Rates)
Recommendation 2

Align Compensation Strategy with Risk Profiles

The salary–AI risk correlation of r = -0.2448 indicates that higher-risk roles tend to pay less, which may create retention problems if workers in these roles perceive limited upside. Use risk-category salary benchmarking (Low Risk median: $34,105; High Risk median: $21,683) to restructure compensation bands and create financial incentives for internal mobility toward resilient roles.

Data basis: Section 3 (Salary × AI Risk Correlation)
Recommendation 3

Invest in High-Growth Skill Development

Java shows the largest demand score increase (+2.1 points) between the early (2015–2017) and late (2033–2035) periods. Build training pipelines around this and adjacent skills to ensure your workforce has the competencies the market will reward most. Skills with declining or stagnant demand should be de-prioritized in L&D budgets.

Data basis: Section 4 (Skill Demand Growth Chart)
Recommendation 4

Differentiate Talent Strategy by Geography

Median salaries vary substantially by country—USA leads at $43,422—yet AI risk scores are relatively uniform across geographies. This creates arbitrage opportunities: building teams in countries with lower salary baselines but equivalent skill availability can reduce cost while maintaining resilience. Target countries with strong survival rates and below-median compensation.

Data basis: Section 3 (Salary by Country) and Section 2 (Risk by Country)
Recommendation 5

Elevate Experience-Level Planning

Senior professionals show higher survival rates (94.5%) compared to entry-level (83.9%). Protect senior expertise through knowledge-transfer and mentoring programs while strengthening entry-level onboarding with AI-complementary skills.

Data basis: Section 2 (Risk by Experience) and Section 5 (Survival by Experience × Job Title)
Recommendation 6

Monitor Job Openings as a Leading Indicator

Total job openings shifted +5.8% from 2015 to 2035. Use year-over-year opening trends by role as an early-warning system: declining openings in a category signal contraction 2–3 years before layoffs become necessary. Integrate this metric into quarterly workforce planning reviews.

Data basis: Section 5 (Job Openings Trends)
