Executive Overview

High-level summary of dataset scope and critical findings on AI's impact across the 2015–2035 horizon.

Dataset Scope

12,343 records spanning 2015–2035 across 6 countries, 10 distinct job roles, and 9 primary skills. Covers Entry, Mid, and Senior experience tiers with Bachelor, Master, and PhD education levels.

Historical & Projected Periods

2015–2024 represents historical / observed data. 2025–2035 represents simulated projections based on current AI adoption trajectories and labor market modeling. All future figures should be interpreted as trend projections, not predictions.

Critical Finding 1: The average AI risk score climbs from 0.295 in 2015 to 0.508 in 2035 — a +21% increase. This signals a structural shift in automation exposure across nearly all roles and geographies. No job category is fully immune, though the pace of risk escalation varies significantly by role type.
Critical Finding 2: There is a strong negative correlation (r = -0.809) between median salary and AI risk. Higher-paying, specialized roles — particularly those requiring advanced degrees and technical mastery — carry substantially lower automation risk. Conversely, lower-salary roles in more routine-oriented functions face the highest displacement threat. This creates a compounding inequality risk: AI disruption disproportionately threatens lower-income workers.
Critical Finding 3: Overall job survival stands at 89.0% when counting roles classified as moderate (class 1) or strong (class 2) survival. However, this aggregate masks significant variation. By 2035, roles requiring Java expertise, advanced degrees, and located in high-investment markets show the strongest resilience profiles.
