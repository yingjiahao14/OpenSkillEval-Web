  • Automation risk accelerates materially: average AI risk rises from 0.30 in 2015 to 0.51 in 2035, a 72.0% increase; the high-risk share more than doubles from 10.7% to 23.0%.
  • Survival remains majority-positive but deteriorates: the survival rate declines from 91.1% to 83.7%, a -7.4 pp change, indicating rising displacement pressure despite continued market demand.
  • Risk is concentrated by role, not evenly spread: Business Analyst (0.75) and Data Analyst (0.65) carry the highest average AI risk; AI Researcher (0.15), Cybersecurity Analyst (0.25), and ML Engineer (0.25) are most resilient.

  • Salary growth continues despite risk: median salary increases from $24,247 to $36,763 (51.6%), but growth is strongest in some technical roles and markets rather than in the most exposed categories.
  • Openings are broadly stable, not explosive: total annual openings rise 5.8% from 2015 to 2035, while skill demand changes only 1.7%; this points to strategic reallocation more than simple labor-market expansion.

Strategic Recommendations

The recommendations below are tied directly to the observed dataset findings and are intended for workforce planning, reskilling investment, and talent acquisition strategy.

Priority 1
Protect

Redesign and reskill Business Analyst and Data Analyst work

These are the two highest-risk roles in the dataset: Business Analyst average risk is 0.75 with 43.0% survival, and Data Analyst average risk is 0.65 with 56.5% survival. Create targeted reskilling paths from Excel/SQL-heavy execution into AI-assisted analysis, Python, product analytics, governance, and decision-support roles.

Priority 2
Invest

Scale AI-native and security talent pipelines

AI Researcher, ML Engineer, Cybersecurity Analyst, and DevOps Engineer all show 100.0% overall survival in the dataset. Cybersecurity Analyst openings grow 49.1% by 2035, making security a high-confidence growth lane; Deep Learning combines the lowest skill risk (0.15) with the highest median salary ($42,845).

Priority 3
Segment

Use salary-risk segmentation for intervention budgeting

The salary-risk correlation is negative (Pearson r=-0.24), and high-risk jobs have much lower median pay ($21,683) than medium-risk jobs ($34,710). Allocate reskilling funding toward high-risk, lower-paid groups where individual self-funded transition capacity may be lower.

Priority 4
Plan

Adopt country-specific workforce plans rather than a single global playbook

India shows the highest average country risk (0.401) and lowest median salary ($12,448), while the USA has the highest median salary ($43,422) and elevated risk (0.399). Use local risk, salary, and openings data to size redeployment, hiring, and automation governance.

Priority 5
Monitor

Track survival rate and high-risk share as annual workforce KPIs

Average AI risk rises 72.0% and high-risk share rises 115.2% from 2015 to 2035, while survival falls -7.4 pp. Add these metrics to workforce dashboards and require annual business-unit action plans where risk outpaces reskilling completion.

Methodology & Data Integrity Notes

  • All calculations use /app/benchmark/data.csv; no external numbers or fabricated estimates are included.
  • The source brief identifies the dataset as synthetic and trend-based. Values from 2025–2035 should be interpreted as simulated projections, not observed outcomes.
  • Job survival rate is defined exactly as requested: records with job_survival_class >= 1 divided by total records.
  • Trend changes compare 2015 with 2035 unless otherwise noted. Median salary is used for salary trend KPIs to reduce outlier sensitivity.
  • Salary values are in USD and are not purchasing-power adjusted, so cross-country salary comparisons should be interpreted with caution.
