Key Findings

Finding 1 — AI risk is rising across the board. The global average AI risk score has increased by 72.0% from 2015 to 2035, climbing from 0.295 to 0.508. Every job category shows upward risk pressure, with the steepest acceleration post-2025 as projected AI adoption intensifies.
Finding 2 — Business Analysts and Data Analysts face the highest automation exposure. With average risk scores of 0.750 and 0.649 respectively, these roles—heavily reliant on structured data processing and rule-based analysis—are the most vulnerable. Conversely, AI Researchers (0.150) and ML Engineers (0.251) occupy the lowest-risk tier.
Finding 3 — Salary and AI risk are negatively correlated (r = -0.245). Lower-salary roles face higher automation risk, indicating that AI disproportionately threatens lower-compensation positions. The median salary for high-risk roles stands at $21,683, compared to $34,105 for low-risk roles.
Finding 4 — Deep Learning and Python skills show the strongest future-proofing signal. These skills combine high demand scores with low associated AI risk, making them the most resilient competencies through 2035. Excel, while high in demand, is tied to the highest-risk roles.
Finding 5 — Job survival rates remain robust overall but vary sharply by role and education. AI Researcher roles show the highest survival rate (100.0%), while Business Analyst roles lag at 43.0%. Education and seniority provide meaningful protection: PhD-educated and Senior-level professionals show survival rates of 89.7% and 94.5% respectively.

6. Strategic Recommendations

Each recommendation below is grounded in specific data findings from this analysis.

1. Prioritize Reskilling for High-Risk Analytical Roles
Business Analysts and Data Analysts carry the highest AI risk scores (0.750 and 0.649 respectively). Invest in structured reskilling programs that transition these professionals toward AI-augmented analytics, machine learning operations, and strategic data interpretation—competencies where human judgment remains essential.
Data source: AI Risk Score by Job Title analysis (Section 2.2)
2. Invest in Deep Learning and Python Talent Pipelines
Deep Learning and Python combine high demand scores with the lowest associated AI risk. These skills are foundational to the AI economy itself, making their practitioners the least vulnerable to displacement. Expand hiring, training, and retention programs for these competencies.
Data source: Skill Demand vs AI Risk analysis (Section 4.3); Average Skill Demand Score by Primary Skill (Section 4.1)
3. Decouple Salary Strategy from Risk Assumptions
The negative correlation between salary and AI risk (r = -0.245) indicates that higher compensation does guarantee lower automation exposure. Lower-paid routine roles face disproportionate risk. Compensation strategy should be supplemented with role-specific risk mitigation plans rather than assuming that senior/high-paid positions are inherently safe.
Data source: Salary vs AI Risk correlation (Section 3.4); Dual-axis trend (Section 3.4)
4. Phase Out Excel-Dependent Workflows
Excel, while still in high demand, is tied to the highest AI risk category and the lowest survival rates. Accelerate the migration of Excel-centric processes to AI-augmented analytics platforms and upskill Excel-dependent staff on Python, SQL, and BI tools that enhance rather than compete with AI capabilities.
Data source: Skill Demand vs Job Survival (Section 4.3); Excel risk profile analysis
5. Leverage Education and Experience as Survival Multipliers
PhD-educated professionals show a 89.7% survival rate, and Senior-level professionals show 94.5%. Invest in continuous education sponsorship and mid-career advancement programs. The data shows that advanced education and deeper experience provide meaningful buffers against AI displacement.
Data source: Job Survival by Education and Experience Level (Section 5)
6. Monitor Convergence of Risk Across Roles
Risk scores are converging across all job titles, with even low-risk roles like AI Researchers and ML Engineers showing upward risk trajectories. This signals that current safe-haven roles may face growing pressure by 2030–2035. Establish a biannual risk audit to reassess role vulnerability and adjust workforce strategy proactively.
Data source: Risk Evolution by Role Over Time (Section 2.4); Convergence trend
7. Regionalize Workforce Planning
While AI risk is relatively evenly distributed across countries (range: 0.393–0.401), salary disparities are significant (median salary range: $12,448–$43,422). Workforce plans should account for this asymmetry: the same risk level carries different financial implications depending on geography.
Data source: Risk by Country (Section 2.3); Salary by Country (Section 3.3)
