  • Average AI risk score rises from 0.295 (2015) to 0.508 (2035), a +0.213 absolute increase (+72.0%).

  • Total job openings increase from 14,024,622 (2015) to 14,838,117 (2035), +813,495 (+5.8%).
  • Compensation inversely correlates with AI risk: corr(salary, ai_risk_score) = -0.245 overall.

Strategic Recommendations

Each recommendation traces back to a quantified finding above.

  1. Prioritize transition plans for the highest-risk roles (e.g., Business Analyst, Data Analyst) where average risk is 0.75–0.65, and High Risk survival rate is only 46.8%.
  2. Protect and expand pipelines into lower-risk, higher-compensation roles (e.g., AI Researcher median $42,845; Low/Medium Risk medians $34,105/$34,710).
  3. Align reskilling to skills with the strongest upward demand signals (Java, Python, AWS, SQL), each rising by ~2.65–3.95 points on average between 2015 and 2035.
  4. Use compensation as a risk-screening signal: corr(salary, AI risk) = -0.245, with High Risk median pay $21,683 below Low/Medium Risk $34,105/$34,710.
  5. Bias hiring toward roles with positive openings CAGR (Cybersecurity Analyst, Product Manager, Business Analyst) and scrutinize roles with sustained contractions (DevOps Engineer, Data Scientist, AI Researcher).
