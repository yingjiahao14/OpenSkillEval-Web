Most critical takeaways:
  • AI exposure rises materially over the horizon: average risk increases from 0.30 in 2015 to 0.51 in 2035, a 72% increase.
  • Labor market resilience weakens at the margin: the survival rate declines from 91.1% to 83.7%, while job openings grow only 5.8% between endpoint years.
  • Automation risk is not salary-neutral: salary and AI risk have a Pearson correlation of -0.24; high-risk roles show a median salary of $21,683, 37.5% below medium-risk roles.
  • Risk is concentrated by role more than by country: Business Analyst (0.75) and Data Analyst (0.65) lead average risk, while AI Researcher (0.15) is the lowest-risk role.
  • Future-proofing clusters around AI, cloud, security, and engineering roles: AI Researcher, ML Engineer, DevOps Engineer, and Cybersecurity Analyst all show 100.0% survival rates in this dataset.

Important limitation: future years 2025–2035 are simulated projections in the source dataset, not observed labor-market outcomes. All reported figures are calculated directly from data.csv.

Highest-Risk Pattern

Business Analyst and Data Analyst are the only roles with average AI risk above 0.60. Their survival rates are 43.0% and 56.5%, respectively, far below the dataset-wide 89.0% survival rate.

Geographic Pattern

Country-level average risk is tightly clustered: India is highest at 0.40, while the UK is lowest at 0.39. Survival varies more sharply, from India at 78.6% to the USA at 93.8%.

Salary Trends & Segmentation

Median salary rises from $24,247 in 2015 to $36,763 in 2035, a 51.6% endpoint increase. The increase is broad, but the size of gains differs materially by role and geography.

Salary × Automation Risk Correlation

The dataset shows a negative salary-risk relationship: Pearson correlation is -0.24. High-risk records have a median salary of $21,683, compared with $34,710 for medium-risk and $34,105 for low-risk records. This suggests the most automatable job segments are also relatively lower-compensated in the dataset.

Correlation is calculated at the record level between salary and ai_risk_score; it should not be interpreted as causal.

Strategic Recommendations

1. Prioritize reskilling for analytics and business operations roles.Data basis: Business Analyst average AI risk is 0.75 with 43.0% survival; Data Analyst risk is 0.65 with 56.5% survival. Focus transition pathways from Excel/SQL reporting toward Python, cloud analytics, security, and AI-enabled decision support.
2. Protect and scale AI-native, cloud, DevOps, and security talent.Data basis: AI Researcher, ML Engineer, DevOps Engineer, and Cybersecurity Analyst each show 100.0% survival, with AI Researcher carrying the lowest average risk (0.15). These roles form the most resilient workforce core through 2035.
3. Use risk-adjusted compensation planning.Data basis: salary-risk correlation is -0.24; high-risk records have median salary $21,683 versus $34,710 for medium-risk records. Compensation strategy should distinguish automatable capacity roles from scarce transformation roles, rather than using one enterprise-wide salary inflation assumption.
4. Localize workforce strategy by geography.Data basis: country average risk is similar, but survival ranges from 78.6% to 93.8%. India combines the highest country risk (0.40) with the lowest survival (78.6%), making it the highest-priority market for redeployment and skills investment in this dataset.
5. Manage openings growth with scenario-based workforce planning.Data basis: annual openings increase only 5.8% from 2015 to 2035 while survival declines -7.4 pp and AI risk rises 72%. Workforce plans should model productivity substitution, role redesign, and redeployment capacity together instead of relying on openings growth alone.

Data and methodology: Analysis uses `/app/benchmark/data.csv` only. Salary values are annual USD. Survival rate is calculated as the proportion of records where job_survival_class >= 1. Trend changes compare 2015 and 2035 endpoint aggregates unless otherwise stated.
