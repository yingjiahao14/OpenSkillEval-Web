The dataset combines historical patterns (2015–2024) with trend-based projections (2025–2035) to assess how AI is reshaping the global workforce. Critical Finding: AI risk scores have risen by 72% from 0.30 in 2015 to a projected 0.51 in 2035. The acceleration is sharpest post-2030, with the last five years alone contributing a +9.5-point increase. This trajectory demands proactive workforce planning now — roles that appear secure today may face significant automation pressure within a decade.

The Five Takeaways Every Executive Needs

  1. Automation risk is accelerating, not linear. After hovering around 0.33–0.37 through 2025, the mean AI risk score accelerates to 0.51 by 2035. The post-2030 period shows the steepest climb — organizations have a narrowing window to act.
  2. A stark bifurcation is forming between AI-resilient and AI-vulnerable roles. Business Analysts (risk: 0.75) and Data Analysts (risk: 0.65) face existential automation threat, while AI Researchers (risk: 0.15), Cybersecurity Analysts (risk: 0.25), and ML Engineers (risk: 0.25) remain resilient. The survival rate gap between these groups exceeds 55 percentage points.
  3. Salary is a strong proxy for AI resilience. There is a significant negative correlation (−0.66) between job survival class and AI risk score, and a positive correlation (+0.62) between salary and survival class. High-risk roles command median salaries 40–50% lower than low-risk counterparts.
  4. Skill choice is the single strongest determinant of survival. Deep Learning, Security, and Docker — associated with AI Research, Cybersecurity, and DevOps roles — show 100% survival rates. In contrast, Excel and SQL — tied to Business Analysis and Data Analysis — show survival rates below 57%.
  5. Geography and seniority matter, but role matters more. India's survival rate (78.6%) lags the USA (93.8%) by 15 points, and Entry-level workers face a 10-point survival gap vs. Senior workers. However, even Entry-level AI Researchers achieve strong survival outcomes, while Senior Business Analysts in India struggle significantly.

6. Strategic Recommendations

Each recommendation below is directly grounded in a specific data finding from this report. No generic advice — every action is traceable to empirical evidence.

Recommendation 1

Establish an AI-Resilience Reskilling Pipeline for Business Analysts and Data Analysts

Data Basis: Business Analysts (AI risk: 0.75, rising to 0.85 by 2035) and Data Analysts (0.65 → 0.75) represent 21% of all records and face survival rates of just 43% and 57% respectively. These roles account for 66.7M total job openings — the largest combined volume — yet are on a clear trajectory toward AI displacement.

Action: Create structured transition pathways from these roles into adjacent, AI-resilient positions: Business Analyst → Product Manager (risk 0.40, survival 99.5%); Data Analyst → Data Scientist (risk 0.35, survival 99.8%). The skill delta is manageable: Python proficiency and strategic thinking for the former, machine learning fundamentals for the latter.

Recommendation 2

Weight Talent Investment Toward Skills, Not Titles

Data Basis: Skill demand scores are nearly uniform across all nine primary skills (range: 78.9–79.9), but survival rates range from 43% (Excel) to 100% (Deep Learning). Demand scores are essentially non-informative as standalone signals — the correlation between skill demand and job survival is only +0.27, far weaker than the role-survival link (−0.66 for AI risk).

Action: Build hiring and L&D frameworks around skill-role pairs rather than skills in isolation. Python + Data Science = resilience; Python + Data Analysis = vulnerability. Investment in AI/ML, security, and DevOps competencies delivers the highest survival ROI regardless of initial role.

Recommendation 3

Concentrate Reskilling Investment in India and Other High-Risk Geographies

Data Basis: India exhibits a 78.6% survival rate — 15 points below the USA — driven by a heavy concentration of Business Analyst and Data Analyst roles and the lowest median salary ($12,448) among all countries. Entry-level Business Analysts in India have a near-zero survival class (0.03) with a median salary of $6,436.

Action: Prioritize reskilling programs in India, targeting the Business Analyst → Product Manager and Data Analyst → Data Scientist pathways. The wage uplift from successful transitions (>2× salary improvement) generates a strong ROI for training investment.

Recommendation 4

Use Salary Premiums as an AI-Resilience Early Warning System

Data Basis: The salary–survival correlation (+0.62) and the salary–AI risk correlation (−0.24) establish compensation as a reliable leading indicator of automation exposure. High-risk roles earn a 36% median salary penalty compared to low-risk roles. The correlation is stronger than skill demand or job opening volume.

Action: Integrate salary benchmarking into workforce risk assessments. Roles where median compensation is stagnant or declining relative to inflation — after controlling for experience and location — should be flagged for proactive reskilling review, even if current demand scores appear healthy.

Recommendation 5

Accelerate Senior-Level Hiring in AI-Adjacent Roles Before 2030

Data Basis: The AI risk score accelerates sharply post-2030, rising from 0.43 to 0.51 in the final five years — a 9.5-point increase vs. 7.9 points over the preceding 15 years. Senior roles maintain the highest survival rate (94.5%) and command the highest salaries ($48,318 median), but the talent pool is limited.

Action: Front-load senior hiring in AI Research, ML Engineering, Cybersecurity, and DevOps through 2030. The combination of highest survival rates and an accelerating risk environment means competition for these profiles will intensify. Build internal talent pipelines via accelerated mid-to-senior promotion tracks in these disciplines.

Recommendation 6

Build an AI-Literate Workforce at All Education Levels

Data Basis: Education level shows no meaningful differentiation in AI risk (Bachelor: 0.395, Master: 0.397, PhD: 0.396) or job survival (88.1–89.7%). The education-survival gap is less than 2 percentage points. What matters is which skills are acquired, not which degree is held.

Action: Shift L&D investment from credential-based programs to competency-based AI literacy programs. Python, cloud infrastructure, and machine learning fundamentals should be accessible to employees at all education levels — not reserved for advanced degree tracks.

Methodology & Data Notes

This report analyzes 12,343 synthetic records from the Kaggle "AI Job Risk and Salary Dataset 2015–2035" (shree0910). The dataset spans 10 job titles, 6 countries, 3 experience levels, and 3 education levels. Historical years (2015–2024) reflect realistic patterns; projected years (2025–2035) represent forward-looking simulations. All values cited are computed directly from the dataset. No data was fabricated or extrapolated beyond what the dataset provides. The dataset contains no missing values or encoding anomalies. Salary values are in nominal USD; purchasing power parity adjustments are not applied.
