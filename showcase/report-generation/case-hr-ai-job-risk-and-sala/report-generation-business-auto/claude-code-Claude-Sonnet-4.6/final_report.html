Critical Takeaways for Leadership
Critical Risk

AI automation risk is rising from 0.295 in 2015 to a projected 0.508 by 2035 — a 72% increase. Business Analysts (0.750) and Data Analysts (0.649) face the highest near-term displacement risk.

Salary Divergence

High-risk roles earn 37% less (median $21,683) than low-risk roles ($34,105), revealing a growing premium for automation-resistant skills. India-based workers face compounded risk with both high AI scores and lowest median salaries ($12,448).

Resilient Roles

AI Researchers (risk: 0.150), Cybersecurity Analysts (0.251), and ML Engineers (0.253) are the most automation-resistant roles. USA-based workers command the highest median salary ($43,422) globally — nearly 3.5× India's level.

Survival Outlook

Overall job survival rate is currently 89% but is trending down toward 83.7% by 2035. Investing in Deep Learning, Python, and Security skills dramatically improves survival probability across all experience levels.

Skills Premium

All 9 primary skills show near-identical average demand scores (~79–80), but roles using Python and Deep Learning cluster in the lowest-risk, highest-salary segments — making them the most strategically valuable upskilling targets.

Strategic Recommendations
Data-grounded actions for executive leadership and workforce strategists
1
Immediately Prioritize Reskilling of Business Analysts and Data Analysts
These two roles carry average AI risk scores of 0.750 and 0.649 respectively — the two highest in the dataset. Organizations with significant workforce concentrations in these roles face the greatest near-term disruption. Structured reskilling programs into Python, ML Engineering, or cloud infrastructure should begin immediately, with a 12–24 month conversion roadmap per cohort.
Evidence: Business Analyst risk = 0.750 (5× AI Researcher); Data Analyst risk = 0.649; both Excel/SQL primary skills carry the highest risk-value burden in the skills matrix.
2
Invest in AI Research and Cybersecurity as Core Strategic Capability Pillars
AI Researchers (risk 0.150) and Cybersecurity Analysts (risk 0.251) are the two lowest-risk, highest-future-relevance roles in the analysis. Building internal pipelines for these disciplines — through university partnerships, apprenticeship programs, and competitive compensation benchmarking against the USA/UK market — will create durable competitive advantages through 2035.
Evidence: AI Researcher and Cybersecurity Analyst consistently achieve job_survival_class 2 (Strong Survival) across all years analyzed; AI risk scores have remained stable for these roles even as the market average rises toward 0.50.
3
Adopt a "Python + Deep Learning" Skills-First Hiring and Promotion Framework
The analysis demonstrates that roles primarily using Python and Deep Learning cluster in the lowest-risk quadrant of the skills matrix. HR and workforce planning functions should integrate skill-level proficiency assessments for these technologies into all hiring evaluations and internal promotion frameworks. Compensation modeling should price Python/Deep Learning expertise at a premium consistent with the 37% salary gap between high-risk and low-risk roles.
Evidence: ML Engineers (Python) earn median salaries significantly above market average; AI Researchers (Deep Learning) command the highest individual salaries in the dataset ($113,589 peak). Low-risk roles command $12,422 annual premium over high-risk roles (median gap).
4
Develop a Differentiated India Market Strategy Addressing the Dual-Risk Challenge
India-based workers face the most adverse combination in the dataset: median salary of $12,448 (71% below USA) AND the highest average AI risk score (0.401). For organizations with significant India-based workforce concentrations, a targeted upskilling program focusing on Python, Deep Learning, and Cloud Engineering roles — combined with compensation equity benchmarking — will reduce both business continuity risk and talent attrition. This is the single geography where intervention ROI is highest.
Evidence: India median salary = $12,448 (vs. USA $43,422); India avg risk = 0.401 (highest among 6 countries). India has the largest volume of Entry-level high-risk roles in the dataset.
5
Prepare for a Sub-85% Job Survival Environment by 2035 — Build Transition Infrastructure Now
The survival rate trend clearly shows decline from ~91% (2015) toward 83.7% (2035). Organizations should not treat workforce transition as a reactive event. Proactive measures include establishing internal mobility programs, creating cross-functional "AI Transition" squads, developing early-warning metrics that monitor role-level risk scores for company-specific workforce segments, and partnering with educational institutions to create tailored micro-credential pathways for at-risk employees.
Evidence: Job survival rate declined from 91.0% (2015) to a projected 83.7% (2035); the trend is monotonically declining in the projected 2025–2035 period. Total job openings remain stable (~14–16M/year), confirming the issue is skills mismatch, not demand collapse.
6
Align Executive Compensation Strategy with the Senior Talent Scarcity Premium
Senior-level professionals in AI Research, ML Engineering, and Cybersecurity command compensation that is increasingly detached from entry and mid-level benchmarks. Organizations that rely on static compensation bands will struggle to attract and retain senior AI talent. Leadership should commission annual compensation benchmarking against USA/UK market rates for the six lowest-risk role categories, and build performance incentive structures that reward skills development milestones explicitly (e.g., Python certification, cloud architect credentials).
Evidence: Senior ML Engineers in USA observed at $96,000–$101,956; Senior AI Researchers peak at $113,589. The USA-India salary gap of 3.5× drives significant cross-border talent flows and retention risk for India-based operations of global firms.
