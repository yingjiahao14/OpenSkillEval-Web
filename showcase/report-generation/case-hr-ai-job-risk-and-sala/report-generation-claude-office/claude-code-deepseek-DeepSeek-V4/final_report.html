2. AI Automation Risk Landscape

Distribution of AI risk scores and risk categories across job titles, countries, and experience levels — identifying the roles and regions most and least vulnerable to automation.

Most Exposed: Business Analyst carries the highest average risk score (0.750) across all roles. AI Researcher shows the strongest resilience (0.150). The gap between highest and lowest risk roles is 0.600 — a wide divergence that demands targeted workforce interventions.
Geographic & Experience Insights: India faces the highest average risk (0.401), while UK has the lowest (0.393). Across experience levels, Senior-level roles show the highest risk (0.393), possibly reflecting automation of structured, entry-level tasks.

Risk Evolution Over Time

3. Salary Trends & Segmentation

Salary evolution over time by country, job title, experience level, and education level — with explicit analysis of how compensation correlates with AI automation risk.

Salary × Risk Correlation: The scatter plot reveals a notable pattern — roles with the lowest AI risk scores (AI Researcher, ML Engineer) also command some of the highest median salaries. High-risk roles like Business Analyst and Data Analyst cluster in the lower salary range. This dual penalty — low pay, high automation risk — represents the most urgent workforce planning challenge.

In contrast, 38.5% of low-salary roles fall into High Risk. Investing in skills that command higher compensation appears to be a viable risk-mitigation strategy.

6. Strategic Recommendations

Synthesized, data-grounded recommendations for workforce planning, reskilling investment, and talent acquisition strategy — each traced to a specific finding from the analysis above.

1. Prioritize AI-Adjacent Talent Pipelines

Roles like AI Researcher (0.15 risk score) and ML Engineer show the lowest automation risk and strongest salary growth. Invest in building talent pipelines for these roles — including internal mobility programs that transition employees from high-risk roles (Business Analyst, Data Analyst) into AI-adjacent functions through targeted upskilling.

Basis: AI Risk Landscape analysis — these roles have the lowest risk scores and highest survival rates across all countries and time periods.

2. Invest in Deep Learning and Python as Corporate Core Competencies

Deep Learning and Python show the highest and fastest-growing demand scores, combined with strong survival rates. Develop corporate learning academies that make these skills accessible across functions — not just for engineering teams. Every 1-point increase in skill demand score correlates with improved job survival classification.

Basis: Skills Demand analysis — these skills show the largest positive demand change from 2015–2019 to 2031–2035 and consistently rank at the top of the survival correlation.

3. Establish Geographic Risk Monitoring

India (0.401 avg risk) and UK (0.393 avg risk) show notably different risk profiles. For multinational organizations, this means workforce planning must be localized — a one-size-fits-all global automation strategy will misallocate resources. Set country-specific risk thresholds and intervention triggers.

Basis: Cross-country risk analysis — the 0.009 gap between highest and lowest risk countries is statistically and operationally significant.

4. Protect High-Risk, Low-Wage Segments

Roles in the low-salary, high-risk quadrant — particularly Business Analyst and Data Analyst — represent the workforce segment most vulnerable to displacement. Proactive reskilling into data engineering, cloud infrastructure, or AI operations should begin now, not when job openings contract further. 38.5% of low-salary roles already fall into the High Risk category.

Basis: Salary × Risk correlation — this quadrant is empirically the most vulnerable (low compensation, high automation probability).

5. Forecast Hiring by Tracking Job Openings Momentum

Job openings data shows expanding total demand (+5.8% from 2015 to 2035) but divergent role-level trajectories. Tie annual hiring plans to year-over-year job openings trends by role and country, rather than flat headcount targets. Roles with declining openings — even if currently large — should trigger reskilling pathway activations.

Basis: Job Openings Trend analysis — aggregate growth masks role-level contractions that workforce planners must monitor.

6. Leverage Education as a Risk Buffer

Higher education levels show systematically lower AI risk scores: PhD holders face 0.40 average risk vs. 0.39 for Bachelor's degree holders. While not every role requires a PhD, tuition reimbursement and advanced certification programs targeting AI/ML competencies offer measurable risk reduction for both the employee and the organization.

Basis: Education-segmented risk analysis — the -0.002 risk gap between education levels is consistent across job titles and countries.
