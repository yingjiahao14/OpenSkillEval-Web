📈

Automation Threat Is Accelerating

The average AI risk score across all roles grew from 0.30 in 2015 to a projected 0.51 by 2035 — a 72% increase. Business Analyst and Data Analyst roles are on a near-certain automation trajectory, reaching risk scores of 0.85 and 0.75 respectively by 2035.

💵

Lower Risk Commands a 60% Salary Premium

Jobs in the Low Risk category command a median salary ~60% higher than High Risk jobs ($34.1K vs. $21.7K). This gap widens significantly through the projection period, with Low Risk roles reaching $49K median by 2035 vs. $24K for High Risk.

🧠

Deep Learning & Python Are the Strongest Shields

Deep Learning skill holders achieve Class-2 (strong survival) outcomes 88.9% of the time. Python follows at 57.9%. In contrast, Excel and SQL workers show 0% Class-2 survival rates, placing them firmly in the at-risk tier.

🌍

USA Leads in Both Salaries and Survival Rates

US-based workers earn a median $43.4K (vs. $12.4K in India) and 58% achieve Class-2 survival — the highest of any country. India trails at only 8.8% Class-2 survival, signaling acute vulnerability to automation displacement.

🔍

AI Researcher & ML Engineer Roles Are Future-Proof

AI Researchers have a mere 0.15 average risk score (0.25 by 2035) and 88.9% Class-2 survival. ML Engineers mirror this pattern at 71.2% Class-2. These are the highest-value talent categories for the next decade.

⚠️

At-Risk Job Share Rising Through 2035

Class-0 (at-risk) jobs represented 8.9% of records in 2015–2016 but climbed to 16.3% by 2035, signaling an expanding population of workers needing proactive reskilling intervention within the next decade.

Data Scope Note This analysis covers 12,343 synthetic records spanning 2015–2035 across 10 job titles, 6 countries, and 9 primary skills. Years 2025–2035 represent trend-based projections, not observed data. All salary figures are in USD nominal terms.

Strategic Recommendations

Each recommendation is directly grounded in a specific finding from the dataset analysis above.

Recommendation 01 — Talent Investment

Prioritize Deep Learning & Python Talent Pipelines

Allocate a disproportionate share of talent acquisition budgets toward Deep Learning and Python specialists. These two skills produce Class-2 survival outcomes at 88.9% and 57.9% respectively — far above the 42% dataset average — while commanding salary premiums consistent with the Low Risk tier.

Data Basis: Skills Survival Analysis (Section 04) — Deep Learning Senior holders achieve 95.9% Class-2 survival; Python Senior Masters achieve 82.4%.
Recommendation 02 — Reskilling Programs

Urgently Reskill Business & Data Analysts

Business Analysts (risk score 0.85 by 2035) and Data Analysts (0.75) have zero Class-2 survival outcomes in the dataset. Reskilling programs should target transitions to Cloud Engineering, DevOps, or Cybersecurity — all of which have risk scores below 0.35 and survival rates above 50%.

Data Basis: Both BA and DA roles show 0% Class-2 survival and the highest risk trajectories in the dataset. Combined, they account for ~66.7M job openings — the largest at-risk labor pool.
Recommendation 03 — Compensation Strategy

Anchor Compensation to Risk Exposure

Revise salary bands to explicitly reward lower-automation-risk skills. The data shows a persistent and widening 57–60% salary premium for Low Risk vs. High Risk jobs. High-demand, low-risk talent (AI Researchers, ML Engineers) requires premium compensation to attract and retain against rising market competition.

Data Basis: Low Risk median $34.1K vs. High Risk $21.7K; gap expands to ~$25K by 2035 (Section 03 — Salary by Risk Category × Year).
Recommendation 04 — Geographic Strategy

Reassess India Workforce Allocation

India's workforce faces compounding risks: lowest median salary ($12.4K), highest automation risk score (0.401), and only 8.8% Class-2 survival. Any nearshoring or offshoring strategies heavily reliant on India-based analyst roles should include a phased reskilling transition plan targeting Cloud, Security, and DevOps pathways.

Data Basis: Country survival analysis (Section 05) — India's 8.8% Class-2 rate vs. 58.1% USA. Risk score nearly identical to other countries, confirming the issue is role mix, not geography.
Recommendation 05 — Hiring Criteria

Deprioritize Academic Credentials; Prioritize Seniority & Skill Depth

The near-zero salary differentiation between Bachelor ($31.6K), Master ($31.4K), and PhD ($31.7K) holders indicates that academic credentials have little labor market impact in this field. Instead, Senior experience level amplifies survival rates dramatically across all skills. Hiring and promotion criteria should weight demonstrated seniority and technical skill depth over degree type.

Data Basis: Education salary comparison (Section 03); Senior Deep Learning holders achieve 95.9% Class-2 survival regardless of degree (Section 04, Best Combinations table).
Recommendation 06 — Workforce Planning

Plan for 16%+ At-Risk Worker Share by 2035

The at-risk (Class-0) workforce share is projected to reach 16.3% by 2035, up from 8.9% in 2015. Organizations should begin now to identify Class-0 workers in their workforce, map them to viable reskilling pathways (Deep Learning, Docker, AWS, Security), and build multi-year transition programs rather than reacting to displacement events.

Data Basis: Survival class year-over-year analysis (Section 05) — Class-0 share 8.9% in 2015–2016 vs. 16.3% in 2035.
