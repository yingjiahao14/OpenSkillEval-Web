Executive Overview

This report analyzes AI automation risk, compensation, skill demand, job openings, and survival prospects across the full 2015–2035 dataset. All quantitative statements are calculated directly from data.csv; future years in the dataset are projections from a synthetic, trend-based simulation rather than observed labor-market outcomes.

Critical takeaways for leadership:
  • Average AI risk rises from 0.30 in 2015 to 0.51 in 2035, a +21.3 pp increase across the market.
  • The labor market does not contract uniformly: total openings increase +5.8%, while the survival rate falls from 91.1% to 83.7%.
  • High-risk work expands materially: high-risk records rise from 10.7% of 2015 records to 23.0% in 2035, while low-risk records fall from 58.4% to 8.6%.
  • Compensation and automation risk are inversely related in this dataset: salary has a -0.24 correlation with AI risk, and high-risk roles have a median salary of $21,683 versus $34,105 for low-risk roles.
  • Resilience concentrates in AI-native, infrastructure, security, and strategic roles: AI Researcher, ML Engineer, Cybersecurity Analyst, Cloud Engineer, Data Scientist, and DevOps Engineer show 100.0% 2035 survival in the dataset.

Strategic Recommendations

1. Prioritize reskilling for analyst-heavy functions

Data basis: Business Analyst and Data Analyst have the highest 2035 AI risk scores (0.85 and 0.75) and the largest survival declines (-23.5 pp and -22.9 pp). Action: shift these roles from spreadsheet/query execution toward AI workflow design, data quality governance, stakeholder synthesis, and decision support.

2. Build resilient talent pipelines around Python, cloud, security, and AI-native roles

Data basis: Python has 100.0% 2035 survival and the highest 2035 demand score (82.9); Cloud Engineer, Cybersecurity Analyst, ML Engineer, Data Scientist, DevOps Engineer, and AI Researcher maintain 100.0% 2035 survival. Action: concentrate hiring, internal academies, and rotation programs in these capability pools.

3. Redesign entry-level work before automation erodes the talent bench

Data basis: Entry-level roles show the largest risk increase (+22.7 pp) and largest survival decline (-12.1 pp). Action: create AI-supervised apprenticeship paths where junior staff learn exception handling, prompt-based analysis, quality review, and domain judgment instead of repetitive production tasks.

4. Use compensation strategy to detect automation exposure

Data basis: Salary and AI risk have a negative correlation (-0.24), and high-risk records have lower median pay ($21,683) than low-risk records ($34,105). Action: treat low-compensation, high-volume operational roles as priority candidates for augmentation planning and reskilling budgets.

5. Manage country portfolios by survival momentum, not only salary arbitrage

Data basis: Australia records the largest survival decline (-13.9 pp), while Canada is the only country with a slight survival-rate gain (+0.8 pp). India shows the largest salary growth (+78.4%) but the lowest 2035 survival rate among countries (72.4%). Action: combine location strategy with role mix and reskilling exposure, rather than optimizing solely for wage level.

Methodology & Data Integrity

The analysis uses the provided data.csv only. Metrics are calculated as follows: average AI risk is the mean of ai_risk_score; median salary is the median of salary; survival rate is the proportion of records where job_survival_class >= 1; skill demand is the mean of skill_demand_score; openings are summed from job_openings. Trend deltas compare 2015 with 2035 unless otherwise stated.

Limitation: the source brief states the dataset is synthetically generated with trend-based assumptions. Findings are therefore best used for scenario planning, prioritization, and analytical demonstration rather than as observed labor-market evidence.
