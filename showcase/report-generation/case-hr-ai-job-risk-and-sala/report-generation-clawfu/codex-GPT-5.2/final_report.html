Strategic Recommendations

Recommendations below are explicitly grounded in measured findings above (risk trends, salary-risk relationship, survival rates, and openings shifts) and are intended to be actionable for executive leadership and workforce strategy teams.

1) Build a “risk-tiered” workforce portfolio

  • Why (data): average AI risk increases by + from 2015→2035.
  • Do: segment roles into automation tiers and set different operating models (augment, redesign, or redeploy).
  • Measure: track role-level risk trend and survival rate annually; align reskilling budgets to highest-risk clusters.

2) Use compensation as a signal for where augmentation creates leverage

  • Why (data): salary and AI risk are negatively correlated overall (), and median salary differs by risk category.
  • Do: prioritize AI copilots and process automation to protect/expand output of lower-risk, higher-compensated roles (where leverage is greatest).
  • Measure: productivity uplift vs. cost per seat; retention of high-impact roles.

3) Align talent pipelines to roles with demand growth (not just low risk)

  • Why (data): openings grow strongly for some roles (e.g., Cybersecurity Analyst ~49.1% and Product Manager ~45.4% from 2015→2035), while others decline.
  • Do: expand hiring and internal mobility into the top growth roles; treat declining roles as reskilling sources.
  • Measure: mobility conversion rates; time-to-fill for growth roles; decline-rate tracking by job family.

4) Build durable skill programs anchored in observed demand trends

  • Why (data): skill demand deltas show strongest increases in Java, Python, AWS, SQL (2015→2035).
  • Do: fund learning pathways around these skills and pair them with governance/quality practices (human-in-the-loop).
  • Measure: skill attainment rates; internal placement into higher-survival roles; salary progression for reskilled cohorts.

Data integrity & limitations

  • This report uses only benchmark/data.csv and computes all metrics from it; no external figures are introduced.
  • The dataset is synthetic and projection-based (per source brief). Treat results as scenario signals, not observational truth.
  • Columns are role-level; task-level automation mapping and firm adoption heterogeneity are not available.
