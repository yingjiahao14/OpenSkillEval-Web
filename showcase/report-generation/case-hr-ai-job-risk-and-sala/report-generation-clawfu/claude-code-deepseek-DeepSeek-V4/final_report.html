1. AI Risk Accelerates Sharply After 2030

The average AI risk score climbs from 0.295 in 2015 to 0.508 in 2035, with the steepest acceleration occurring after 2030. By 2035, the proportion of high-risk jobs reaches 18.6% of all records — up from negligible levels in the historical period.

2. A Tale of Two Workforces Emerges

AI Researchers, ML Engineers, and Cybersecurity Analysts maintain 100% survival rates throughout the horizon. In stark contrast, Business Analysts (43.0%) and Data Analysts (56.5%) face severe AI disruption risk, with survival rates dropping significantly in projected years.

3. Salary × Risk: A Strong Inverse Relationship

High-risk roles command a median salary of $21,683 — 37% less than low-risk roles at $34,105. The correlation between AI risk score and salary is r = -0.24, confirming that automation-vulnerable jobs are systematically lower-paid.

4. Job Openings Reallocate, Don't Disappear

Despite rising AI risk, aggregate job openings hold relatively steady — declining only marginally from 14.0M to 14.8M across the horizon. However, openings shift away from DevOps Engineer (-27.8%) and Data Scientist (-13.3%) toward Product Manager (+45.4%) and Cybersecurity Analyst (+49.1%).

5. Education Alone Is Not a Shield — Skill Choice Matters More

Median salaries are nearly identical across Bachelor's ($31,569), Master's ($31,434), and PhD ($31,672) levels, suggesting salary is driven more by role and skill choice than by degree. However, PhD holders combined with Senior experience achieve the highest survival rate (94.9%).

Strategic Recommendations

Data-grounded, actionable recommendations for workforce planning, reskilling investment, and talent acquisition strategy.

1

Prioritize Reskilling for Business Analysts and Data Analysts Now

These two roles account for the entire 11.0% of the workforce classified as "at risk" (survival class 0). Their AI risk scores — 0.65–0.85 by 2035 — are the highest in the dataset. Immediate reskilling pathways into adjacent roles (Data Analyst → Data Scientist, Business Analyst → Product Manager) should be funded, as skill adjacency makes transitions feasible.

Grounded in: Job survival class distribution, AI risk scores by job title, and job opening growth in adjacent roles (+45.4% for Product Manager).
2

Invest in Mid-Career Upskilling for "Transitional" Roles

The 47% of workers in survival class 1 — Cloud Engineers, Data Scientists, Product Managers, Software Engineers — face moderate risk but have viable paths to strengthen their position. Targeted investment in AI/ML literacy (Python: +3.0 demand growth), cloud architecture (AWS: +2.7), and strategic thinking can move these workers toward class 2 resilience.

Grounded in: Skill demand trends showing Python (+3.0) and Java (+3.9) as fastest-growing skills, and the correlation between skill_demand_score and job_survival_class (r = +0.27).
3

Build an AI-Adaptive Talent Acquisition Strategy

ML Engineer openings grow 10.6%, Cybersecurity Analyst openings surge 49.1%, and Product Manager openings jump 45.4%. These three roles offer the strongest combination of job opening growth, low-to-moderate AI risk, and high survival rates. Talent acquisition should prioritize these pipelines now, as competition will intensify as the 2030 acceleration approaches.

Grounded in: Job opening change data (2015–2035), AI risk scores, and survival rate by job title.
4

Reorient Compensation Strategy Around AI-Resilience Premium

Low-risk roles already command a 57% salary premium over high-risk roles ($34,105 vs $21,683 median). As the risk gap widens toward 2035, compensation for AI-resilient skills will continue to rise. Organizations should benchmark against this premium to remain competitive — and use compensation as a retention lever for workers who develop AI-adjacent competencies.

Grounded in: Salary × AI risk correlation (r = -0.24), median salary by risk category, and the widening survival rate gap.
5

Do Not Overweight Education Credentials in Workforce Planning

With median salaries virtually identical across Bachelor's ($31,569), Master's ($31,434), and PhD ($31,672), education level is a poor predictor of both compensation and resilience (survival rates range only 88.9%–89.6% across education levels). Experience level is the dominant differentiator — prioritize skill acquisition and practical experience over degree requirements when designing hiring and promotion criteria.
