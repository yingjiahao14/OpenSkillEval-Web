Strategic Recommendations

1

Prioritize reskilling for high-risk analytical operations roles.

Business Analyst and Data Analyst have the highest average AI risk (0.75 and 0.65) and the lowest survival rates (43.0% and 56.5%). Shift these populations toward Python, cloud, cybersecurity, product strategy, and AI-enabled decision workflows.

2

Protect early-career pipelines with structured AI augmentation training.

Entry-level records show the lowest survival rate at 83.9% versus 94.5% for senior roles. Build apprenticeship models that combine domain judgment, automation tooling, and senior mentorship.

3

Invest selectively in resilient, higher-pay technical roles.

AI Researcher, ML Engineer, Cybersecurity Analyst, and DevOps Engineer each show 100.0% survival rates in the dataset; AI Researcher also has the lowest average risk (0.15) and high median salary ($42,845). Use these areas for retention, internal mobility, and strategic hiring.

4

Do not use salary as a proxy for automation risk.

The salary-risk correlation is -0.24 and high-risk records have lower median salary than low-risk records. Workforce risk scans should combine role, task profile, skill, survival class, and openings rather than compensation bands alone.

5

Tailor geography strategy to survival and compensation differences.

Country-level risk is tightly clustered, but survival ranges from 78.6% in India to 93.8% in the USA, while median salary ranges from $12,448 to $43,422. Local workforce plans should therefore emphasize market economics and resilience, not only automation exposure.

Methodology & Limitations

  • Source: Kaggle-derived synthetic and trend-based dataset documented in /app/benchmark/source_brief.md.
  • Coverage: 12,343 records, 10 job titles, 6 countries, 9 primary skills, and 2015–2035 annual observations.
  • Metrics: Survival rate is calculated as the proportion of records where job_survival_class >= 1; strong survival is job_survival_class = 2.
  • Data integrity: The report uses only values derived from data.csv. No external benchmarks or fabricated values are introduced.
  • Interpretation: Years 2025–2035 are simulated projections in the source data and should be treated as planning scenarios, not empirical forecasts.
