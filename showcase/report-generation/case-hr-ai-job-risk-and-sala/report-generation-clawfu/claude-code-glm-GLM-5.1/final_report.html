Key Takeaway 1 — Automation risk is rising uniformly. Every tracked role sees its AI risk score increase by exactly +0.10 per decade. By 2035, no role falls below a 0.25 risk score, meaning even the safest roles face measurable AI exposure.

Key Takeaway 2 — The divergence is in starting position, not trajectory. Business Analysts began at 0.65 in 2015 and reach 0.85 by 2035; AI Researchers began at 0.05 and reach 0.25. The gap between highest and lowest risk roles remains roughly 0.60 throughout — the relative ranking never shifts.

Key Takeaway 3 — High-risk roles cluster at lower salary levels. Median salary for High Risk roles is $21,683, versus $34,105 for Low Risk and $34,710 for Medium Risk. This creates a compounding vulnerability: workers most exposed to automation are also least compensated and therefore least able to invest in reskilling.

Key Takeaway 4 — Skill choice is the strongest survival lever. Deep Learning and Security skills correlate with 100% job survival; Excel and SQL correlate with 43% and 56.5% survival respectively. The skill-to-survival relationship is stronger than country or education effects.

Key Takeaway 5 — India faces disproportionate risk. India's job survival rate is 78.6%, versus 93.8% in the USA. Business Analyst + Excel combinations in India drop below 8% survival by the late 2020s, the weakest profile in the entire dataset.

Section 6

Strategic Recommendations

Each recommendation below traces directly to a specific data finding. No generic advice — every action item is grounded in the analysis above.

1. Prioritize Role-Based Reskilling Over Geographic Programs

AI risk varies 5× across roles (0.15–0.75) but is essentially flat across countries (0.393–0.401) and experience levels (0.393–0.399). Target reskilling budgets at Business Analysts and Data Analysts regardless of their location, rather than spreading investment evenly across geographies.

Source: Section 2 — Risk by country (range 0.393–0.401) vs. risk by title (range 0.15–0.75).

2. Redirect Reskilling From Analytics Skills to AI-Adjacent Skills

Excel and SQL correlate with 43% and 56.5% survival respectively, while Deep Learning, Security, and Docker correlate with 100% survival. Reskilling programs should move Business Analysts and Data Analysts toward Python, cloud, and security competencies — not deeper analytics specialization.

Source: Section 4 — Survival by skill (Excel 43%, SQL 56.5% vs. Deep Learning/Security/Docker 100%).

3. Address the Compounding Low-Salary, High-Risk Trap

High Risk roles earn a median of $21,683 versus $34,105 for Low Risk roles. This $12,400+ gap means at-risk workers have fewer financial resources to invest in upskilling. Design funded (employer-subsidized or government-backed) reskilling programs that remove the financial barrier rather than relying on self-funded career transitions.

Source: Section 3 — Salary by risk category; Pearson r = −0.24.

4. Establish India-Specific Intervention Programs

India's 78.6% job survival rate trails the next-lowest market (Germany, 89.9%) by over 11 percentage points. Business Analyst + Excel profiles in India are near-extinction (<8% survival). A dedicated India workforce transition task force should be created with targeted timelines tied to the accelerating risk trajectory.

Source: Section 5 — Survival by country (India 78.6% vs. USA 93.8%); Bottom survival profiles.

5. Do Not Rely on Education as a Standalone Buffer

Within the most vulnerable profiles, education provides negligible protection (PhD Business Analysts in India: 7% survival vs. Bachelor: 5%). In low-risk profiles, survival is already near 100% regardless of degree. Education strategy should focus on what is studied (skill content), not how much (degree level).

Source: Section 5 — Bottom survival profiles; survival by education (PhD 89.7%, Bachelor 89.3%, Master 88.1%).

6. Accelerate AI Researcher and ML Engineer Talent Pipelines

AI Researchers and ML Engineers show 100% survival, the lowest risk scores (0.15 and 0.25), and the highest salaries ($42,845 and $39,327). Yet job openings for these roles are not expanding faster than average. Organizations should proactively build these pipelines through internal mobility programs, not rely on external hiring alone.

Source: Section 2 & 3 — Survival, risk, and salary data for AI Researcher and ML Engineer roles.
Implementation Priority Matrix: Recommendations 1 and 2 (role-based reskilling toward AI-adjacent skills) should be treated as the highest-priority actions because they address the largest population at risk with the most direct lever (skill change). Recommendation 3 (funded reskilling) is an enabler for Recommendations 1–2. Recommendations 4–6 are important but more narrowly scoped.
