Automation exposure increases materially. Average AI risk rises from 0.30 in 2015 to 0.51 in 2035; the high-risk category accounts for 18.6% of all records across the full dataset.
Survival remains majority-positive but deteriorates. Records classified as surviving or moderately surviving decline from 91.1% in 2015 to 83.7% in 2035, with at-risk records rising to 16.3% by 2035.

AI Automation Risk Landscape

Automation risk rises steadily over the 21-year horizon. The average risk score increases by 21.3 pp between 2015 and 2035, with the steepest late-period level occurring in 2035 (0.51). Across all records, 48.7% are medium risk, 32.7% low risk, and 18.6% high risk.

Role and Region Findings

  • Highest-risk role: Business Analyst has the highest average risk score (0.75); the lowest is AI Researcher (0.15).
  • Fastest risk escalation by role: Software Engineer increases by +0.20 risk-score points from 2015 to 2035.
  • Geographic spread is narrower than role spread: India has the highest country average (0.40) and UK the lowest (0.39).
  • Experience segmentation: senior, mid, and entry levels cluster tightly, with average risk ranging from 0.39 to 0.40; in this dataset, role and skill explain more variation than experience level alone.

Salary Trends & Segmentation

Median salary rises from $24,247 in 2015 to $36,763 in 2035, a 51.6% increase. The trend is upward but not linear: the 2035 median is below the 2034 peak of $39,801, reflecting year-to-year variation in the synthetic sample mix.

Salary × Automation Risk Correlation

Compensation is not a complete shield from automation exposure, but the dataset shows a weak inverse relationship. Across all records, salary and AI risk correlate at -0.24; across role averages, the correlation is -0.81. The highest-median-salary role is AI Researcher at $42,845, with an average AI risk score of 0.15.

Strategic Recommendations

1
Build risk-tiered workforce plans by role, not only by geography.
Finding: role-level AI risk spans 0.15–0.75, while country averages are more compressed. Action: prioritize scenario planning, redeployment, and automation governance for the highest-risk roles, starting with Business Analyst and roles with the largest 2015–2035 risk increases such as Software Engineer.
2
Fund reskilling where demand growth and survival intersect.
Finding: portfolio skill demand is stable, but individual skills vary from -2.5 to +3.9 points of 2015–2035 demand change. Action: allocate learning budgets toward skills with rising demand and above-average survival outcomes, and review skills with flat or negative demand before expanding hiring pipelines.
3
Use salary-risk analytics in talent investment decisions.
Finding: salary and AI risk have a weak negative correlation (-0.24 at record level; -0.81 by role), so higher compensation does not automatically indicate higher automation exposure. Action: avoid blanket salary-cost reduction strategies; instead, evaluate whether premium roles have durable survival, strategic skill demand, and sufficient openings momentum.
4
Protect critical capacity despite rising automation pressure.
Finding: survival rate falls by 7.4 pp from 2015 to 2035 even as total openings rise 5.8%. Action: pair automation deployment with retention plans for resilient roles and transition pathways for class-0 at-risk populations.
5
Treat 2025–2035 outputs as planning scenarios.
Finding: the dataset is synthetic and future years are simulated projections. Action: combine these directional insights with internal HRIS data, productivity metrics, and external labor-market feeds before making workforce commitments.

Methodology & Limitations

  • Source data: /app/benchmark/data.csv, 12,343 records and 14 columns; no missing values were detected.
  • Trend metrics: average AI risk, median salary, average skill demand, total job openings, and survival rate where job_survival_class >= 1.
  • Aggregation: analyses use mean risk and demand, median salary, summed openings, and proportional survival rates by year, role, country, experience level, skill, and selected combinations.
  • Limitation: the dataset is synthetic + trend-based; causal claims, external market sizing, inflation adjustment, and purchasing power normalization are outside the data provided.
