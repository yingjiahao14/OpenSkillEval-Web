AI Impact on the Global Job Market: Risk, Salary & Skills Trend Analysis (2015–2035)

A comprehensive trend analysis examining how AI automation risk, salary dynamics, skill demand, and job survival prospects are evolving across roles, countries, and experience levels from 2015 to 2035.

Executive Overview

This report analyzes a 2015–2035 synthetic dataset (12,343 records) to quantify how AI automation risk, salary dynamics, skill demand, and job survival classifications evolve across roles, countries, experience levels, and primary skills. Future years (2025–2035) are modeled projections within the dataset and should be interpreted as scenario outputs rather than observed labor market facts.

Across 2015→2035, average AI risk rises from 0.295 to 0.508 (+0.213). High-risk mix increases from 10.7% to 23.0%.

Median salary increases from $24,247 to $36,763 (51.6%), while total job openings increase 5.8% over the same period.

Salary vs risk correlation is negative overall (r=-0.245), indicating higher-paid records tend to have lower AI risk in this dataset.

Salary Trends & Segmentation

Salaries rise over the 2015–2035 horizon and the distribution shifts toward the 'High' salary bucket. However, compensation does not uniformly move with risk: in aggregate, salary and AI risk are negatively correlated in this dataset.

Interpretation note: correlation describes association within this dataset; it does not establish causality. Segment-level patterns can differ from the aggregate; always validate at the role/country level before translating into workforce strategy.

Job Survival & Market Outlook

Job survival mix degrades over time: the share of 'At Risk (0)' increases by 2035 while 'Strong (2)' shrinks. Meanwhile, job openings do not collapse in aggregate; instead, growth concentrates in specific roles.

Strategic Recommendations

Recommendations below are directly grounded in the dataset findings above.

  1. Prioritize reskilling for the highest-risk roles first: 'Business Analyst' has the highest average risk (0.750). Build role-specific transition paths toward lower-risk roles such as 'AI Researcher' (avg risk 0.150).
  2. Use compensation as a triage signal, not a guarantee: overall salary×risk correlation is negative (r=-0.245). Validate risk exposure explicitly even for high-paying roles (e.g., 'AI Researcher' has median salary $42,845).
  3. Anchor curricula in skills with sustained and rising demand. The fastest-growing demand shift is 'Java' (+3.95 demand points from 2015 to 2035).
  4. Align talent acquisition with demand growth roles: 'Cybersecurity Analyst' shows the largest openings expansion (49.1% from 2015→2035). Focus sourcing and internal mobility pipelines accordingly.
  5. Plan for a structurally higher at-risk baseline: 'At Risk (0)' share rises from 8.9% in 2015 to 16.3% in 2035. Bake continuous redeployment capacity into workforce plans rather than treating it as episodic.
