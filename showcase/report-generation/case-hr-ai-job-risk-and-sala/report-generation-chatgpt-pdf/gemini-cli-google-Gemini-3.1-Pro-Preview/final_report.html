Risk, Salary & Skills Trend Analysis (2015-2035)

Prepared by: Strategic Analytics Team | Date: April 2026

Executive Overview

This report provides a comprehensive trend analysis of how AI automation is reshaping the global job market across a 21-year horizon (2015–2035). Analyzing over 12,000 workforce records, the analysis uncovers critical shifts in automation risk, salary dynamics, skill demand, and overall job survival prospects.

Insight: Across all observed years, the average AI automation risk score is 0.40, with an overall job survival rate of 89.0%. The median salary stands at $31,573.

  • Automation Risk Landscape: Entry-level roles and certain technical niches face disproportionate risk, while senior and strategic roles remain resilient.
  • Salary & Risk Correlation: Higher AI risk scores consistently correlate with suppressed salary growth trajectories over the next decade.
  • Skills Future-proofing: Emerging skills exhibit rapidly increasing demand scores, significantly boosting job survival prospects.

Salary Trends & Segmentation

Compensation dynamics reflect the market's evolving valuation of human capital amidst AI advancements. Tracking median salaries from historical data through future projections underscores the widening gap between at-risk and highly differentiated roles.

Insight: Jobs classified as 'High Risk' show noticeably flatter salary curves over time, while 'Low Risk' roles experience compounding compensation growth as their unique human skills command market premiums.

Job Survival & Market Outlook

Macro labor demand and ultimate job survival prospects define the future market outlook. A survival classification >= 1 indicates a moderate to strong resilience to AI.

Insight: While aggregate job openings indicate shifting labor demands, the survival rate serves as the ultimate metric of a role's long-term viability. Investments must prioritize roles with both strong demand and high survival probabilities.

Strategic Recommendations

Based on the data-driven trend analysis from 2015 to 2035, we recommend the following strategic actions for executive leadership and workforce planners:

  • Aggressive Reskilling in High-Risk Roles: Direct immediate training budgets toward employees in 'High Risk' categories (e.g., specific entry-level tech roles), pivoting them towards the top resilient skills identified.
  • Compensation Alignment: Adjust long-term talent acquisition budgets. Expect 'Low Risk', high-demand roles to require compounding premium compensation, while easily automated roles will stagnate.
  • Strategic Hiring Criteria: Prioritize candidates whose primary competencies match the top survival-rate skills, shifting focus away from easily automated hard skills to complex, cognitive, or niche technical capabilities.
  • Experience-Level Investment: Entry-level roles bear the brunt of automation risk. Invest in structured mentorship and rapid upskilling pipelines to accelerate junior talent into 'Mid' and 'Senior' tiers where AI risk significantly drops.
