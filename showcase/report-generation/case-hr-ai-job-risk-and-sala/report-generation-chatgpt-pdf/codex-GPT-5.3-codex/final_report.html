Audience: Executive Leadership & Workforce Strategists | Dataset: 12,343 records across 2015–2035 (6 countries, 10 roles)

This report quantifies AI-driven labor-market shifts in automation risk, pay, skill demand, and survival outlook. All numbers are derived directly from /app/benchmark/data.csv; 2025–2035 values are simulated projections per source documentation.

Executive Overview

Top decision-critical takeaways
  • Average AI risk is 0.396. Highest-risk role: Business Analyst (0.75); lowest-risk role: AI Researcher (0.15).
  • Median salary is $31,573. Salary-to-risk correlation is -0.24, showing compensation levels move with automation exposure.
  • Job survival rate (class ≥1) is 89.0%. Openings shift materially by role; largest gain: Product Manager (+546,788), largest decline: DevOps Engineer (-437,347).
  • Fastest-growing skill demand is Java (+3.9); weakest is Docker (-2.5).
  • Strategic workforce plans should prioritize combinations with high survival rates and strong openings momentum.

Strategic Recommendations

  • Prioritize reskilling in high-risk roles (e.g., Business Analyst at 0.75 avg risk), while preserving growth pathways in lower-risk roles (e.g., AI Researcher at 0.15).
  • Embed automation exposure into compensation governance: salary-to-risk correlation is -0.24, so pay strategy should be tracked alongside AI risk movement.
  • Concentrate upskilling budget on fastest-growing skills, led by Java (+3.9 demand points, 2015→2035), and reassess investment in slower skills like Docker (-2.5).
  • Rebalance hiring toward demand-expanding roles: Product Manager shows the largest openings increase (+546,788), while DevOps Engineer shows the steepest decline (-437,347).
  • Use high-survival combinations of role, country, skill, and education as priority talent pipelines to reduce displacement risk through 2035.
