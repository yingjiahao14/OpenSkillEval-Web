▶ Five Critical Executive Takeaways

Finding 01 • Automation Risk Is Accelerating
The average AI risk score has risen from 0.30 in 2015 to 0.51 by 2035 — a 72% increase. Business Analysts (0.75) and Data Analysts (0.65) face existential automation threat. Organizations heavily staffed with these roles must act urgently.
Finding 02 • High Risk = Low Pay
Roles with high AI risk command significantly lower compensation: High-Risk jobs median at $21,683 vs. $34,105 for Low-Risk roles — a 57% premium for automation-resilient work. Salary is the market's forward signal of automation vulnerability.
Finding 03 • AI/ML Roles Are Future-Safe
AI Researchers (risk: 0.15) and ML Engineers (risk: 0.25) sit at the opposite end of the risk spectrum and rank #1 and #2 in survival prospects. Python and Deep Learning skills define this safety corridor — reskilling investment should prioritize these pathways.
Finding 04 • Survival Rate Is Declining
The proportion of job-years classified as "at risk" (class = 0) has risen from ~9% in 2015 to ~16% by 2035. Strong survival (class = 2) is also declining from 46% to 36%. The window for proactive reskilling is narrowing — investment must begin now.
Finding 05 • USA Leads; India Most Exposed
US-based roles offer the highest median salary ($43,422) and the highest survival rate (93.8%). India presents the starkest risk: lowest salary ($12,448) and lowest survival rate (78.6%). Geographic portfolio diversification strategy must account for this structural gap.

Strategic Recommendations

Actionable workforce planning priorities grounded in the data findings above

Priority 1 • Immediate Action

Urgently Transition High-Risk Roles

Business Analysts (avg risk 0.75) and Data Analysts (0.65) represent the most exposed workforce segments. With median salaries 37% below automation-resilient peers and survival scores averaging 0.43–0.56, these roles face structural displacement by 2030. Begin managed transition programs within 12 months.

Data basis: Business Analyst avg risk 0.75, survival score 0.43; Data Analyst avg risk 0.65, survival score 0.56; High-Risk median salary $21,683 vs. Low-Risk $34,105
Priority 2 • Immediate Action

Invest in Python & Deep Learning Reskilling

Python (demand 82.9 in 2035, highest gain) and Deep Learning (highest survival class 1.89) define the safety corridor against automation. AI Researchers and ML Engineers — both Python/Deep Learning roles — hold risk scores below 0.25. Structured reskilling from Excel/SQL toward Python/ML should be the primary workforce investment.

Data basis: AI Researcher avg risk 0.15, ML Engineer 0.25; Python demand growth 79.9→82.9; Deep Learning avg survival class 1.89
Priority 3 • Near-Term (12–24 months)

Prioritize Senior-Level Talent Retention

Senior roles command 2.1× the median salary of Entry-level positions ($48,318 vs. $22,924). Given that seniority substantially improves compensation without meaningfully changing automation risk, organizations should focus retention incentives on senior-level staff in low-risk disciplines — particularly Senior AI Researchers, ML Engineers, and Cybersecurity Analysts.

Data basis: Median salary Entry $22,924, Mid $32,996, Senior $48,318; experience level does not change avg risk score (<1% variance)
Priority 4 • Near-Term (12–24 months)

Diversify Geographic Talent Strategy

India offers a 3.5× salary cost advantage over the USA ($12,448 vs. $43,422) but carries the lowest survival rate (78.6%) and highest structural risk concentration. A balanced strategy should leverage India for high-volume, transitional roles while locating AI research and cybersecurity functions in USA, UK, or Canada where survival rates exceed 90%.

Data basis: Median salary USA $43,422 vs. India $12,448; survival rate USA 93.8% vs. India 78.6%; Canada 91.0%, UK 90.3%
Priority 5 • Strategic (2–4 years)

Build Cybersecurity & Cloud Bench Strength

Cybersecurity Analysts (risk 0.25, survival 1.53) and Cloud Engineers (risk 0.35, survival 1.48) represent the second tier of automation-resilient roles. With Security and AWS skill demand both above 78 through 2035 and stable job openings (~14–16M/yr), these disciplines offer reliable long-term career pathways. Building internal bench depth in these areas hedges against talent market tightening.

Data basis: Cybersecurity avg risk 0.25, survival 1.53; Cloud Engineer avg risk 0.35, survival 1.48; Security demand 78.9, AWS demand 79.5
Priority 6 • Ongoing Monitoring

Track Survival Rate Decline as a Leading Indicator

The 7.4 percentage-point decline in overall survival rate (91.1% → 83.7%) and 21% relative decline in "strong survival" classification (2015–2035) signal a structural workforce shift. Organizations should establish annual AI risk monitoring for all role families, using the ai_risk_score trend as an early-warning system to trigger reskilling programs before displacement becomes acute.

Data basis: Survival rate 2015: 91.1%, 2035: 83.7%; strong survival (class=2) 45.6% → 36.0% over same period
