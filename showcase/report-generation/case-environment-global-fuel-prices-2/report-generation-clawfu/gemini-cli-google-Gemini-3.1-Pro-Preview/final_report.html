6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

  • Market Transmission is High but Delayed: The strong correlation (0.26) between Brent Crude and retail petrol demonstrates high pass-through, though policy buffers can slightly delay the timing of spikes at the pump.
  • Subsidies Effectively Shield Consumers, but at Fiscal Cost: High-subsidy countries achieved a 79.5% price reduction compared to low-subsidy countries. Policymakers must weigh consumer stability against national debt expansion during prolonged commodity crises.
  • Tax Regimes Exacerbate Regional Spreads: High-income nations utilize higher tax percentages to manage demand and fund infrastructure, driving massive regional disparities. During extreme volatility (e.g., 2022), dynamic taxation (lowering taxes during peaks) can serve as a potent tool.
  • Policy Recommendation - Subsidy Targeting: Broad, untargeted subsidies predominantly benefit wealthier segments with higher fuel consumption. Analysts recommend transitioning to targeted cash-transfer mechanisms for Low- and Middle-income groups to optimize fiscal efficiency.
  • Policy Recommendation - Dynamic Tax Calibration: To manage future energy shocks (similar to 2020/2022 events), implementing automatic tax-stabilizer rules that inversely peg fuel taxes to Brent Crude prices can protect end-consumers without requiring ad-hoc legislative action.
