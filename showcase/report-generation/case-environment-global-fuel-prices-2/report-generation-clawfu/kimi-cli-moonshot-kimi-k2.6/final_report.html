6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

Synthesized insights and actionable recommendations for subsidy design, tax calibration, and price stabilization.

Key Findings

  1. Near-Perfect Brent-to-Retail Transmission: Weekly correlations between Brent Crude and global average retail petrol, diesel, and LPG prices are all ≥0.999. Global crude remains the dominant price setter; policy operates at the margin.
  2. Subsidies Are Highly Effective at Dampening Pass-Through: High-subsidy countries reduce Brent pass-through by 7× (slope 0.004 vs 0.028 USD/L per USD/bbl). The subsidy price dampening effect on average retail petrol is 84.5%.
  3. Regional Spreads Are Widening: The gap between highest- and lowest-priced regions grew 78% over the period (1.61 → 2.87 USD/L), driven by divergent tax and subsidy trajectories, not crude volatility.
  4. Tax Policy Scales with Income, Subsidies Do Not: High-income countries levy 34.7% average fuel taxes; low-income countries levy 22.9%. Yet high-subsidy regimes exist across all income levels, creating fiscal sustainability risks for poorer nations.
  5. High-Subsidy Countries Exhibit Higher Price Volatility at the Country Level: Despite buffering global shocks, individual high-subsidy countries (e.g., Venezuela CV 0.595, Iran CV 0.496) show extreme domestic volatility — often due to abrupt subsidy adjustments or currency crises.
  6. Low-Subsidy Countries Transmit Brent Shocks Faster and Deeper: During the 2022 crisis, low-subsidy retail prices rose 1.92 USD/L between low- and high-Brent periods; high-subsidy prices moved only 0.30 USD/L — but the fiscal cost of maintaining that gap is substantial and opaque in this dataset.

Policy Implications

  1. Targeted Subsidy Reform: Universal fuel subsidies are fiscally expensive and regressive. Policymakers should shift from blanket price caps to targeted cash transfers or sector-specific exemptions (e.g., agriculture, public transport) that protect vulnerable groups without distorting entire markets.
  2. Tax Calibration for Revenue and Climate Goals: High-income countries demonstrate that fuel taxes can fund infrastructure and dampen demand without triggering severe political backlash. Middle-income nations with low tax rates (avg 21.1%) have room to raise revenues while remaining below high-income price levels.
  3. Price Stabilization Funds: Rather than fixed subsidies, low- and middle-income countries should consider counter-cyclical stabilization funds that smooth prices during spikes (like 2022) and allow gradual pass-through during collapses (like 2020), reducing both fiscal strain and consumer volatility.
  4. Regional Coordination: The widening regional price spread (now 2.87 USD/L) encourages fuel smuggling and arbitrage. Neighboring countries with large price differentials should align tax and subsidy policies or strengthen border controls.
  5. Transparency in Tax Composition: The near-zero correlation between tax percentage and retail price (r = 0.004) within countries suggests tax estimation methodology is noisy or non-transparent. Standardizing tax reporting would improve policy credibility and public trust.
