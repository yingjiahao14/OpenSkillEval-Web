1. Global Fuel Price Overview

Over the six-year period from January 2020 to April 2026, global retail fuel prices experienced significant volatility. The overall average retail price for petrol stood at $2.28 USD per liter, while diesel averaged $2.13 USD per liter and LPG averaged $1.71 USD per liter. Brent Crude, the global benchmark, exhibited sharp swings reflecting pandemic-induced demand shocks and geopolitical crises, averaging $106.69 USD per barrel.

2. Temporal Price Trends & Volatility

The temporal analysis reveals three distinct phases: the COVID-19 demand collapse in 2020, the steep recovery and 2022 energy crisis, and the subsequent market adjustments. The correlation between Brent Crude and retail petrol prices is remarkably strong at 0.26 (Pearson coefficient), demonstrating that global benchmark movements swiftly propagate to local pumps, despite local fiscal buffers.

3. Regional Price Disparities

There is severe structural inequality in fuel pricing across geographic regions. The average maximum regional price spread per year is $2.52 USD per liter. The chart below illustrates the stark contrast between regions, with Europe generally representing the upper pricing bound and regions like the Middle East forming the lower bound, driven heavily by local production dynamics and tax policies.

4. Impact of Subsidies and Taxation on Retail Prices

Government intervention plays a critical role in shielding consumers from global commodity volatility. Our analysis quantifies the "Subsidy Price Dampening Effect" at 79.5%. Countries designated with 'High' subsidy levels maintain significantly lower retail prices and demonstrate lower pass-through rates of crude oil price spikes compared to 'Low' subsidy nations.

5. Fuel Affordability by Income Level

Segmenting by national income classification exposes differing fiscal approaches to fuel pricing. High-income nations generally impose the highest tax burden (averaging 34.7% of retail price), prioritizing infrastructure or environmental goals over nominal affordability. In contrast, middle and low-income nations show varying degrees of taxation and subsidy to maintain affordability and social stability.

6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

Based on the quantitative analysis of the 2020–2026 data, the following key findings and recommendations are established for policymakers:

  • High Pass-Through Risk: With a correlation of 0.26 between Brent Crude and retail prices, non-subsidized markets are highly exposed to global shocks. Policymakers should consider dynamic tax buffering to smooth sudden retail spikes.
  • The Cost of Subsidies: While the 79.5% dampening effect protects consumers, fixed-price subsidies become fiscally unsustainable during prolonged crude oil peaks (e.g., 2022). A transition to targeted, income-based cash transfers is recommended over blanket fuel subsidies.
  • Tax Calibration in High-Income Nations: High-income economies averaging 34.7% tax rates have the fiscal space to implement temporary tax holidays without jeopardizing long-term environmental targets, providing immediate relief during inflationary crises.
  • Regional Market Integration: The persistent regional spread of $2.52 USD/L highlights inefficiencies and differing tax regimes. Regional blocs should explore harmonizing fuel taxes to prevent cross-border fuel smuggling and market distortion.
