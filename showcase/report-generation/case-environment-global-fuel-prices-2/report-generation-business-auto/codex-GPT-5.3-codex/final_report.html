Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Global average petrol price was $2.282/L (range $0.010–$6.779).
  2. Brent-to-retail correlations are strong for petrol (1.000) and diesel (1.000), with LPG at 1.000.
  3. High-subsidy countries show lower average prices than low-subsidy countries: petrol dampening effect 79.5%, diesel 80.0%, LPG 79.5%.
  4. Average country-level Brent→petrol pass-through correlation is 0.986 in high-subsidy regimes vs 0.999 in low-subsidy regimes.
  5. Europe has the highest average petrol price ($3.702/L) while Middle East is lowest ($1.226/L).
  6. Regional price inequality peaked in 2026 with a spread of $2.870/L between highest and lowest regions.
  1. Tax calibration: Use adaptive fuel tax bands that partially offset Brent spikes to avoid pro-cyclical consumer burden.
  2. Subsidy targeting: High-subsidy models dampen price transmission, but targeting should prioritize vulnerable income groups to limit fiscal leakage.
  3. Stabilization design: Build rule-based stabilization funds linked to Brent thresholds and transparent trigger rules.
  4. Regional benchmarking: Track annual regional spread KPI as a policy inequality signal; widening spreads indicate uneven pass-through management.
