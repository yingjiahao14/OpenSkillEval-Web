Impact of Subsidies and Taxation on Retail Prices

Important data note: The dataset contains four subsidy labels (Low, Medium, High, Very High). To satisfy the report requirement of “High vs Low” subsidy comparison, this report bins High+Very High as High-subsidy and Low+Medium as Low-subsidy. All statistics below are computed using this binning.

Average price gap (petrol): Low-subsidy mean 2.931 USD/L vs High-subsidy mean 0.454 USD/L, implying a 84.51% lower average petrol price in the High-subsidy bin.

Key Findings & Policy Implications

Key findings (data-derived)

  1. Weekly global averages show near-perfect co-movement between Brent and retail prices (Pearson r: petrol 0.99999, diesel 0.99998).
  2. The highest 8-week volatility in Brent and retail returns occurs around 2020-03-16 (rolling 8-week std of Brent returns peaks at 4.03%), aligning with the 2020 demand shock period.
  3. The observed Brent range is 47.97–130.00 USD/bbl; the global-average petrol range is 1.024–2.784 USD/L.
  4. Across 2020–2026, Europe has the highest average retail prices (petrol 3.702 USD/L), while the Middle East is lowest (petrol 1.226 USD/L).
  5. Regional petrol price inequality increases over time: annual max-min spread reaches 2.870 USD/L in 2026.
  6. High-subsidy regimes (High+Very High) have substantially lower mean petrol prices than low-subsidy regimes (Low+Medium): 0.454 vs 2.931 USD/L (difference 84.51%).

Policy implications (actionable)

  1. Use explicit stabilization goals: if the objective is to reduce pass-through volatility, monitor Brent-to-retail correlations and rolling volatility metrics as operational targets.
  2. Calibrate subsidy schemes with fiscal reality: large average price gaps between subsidy regimes indicate potential consumer shielding, but require sustainability checks (outside this dataset).
  3. Target affordability by income: the dataset shows higher average `tax_percentage` in high-income countries; consider whether tax levels should be counter-cyclical or paired with targeted transfers during shocks.
  4. Benchmark regional spreads annually: rising max-min regional spreads imply increasing distributional divergence; use spreads as a KPI for equity-focused policy review.
Method notes: Correlations use Pearson r on weekly global averages (Brent is constant across countries per week). Volatility uses rolling 8-week standard deviation of weekly percentage changes. All currency values are already normalized to USD in the dataset.
