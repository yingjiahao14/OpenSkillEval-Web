Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Volatility Exposure: A high correlation (r=0.26) between retail prices and Brent Crude demonstrates significant pass-through risk for non-subsidized markets.
  2. Subsidy Effectiveness: High-subsidy policies effectively dampened retail price swings, lowering average consumer costs by 79.5% compared to low-subsidy equivalents.
  3. Regional Disparities: Geographic and regulatory differences create an average spread of $2.48 per liter between the most and least expensive regions globally.
  4. Regressive Taxation Potential: The observed tax-to-price ratios emphasize the need for targeted fiscal recalibration to protect lower-income consumers without jeopardizing public revenue.
  5. Policy Recommendation: Policymakers should evaluate dynamic tax-and-subsidy models that auto-adjust based on global crude price thresholds (e.g., buffering when Brent exceeds $80/bbl, recovering costs below $60/bbl).
