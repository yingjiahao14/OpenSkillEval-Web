Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Brent-to-retail linkage is near-perfect at the global level.
    Weekly global averages show Pearson r=0.999986 for Brent vs petrol and r=0.999983 for Brent vs diesel.
  2. The 2020 COVID shock and 2022 crisis transmitted with similar proportional magnitude into retail prices.
    Within Feb–Jun 2020, Brent moved +49.3% from trough-to-peak and global average petrol moved +49.3%; within 2022, Brent moved +36.6% and petrol moved +36.8% (ranges computed on weekly global averages).
  3. Subsidy regimes strongly separate retail price levels.
    Binning subsidy as High={High, Very High} vs Low={Low, Medium}, the mean petrol price is $0.454 vs $2.931, a 84.5% lower level in High-subsidy countries.
  4. Regional inequality in petrol prices widened post-2020 and is highest in 2026.
    In 2026, the regional spread (max–min regional annual mean petrol price) is $2.870; Europe is consistently the highest-price region and the Middle East consistently the lowest across 2020–2026.
  5. Tax burdens rise with income level in this dataset.
    Average tax percentage is 22.9% (Low), 21.1% (Middle), 34.7% (High).

Implications for policy design (data-grounded)

  • Stabilization mechanisms: given r≈1 at the global level, any policy targeting consumer volatility should explicitly account for Brent-linked dynamics and focus on buffering short-term shocks (e.g., smoothing rules) rather than expecting weak pass-through by default.
  • Subsidy targeting: the dataset shows very large differences in average price levels between subsidy regimes; targeting can materially shift consumer price levels, but effectiveness should be evaluated against fiscal sustainability and leakage (not observable in this dataset).
  • Tax calibration: tax% is materially higher in high-income groups on average; any reform should be evaluated jointly on price levels and equity, because tax changes can compound baseline cross-country price differences.
  • Regional benchmarking: the Europe–Middle East spread is persistent and highest in 2026; regional peer comparisons can help identify outliers for deeper country-level review.

Data Integrity & Limitations

  • The dataset includes 4 subsidy categories (Low/Medium/High/Very High), while the task specification calls for High vs Low. This report bins {High, Very High} as High and {Low, Medium} as Low; results should be interpreted accordingly.
  • Brent crude is a global benchmark and is identical across countries for each week (verified by checking that each week has a single Brent value). This makes retail-vs-Brent correlation primarily a time-series relationship rather than cross-sectional.
  • Tax percentage varies week-to-week within countries; the report treats it as observed (no smoothing or country-level fixing), so tax-related relationships are descriptive rather than causal.
