Key Findings & Policy Implications

The following findings translate the statistical patterns into policy-relevant implications for subsidy design, tax calibration, and price stabilization.

Retail prices tracked Brent extremely tightly.

Weekly Brent-to-retail level correlations are 1.000 for petrol, 1.000 for diesel, and 1.000 for LPG; weekly-change correlations remain above 0.991. Stabilization policies should assume global crude shocks transmit rapidly unless actively buffered.

Subsidies materially dampened both levels and pass-through.

High-subsidy petrol prices averaged 79.5% below Low-subsidy prices, while High-subsidy petrol pass-through was 79.4% lower than Low-subsidy pass-through. Very High subsidies produced the strongest insulation, but such regimes require fiscal sustainability review.

Low-subsidy systems carry materially higher tax and retail burdens.

Low-subsidy countries averaged $3.45/L petrol and a 37.5% tax share, compared with $0.71/L and 15.1% in High-subsidy countries. Tax calibration should distinguish revenue goals from stabilization goals.

Regional inequality widened as prices rose.

The Europe–Middle East petrol gap dominated every year, widening from $1.61/L in 2020 to $2.87/L in 2026. Analysts should evaluate regional benchmarks separately instead of relying only on global averages.

High-income markets pay more largely through policy structure.

High-income countries averaged $3.55/L petrol and a 34.7% tax share, versus $1.39/L and 22.9% in low-income countries. This indicates greater scope for tax smoothing in high-income systems, while lower-income systems need targeted protection rather than broad universal subsidies.

Recommended policy approach: automatic stabilizers with sunset rules.

Use variable excise buffers or targeted transfers that activate during Brent spikes, preserve price signals outside shock windows, and include transparent fiscal caps. The data show broad subsidies lower prices, but the strongest policy design challenge is balancing consumer protection with budget exposure.

Methodology and Data Notes

Source: /app/benchmark/data.csv, documented in /app/benchmark/source_brief.md. All reported numbers are computed directly from this file.

Calculations: country-week retail averages are arithmetic means; yearly metrics average weekly global means; correlations are Pearson correlations between weekly Brent crude and weekly average retail prices; pass-through slopes are simple linear slopes in USD/liter per USD/barrel.

Limitations: Brent crude is common to all countries for a given week, tax percentages vary week-to-week by country, and the subsidy field contains Low, Medium, High, and Very High categories despite the brief describing a binary High/Low field. No household income, consumption volume, exchange-rate uncertainty, or fiscal cost data are included.

Self-contained report except for Plotly loaded from CDN per output requirements; all chart data are embedded in this HTML.
