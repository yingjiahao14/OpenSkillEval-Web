6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

  • Correlation with Global Benchmarks: The high positive correlation (1.00) between Brent Crude and retail petrol prices demonstrates that domestic pricing heavily follows global commodity shocks. Policymakers should build robust national strategic reserves to cushion against international price spikes.
  • Subsidy Effectiveness and Cost: High-subsidy policies effectively dampen retail costs by roughly 79.5% compared to low-subsidy equivalents. While effective at shielding consumers during the 2022 energy crisis, untargeted subsidies place a heavy fiscal burden on governments. Moving toward targeted, income-based cash transfers is recommended.
  • Regional Inequalities: Significant spreads exist regionally (averaging $2.52 spread per year), largely driven by divergent local taxation, logistical costs, and domestic production capacities.
  • Tax Burden Inequity: The Tax-to-Price ratio varies significantly across national income brackets. High-income nations generally impose higher fuel taxes to fund infrastructure and green transitions, while low-income countries rely heavily on fuel affordability. We recommend dynamic tax calibration to shield low-income citizens during periods of extreme Brent Crude volatility.
