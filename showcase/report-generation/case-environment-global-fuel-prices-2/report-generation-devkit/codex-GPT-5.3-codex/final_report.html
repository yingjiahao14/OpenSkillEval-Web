6) Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Brent pass-through is substantial but moderated. High Brent-retail correlations confirm benchmark pricing influence, while lower retail volatility indicates buffering through policy and market structure.
  2. Volatility shocks differ by episode. 2020 (demand-collapse dynamics) and 2022 (supply/geopolitical disruptions) both transmit to retail fuel markets, but with dampened magnitude.
  3. Subsidies reduce average retail burden. Observed dampening (79.5%) supports subsidies as short-term stabilizers for consumers.
  4. Design quality matters beyond subsidy label. Segment-level pass-through metrics vary across income/subsidy groups, indicating heterogeneous effectiveness.
  5. Regional inequality persists. Mean annual regional spread is 2.516 USD/L, peaking at 2.870 USD/L in 2026.
  6. Tax policy is a key calibration lever. Income-stratified tax burdens suggest scope for dynamic tax instruments to mitigate peak-crude consumer shocks.
