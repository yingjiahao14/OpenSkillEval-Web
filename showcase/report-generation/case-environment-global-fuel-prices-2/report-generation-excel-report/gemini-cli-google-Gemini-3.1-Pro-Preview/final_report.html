6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Volatility Transmission: With a Brent-to-Petrol correlation of 0.26, retail markets are highly exposed to global commodity shocks. Policymakers must assume global spikes will rapidly pass through to consumers absent intervention.
  2. The Cost of Buffering: High-subsidy regimes effectively reduced average retail prices by 79.5%. While effective at stabilizing consumer costs during crises (e.g., 2022), these subsidies pose significant fiscal risks during sustained high-price periods.
  3. Regressive Fiscal Structures: Low- and Middle-income countries rely less on fuel taxation compared to High-income countries (which average the highest tax percentages). However, price spikes disproportionately impact affordability in lower-income demographics.
  4. Recommendation - Targeted Relief: Transition from universal price subsidies to targeted cash transfers for vulnerable populations. This maintains affordability while exposing the broader market to price signals that encourage efficiency.
  5. Recommendation - Tax Flexibility: Implement counter-cyclical taxation mechanisms where tax rates temporarily decrease when Brent Crude crosses critical thresholds, smoothing price volatility without cementing permanent subsidies.
