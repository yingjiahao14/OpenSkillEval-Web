6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

Objective: Synthesize the most important insights into actionable findings for policymakers on subsidy design, tax calibration, and price stabilization mechanisms.
  1. Strong Brent-to-Retail Pass-Through (r = 1.00):
    Global crude price movements translate directly into retail fuel costs with minimal lag. Policymakers cannot insulate consumers from global market forces without active intervention.
  2. High Subsidies Effectively Reduce Prices but Create Fiscal Risk:
    High-subsidy countries maintain prices 79.5% lower than low-subsidy counterparts. However, this benefit comes with fiscal exposure — subsidies can become unsustainable during prolonged price elevation.
  3. 2022 Energy Crisis Caused Greatest Regional Divergence:
    The regional price spread widened significantly in 2022, reaching $2.717/L. Countries with active subsidy mechanisms limited consumer impact while others saw dramatic price increases.
  4. Tax Burden Inversely Correlates with Economic Development:
    High-income countries collect 34.7% of fuel prices in tax while low-income countries collect only 22.9%. This reverses the traditional progressivity expectation for energy taxation.
  5. LPG Offers Most Affordable Path for Low-Income Households:
    LPG averages $1.711/L vs petrol at $2.282/L. Expanding LPG access should be a priority for energy equity initiatives in developing economies.
  6. Volatility Stabilized Post-2022 but Remains Elevated:
    Price volatility peaked in 2022 but has not returned to pre-COVID lows. Current price levels remain 71% higher than 2020 averages, suggesting a structural shift in global fuel pricing.

Recommendations for Policy Design

For High-Subsidy Countries

  • Implement automatic stabilizer mechanisms tied to crude price thresholds
  • Build fiscal buffers during low-price periods to sustain subsidies during crises
  • Consider targeted assistance for vulnerable populations rather than blanket subsidies

For Low-Subsidy Countries

  • Deploy temporary tax relief when crude prices spike above defined thresholds
  • Communicate price movements transparently to allow consumer adaptation
  • Align fuel tax policy with broader climate and revenue objectives
