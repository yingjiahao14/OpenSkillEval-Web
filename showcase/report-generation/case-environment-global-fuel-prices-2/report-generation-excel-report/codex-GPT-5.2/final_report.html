Key Findings & Policy Implications

Synthesize the most important insights from the analysis into actionable findings, including recommendations for policymakers on subsidy design, tax calibration, and price stabilization mechanisms.

Key findings (executive-ready)

  1. 1. Retail prices closely track Brent (near-perfect weekly correlation)
    Weekly global averages show Pearson correlation of 0.999986 for petrol and 0.999983 for diesel versus Brent.
    Policy takeaway: Assume commodity-linked volatility unless explicitly buffered by fiscal measures.
  2. 2. High-subsidy countries report materially lower retail price levels than Low-subsidy countries
    Across the full period, High-subsidy average petrol is 0.709 USD/L versus 3.451 USD/L in Low-subsidy countries (discount: 79.5%).
    Policy takeaway: Subsidies can suppress average consumer prices, but require fiscal capacity and targeting.
  3. 3. Regional price inequality widened after 2020 and remains elevated
    The annual regional spread (max–min of regional average petrol) is 1.612 USD/L in 2020 and 2.870 USD/L in 2026.
    Policy takeaway: Cross-region comparability matters for assessing affordability and competitiveness.
  4. 4. Higher-income countries show higher average tax percentages in this dataset
    Average tax percentage is High: 34.7%, Middle: 21.1%, Low: 22.9%.
    Policy takeaway: Tax calibration is a major lever; distributional impact depends on household fuel intensity and alternatives.
  5. 5. Shock periods show distinct price levels but similar Brent-to-retail linkage
    During the COVID-19 demand shock (2020-03-01 to 2020-06-30), Brent ranges 47.97–70.53 USD/bbl; during the 2022 crisis (2022-02-01 to 2022-09-30), Brent ranges 109.67–130.00 USD/bbl. Window correlations remain >0.999 in both windows.
    Policy takeaway: Crisis-response tools should focus on dampening pass-through, not expecting decoupling from Brent.

Policy implications

  • Use automatic, rules-based stabilization (e.g., sliding excise or price-smoothing funds) to avoid ad-hoc changes that lag weekly commodity moves.
  • Target subsidies tightly (or cap volumes) to limit fiscal leakage while preserving consumer protection during spikes.
  • When subsidies are not feasible, prioritize tax design that is counter-cyclical and transparent, and pair with direct transfers for vulnerable groups.
  • Monitor pass-through metrics (slope/correlation, weekly change volatility) as routine KPIs to evaluate whether interventions are stabilizing prices.
