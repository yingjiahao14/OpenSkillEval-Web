Key Volatility Observations

  • COVID-19 demand shock (2020): Brent collapsed to $47.97/bbl in April 2020. Global average petrol fell to $1.35/L (March 2020 monthly avg), the lowest level in the dataset.
  • 2022 energy crisis: Brent surged to $130/bbl (March–May 2022), pushing global petrol to $2.51/L yearly average — a 68.7% increase from 2020.
  • Post-crisis plateau (2023–2026): Brent stabilized around $104–124/bbl. Retail prices followed with reduced volatility, though they remain structurally higher than pre-pandemic levels.
  • Correlation insight: The overall Brent-to-retail correlation is modest (0.264 for petrol) because high-subsidy and very-high-subsidy countries decouple domestic prices from crude movements. In low-subsidy markets, the correlation rises to 0.554.

Subsidy & Tax Findings

  • Massive dampening effect: High and very-high subsidy countries average $0.43/L vs. $3.45/L in low-subsidy countries — an 86.8% reduction in retail price.
  • Pass-through varies sharply: Low-subsidy markets show Brent-to-petrol correlation of 0.554; very-high-subsidy markets show only 0.306. Medium-subsidy markets actually show the highest correlation (0.780), likely because partial subsidy adjustments lag crude swings.
  • Tax burden is regressive by design in high-income countries: High-income nations levy 34.7% average tax vs. 21.1–22.9% in middle- and low-income nations. This creates a dual insulation mechanism: subsidies protect low-income consumers from crude volatility, while taxes fund public budgets in wealthy nations.
  • Income-subsidy cross-section: Even within high-income countries, high-subsidy regimes (e.g., oil exporters) hold petrol at $0.91/L on average, while low-subsidy high-income countries average $4.00/L.

6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

Actionable insights for energy policymakers on subsidy design, tax calibration, and price stabilization.

Brent-to-retail pass-through is highly policy-dependent. The global correlation of 0.264 masks enormous heterogeneity: low-subsidy markets track crude closely (0.554), while very-high-subsidy markets decouple almost entirely (0.306). Policymakers should calibrate automatic pricing mechanisms based on desired consumer exposure.
Subsidies are the dominant price-stabilization tool but carry fiscal risk. The 86.8% price dampening effect is unmistakable. However, very-high-subsidy countries show the lowest price volatility (std = 0.087) but also the weakest market signals for energy efficiency. Targeted, temporary subsidies — rather than blanket price controls — can buffer shocks without chronic fiscal drain.
Regional inequality is widening, not narrowing. The regional price spread grew from $1.61/L (2020) to $2.87/L (2026). Europe and Oceania’s high-tax, high-price model funds infrastructure but may exacerbate energy poverty if not paired with targeted transfers. Middle East and South America’s low-price model shields consumers but suppresses public revenue.
Tax policy is the primary lever in high-income countries; subsidies dominate in low-income ones. High-income nations average 34.7% fuel tax vs. ~22% in lower-income groups. Policymakers in wealthy markets should focus on tax smoothing (e.g., variable excise duties) to dampen volatility without sacrificing revenue. Low-income policymakers should prioritize subsidy targeting to protect vulnerable households while allowing partial pass-through to state budgets.
The 2022 crisis proved that neither pure market pricing nor full subsidy isolation is optimal. Low-subsidy countries saw rapid consumer pain (68.7% price surge); very-high-subsidy countries faced unsustainable fiscal costs. A hybrid model — e.g., price bands with adjustable tax/subsidy margins — offers the best balance of stability and fiscal sustainability.
Medium-subsidy regimes showed the highest Brent correlation (0.780), indicating policy lag risk. When subsidies are partial and adjusted irregularly, consumers experience the worst of both worlds: delayed price spikes and unpredictable fiscal costs. Monthly or quarterly automatic adjustment formulas reduce this lag and improve predictability.
