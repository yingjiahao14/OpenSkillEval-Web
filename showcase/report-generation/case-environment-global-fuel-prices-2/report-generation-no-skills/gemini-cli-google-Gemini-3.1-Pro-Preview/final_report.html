1. Global Fuel Price Overview

Between January 2020 and April 2026, the global average retail price of petrol stood at $2.28 per liter, closely followed by diesel at $2.13 per liter, while LPG remained an affordable alternative at $1.71 per liter. During this period, Brent Crude served as the foundational benchmark, driving significant portions of retail cost variations.

2. Temporal Price Trends & Volatility

The time series reveals the immediate demand collapse during the 2020 COVID-19 pandemic, followed by a sharp and sustained surge peaking during the 2022 global energy crisis. The strong correlation (r = 0.26) between Brent Crude and retail petrol demonstrates that despite local buffers, global commodity shocks reliably dictate the overarching trajectory of consumer prices.

3. Regional Price Disparities

Fuel costs vary dramatically by geographic region, heavily influenced by local production capacities, proximity to refineries, and fiscal policies. The average yearly price spread between the most and least expensive regions is $2.52 per liter. While some regions consistently maintain lower prices through subsidies or production, others structurally bear higher baseline costs.

4. Impact of Subsidies and Taxation on Retail Prices

Government intervention plays a critical role in shielding consumers from price shocks. Our analysis indicates a 79.5% price dampening effect in High-subsidy countries compared to Low-subsidy countries. This buffering effect is notably more pronounced in lower- and middle-income nations that prioritize energy affordability.

5. Fuel Affordability by Income Level

National wealth closely correlates with fiscal strategies on fuel. High-income nations generally apply higher tax burdens on fuel, pushing the average tax percentage to 34.7%. In contrast, low-income nations maintain lower taxation rates (22.9%) and frequently utilize subsidies to maintain social stability and economic accessibility.

6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

  • Global Shocks Pass Through Despite Local Policies With a correlation of 0.26, Brent Crude prices heavily dictate domestic retail prices. Policymakers should build dynamic strategic reserves and consider floating tax buffers to mitigate sudden global price spikes.
  • Subsidies Are Effective but Expensive High subsidies reduce retail costs by 79.5%, successfully shielding consumers. However, untargeted subsidies disproportionately benefit higher-consumption groups. Transitioning to targeted cash transfers or income-based fuel credits can improve fiscal efficiency.
  • Income Levels Dictate Fiscal Approaches High-income nations consistently treat fuel as a revenue source (taxes ~34.7%), while low-income nations treat it as a social good. As global markets transition to renewables, low-income nations must gradually phase out fossil fuel subsidies to free up fiscal space for green energy investments.
  • Regional Disparities Highlight the Need for Integration A persistent spread of $2.52 between the cheapest and most expensive regions indicates inefficiencies. Strengthening cross-border energy infrastructure and regional trade agreements could help normalize these extreme price disparities.
