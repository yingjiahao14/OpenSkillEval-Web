Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Brent-to-retail co-movement is extremely tight at the global weekly level (petrol r={_fmt_num(corr_petrol,4)}; diesel r={_fmt_num(corr_diesel,4)}), implying limited aggregate decoupling from crude over 2020–2026.
  2. Volatility transmission is similar in magnitude across Brent and retail weekly means (weekly % change std ~{_fmt_num(metrics['volatility']['brent_weekly_pct_std']*100,2)}–{_fmt_num(metrics['volatility']['diesel_weekly_pct_std']*100,2)} percentage points), suggesting short-run shocks propagate rapidly in the aggregate.
  3. Subsidy labeling is associated with large retail price level differences: High-subsidy petrol averages {_fmt_num(metrics['dampening_high_vs_low']['petrol_usd_liter']['high_avg'],3)} USD/L vs Low-subsidy {_fmt_num(metrics['dampening_high_vs_low']['petrol_usd_liter']['low_avg'],3)} USD/L (−{_fmt_num(damp_petrol_pct,1)}%).
  4. Pass-through varies by subsidy regime (e.g., Very High has lower r than Low), but correlations remain high across groups, indicating that subsidies in this dataset appear to reduce price levels more than they fully insulate against benchmark movement.
  5. Regional inequality widened in this dataset: petrol regional spread increased from {_fmt_num(metrics['regional_spread_yearly'][2020],3)} USD/L (2020) to {_fmt_num(metrics['regional_spread_yearly'][2026],3)} USD/L (2026).
Policy action cues (derived from the above): prioritize rules-based stabilization (e.g., variable excise bands) where pass-through is high; target subsidies where affordability constraints are binding; monitor fiscal exposure when tax shares are high and retail volatility tracks Brent.
