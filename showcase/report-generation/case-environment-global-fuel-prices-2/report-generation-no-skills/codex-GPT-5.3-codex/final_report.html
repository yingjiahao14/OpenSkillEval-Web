Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Retail prices strongly track Brent (correlations 1.000 petrol; 1.000 diesel), confirming commodity-price transmission as the central driver.
  2. Shock periods materially alter consumer exposure: during Feb–Dec 2022 Brent moved -17.7% while retail prices changed less, indicating damped but significant pass-through.
  3. Subsidies measurably shield consumers: high-subsidy countries show lower average prices (petrol dampening 79.5%) and generally weaker slope sensitivity to Brent.
  4. Regional inequality is persistent and counter-cyclical: spread peaked at $2.870/L in 2026 and widened during high-volatility windows.
  5. Policy design implication: combine targeted subsidies with flexible tax calibration to smooth shocks without broad fiscal leakage.
  6. Operational recommendation: institutionalize weekly monitoring of pass-through correlation/slope by subsidy and income segments to trigger pre-defined stabilization actions.
