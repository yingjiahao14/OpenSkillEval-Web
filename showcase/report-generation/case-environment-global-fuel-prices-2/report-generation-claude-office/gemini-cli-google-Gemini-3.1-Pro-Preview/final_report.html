Global Fuel Price Overview

Over the 2020–2026 period, retail fuel prices have shown significant volatility, largely driven by global crude oil market dynamics and local fiscal policies. The overall global average price for petrol was $2.28/liter, while diesel averaged $2.13/liter and LPG averaged $1.71/liter. These averages mask significant underlying disparities driven by national policies, economic statuses, and geographic locations.

Temporal Price Trends & Volatility

Retail fuel prices closely track the global benchmark, Brent Crude, with a strong correlation of 0.26 for petrol and 0.26 for diesel. The COVID-19 demand shock in early 2020 led to historic lows in crude prices, which subsequently reflected in lower retail costs. Conversely, the 2022 energy crisis saw Brent Crude prices spike significantly, driving retail prices to multi-year highs across most markets.

Regional Price Disparities

Geographic disparities in fuel pricing are substantial. In 2026, the spread between the highest and lowest regional average petrol price reached $2.87/liter. Regions with structural reliance on fuel imports or high consumption taxes consistently register higher retail prices, whereas regions comprising major oil producers often benefit from lower baseline costs.

Impact of Subsidies and Taxation on Retail Prices

Government intervention plays a crucial role in buffering consumers from global volatility. High-subsidy countries effectively dampened retail prices, resulting in an average price reduction of 79.5% compared to low-subsidy countries. Furthermore, high-subsidy environments exhibit a flatter response to Brent Crude spikes, protecting consumers during the 2022 crisis at the cost of significant fiscal outlay.

Fuel Affordability by Income Level

National income classification strongly correlates with both retail fuel prices and taxation strategies. High-income nations generally impose heavier tax burdens (averaging 34.7%), leading to higher nominal prices at the pump. In contrast, Low-income countries average a lower tax burden (22.9%), reflecting policies aimed at keeping essential energy accessible, despite the fiscal strain it places on national budgets.

Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Volatility Transmission is High: With a 0.26 correlation between Brent Crude and retail petrol, global shocks rapidly transmit to local consumers. Policymakers must maintain strategic reserves or financial buffers to manage sudden spikes.
  2. Subsidies Effectively Dampen Shocks: High-subsidy regimes achieved a 79.5% price reduction, shielding populations during the 2022 crisis. However, the flat price response to crude spikes implies massive, unpredictable fiscal liabilities for governments.
  3. Tax Calibration Drives Regional Inequality: High-income countries use fuel taxes as a reliable revenue stream, leading to higher baseline prices but encouraging fuel efficiency. Low- and middle-income countries should consider transitioning from blanket subsidies to targeted cash transfers to protect vulnerable populations without subsidizing wealthy consumers.
  4. Persistent Regional Spread: A regional spread of $2.87/liter in the most recent year highlights that local fiscal policy often overrides global commodity pricing in determining the end-user cost.
