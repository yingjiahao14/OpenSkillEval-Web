6. Key Findings & Policy Implications

  1. Retail fuel prices closely tracked Brent. Weekly correlations with Brent were 1.000 for petrol and 1.000 for diesel, so crude benchmarks remain the dominant external risk factor for pump-price stabilization strategies.
  2. Subsidies dampened both level and pass-through. High-subsidy petrol prices were 79.5% below Low-subsidy prices, and the High-subsidy pass-through slope was 79.4% lower than Low subsidy. Policymakers should evaluate whether this consumer shielding justifies fiscal cost and targeting leakage.
  3. Very High subsidy regimes showed the strongest buffer. Very High-subsidy markets averaged $0.148 per liter for petrol and reduced the pass-through slope by 95.7% versus Low subsidy; this is effective stabilization but likely requires explicit budget stress-testing during Brent spikes.
  4. Regional inequality remained substantial. The widest annual regional petrol spread was $2.870 in 2026; maintaining regional monitoring can identify import-dependent or high-tax markets where volatility relief is most needed.
  5. Tax calibration is central to affordability. Average tax share was 26.8%, with income-level variation shown in the tax table. Temporary tax smoothing rules can reduce consumer shock while preserving medium-term revenue if paired with automatic reinstatement triggers.
  6. Price caps alone should not be treated as full insulation. Even subsidized groups retained positive Brent correlations, so durable stabilization should combine targeted transfers, transparent fuel tax bands and fiscal buffers rather than relying solely on administratively suppressed retail prices.

Methodology & Limitations

All statistics are computed directly from data.csv. Correlations and pass-through slopes use weekly segment averages to avoid giving Brent crude repeated country-level weight within the same week. Pass-through slopes are simple linear slopes and should be interpreted as descriptive, not causal.

Limitations: subsidy labels are categorical and do not include fiscal spending amounts; tax percentages vary weekly and are treated as provided; the dataset does not include exchange-rate uncertainty, household income, consumption volumes or policy announcement dates.
