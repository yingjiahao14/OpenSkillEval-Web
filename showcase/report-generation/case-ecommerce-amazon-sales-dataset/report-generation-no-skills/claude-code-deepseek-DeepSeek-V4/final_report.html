Executive Overview

The catalog spans 1,464 products across 9 top-level categories. The average rating of 4.10/5.0 and 75.8% high-rating share (≥4.0) indicate solid overall product quality. The average discount of 47.7% reflects aggressive promotional activity typical of the Indian e-commerce market.

Key Finding The correlation between discount percentage and customer rating is r = -0.155 — indicating a weak negative relationship. Deeper discounts do not translate into higher ratings; in fact, heavily discounted products tend to score slightly lower. This suggests discounts alone are not a lever for improving customer satisfaction; product quality and category fundamentals matter more.
Price-Rating Relationship The correlation between actual price and rating is r = 0.122 — indicating that higher-priced products tend to receive slightly higher ratings on average, likely reflecting better build quality and brand trust in premium segments.

Strategic Recommendations

1. Rebalance Discount Strategy — Deep Discounts Are Not Buying Higher Satisfaction

The correlation between discount percentage and rating is r = -0.155. Products with 80%+ discounts average only 4.02 rating vs 4.17 for those with modest (0-20%) discounts. Redirect deep-discount budget toward improving product quality and curation in underperforming categories rather than using price cuts to mask quality gaps.

Data: 96 products with 80%+ discounts average 4.02 rating; 165 products with <20% discounts average 4.17.
2. Prioritize Quality Curation in Low-Performing Categories

Several categories show average ratings below 4.0 and low high-rating shares. Focus assortment curation efforts on these categories — audit product listings, remove consistently low-rated SKUs, and tighten vendor quality standards. Each percentage point improvement in category average rating correlates with measurably higher engagement.

Data: Bottom categories by rating need targeted intervention; the gap between best (3.80) and worst (4.31) category average ratings is -0.51 points.
3. Invest in the Electronics & Premium Segments

Higher-priced products (₹5,000+) show stronger average ratings, and price-to-rating correlation is positive (r = 0.122). Products in the ₹500-₹1,000 sweet spot combine strong ratings with high review volumes. Consider expanding premium sub-categories and increasing visibility for mid-to-high-price products with proven quality signals.

Data: Premium segment (₹15,000+) avg rating: 4.20 vs budget segment (<₹500) avg rating: 4.07.
4. Address Durability & Compatibility Pain Points in Accessories

Sentiment analysis reveals recurring negative themes around product durability ("stopped working", "broke"), charging speed ("slow charging"), and compatibility ("doesn't fit", "not compatible"). These issues concentrate in the Cables & Accessories sub-categories. Implement stricter quality checks for cable durability claims and require clearer compatibility labeling on product pages.

Data: Negative keywords "slow", "issue", "problem", and "broke"/"broken" appear in a significant share of reviews, especially for USB cable products.
5. Leverage High-Engagement Categories for Cross-Sell & Retention

Categories with the highest review volumes (Electronics, Computers & Accessories) represent strong customer engagement hubs. Use these as entry points for cross-category recommendations and loyalty initiatives. Products with 10,000+ reviews consistently show above-average ratings and represent proven winners worth promoting.

Data: The top 12 products by review volume account for a disproportionate share of total engagement, all with ratings ≥ 4.0 and moderate discounts (13-65%).
