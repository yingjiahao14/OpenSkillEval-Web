Pricing & Discount Strategy Analysis

This section quantifies whether discounts translate into higher customer satisfaction or merely inflate review volume.

Avg Rating by Discount Band

Avg Engagement by Discount Band

Critical finding: Products with 0–20% discounts average the highest rating (4.17). As discounts deepen beyond 60%, average ratings decline to ~4.08 and plunge to 3.98 in the 81–100% band. Engagement (average rating count) does not systematically rise with discount depth—correlation is effectively zero (r = 0.011). Deeper discounts are not buying love; they are buying volume at best and eroding perceived quality at worst.

Price Segment Performance

Insight: Luxury products (>₹5,000) achieve the highest average rating (4.17) with the lowest discounting (33.5%). Budget products (≤₹500) are heavily discounted (55.7%) yet score lower (4.07), reinforcing that premium positioning—not deep discounting—drives satisfaction.

Strategic Recommendations

1
Reduce deep-discount dependency in low-rated sub-categories.

Headphones, Earbuds & Accessories (avg rating 3.93, discount 59.5%) and Wearable Technology (4.02, 69.8%) combine the deepest cuts with the weakest scores. Shift promotional spend from blanket percentage discounts to bundle offers or warranty upgrades that improve perceived value without slashing list price.

2
Protect margin on 77 “price-commanding” SKUs.

These products maintain ≥4.3 ratings with ≤20% discount. They are the catalog’s profit anchors. Avoid placing them in site-wide sales; instead, use targeted loyalty coupons or limited-time flash events to preserve price integrity while driving repeat purchases.

3
Prioritize Home & Kitchen for quality uplift.

Home & Kitchen has the lowest median engagement (2,311) and the weakest rating (4.04) among the Big Three categories. Review themes highlight “average” and “slow” issues. Investing in supplier quality audits and clearer product specifications should lift ratings and, in turn, organic traffic.

4
Reallocate marketing budget toward high-engagement, low-discount segments.

Luxury products (>₹5,000) achieve the highest satisfaction (4.17) with only 33.5% average discount. Their buyers are less price-sensitive and more feature-driven. Increase visibility through premium placements and comparison content rather than competing on discount depth.

5
Use review-theme data to fix the “value vs. quality” gap.

Because “value for money” and “quality” are the most cited themes, products scoring below 4.0 should be audited against these two dimensions specifically. A 1-point rating improvement in the 83 high-discount/low-rating SKUs could unlock higher conversion without additional promotional spend.
