Strategic Recommendations

Each recommendation below links directly to measured data in the report.

1

Stop using deeper discounts as a proxy for satisfaction improvement.

Discount percentage has a weak correlation with rating (-0.155, n=1,464). Use discounts for traffic or inventory objectives, but require margin guardrails and category-specific tests before expanding depth.

2

Scale learnings from the strongest-rated category.

Office Products leads eligible top categories with a 4.31 average rating across 31 products. Review its assortment, content quality, and seller standards as benchmarks for lower-rated categories.

3

Prioritize remediation for the lowest-rated category cohort.

Home & Kitchen has the lowest eligible top-category average rating at 4.04 across 448 products. Audit product detail pages, defect-related reviews, and seller quality before adding more SKUs.

4

Use engagement leaders to improve catalog merchandising.

Electronics generates 15,778,848 total ratings, the highest category engagement. Feature proven review-rich products in search, bundles, and comparison placements while monitoring rating stability.

5

Segment pricing actions by observed price-band performance.

The highest average rating price segment is ₹10k+ at 4.20; the lowest is <₹500 at 4.07. Manage premium and entry tiers with distinct quality thresholds rather than a single catalog-wide discount rule.

Method notes: price fields were parsed by removing ₹ symbols and commas; discount percentages were parsed from percentage strings; malformed numeric values were excluded pairwise from calculations; rating-count correlation uses log10(rating_count + 1) to reduce skew.
