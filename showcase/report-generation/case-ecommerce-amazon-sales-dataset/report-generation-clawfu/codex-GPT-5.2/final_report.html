Strategic Recommendations

Treat deep discounting as a margin lever, not a satisfaction lever
Discount%→Rating correlation is -0.155 (Pearson), so deeper discounts do not systematically correspond to better ratings in this dataset.
Don’t assume discounts drive review volume
Discount%→rating_count correlation is 0.012 (Pearson), indicating little relationship between discount depth and engagement volume.
Prioritize remediation in the lowest-performing large sub-category
Lowest-rated sub-category among segments with ≥25 products: Electronics → Headphones,Earbuds&Accessories (avg rating 3.93 across 66 products). Audit listing quality, product defects, and expectation-setting content.
Replicate playbooks from the highest-performing large sub-category
Best-rated sub-category among segments with ≥25 products: OfficeProducts → OfficePaperProducts (avg rating 4.29 across 27 products). Review pricing position, discounting pattern, and product content to scale best practices.
Use segment tables to set guardrails
Operationalize policy by monitoring rating + engagement at the price-band and discount-band levels; treat any band with declining rating as a QA / assortment flag rather than increasing discount depth.
