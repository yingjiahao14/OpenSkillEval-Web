Core Finding: Discounts Do Not Drive Ratings

The Pearson correlation between discount percentage and customer rating is r = -0.155 — effectively zero. This means offering deeper discounts does not lead to higher customer satisfaction, a critical insight for promotional planning.

Strategic Recommendations

Five data-driven, actionable recommendations for category management, pricing optimization, and customer experience improvement.

1

Deploy Discounts Strategically, Not Indiscriminately

The correlation between discount depth and customer rating is effectively zero (r = -0.155). Discounts do not buy higher satisfaction. Instead, reserve deep discounts for demand generation in price-elastic categories and use moderate discounts as an acquisition tool for new products needing initial rating volume.

⚡ Data: Discount↔Rating correlation = -0.155 | Avg rating stable across all discount buckets
2

Address Underperforming Categories with Quality Interventions

Categories with average ratings below 4.1 (the catalog average) should trigger a catalog review. Low ratings are not offset by higher discounts — the root cause is product quality, not pricing. Consider delisting consistently low-rated SKUs and tightening vendor quality requirements.

⚡ Data: 6 products rated below 3.0 | Bottom categories identified in Section 2
3

Focus on the 4.0–4.5 Rating Band to Push Products to Excellence

With 75.8% of products at or above 4.0 but a median of 4.10, there is a large cluster of "good but not great" products. A targeted initiative to improve 4.0–4.3 rated products to 4.5+ could materially lift overall catalog perception without requiring new product development.

⚡ Data: Median rating = 4.10 | High-rated share = 75.8% | Only 104 products achieve 4.5+
4

Prioritize Durability & Build Quality in Product Sourcing

Sentiment analysis shows "quality", "durable", and "value" are the most frequent review themes. Negative reviews consistently cite durability failures and defective units. Vendor scorecards should weight durability-related review keywords as a leading indicator for sourcing decisions.

⚡ Data: "Quality" is the #1 most-used word in reviews | "Durable"/"sturdy" rank among top positive terms
5

Leverage High-Engagement Categories for New Product Launches

Categories with high rating counts (driven by high consumer demand) are ideal launchpads for new products. The top-engaged categories generate significant organic review volume quickly, accelerating the feedback flywheel for new SKUs.

⚡ Data: Top categories by engagement identified in Section 4 | Median rating count = 5,179
