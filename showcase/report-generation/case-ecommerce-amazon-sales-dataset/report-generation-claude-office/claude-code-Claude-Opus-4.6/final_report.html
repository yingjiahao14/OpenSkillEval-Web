6 Strategic Recommendations

Data-backed actions for category management, pricing, and customer experience.

  1. Reduce discount dependency in Computers&Accessories and Electronics These categories average 54% and 51% discounts respectively, yet the correlation between discount depth and rating is negative (r = −0.155). Every 10pp increase in discount corresponds to roughly a 0.02-point drop in average rating. Shift budget from blanket discounts to perceived-value levers: bundling, enhanced product content, and targeted promotions on high-potential SKUs.
  2. Invest in Home&Kitchen catalog quality Home&Kitchen has the lowest average rating (4.04) and lowest median reviews (2,311) — 78% lower engagement than Electronics. Audit low-rated SKUs in this category, improve product descriptions, and consider removing sub-3.5-star items that drag down category perception.
  3. Expand the OfficeProducts playbook OfficeProducts achieves the highest rating (4.31) with the smallest discounts (12.4%). This "quality-at-fair-price" model should inform strategy for other categories. Identify OfficeProducts-like segments in other categories (low discount, high rating) and protect them from discount escalation.
  4. Address reliability complaints proactively "Not working" (72 mentions) and "defective" (21 mentions) are the most actionable negative signals. Cross-reference these with specific sub-categories and sellers to identify quality control gaps. A 1% defect reduction could shift dozens of products from the 3.5–4.0 band into the 4.0+ tier, directly improving the high-rating share above 75.8%.
  5. Optimize the 20-30% discount sweet spot The 20-29% discount band shows the highest average review volume (22,009) while maintaining a strong 4.16 rating — better than any band above 30%. This suggests a "moderate discount" strategy maximizes both engagement and satisfaction. Products currently at 50%+ discounts should be tested at lower discount levels.
