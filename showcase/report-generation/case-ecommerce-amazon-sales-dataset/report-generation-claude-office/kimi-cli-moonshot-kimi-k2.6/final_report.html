Pricing & Discount Strategy Analysis

This section directly addresses management’s highest-priority question: Do deeper discounts drive higher customer satisfaction, or merely higher review volume?

Quantified Finding: The correlation between discount percentage and customer rating is r = −0.155 (p < 0.001). This is a statistically significant negative relationship: every 10-point increase in discount is associated with roughly a 0.015–0.02 star decline in average rating. The relationship holds across all three major categories: Electronics (−0.179), Home & Kitchen (−0.239), and Computers & Accessories (−0.129).
Deep Discount Risk Zone: 38 products have discounts ≥70% and ratings below 3.8. Electronics contributes 18 of these, Home & Kitchen 12. These SKUs are likely eroding margin and damaging brand perception.
Low-Discount Quality Zone: 77 products achieve ratings ≥4.3 with discounts ≤20%. Home & Kitchen leads with 25 such products, followed by Electronics (21) and Office Products (16). These are strong candidates for margin protection.

Customer Engagement & Rating Distribution

The rating distribution is heavily left-skewed: 77.1% of products fall between 3.8–4.4 stars. Only 3 products achieve a perfect 5.0 rating, while just 42 products (2.9%) score below 3.5. This indicates a generally healthy catalog but with limited "wow" experiences.

High-Engagement Profile: The top 10% most-reviewed products (≥39,640 ratings) share these characteristics: average rating 4.17, average discount 49.1%, average actual price ₹4,167, and 60% belong to Electronics. Notably, 7 of the top 10 are AmazonBasics or boAt branded cables/earphones — indicating that strong brand recognition + aggressive pricing is the engagement formula.

Strategic Recommendations

1

Stop the Discount-Rating Death Spiral in Home & Kitchen

Home & Kitchen has the lowest rating (4.04) and weakest engagement (2,311 median ratings) despite 40% average discounts. With 20 of 42 low-rated products in this category, management should audit the bottom-quartile SKUs for quality defects and pause deep promotions until ratings recover. Data: 20 products rated <3.5 vs. 8 in Computers and 14 in Electronics.

2

Protect Margin on Low-Discount, High-Rating Products

77 products achieve ≥4.3 stars with ≤20% discounts. These are proven quality winners that do not need promotional support. Shift marketing spend from discounting to visibility (e.g., "Top Rated" badges, search placement) to preserve margin. Data: Office Products alone contributes 16 such SKUs with an average rating of 4.31.

3

Investigate and Delist or Reform High-Discount, Low-Rating SKUs

38 products sit in the danger zone (≥70% discount, <3.8 rating). These items likely generate negative word-of-mouth and drag down category averages. A SKU-level profitability review should determine whether to reformulate, replace the supplier, or delist. Data: Electronics contributes 18 of these; Accessories sub-category is the biggest culprit.

4

Replicate the High-Engagement Formula: Brand + Value

The top 10 most-reviewed products are dominated by AmazonBasics and boAt — recognizable brands priced aggressively. New assortment decisions should prioritize private-label or established brand partnerships in high-frequency categories (cables, earphones, HDMI). Data: Top 10 products average 4.2 stars and 158K+ ratings.

5

Shift Mid-Range Promotional Strategy from Discount Depth to Bundle Value

The Mid price segment (₹500–2K) has the heaviest discounting (52.3%) with no rating advantage over Premium or Luxury. Instead of deeper cuts, test bundle offers (e.g., cable + adapter) or loyalty incentives. This maintains perceived value while protecting unit margin. Data: Mid-segment correlation of discount vs. rating is −0.14 — the weakest ROI on promotions.
