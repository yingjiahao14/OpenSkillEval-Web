Key Takeaway: The catalog is generally well-received with 75.8% of products scoring 4.0 or above. However, the average discount of 47.7% suggests a highly promotional environment, which warrants closer inspection regarding its actual impact on customer satisfaction.

Category Performance Breakdown

We analyzed product distribution, average ratings, and engagement metrics across the top-level product categories to identify segments driving satisfaction and those requiring improvement.

Category Insights:
  • Office Products and Home & Kitchen exhibit some of the highest average customer ratings.
  • Categories like Electronics show a wide variance in both discounting strategies and resulting customer satisfaction.
  • Discounting alone does not guarantee a higher category average rating; there are categories with lower discounts but higher average ratings, emphasizing product quality over price promotion.

Pricing & Discount Strategy Analysis

Management specifically queried whether deeper discounts correlate with better ratings. We examined the relationship between discount percentage and customer ratings to evaluate the "Price-to-Discount Efficiency".

Pricing Insight: The correlation between discount percentage and customer rating is -0.155. This very weak/negative correlation implies that deeper discounts do not drive higher customer satisfaction. Discounts may temporarily increase sales velocity, but they do not offset poor product quality in reviews. Margin is likely being eroded unnecessarily on products that do not warrant deep promotions.

Customer Engagement & Rating Distribution

Understanding the volume of ratings helps distinguish between niche, highly-rated items and mass-market products with proven track records.

Engagement Insight: The rating distribution is left-skewed, meaning the vast majority of products score between 3.8 and 4.5. Products with the highest review volumes (highest engagement) tend to stabilize around the 4.0 - 4.4 mark, demonstrating consistent, acceptable mass-market quality.

Strategic Recommendations

Based on the data analysis, we recommend the following strategic actions for category and product managers:

  1. Rationalize Discounting Strategies: With a correlation of -0.155 between discount percentage and ratings, we recommend dialing back aggressive discounting (which averages 47.7% across the board). Focus promotional budgets on products that already have strong ratings (4.0+) to amplify conversion, rather than using discounts to salvage poorly rated inventory.
  2. Address Bottom-Tier Categories: Categories appearing lower on the "Average Rating by Main Category" chart require an immediate catalog audit. Implement stricter quality controls or delist consistently underperforming products (sub-3.5 stars) to protect brand equity, as discounts do not improve customer perception here.
  3. Leverage High-Engagement 'Hero' Products: Products with both a high rating (>4.2) and high engagement (review count > 5,179) are the catalog's anchors. Increase visibility for these products through sponsored ads or bundle them with lower-visibility items to improve overall cart size, as they represent proven customer satisfaction.
  4. Optimize Pricing Tiering: Ensure product descriptions and images are highly accurate to prevent expectation mismatches, which drive the lowest ratings. The data shows that "value for money" drives positive sentiment more than absolute low price or high discount percent. Focus marketing copy on value and durability rather than just "percentage off."
