Strategic Recommendations

All recommendations are derived directly from the dataset and supported by specific metrics.

1. Reduce Reliance on Deep Discounts to Drive Satisfaction

The correlation between discount depth and rating is negative (r = -0.156). Products with >70% discounts average 4.02 stars vs. 4.24 for zero-discount items. Shift promotional strategy from margin-eroding discounts toward value-add bundling, warranty extensions, or loyalty incentives.

2. Prioritize Home & Kitchen Quality Initiatives

Home & Kitchen is the lowest-rated major category (4.04 avg) with 447 products. Sub-categories like Heating, Cooling & Air Quality (3.99 avg) drag the segment down. Conduct root-cause analysis on return rates and supplier quality in this category.

3. Protect and Expand the Premium Segment

Premium products (>₹5K) achieve the highest ratings (4.17) with the lowest discount depth (33.5%). This segment signals strong brand trust. Increase assortment depth here and reduce promotional intensity — margins are healthier and customers are already satisfied.

4. Fix Quality Consistency in High-Engagement Electronics Accessories

Headphones, Earbuds & Accessories generates massive review volume (73K+ avg per product) but scores only 3.93 — the lowest among major sub-categories. High engagement with low ratings creates outsized reputational risk. Tighten QC standards and supplier audits for this sub-category immediately.

5. Use "Value for Money" Positioning Over "Deep Discount" Messaging

"Value for money" is the most frequent positive phrase (460 mentions) in customer reviews. Marketing and listing copy should emphasize durability, warranty, and total cost of ownership rather than percentage-off headlines. This aligns customer expectations with actual product performance and reduces disappointment-driven negative reviews.
