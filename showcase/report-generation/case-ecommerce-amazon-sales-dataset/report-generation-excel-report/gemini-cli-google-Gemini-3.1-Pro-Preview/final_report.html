Category Performance Breakdown

Analyzing product distribution and performance across top-level categories. This helps identify which segments are winning with customers and which segments require curation.

Insight: Office Products and Toys & Games categories show high average ratings, while Home & Kitchen tends to have some of the highest discount percentages. High discounts do not automatically guarantee top tier category ratings.

Pricing & Discount Strategy Analysis

Does discounting drive better customer satisfaction? The analysis below examines the relationship between how heavily a product is discounted and its overall customer rating.

Insight: The correlation between discount percentage and customer rating is -0.155, which is statistically negligible. This indicates that deeper discounts do not correlate with higher customer satisfaction. Customers rate based on product quality and utility, not simply the perceived "deal" they received.

Customer Engagement & Rating Distribution

Profiling the distribution of ratings across the catalog. This highlights the overall quality skew of the current product assortment.

Insight: The catalog skews heavily positive, with 75.8% of products scoring 4.0 or above. The distribution peaks around 4.1 to 4.3, indicating a generally healthy and well-curated product selection.

Review Sentiment Snapshot

A high-level qualitative overview of common positive and negative themes mentioned by customers in their reviews and titles.

Insight: Positive sentiment heavily outweighs negative sentiment in raw volume. Value, quality ("good", "nice"), and performance ("great", "perfect") are the leading positive drivers. Negative sentiment primarily revolves around product failures ("bad", "poor", "waste", "defective").

Strategic Recommendations

  • 1. Re-evaluate Deep Discount Strategies

    Since data shows almost zero correlation between discount depth and customer rating (-0.155), management should test reducing discount depths on high-performing categories to preserve margin without sacrificing customer satisfaction.

  • 2. Prune Underperforming Categories

    Categories consistently scoring lower average ratings despite high promotional intensity should undergo aggressive assortment review. Remove consistently defective products driving the "waste" and "poor" sentiment keywords.

  • 3. Leverage Highly-Engaged Products for Cross-Selling

    With a median rating count of 5,179 and several products significantly exceeding this, capitalize on high-traffic, highly-rated items by grouping them with related higher-margin accessories.

  • 4. Quality Control on "Defective" Flags

    Address the specific products driving the "defective" and "broken" keywords. Establishing a threshold (e.g., dropping products that fall below a 3.5 rating) will maintain the current 75.8% high-rating share and protect brand trust.
