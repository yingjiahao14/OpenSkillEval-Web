Strategic Recommendations

Synthesize findings into 3-5 actionable recommendations for category management, pricing optimization, and customer experience improvement.

1. Recalibrate Discount Strategy — Depth Does Not Equal Satisfaction

The correlation between discount percentage and rating is effectively zero (r = -0.155). Heavy discounting erodes margins without improving customer satisfaction. Management should test moderate discount levels (20-40%) against deep discounts (60%+) in A/B pricing experiments to verify margin-optimized pricing can maintain equivalent ratings.

Data Point: r = -0.155 between discount % and rating; avg discount is 47.7% across the catalog

2. Invest in Underperforming High-Engagement Categories

Categories with high review volumes but below-average ratings represent significant brand exposure risk — many customers are seeing sub-par experiences. Target the bottom-rated categories with supplier quality audits and enhanced product listing accuracy checks.

Data Point: 5 categories average below 4.08 rating despite significant product volume

3. Protect Premium Segment Price Integrity

Premium and luxury price tiers (₹1,000+) maintain competitive ratings with significantly lower average discounts than budget segments. This validates a value-based pricing approach for premium products. Avoid importing aggressive discounting tactics from budget categories into premium segments.

Data Point: Price-rating correlation r = +0.122 — higher prices do not harm satisfaction

4. Focus on Quality Control — Defects Are the #1 Dissatisfaction Driver

Review sentiment analysis shows "broken", "not working", "defective", and "waste" as dominant negative themes. Customer satisfaction is primarily quality-driven, not price-driven. Implement stricter pre-listing quality verification for high-return-rate categories and increase supplier accountability metrics.

Data Point: Product defect mentions ('broken', 'defective', 'not working') rank among top 10 negative themes

5. Curate the Catalog for Quality — 76% High-Rating Share Is a Starting Point

While 75.8% of products rate 4.0+, the remaining 24.2% represent an opportunity. Set a target to increase high-rating share to 85%+ through delisting consistently low-rated products and promoting high-rated alternatives. Create a "Customer Favorites" curation featuring products in the 4.5+ rating tier with significant review counts.

Data Point: 75.8% of products currently rated ≥ 4.0; 7.1% rated ≥ 4.5
