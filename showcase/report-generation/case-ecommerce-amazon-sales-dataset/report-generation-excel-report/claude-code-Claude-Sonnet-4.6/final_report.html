💰 Pricing & Discount Strategy Analysis

Does discounting drive satisfaction? Examining discount depth vs. rating and review volume

-0.1557r value

Discount–Rating Correlation: Weak negative (-0.1557)

Deeper discounts show a weak negative correlation with customer ratings (r = -0.1557). Products with the heaviest discounts (80–100%) average 4.02★, while lightly discounted products (0–10%) average 4.21★ — a 0.19-point gap. This suggests heavy discounting does not lift customer satisfaction and may reflect lower-quality products being cleared through promotions.

Discount–Review Volume correlation: 0.0113 (near zero) — discounting does not materially drive review volume either.

Sentiment Interpretation

Positive signals — "Good" (3,936 mentions), "Quality" (761), and "Value" (489) are the dominant positive themes, indicating customers primarily judge products on basic functionality and value-for-money rather than premium features. "Defective" and "Damage" point to fulfillment or quality-control gaps in specific SKUs.

🎯 Strategic Recommendations

Data-driven actions for category management, pricing optimization, and customer experience improvement

01
Rationalize Heavy-Discount SKUs
Products in the 80–100% discount bracket average the lowest ratings (4.02★) and lowest review volume (9,954 avg). These likely represent clearance inventory or low-quality private label. Audit this cohort of 96 products and remove or restructure rather than continuing to promote them at extreme discounts.
80–100% disc. bracket: 4.02★ avg vs. 4.21★ for 0–10% disc.
02
Grow the Premium Segment (₹5K+)
The ₹5K+ price segment delivers the highest average rating (4.17★) with the smallest average discount (33.5%), suggesting these products earn customer satisfaction on merit, not price cuts. Prioritize assortment expansion in this segment — increasing premium SKU share improves catalog quality scores without margin erosion.
₹5K+ segment: 4.17★ avg, 33.5% avg discount (206 products)
03
Elevate Home & Kitchen Category
Home & Kitchen ranks last among the three major categories with a 4.04★ average across 448 products, despite only 40% average discounting (lowest intensity). The lower ratings are not discount-related, pointing to underlying product or listing quality issues. Invest in content quality reviews, stricter supplier ratings thresholds, and sub-category curation for this segment.
Home & Kitchen: 4.04★ vs. catalog avg 4.10★ across 448 products
04
Leverage Office Products as a Quality Benchmark
Office Products achieves the highest average rating (4.31★) with the lowest average discount intensity (12.4%) — a clear signal that this category succeeds through product quality, not promotions. Use Office Products' supplier and content standards as a quality benchmark for other categories, and consider expanding assortment to capitalize on high customer satisfaction.
Office Products: 4.31★, only 12.4% avg discount (31 products)
05
Prioritize High-Engagement SKU Quality Control
The top 10 most-reviewed products (all Electronics, avg 363K+ reviews) set catalog perception for millions of customers. All rate 4.1–4.4★ — healthy but not exceptional. A single quality failure in these SKUs has outsized reputational impact. Implement proactive monitoring and faster listing remediation for products exceeding 100K reviews.
Top 10 products avg 380K+ reviews each; median product: 5,179
