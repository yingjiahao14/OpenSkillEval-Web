Pricing & Discount Strategy Analysis

Central Finding: Discounts Do Not Drive Ratings — They May Slightly Suppress Them The Pearson correlation between discount percentage and customer rating is r = −0.156 (weak negative). Products discounted ≥60% average a rating of 4.06, versus 4.17 for products discounted below 30% — a meaningful 0.11-point gap. High discounts also show no meaningful lift in review volume (r = +0.01).

Strategic Recommendations

Recommendation 1 — High Priority

Reassess Deep-Discount Strategy (>60%) for Low-End SKUs

Products discounted 60–80% average 4.03–4.06 stars, lower than products discounted under 30% (4.17 avg). Discounting is not converting to satisfaction — it may be attracting price-sensitive buyers with higher return rates and complaint rates. Reduce aggressive discounting on sub-₹500 products where quality concerns are most frequent.

Supporting data: r = −0.156 (disc% vs rating); 81–100% disc bracket averages 3.98 ★ vs 4.17 ★ for <30% disc
Recommendation 2 — High Priority

Prioritize Assortment Expansion in Office Products

With only 31 products but the highest average rating (4.31) and lowest average discount (12%), Office Products punches well above its weight on customer satisfaction. Its low engagement metrics (149K total reviews) signal untapped scale opportunity. Growing this category could improve catalog-wide satisfaction with minimal promotional spend.

Supporting data: Office Products avg rating 4.31 at 12% avg discount vs catalog avg 4.10 at 47.7%
Recommendation 3 — Medium Priority

Audit the Headphones & Earbuds Sub-Category for Assortment Quality

66 products in this sub-category average 3.93 stars — the lowest among large Electronics sub-groups — despite carrying the second-highest average discount (59%). Negative sentiment themes (noise, fake, defective) cluster here. An assortment quality audit should identify and delist underperforming SKUs, then replace with better-reviewed alternatives.

Supporting data: Headphones avg rating 3.93 ★; 59% avg discount; "noise" appears in 37 review titles, "fake" in 13
Recommendation 4 — Medium Priority

Leverage High-Engagement Electronics Products for Social Proof

AmazonBasics HDMI cables and boAt Bassheads earphones each exceed 363K reviews at strong 4.1–4.4 ratings. These products represent proven anchors with massive social proof. Feature them prominently in category pages and promotional bundles. Their review volumes signal organic demand that doesn't require heavy discounting.

Supporting data: Top 6 most-reviewed products all in Electronics, 313K–427K reviews each, ratings 4.1–4.4
Recommendation 5 — Medium Priority

Focus Product Improvement Investment on Functional Reliability

Review sentiment analysis shows "working," "fast charging," and "easy" are the primary positive drivers — not aesthetics or features. Customer disappointment centers on quality consistency, defects, and unmet functional expectations. Vendor quality standards should be tightened for categories below 4.0 avg rating, with particular focus on Heating/Cooling products (3.99 avg) and Printers & Inks (3.96 avg).

Supporting data: "working" (243), "bad" (109), "issue" (70), "defective" (21) in review titles; Heating/Cooling avg 3.99 ★
Recommendation 6 — Ongoing

Protect Margin on Premium (>₹5,000) Products

The >₹5,000 price segment earns the highest satisfaction (4.17 avg) while carrying the lowest average discount (34%). There is no data-driven justification to increase promotional intensity in this segment. Maintain disciplined pricing and focus discounts on the mid-range (₹500–₹2,000) where competitive pressure is highest.

Supporting data: >₹5K avg rating 4.17 ★ at 34% disc; <₹200 avg rating 4.05 ★ at 58% disc
