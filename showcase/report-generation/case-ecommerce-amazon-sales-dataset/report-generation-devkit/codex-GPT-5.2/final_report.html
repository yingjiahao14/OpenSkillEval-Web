Headline Takeaways

Discount depth correlates negatively with rating (r=-0.155). Deep discounting appears more associated with lower-rated items than better satisfaction.

Strategic Recommendations

Actions, backed by dataset evidence

  1. Don’t use deeper discounts as a proxy for satisfaction improvements. Discount%↔Rating correlation is -0.155, and low-discount items (≤30%) average 4.165 vs 4.029 for ≥70% discounts.
  2. Prioritize quality/experience fixes in sub-categories with low ratings and high discounting. Example watchlist: Electronics → Headphones,Earbuds&Accessories averages 3.926 rating with 59.53% average discount.
  3. Protect and replicate the traits of high-engagement winners. Top 10% engagement products have 87.07% high-rating share vs 74.54% in the rest.
  4. Focus category attention where engagement concentrates. Electronics contributes 15,778,848 total ratings and 35.90% of products, so small rating lifts here disproportionately affect overall perception.
  5. Align discount posture to price tier. Highest price quartile shows the lowest average discount but the highest average rating; maintain margin discipline for premium items and use targeted promotions instead of blanket discounting.
Note: This report uses product-level fields (not review-level rows). Review token analysis is a lightweight frequency snapshot, not sentiment modeling.
