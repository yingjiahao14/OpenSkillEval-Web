Strategic Recommendations

1. Rethink Deep-Discount Promotions

Data shows a weak negative correlation (r = -0.156) between discount depth and customer rating. Discounts above 70% do not yield higher satisfaction or materially higher review volume. Shift promotional budget from blanket deep discounts to targeted category-specific campaigns in mid-discount ranges (30–50%), where ratings hold steady and margins are better protected.

2. Prioritize Quality in Underperforming Categories

Car&Motorbike scores 3.80 — the lowest among all categories — yet carries an average discount of 42.0%. Deep discounts are not compensating for quality gaps. Recommend a quality audit of SKUs in this category, supplier negotiation, or assortment pruning before further margin investment.

3. Double Down on High-Engagement, High-Rating SKUs

75.9% of products already rate 4.0+, but a small subset drives the majority of reviews. Identify the shared attributes of top-engaged products (e.g., price point, brand, feature set) and expand the assortment with similar profiles. Use these as anchor SKUs in marketing rather than loss-leading deep discounts.

4. Address Functional Failure Modes Highlighted in Reviews

Negative review themes repeatedly cite charging failures, durability issues, and compatibility problems. These are preventable quality-control gaps. Implement stricter QC checkpoints and require suppliers to certify longevity standards. A 0.1-point rating improvement at scale can lift category conversion meaningfully.

5. Optimize Price Segmentation

Budget and mid-range segments dominate the catalog but show variable rating performance. Premium segments (₹1.5K–5K) offer an opportunity: they maintain competitive ratings with room for healthier margins. Test expanding premium-tier private-label or exclusive-brand SKUs where discount pressure is lower and customer satisfaction is sustained.
