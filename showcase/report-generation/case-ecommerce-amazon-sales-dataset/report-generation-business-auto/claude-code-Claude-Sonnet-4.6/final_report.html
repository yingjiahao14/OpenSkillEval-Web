06

Strategic Recommendations

Data-backed actions for management
01

Audit and Rationalize the >80% Discount Segment

Products discounted above 80% show the lowest avg rating (3.98) and lowest review engagement (avg 9,588 reviews) — the worst combination in the catalog. This segment likely contains low-quality or counterfeit goods using aggressive markdowns as the sole sales driver.

Data: 59 products, avg rating 3.98 vs. catalog avg 4.10; review volume 58% below 61-80% band
02

Prioritize Home & Kitchen Quality Improvement

Home & Kitchen is the lowest-rated major category (4.04 avg) with the lowest median review count (2,305 vs. Electronics' 10,689). At 448 products, it's nearly as large as Electronics but significantly underperforms on both satisfaction and engagement. A category-level quality review and supplier audit is warranted.

Data: 448 products, avg rating 4.04, median reviews 2,305; Electronics median: 10,689
03

Don't Use Discounts as a Satisfaction Lever — Focus on Product Quality

The correlation between discount percentage and customer rating is −0.155, meaning deeper discounts are weakly associated with lower satisfaction. Discount depth has zero effect on review volume (r = 0.012). The data shows that product quality — not price — is what drives ratings. Investment in quality controls will yield more return than promotional spend.

Data: Corr(discount, rating) = −0.155; Corr(discount, review_count) = +0.012
04

Expand Office Products Category

Office Products has the highest average rating (4.31) with the lowest average discount (12.4%), indicating customers pay full or near-full price for quality in this segment. At only 31 products, the category is underdeveloped relative to its satisfaction performance. Expanding the Office Products assortment presents a high-margin, high-satisfaction growth opportunity.

Data: 31 products, avg rating 4.31 (#1 of all categories), avg discount only 12.4%
05

Investigate "Fake" and "Not Working" Complaints — Counterfeiting Risk

Review titles contain 13 mentions of "fake" and 72 of "not working" — the most concentrated negative signals in the dataset. While small in absolute volume, counterfeiting complaints represent brand and legal risk disproportionate to their frequency. Identify the sub-categories where these appear and initiate supplier verification for those ASINs.

Data: "fake" = 13 review title mentions; "not working" = 72 mentions; "broken" = 11
