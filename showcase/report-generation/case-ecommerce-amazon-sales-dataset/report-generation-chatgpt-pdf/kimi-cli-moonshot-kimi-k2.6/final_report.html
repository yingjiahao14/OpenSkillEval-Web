Pricing & Discount Strategy Analysis

This section directly addresses management’s highest-priority question: Are discounts driving satisfaction, or just volume?

Key Finding: The correlation between discount percentage and customer rating is -0.155 — a weak but consistent negative relationship. Deeper discounts do not produce higher star ratings. In fact, products with >60% discount average 4.06 stars versus 4.16 stars for products with <30% discount.
Volume Insight: Discount depth is essentially uncorrelated with review volume (r = 0.012). Heavily discounted items do not generate materially more customer feedback, undermining the argument that deep promotions seed organic engagement.

Review Sentiment Snapshot

A lightweight text analysis of 8,521+ sentiment-bearing words across review titles reveals dominant customer themes.

Positive Themes

  • Quality & Value: "good" (3,819), "quality" (720), "worth" (315) — customers frequently validate purchase decisions against durability and price-performance.
  • Speed & Performance: "fast" (143), "best" (495) — charging speed and responsiveness are recurring praise points, especially in cables and accessories.
  • Satisfaction: "nice" (875), "great" (253), "excellent" (166) — broad approval language indicates expectations are generally met.

Negative Themes

  • Negation & Dissatisfaction: "not" (746) is the most common negative signal, often paired with functional complaints (e.g., "not working", "not durable").
  • Quality Defects: "bad" (101), "poor" (43), "defective" (21) — physical product failures and below-par materials drive frustration.
  • Operational Issues: "issue" (38), "problem" (31), "slow" (27) — performance degradation over time and compatibility problems surface repeatedly.
Sentiment ratio: Positive sentiment words outnumber negative words by roughly 6:1 in review titles, reinforcing the quantitative finding that the catalog is broadly well-received, but negative feedback clusters around functional reliability rather than price complaints.

Strategic Recommendations

1. Rebalance promotional intensity in Budget and Home & Kitchen segments.
Data: Budget products discount at 55.8% but rate 4.07 stars — lower than Luxury’s 4.17 stars at 33.5%. Home & Kitchen discounts 40.1% yet scores lowest among major categories.
Action: Shift from blanket %-off campaigns to value-add bundling and quality-assurance messaging. Test reducing discounts 10–15% in these segments and monitor conversion rather than star-rating erosion.
2. Protect and expand the Office Products playbook.
Data: Office Products achieves the highest rating (4.31) with the lowest discount intensity (12.4%).
Action: Analyze assortment and supplier quality drivers in Office Products. Apply those sourcing and listing standards to underperforming subcategories such as Heating, Cooling & Air Quality and Headphones.
3. Investigate the 24% of products below 4.0 stars as priority remediation.
Data: 356 products sit below the high-rating threshold, concentrated in Home & Kitchen and Electronics subcategories with deep discounting.
Action: Run a root-cause audit on 1- and 2-star reviews for these SKUs. If quality issues dominate (as sentiment analysis suggests), delist or upgrade rather than discount further.
4. Leverage high-engagement Electronics SKUs as catalog anchors.
Data: The top 5 most-reviewed products are all Electronics/Accessories, each with 360K+ reviews, driving massive organic visibility.
Action: Use these items as traffic anchors in cross-sell modules. Pair them with higher-margin, lower-engagement SKUs to improve blended basket economics.
5. Replace discount-depth KPIs with price-quality efficiency metrics.
Data: The discount-rating correlation is negative (r = -0.155), and discount-review correlation is near zero (r = 0.012).
Action: Institute a category-level "Quality per Rupee Discounted" score that weights rating and margin over raw discount %. Align buyer incentives with margin-preserving quality improvements.
