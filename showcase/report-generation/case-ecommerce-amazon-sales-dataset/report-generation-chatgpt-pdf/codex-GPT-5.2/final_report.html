Strategic Recommendations

Actions tied directly to observed data

  1. Stop assuming deeper discounts lift satisfaction. Discount% has a weak negative relationship with rating (r=-0.155). Ratings average ~4.18 in low-discount bands (0–20%) versus ~3.99 in very high-discount bands (70–90%). Use discounts primarily to drive conversion/clearance, not as a quality lever.
  2. Prioritize quality fixes in the worst-performing large sub-categories. Among sub-categories with ≥30 products, the bottom performers include: Electronics / Headphones,Earbuds&Accessories (avg rating 3.93), Home&Kitchen / Heating,Cooling&AirQuality (avg rating 3.99), Electronics / WearableTechnology (avg rating 4.02). Target listing accuracy, accessories compatibility, and post-purchase issues highlighted in reviews (see 'issues', 'replacement', 'faulty' signals).
  3. Protect margin where ratings are already strong without heavy discounting. Example: top-level OfficeProducts averages 4.31 rating with 12.4% average discount. Treat these as candidates for discount pullbacks / tighter promo guardrails.
  4. Run category-level promo experiments with engagement as the outcome, not ratings. Discount% vs rating_count correlation is near zero (r=0.012). If promo goals are engagement, measure it explicitly (rating_count lift by band/category) and A/B at the category/sub-category level.
  5. Audit engagement leaders as templates. The top 10 products by rating count can be used to benchmark content quality (imagery, specs, claims), positioning, and price points in their respective categories.
