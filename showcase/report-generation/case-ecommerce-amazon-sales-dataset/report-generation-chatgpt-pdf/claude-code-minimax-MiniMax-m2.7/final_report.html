6. Strategic Recommendations

Data-driven actions for category management, pricing optimization, and customer experience improvement.

1 Fix Home&Kitchen Quality Gap — Don't Rely on Discounts

Supporting data: Home&Kitchen has the lowest average rating (4.04) despite 448 products and the shallowest average discounts (40%). Discount correlation with ratings is r = -0.156 — discounts do not fix quality problems.

Action: Conduct specification audits on low-rated products. Enforce minimum quality thresholds for listing approval. Shift from discount-based promotion to quality improvement investment for this category.

2 Protect & Grow Electronics — The Engagement Engine

Supporting data: Electronics has the highest median rating count (10,689) and the largest catalog (526 products). Above-average rating (4.08) with maximum engagement makes this the flagship category.

Action: Maintain quality standards stringently here. Use Electronics performance as a benchmark for onboarding new sellers. Prioritize restocking top-rated products over expanding low-rated sub-categories.

3 Re-evaluate Products with >60%% Discounts

Supporting data: 439 products carry discounts exceeding 60%%, yet these do not produce higher ratings. Deep discounting is eroding margin without a corresponding satisfaction benefit.

Action: Audit all products with discounts >60%% that rate below 4.0. For each, determine whether the discount is compensating for a quality deficit. If so, address the root cause or delist. If margins still work at 40–50%% discounts, test price-only promotions without exceeding 60%% depth.

4 Launch a Durability Assurance Program

Supporting data: Review sentiment analysis shows "not durable," "stopped working," and "quality issue" recurring across cables and accessories — categories that are backbone of the catalog.

Action: Introduce a durability scoring metric alongside rating. Partner with sellers to enforce better component standards. Consider a "Durability Certified" badge for products that pass stress-testing benchmarks — this can become a differentiation lever and a trust signal for buyers.

5 Leverage OfficeProducts as a Quality Benchmark

Supporting data: OfficeProducts achieves the highest average rating (4.31) across all categories, with minimal promotional intensity (12%% average discount). This demonstrates that customer satisfaction and margin can coexist.

Action: Study the product selection and seller quality standards in OfficeProducts. Document the playbook and replicate seller quality requirements across other categories, especially Home&Kitchen and Electronics sub-categories that underperform.

Bottom Line: The catalog's strength (75.8%% products rated ≥4.0) is real, but the path to improvement is through quality and durability — not deeper discounting. OfficeProducts and Electronics provide the models; Home&Kitchen and high-discount low-rated products are the priority fixes.
