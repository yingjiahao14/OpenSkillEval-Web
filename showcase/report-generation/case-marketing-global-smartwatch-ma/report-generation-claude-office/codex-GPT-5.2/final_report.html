4) Case Size & Price Premium Analysis

Bins are 4mm wide; only bins with n≥20 per brand are plotted.

What the data supports

  • Correlation is weak: Pearson correlation between case size and price is near-zero for Apple (0.033) and Samsung (0.035), and small for Garmin (0.107).
  • But bin medians can show premiums: Apple’s 46–50mm bin median ($566.51) exceeds its 38–42mm median ($495.00).
  • Interpretation: case size alone is a weak predictor; any observed premium likely mixes model/series effects with size.

Operational implication

Treat case size as a feature for segmentation (inventory clustering / pricing guardrails), not as a primary pricing driver without controlling for model family.

6) Strategic Recommendations

  1. Build sourcing playbooks around the largest, high-volume country deltas (brand-specific): UK appears structurally cheaper for Apple (median ~28% below Apple global median), while UK is structurally more expensive for Garmin and Samsung. This supports an arbitrage thesis of Apple sourcing in UK vs selling into higher-median regions (subject to model mix + tax/shipping constraints).
  2. Use medians (not means) for pricing guardrails: Apple median is ~$550.00, Garmin ~$566.98, Samsung ~$530.00. The dataset includes extreme max outliers, so median/P10/P90 bands are safer for automated repricing rules.
  3. Treat Samsung “New” inventory differently: Samsung shows a materially higher average New-vs-Used premium (≈44.3%) relative to Apple/Garmin (~3–4%). If you acquire Samsung supply, condition grading accuracy has larger pricing impact.
  4. Don’t over-weight case size as a pricing lever: Case size–price correlations are weak (|r| ≈ 0.03–0.11). Use case size primarily for filtering (e.g., ensure consistent comps) rather than as a standalone premium.
  5. Partnership with power sellers is a scale lever, not a discount lever: Top-5% sellers contribute 48.33% of listings and have higher medians than long-tail sellers across brands (e.g., Samsung power-seller median is $609.59 vs $488.77). Partner for throughput and supply reliability; expect less price slack.
