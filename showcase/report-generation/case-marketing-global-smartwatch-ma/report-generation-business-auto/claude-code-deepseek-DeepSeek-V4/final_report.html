Key Takeaway — Counterintuitive Finding: Power sellers systematically overprice relative to casual sellers, not underprice. Across all three brands, power sellers' median prices are 11.7%–25.9% higher than casual sellers' — with Samsung showing the starkest gap (+25.9%). 6. Strategic Recommendations

Synthesis of findings into actionable recommendations for secondary-market operators.

1

Source Apple Watches in the UK, Sell in Australia and Japan

UK Apple Watch listings sit at a median of just $395 — a 28% discount to the $550 global median and less than half the $792 Australian median. This is the single largest geographic arbitrage window in the dataset. With 215 UK Apple listings (adequate liquidity), an operator can build a steady sourcing pipeline. After estimated cross-border costs (~15–20%), net margins of 25–35% are achievable on UK→Australia or UK→Japan routes.

Source: Section 3 — Geographic Pricing Arbitrage (Apple: UK $395 vs. Australia $792)
2

Make Garmin Your Margin Anchor — Source in Japan

Garmin commands the highest median resale price ($567) and shows strong value retention. Japan is the cheapest sourcing market for Garmin at $506 median (with 161 listings providing excellent liquidity), while Canada ($890) and the UK ($775) are the premium sell-into markets. Garmin also shows the strongest case-size premium (+15.9%), so prioritize 46mm+ models. The Japan→Canada Garmin spread of $384 (76%) is the largest theoretical margin in the dataset.

Source: Sections 2, 3, 4 — Garmin Brand Pricing, Japan Geographic Data, Case Size Premium
3

Target Casual Sellers for Inventory, Not Power Sellers

Power sellers (those with >3 listings) systematically overprice by 11.7%–25.9% across all brands. The 1,659 casual sellers (94.8% of all sellers) price closer to or below market median, making them better sourcing targets. An outreach program targeting casual sellers — particularly those listing premium models below market — could build proprietary inventory access that power sellers cannot replicate through bulk alone. The top 5% of sellers control 48.2% of listings, but their premium pricing leaves room for a value-focused competitor.

Source: Section 5 — Power Seller Behavior (power sellers overprice by 12–26%)
4

Avoid Samsung as a Core Inventory Play — Treat as Opportunistic

Samsung shows the widest price dispersion (P90/P10 = 2.39x), the weakest case-size premium (+2.1%), and near-zero new-vs-used differentiation (+1.4%). These signal an inefficient, unpredictable market with poor pricing anchors. However, Samsung has a notable arbitrage between Japan ($437 median) and the UK ($825 median). Treat Samsung as an opportunistic, not strategic, play: buy only when a clear arbitrage spread exists and avoid holding Samsung inventory as a core position.

Source: Sections 2, 3, 4 — Samsung price variance, geographic data, and size analysis
5

Build an International Fulfillment Capability as a Competitive Moat

Only 27% of sellers offer worldwide shipping, yet the largest arbitrage spreads are cross-border (UK→Australia, Japan→Canada, Japan→UK). An operator who solves international logistics — customs, duties, multi-currency pricing, localized listings — captures spreads that 73% of the market cannot access. Start with the UK↔Japan↔US triangle, which covers the three deepest arbitrage lanes identified in this analysis (Apple UK→US/Japan, Garmin Japan→UK, Samsung Japan→UK).

Source: Section 1 (27% worldwide shipping rate) + Section 3 (all geographic spreads)
6

Prioritize ≥42mm Case Sizes for Apple and Garmin Inventory

Watches ≥46mm command a 13.3% premium over <42mm across all brands. The premium is most reliable for Apple (+14.4%) and Garmin (+15.9%). When sourcing, filter aggressively for 42mm+ listings — the small-size discount is a real effect, not a data artifact. For Samsung, ignore size entirely; it has no measurable impact on price (+2.1%).

Source: Section 4 — Case Size Premium Analysis
