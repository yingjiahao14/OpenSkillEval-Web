4) Case Size & Price Premium Analysis

Case size coverage: 2,724 of 3,586 listings (76.0%) include Case_Size_mm. Correlations are computed only on non-null rows.

  • Overall signal strength is weak: Pearson r values are near zero for all brands (Apple 0.033, Garmin 0.107, Samsung 0.035). Size alone is a poor price predictor in this dataset.
  • Operational takeaway: Use case size as a secondary sort key for merchandising, but don’t price primarily off size without controlling for model line (via title/model extraction) and condition.

5) Power Seller Behavior & Pricing Patterns

Power sellers are defined as the top 5% of sellers by listing volume (top 88 of 1750 sellers), covering 48.3% of listings — a highly concentrated market.

Even the top 1% of sellers account for 34.1% of listings in the Apple/Garmin/Samsung subset.

  • Pricing pattern: Power sellers price above the brand median on a median basis (price index > 100) across all three brands, suggesting they’re not systematically undercutting; they may be selling higher-end mixes, stronger conditions, or have better demand capture.
  • Partnership implication: If your strategy relies on “discount supply”, power sellers may not be the cheapest channel; they may be better for consistent volume and selection.

6) Strategic Recommendations

  • Prioritize geo-arbitrage pilots by brand: Use the heatmap’s most-negative stable deltas as a short list of sourcing geos per brand (≥15 listings). Start with small test buys, and benchmark landed margin against your domestic baseline.
  • Price to medians, not means: Price distributions include extreme outliers (see Samsung). Use country×brand medians for guardrails; treat max values as unreliable for day-to-day pricing.
  • Don’t over-invest in case-size segmentation: With correlations near zero, case size should not drive major SKU/assortment bets without further model-level parsing from title.
  • Be selective with power-seller partnerships: With 48.3% of listings controlled by top 5% sellers, partnerships can unlock supply consistency. But expect median pricing above the brand median; negotiate on volume/logistics rather than expecting systematic discounts.
  • Condition strategy: The “New” label does not uniformly command a premium (Apple is -0.5% on average). Validate condition premiums at the model level before using them in automated repricers.

Footnotes & Data Integrity Notes

  • Scope control: Required analyses are computed on Apple/Garmin/Samsung only (3,586 listings). The raw file also contains 21 listings from other brands.
  • Used-like bucket: For the premium KPI, “Used-like” includes Used/Open box/Refurbished/For parts. This avoids comparing “New” only to a narrow “Used” label when marketplaces use varied condition schemas.
  • Case size missingness: 24.0% of rows lack Case_Size_mm (size extracted from titles when present).
