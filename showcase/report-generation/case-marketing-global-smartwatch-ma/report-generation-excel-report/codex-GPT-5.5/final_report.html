Executive Readout

Garmin holds the strongest central resale price with a $566.98 median, ahead of Apple at $550.00 and Samsung at $530.00.

Geographic arbitrage is material. Apple spans $399 between sufficiently represented country medians; Garmin spans $384; Samsung spans $388.

Seller concentration is operationally relevant. The top 88 sellers (5.0% of sellers) hold 48.3% of major-brand listings.

Arbitrage Interpretation

Apple: United Kingdom supply is -28.6% below Apple’s global median, while Australia is +44.0% above it.

Garmin: Japan, Germany, and Poland are discounted; Canada and the United Kingdom are premium markets.

Samsung: Japan and Israel are discounted, while the United Kingdom is the clearest premium market at +55.7% above Samsung’s global median.

These are listing-price signals, not landed-margin guarantees. Operators should subtract shipping, duties, FX, return risk, and platform fees before execution.

Strategic Recommendations

  1. Source Apple in the United Kingdom before premium-market resale. Apple listings in the United Kingdom show a -28.6% median discount versus Apple’s global median ($393, n=214), while Australia prices at +44.0% ($792, n=14). Use this spread as the first sourcing lane to test after shipping, taxes, and platform fees.
  2. Treat Garmin as the strongest brand-value anchor, not just a volume play. Garmin posts the highest median listing price at $566.98 and the smallest major-brand price range ($1,104), making it the cleanest benchmark for disciplined buying and repricing.
  3. Prioritize Garmin Canada / UK demand with Japan, Germany, and Poland supply checks. Garmin country medians run as low as Japan at $506 (-10.7%) and as high as Canada at $890 (+57.0%), a $384 spread across sufficiently represented markets.
  4. Use power sellers for assortment depth, but negotiate against their premium posture. The top 5% of sellers control 48.3% of major-brand listings and show a higher median price ($598 vs. $520). They are partnership candidates, but the data supports asking for volume discounts rather than accepting their listed price book.
  5. Do not over-model case size alone. Listings above each brand’s median case size show a +15.4% median uplift, but the all-brand price correlation is only 0.021. Use size as a merchandising and filter variable, not as a standalone price predictor.
  6. Apply condition premiums brand-by-brand. Average New listings command +18.2% over Used for Samsung and +8.2% for Apple, but Garmin’s average premium is only +4.7%. New-condition buying thresholds should be strictest for Garmin unless model mix justifies the spread.

Method Notes

All calculations are derived from /app/benchmark/data.csv. Brand-specific analysis is restricted to Apple, Garmin, and Samsung per the brief; the raw file contains 3,607 rows, of which 3,586 match those brands. Geographic comparisons use median price and require at least 5 listings per country-brand cell. Case-size analysis uses only rows with non-null Case_Size_mm. Power sellers are defined as the top 5% of major-brand sellers by listing count, using ceiling rounding (88 sellers out of 1,750).
