6. Strategic Recommendations

Synthesize findings into actionable recommendations for secondary-market operators regarding sourcing regions, brand focus, pricing strategies, and seller partnership opportunities.

1. Target Sourcing in Discount Markets

Geographic arbitrage is the highest-margin lever available. The $399 Apple spread and $269 Garmin spread between country medians represent real sourcing margins. Identify the 2–3 cheapest countries for each brand with sufficient inventory (>10 listings) and establish local buying channels there. Each percentage point of discount captured on sourcing flows directly to gross margin.

2. Prioritize Garmin for Premium Inventory

Garmin's median resale value of $567 — significantly above Apple and Samsung — makes it the highest per-unit revenue brand in the secondary market. Allocate sourcing budget disproportionately toward Garmin, particularly Fenix and Tactix model families. For condition-based arbitrage, Apple listings carry the strongest New premium at +17.2%. Garmin's higher price variance also enables opportunistic buying — the top end of the market is where margins live.

3. Use Case Size as a Sourcing Heuristic

Case size correlates r=0.021 with price across all brands. Large-case watches (>46mm) command a measurable premium in every brand. When screening listings where full model details are unavailable, case size alone is a reliable proxy for value tier. Prioritize 46mm+ listings for higher-margin resale opportunities, especially for Apple (Ultra models) and Garmin (Fenix 7X/Epix Pro).

4. Partner Selectively with Power Sellers

The top 5% of sellers control 48.3% of inventory and price +14.7% relative to casual sellers. This concentration is moderate — the market is not dominated by a few operators. Power sellers charge a premium for higher-quality inventory; negotiate wholesale-based partnerships that capture the value spread between their pricing and selling-platform pricing. Target the remaining 95% of casual sellers as a fragmented, less-price-disciplined sourcing pool.

5. Optimize for Condition Arbitrage

The New vs Used price premium varies significantly by brand: +17.2% for Apple, -1.8% for Garmin, +1.4% for Samsung. Apple's new-condition premium is the highest — sourcing new-in-box inventory for this brand and selling in markets where condition commands the largest premium maximizes per-unit profit.

6. Build Geographic Sales Routing

Each brand has different premium and discount markets. Routing sourcing from discount countries to premium countries within the same brand can capture 399+ in spread per unit. For example, countries with Garmin prices +10% above global median should be sales destinations; countries -10% or below should be sourcing origins. This is a systematic, repeatable arbitrage model — not a one-off opportunity.
