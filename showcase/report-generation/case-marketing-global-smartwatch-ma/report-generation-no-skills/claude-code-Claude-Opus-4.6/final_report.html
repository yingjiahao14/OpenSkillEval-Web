6 Strategic Recommendations

Each recommendation below is anchored to a specific finding from the analysis above.

1. Source inventory from low-cost geographies. Geographic price spreads are the single largest margin lever in this dataset. Prioritize sourcing: Apple from Ireland ($350 median, 129% below the priciest market); Garmin from Israel ($440 median, 128% below the priciest market); Other from United States ($500 median, 0% below the priciest market); Samsung from Germany ($371 median, 6733% below the priciest market); Xiaomi from Japan ($214 median, 160% below the priciest market). Focus on sellers who already offer worldwide shipping to minimize logistics friction.
2. Prioritize Garmin for value-retention inventory. Garmin commands the highest median resale price ($566.98) and demonstrates strong price variance (IQR: $282.47), meaning both entry-level and premium models hold value.
3. Exploit the condition premium gap. Apple new-in-box listings command a +17.7% premium over used — the largest of any brand. Sourcing sealed Apple inventory at or near used-market prices converts directly to margin. Garmin shows the smallest condition premium (-0.3%), suggesting used Garmin inventory is viable without significant price penalty.
4. Build power-seller partnerships for scale and shipping coverage. The top 5% of sellers (91 accounts) control 48.8% of listings and offer worldwide shipping at 47.0% (vs. 8.6% for regular sellers). Power sellers price $75.30 higher at the median, reflecting premium inventory positioning. Partnering with high-volume sellers in low-cost geographies (e.g., top sellers in Ireland) combines both advantages.
5. Deprioritize case size in pricing models. The overall size–price correlation (0.021) is weak, but Garmin shows a meaningfully stronger relationship (0.107). Larger-case models (49mm+) can be priced at a premium for brands where the correlation is stronger.
