6. Key Findings and Policy Recommendations

Priority Recommendations

1. Address Internet Access Gaps

The data shows students without internet access decline in GPA by 0.020 points, while those with access improve by 0.059 points. This 0.079 point gap is the largest measurable effect in the dataset. Priority: Invest in internet infrastructure and device access for students in lower-income brackets, particularly in Rangpur, Khulna, and Sylhet districts where GPA progression is negative.

2. Target District-Specific Interventions

Rangpur shows the worst GPA progression (-0.057) with 52% public school enrollment. Khulna and Sylhet also show negative progression. These districts need targeted support including teacher training, learning materials, and after-school programs. Rajshahi and Dhaka show the strongest improvement, suggesting successful interventions that could be replicated.

3. Replicate Rajshahi and Dhaka Success Factors

Rajshahi (+0.078) and Dhaka (+0.058) demonstrate the highest GPA improvement. Investigating what distinguishes these districts—teaching quality, parental engagement, school management, or resource availability—could provide a blueprint for national improvement.

4. Reassess Attendance-Based Metrics

The near-zero correlation (r = -0.014) between attendance and performance suggests that simply increasing attendance may not improve outcomes. Focus on quality of instruction and learning outcomes rather than mere presence. Explore what specific factors drive improvement in the 90%+ attendance group (avg HSC 4.035).

5. Support Low-Income Student Progression

Students in the lowest income quartile (avg 15,969 BDT/month) show negative GPA progression (-0.016) while highest income students show +0.077. This 0.093 point gap indicates that economic barriers translate to learning gaps over time. Consider targeted scholarships, free tutoring, and learning resource access for students from low-income families.
