3. Socio-Economic Factors and Their Impact on Performance

This is the most critical analysis segment, detailing how external factors influence academic outcomes.

Socio-Economic Insights:

  • Family Income: The correlation between family income and HSC result is negligible (r = -0.007). Grouping by income quintiles shows minor fluctuations, with the lowest and second-highest quintiles performing slightly better.
  • Parent Education: Students whose parents have Secondary education achieved the highest average HSC results (4.05), closely followed by Higher Secondary (4.03).
  • Internet & Tuition: Access to internet shows a slight positive edge (4.02 vs 4.00 for no access). Similarly, private tuition offers a marginal benefit (4.02 vs 4.01). While these factors provide a small boost, they are not dominant drivers of high performance in this dataset.

4. Study Behavior and Attendance Patterns

Contrary to common expectations, raw time invested does not strongly correlate with better results.

  • Study Hours: The correlation between weekly study hours and HSC result is slightly negative (r = -0.022). Students studying 0-10 hours average 4.03, while those studying 10-20 hours average 3.99.
  • Attendance: The correlation between attendance and HSC result is also slightly negative (r = -0.014). Students with <60% attendance averaged higher (4.09) than those with >85% attendance (3.98). This suggests that self-directed learning or other unmeasured factors might play a larger role than mere presence in class.

6. Key Findings and Policy Recommendations

1. Target Regional Interventions over Socio-Economic Subsidies: The data shows minimal impact from family income or internet access, but significant gaps between districts. Action: Reallocate funding and pedagogical support to underperforming districts, specifically Rangpur and Khulna. Investigate the successful educational models in Barisal and Rajshahi for national adaptation.

2. Re-evaluate Attendance Policies: High attendance does not correlate with high performance in this demographic. Action: Shift administrative focus from merely enforcing attendance to evaluating the quality of in-class instruction and supporting alternative learning pathways.

3. Investigate the "Efficiency" of Study Time: With lower study hours sometimes yielding better results, students may be struggling with study efficiency. Action: Introduce study skills, time management, and effective learning technique workshops into the standard curriculum, rather than simply encouraging "more studying."

4. Address the Progression Drop in Specific Regions: While the national GPA progression is stable, districts like Rangpur and Sylhet show students declining from their previous GPA to their HSC results. Action: Implement early-warning tracking systems in these districts bridging the transition between SSC and HSC to prevent performance drop-off.
