6. Key Findings and Policy Recommendations

Summary of Key Findings

  1. No At-Risk Students: All 1,000 students achieved HSC results ≥ 3.0, suggesting the minimum competency threshold is universally met
  2. Socio-Economic Neutrality: Income, parent education, internet access, and private tuition show negligible correlation with outcomes — suggesting other institutional or individual factors dominate
  3. GPA Regression to Mean: Students with lower previous GPAs improve at higher rates (86.5% for lowest quartile) while top performers often decline
  4. Regional Disparities: A 0.20 GPA point gap exists between highest (Barisal) and lowest (Rangpur) performing districts
  5. Study Time Ineffective: Neither study hours nor attendance correlate meaningfully with outcomes, suggesting quality over quantity

Policy Recommendations

1. Target Underperforming Districts for Resource Allocation

Rangpur and Khulna districts show systematically lower performance. Prioritize these regions for additional educational resources, teacher training programs, and infrastructure investment to close the 0.20-point gap with Barisal.

2. Investigate Dhaka's Public School Advantage

Dhaka's public schools significantly outperform private schools (4.18 vs. 3.95). Study what public institutions in Dhaka are doing effectively and disseminate best practices to private schools and other districts.

3. Focus on Student Support for High-Previous-GPA Students

86.5% of students with low previous GPAs improve, but 86% of previously excellent students decline. Implement transition support programs for top-performing students entering HSC to maintain their achievement levels.

4. Reassess Private Tuition Utility

Private tuition shows virtually no impact on outcomes (+0.01 GPA difference). Policy should reconsider subsidies or encouragement of private tutoring; instead, invest in classroom quality improvements.

5. Address Data Anomalies

The weak socio-economic correlations and negative study-hours correlation are counterintuitive. Conduct data validation to ensure measurement quality, and consider collecting additional variables (e.g., school quality metrics, teacher qualifications) that may better explain variance.
