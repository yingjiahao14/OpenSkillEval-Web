Cohen's d effect sizes are negligible across all comparisons: Graduate vs. Primary parent education (d = 0.063), Internet access (d = 0.029), and private tuition (d = 0.020). Income-HSC correlation is −0.008. These near-zero effects warrant careful interpretation — see analysis below.

Policy analysts should consider collecting data from a wider socio-economic spectrum before concluding that these factors are unimportant.

6. Key Findings and Policy Recommendations

Summary of Key Findings

  1. Performance is concentrated in the 3.0–5.0 range with zero at-risk students, but nearly half (49.4%) remain in the "Average" classification.
  2. Socio-economic factors show negligible effect sizes — income, parent education, internet access, and private tuition each explain virtually none of the variance in HSC results.
  3. Regional disparities are the most significant pattern: Barisal outperforms Rangpur by 0.20 GPA points, with excellent rates varying from 20.3% to 36.8%.
  4. The SSC-to-HSC transition reveals a systemic GPA dip across all districts, suggesting structural factors at the HSC examination stage.
  5. Public schools show a slight positive GPA progression (+0.042) compared to flat private school progression (−0.005).
  6. Attendance and study hours have near-zero correlation with outcomes, challenging quantity-focused interventions.
HIGH PRIORITY

Investigate Regional Disparities

  • Conduct deep-dive audits in Rangpur and Khulna — the two lowest-performing districts — to identify root causes (teacher quality, infrastructure, curriculum delivery)
  • Study what Barisal and Rajshahi are doing differently that drives higher excellent rates and positive GPA deltas
  • Consider district-specific intervention budgets weighted by performance gaps
HIGH PRIORITY

Address the SSC-to-HSC Transition Gap

  • Investigate why SSC results (mean 4.26) systematically exceed HSC results (mean 4.01) across all districts
  • Evaluate HSC exam design and grading calibration relative to SSC
  • Strengthen HSC-level teaching support, particularly in districts with negative GPA deltas
MEDIUM PRIORITY

Shift from Quantity to Quality Metrics

  • De-emphasize attendance mandates as a lever for performance improvement
  • Invest in pedagogical quality assessment and teacher training programs
  • Track instructional quality indicators alongside attendance in school evaluations
MEDIUM PRIORITY

Expand Data Collection

  • Include a broader income range (below ৳10,000 and above ৳60,000) to capture socio-economic extremes
  • Add variables for teacher qualifications, school infrastructure, and class sizes
  • Collect longitudinal data to separate cohort effects from genuine progression
ONGOING

Move 49.4% "Average" Students Upward

  • Target the GPA 3.5–3.99 band (roughly 190 students) as the highest-potential cohort for uplift into "Good" classification
  • District-specific tutoring and mentoring programs, prioritizing Rangpur, Khulna, and Sylhet
ONGOING

Leverage Public School Strengths

  • Public schools show a positive GPA progression (+0.042) — investigate what practices drive this and disseminate to private sector
  • In Dhaka specifically, public schools outperform private by +0.23 GPA points — a model worth studying
