Interpretation for Policy The absence of strong socio-economic gradients in HSC outcomes suggests either (a) an effective baseline of equalization mechanisms — such as public schooling, stipend programs, or community tutoring — that neutralize income effects, or (b) a dataset where sampling compressed socio-economic variation.   • 🎯
    Priority Intervention: Rangpur District Targeted Support Program
    Rangpur ranks last on average HSC GPA (3.91), excellent rate (20.3%), and shows negative GPA progression (−0.06 delta). Allocate dedicated resources including additional qualified teachers, HSC-level tutoring support, and school improvement funds. Set a 3-year target to close the 0.20-point Barisal–Rangpur gap by 50%.
  • 📈
    Address the SSC-to-HSC Excellence Dropout: Transition Support Programs
    The excellent rate drops 13 percentage points between SSC (39.8%) and HSC (26.8%), representing ~130 students who could have achieved excellence but did not. Implement bridging programs, mentorship, and HSC-specific academic counseling at the SSC-to-HSC transition. Focus on curriculum alignment and exam preparation quality, not just additional hours of study.
  • 🏫
    Recognize and Replicate Public School Value-Add Model
    Public schools outperform private on GPA progression (+0.042 vs −0.005) and lead in Dhaka (+0.23 gap) and Sylhet. Conduct school effectiveness studies in top-performing public schools — particularly in Barisal and Dhaka — to identify replicable instructional practices and replicate them across lagging districts, especially Rangpur and Khulna.
  • 🔬
    Shift Monitoring Metrics from Quantity to Quality
    Study hours (r = −0.022) and attendance (r = −0.014) show no predictive value for HSC outcomes in this cohort. Policy reporting frameworks should supplement attendance-tracking with formative assessment scores, teacher quality ratings, and student engagement indicators. Funding based purely on enrollment or attendance metrics will not improve outcomes.
  • 💡
    Investigate Why Socio-Economic Factors Are Neutral — Then Protect What Works
    The near-zero effect of family income and internet access on HSC outcomes is a positive signal that equity mechanisms are functioning. Policymakers should audit which specific programs (stipends, free textbooks, school feeding) are responsible for this neutralization and protect their funding. Avoid cutting equity programs on the basis that "income doesn't matter here" — it may be precisely because those programs exist.
  • 📊
    Deepen District-Level Data Collection for Causal Analysis
    This dataset captures outputs (GPA) but limited process variables (teacher qualifications, school infrastructure, class sizes). A follow-up district-stratified survey capturing teacher-student ratios, infrastructure quality, and pedagogical methods in Barisal vs Rangpur would enable causal attribution and higher-confidence policy design. Recommend a structured data collection protocol across all 8 districts within 6 months.
