1

Prioritize Regional Equity Interventions for Rangpur and Khulna Districts

Rangpur (mean HSC: 3.909) and Khulna (3.947) are the lowest-performing districts, trailing the national average by 0.104 and 0.066 points respectively — and lagging Barisal, the top district, by 0.204 points. Policy-makers should commission district-level qualitative assessments to identify structural barriers and deploy targeted teacher training, infrastructure support, and resource allocation to these underperforming regions.

Evidence: Barisal mean HSC = 4.113 vs Rangpur mean HSC = 3.909 (gap = 0.204 GPA points, n=125 vs n=123)
2

Investigate the Average-Band Bottleneck (49.4% of Students)

Nearly half the cohort (494 students) sits in the Average grade band (HSC 3.0–3.99), with 0 students in the Poor band. This clustering suggests either a systemic achievement ceiling or dataset selection effects. Education administrators should examine curriculum difficulty calibration and assessment practices to understand whether barriers to Excellent/Good grades (currently 50.6% combined) can be removed through pedagogical improvements.

Evidence: HSC classification — Excellent: 268 (26.8%), Good: 238 (23.8%), Average: 494 (49.4%), Poor: 0 (0.0%)
3

Re-evaluate Private Tuition Policy Given Negligible Academic Return

With 50.3% of students receiving private tuition and a Cohen's d of only 0.021 vs non-tutored peers (mean HSC difference: 0.012 GPA points), private tuition shows no meaningful academic benefit in this dataset. Families are likely incurring significant financial costs for negligible gains. Policy communications should inform parents of this evidence while redirecting education budgets toward interventions with demonstrated effectiveness.

Evidence: Tutored mean HSC = 4.019, Non-tutored = 4.007, Cohen's d = 0.021 (negligible), n=503 vs 497
4

Expand Internet Connectivity Without Overestimating Academic Impact

While 52.2% of students lack internet access and internet access shows negligible effect on HSC results (d = 0.029), connectivity remains important for broader digital equity and educational access goals. Policy should prioritize expanding internet access in underserved areas as a foundational equity measure, while acknowledging that connectivity alone will not close academic performance gaps without accompanying pedagogical support.

Evidence: Internet Yes mean HSC = 4.022, No = 4.004, Cohen's d = 0.029 (negligible), 47.8% vs 52.2%
5

Promote Public School Parity — Public Schools Marginally Outperform Private

Public schools achieve a marginally higher mean HSC (4.018) than private schools (4.007), with a negligible effect size (d = −0.019). This is significant for policy because it counters common assumptions that private schooling provides superior outcomes. Resource allocation decisions should not systematically favor private institutions. Public school quality reinforcement programs should be continued and strengthened.

Evidence: Public mean HSC = 4.018 (n=476), Private mean HSC = 4.007 (n=524), Cohen's d = −0.019
6

Address Gender-School Type Interaction Patterns with Targeted Support

Male students in public schools (mean HSC: 4.043) outperform male students in private schools (3.968) by 0.075 GPA points. Conversely, female students perform marginally better in private schools (4.049 vs 4.000). These interaction effects, though small, suggest gender-differentiated learning environments may be at play. Gender-sensitive school improvement programs should examine classroom dynamics, teacher gender distribution, and school culture in both public and private settings.

Evidence: Male-Public 4.043 (n=220) vs Male-Private 3.968 (n=270); Female-Private 4.049 (n=254) vs Female-Public 3.998 (n=256)
7

Invest in Longitudinal Data Collection to Identify True Performance Drivers

This dataset reveals that commonly assumed determinants of academic performance (income, internet access, private tuition, school type, study hours, attendance) all show negligible effects in this cohort. The near-zero correlations (all |r| < 0.03) suggest either that this is a highly homogeneous, pre-selected population, or that unmeasured factors (teacher quality, mental health, peer effects, classroom resources) are the true drivers. A longitudinal study design with richer qualitative data is essential to develop evidence-based education policy for Bangladesh.

Evidence: All correlations with HSC Result — Income: r=−0.008, Internet: d=0.029, Tuition: d=0.021, Study Hours: r=−0.022, Attendance: r=−0.014
