Key Findings and Policy Recommendations

Summary of Key Findings

  1. Minimal Socio-Economic Impact: Neither family income (r = -0.0075) nor parent education level shows meaningful correlation with HSC outcomes. This finding suggests other factors may be more determinative of student success.
  2. Static Performance Trajectory: The GPA progression delta is near zero (+0.018), indicating students largely maintain their baseline performance without systematic improvement or decline.
  3. Attendance Is Not a Predictor: The attendance-performance correlation is negligible (r = -0.014), contradicting common assumptions about attendance as an academic indicator.
  4. Regional Variation Exists: Barisal and Rajshahi lead in mean HSC performance, while Rangpur and Khulna lag. This 0.20 GPA spread represents meaningful geographic disparity.
  5. Private Tuition Shows Minimal Effect: Students with private tuition average only 0.012 GPA points higher than those without.

Target Underperforming Districts

Prioritize interventions in Rangpur and Khulna districts where mean HSC results are 0.104 and 0.066 below the national average respectively.

Rethink Attendance Policies

Since attendance does not correlate with outcomes, schools should investigate other quality indicators. Focus resources on teaching quality and curriculum effectiveness rather than mere attendance enforcement.

Investigate Null Income Effect

The unexpected finding that income does not predict outcomes warrants deeper investigation. This may indicate effective equitable resource distribution or may mask within-district disparities.

Study the SSC-to-HSC Gap

With SSC results averaging 4.257 but HSC averaging 4.013, there is a slight decline. Understanding this drop could reveal critical transition-period challenges.

Public School Investment

Public schools show a slightly higher GPA progression delta (+0.042 vs -0.005) despite comparable mean HSC scores. This suggests public schools may be more effective at nurturing improvement.

Data Quality Note

The dataset shows unusually constrained ranges (no Poor classification, no at-risk students with GPA < 3.0). Policymakers should verify whether this reflects reality or data collection limitations.
