7. Key Findings and Policy Recommendations

Key Findings

  1. Minimal Socio-Economic Impact: Family income, parent education, internet access, and private tuition show surprisingly weak correlations with HSC performance. The variance explained by these factors is minimal.
  2. Stable Performance Trajectories: Students maintain nearly identical GPA from Previous GPA to HSC (delta: +0.018). Performance patterns are established early and persist.
  3. Regional Variation Exists: District-level differences of 0.204 GPA points suggest geographic factors play a larger role than individual socio-economic factors.
  4. Attendance and Study Hours Not Predictive: Neither attendance rate nor study hours show meaningful correlation with HSC outcomes.
  5. No Severe At-Risk Population: Zero students fall below the at-risk threshold (GPA < 3.0), indicating no students with severe academic failure in this dataset.

Policy Recommendations

1. Target Regional Disparities

Focus resources on Rangpur and lower-performing districts. Geographic accessibility and local school infrastructure may be more impactful than socio-economic interventions.

2. Early Intervention Strategies

Since performance trajectories are stable, identify and support at-risk students early (before SSC). Late interventions have limited upside given the flat progression pattern.

3. Reassess Attendance Policies

With attendance showing no correlation to outcomes, rigid attendance requirements may not improve academic results. Consider quality of instruction over mandatory presence.

4. Investigate Unmeasured Factors

The weak correlations across all measured variables suggests other factors (teacher quality, school management, peer effects, psychological factors) dominate student outcomes.

5. Differentiate by District, Not Socio-Economic Status

Policy should be geographically differentiated based on district performance data, rather than targeting students by family income or parent education level.

6. Further Research Needed

Longitudinal data on teacher quality, school facilities, curriculum implementation, and student psychosocial factors would help explain the large within-district variance observed.
