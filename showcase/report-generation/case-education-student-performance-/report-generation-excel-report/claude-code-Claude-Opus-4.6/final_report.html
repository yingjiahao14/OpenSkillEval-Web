3. Socio-Economic Factors and Their Impact on Performance

How family income, parent education, internet access, and private tuition relate to HSC outcomes

Critical Finding: Socio-economic factors show no meaningful correlation with HSC results in this dataset. Family income (r = −0.007), parent education (max gap: 0.086 GPA), internet access (gap: 0.018 GPA), and private tuition (gap: 0.012 GPA) all demonstrate negligible effect sizes. This is a striking departure from education research globally, where socio-economic status is typically a strong predictor of outcomes. Policy analysts should interpret these findings with caution — they likely reflect properties of the dataset rather than the actual Bangladesh education system.

1Investigate data representativeness before acting on findings

The absence of at-risk students and near-zero correlations across all socio-economic variables is inconsistent with published research on Bangladesh's education system. Before using these findings for resource allocation, validate the sample against national-level data (e.g., BANBEIS statistics). If the sample is drawn from a specific population (e.g., high-performing schools or a selective exam cohort), the findings reflect that population, not the system at large.

2Prioritize regional equity — target Rangpur, Khulna, and Mymensingh

While absolute differences are small, the consistent underperformance of Rangpur (avg HSC: 3.909, Excellent Rate: 20.3%) and Khulna (3.946, 22.9%) relative to Barisal (4.113, 36.8%) suggests geographic factors matter more than individual-level socio-economics. Conduct deeper regional audits to identify what drives Barisal's advantage — teacher quality, curriculum implementation, or community factors.

3Address the 43% of students who declined from Previous GPA to HSC

Nearly half of students saw their GPA fall between their previous assessment and HSC. This "churn" is hidden by stable averages. Implement early-warning systems that flag students whose in-term performance diverges from their prior trajectory, and provide targeted support before HSC examinations.

4Shift the policy goal from access to excellence

With 100% pass rate and 0% at-risk rate, the baseline performance floor is solid. The policy challenge is moving the 49.4% of students in the "Average" classification (3.0–3.99) into "Good" or "Excellent" territory. Consider differentiated instruction programs and enrichment opportunities that target this middle tier.

5Re-evaluate assumptions about private tuition and internet access

Neither private tuition (gap: 0.012 GPA) nor internet access (gap: 0.018 GPA) produces measurable improvement in this dataset. If these findings hold under validation, resources directed toward subsidizing tuition or connectivity may have better impact if redirected to school-level interventions such as teacher training or curriculum enrichment.
