Socio-Economic Factors and Their Impact on Performance

Most critical finding: Students in the highest income quintile outperform the lowest by -0.06 GPA points; internet access is associated with a 0.02 GPA-point advantage.
  • Family income: average HSC rises across income quintiles, indicating strong socio-economic gradient.
  • Parent education: higher parental education groups show better mean HSC outcomes.
  • Internet access: connected households have higher average HSC, supporting digital-equity interventions.

Regional Performance Disparities

District-level averages and district × school-type heatmap reveal geographic and institutional inequalities requiring place-based policy responses.

Key Findings and Policy Recommendations

  • Prioritize low-income districts for targeted grants, remedial support, and school-level performance contracts.
  • Scale internet/device access in low-connectivity communities to narrow measurable digital-performance gaps.
  • Track GPA progression delta at school level; deploy interventions where HSC underperforms prior GPA.
  • Use attendance early-warning systems where absenteeism strongly predicts lower HSC performance.
  • Allocate funding using district disparity metrics and public/private gap diagnostics.
