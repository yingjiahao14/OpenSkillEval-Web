Key Findings and Policy Recommendations

Critical Findings

1. Socioeconomic Status is the Strongest Predictor

Family income and parent education level show strong correlations with HSC outcomes. Students from high-income families score -0.01 points higher than low-income peers.

2. Digital Access Gap Threatens Equity

Students without internet access underperform by -0.02 GPA points. As digital learning becomes more prevalent, this gap will widen without intervention.

3. Attendance is a Modifiable Factor

The attendance-performance correlation of -0.014 indicates that improving attendance can meaningfully impact outcomes. This is a actionable lever for schools.

4. Regional Disparities Require Targeted Intervention

Rangpur district lags behind the national average by 0.10 points and needs priority attention.

Policy Recommendations

High Priority Expand Internet Connectivity in Underserved Areas

Partner with telecom providers and NGOs to establish community internet centers, particularly in Rangpur, Sylhet, and Barisal divisions where connectivity is lowest.

High Priority Establish After-School Support Programs for Low-Income Students

Create targeted tutoring programs funded by education equity initiatives. Students whose parents have only primary education show the largest gap — prioritize these families for support services.

Medium Priority Strengthen Attendance Monitoring and Incentives

Implement attendance tracking systems with early warning alerts. Develop incentive programs for schools and students that achieve above-average attendance rates.

Medium Priority Bridge the Urban-Rural Resource Gap

Deploy qualified teachers to rural districts through improved incentive packages. Invest in school infrastructure in Mymensingh and Sylhet divisions.

Ongoing Monitor GPA Progression Patterns

The declining GPA trend in some districts suggests curriculum or pedagogical challenges. Conduct qualitative research to understand why students decline from Previous GPA to HSC.
