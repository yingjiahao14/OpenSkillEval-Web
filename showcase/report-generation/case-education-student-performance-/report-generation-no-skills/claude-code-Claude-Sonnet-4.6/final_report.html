Socio-Economic Factors Show Negligible Effect

Internet access, private tuition, family income, and school type each show Cohen's d below 0.05 — all well below the conventional "small" threshold of 0.2. These factors do not meaningfully stratify HSC outcomes in this cohort.

Parental Education Shows Most Variation

Students with Secondary-educated parents score 4.050 vs. Primary-educated parents at 3.964 — an 0.086 gap. Though still small, this is the largest socio-economic differential observed, suggesting parental literacy and educational norms matter more than income.

Public Schools Marginally Outperform Private

Public school students average 4.018 vs. private school students at 4.007 — a 0.011 gap (d = −0.019). This challenges the common assumption that private schooling confers significant academic advantage in this population.

Counter-Intuitive Study Hour Pattern

Students studying only 0–5 hours/week have the highest mean GPA (4.129), while those studying 16–20 hours perform slightly below average (3.950). This non-linear relationship suggests the quality of study matters more than quantity in this cohort, or that high-ability students study less.

Sweet Spot: 80–89% Attendance

Students with 80–89% attendance achieve the highest mean GPA (4.082). Perfect attendance (90–100%) corresponds to a lower mean (3.948). This U-shaped pattern warrants further investigation — students with very high attendance may face other compensatory pressures.

Broad GPA Distribution at All Hours

The scatter charts reveal that at every study hour level (1–30 hrs) and attendance level (60–100%), students achieve GPA scores across the full 3.0–5.0 range. This variance indicates unmeasured factors are the primary performance drivers.

6Key Findings and Policy Recommendations
Summary of Key Findings
Finding 1: All 1,000 students passed (GPA ≥ 3.0) — zero at-risk students. The cohort represents above-average academic performers.
Finding 2: Socio-economic factors (income, internet, tuition) show negligible effect sizes (<0.05 Cohen's d) on HSC outcomes.
Finding 3: Parental education shows the largest socio-economic differential — Secondary-educated parents correlated with +0.086 GPA vs. Primary.
Finding 4: Regional disparities are real — 0.204 GPA gap between Barisal (top) and Rangpur (bottom).
Finding 5: Study hours and attendance correlate near-zero with HSC results (r < 0.025 in absolute value).
Finding 6: SSC performance declines at HSC — Excellent rate drops from 34.7% (SSC) to 26.8% (HSC).
01

Prioritise Regional Equity Interventions in Rangpur and Khulna

Rangpur (3.909) and Khulna (3.947) fall significantly below the national mean. A targeted resource allocation programme — including improved teacher training, infrastructure upgrades, and supplementary academic support — should be deployed in these two divisions. Performance gap with Barisal: 0.204 GPA points.

02

Investigate and Address the SSC-to-HSC Performance Decline

The Excellent classification rate drops from 34.7% at SSC to 26.8% at HSC — a 7.9 percentage point regression. This transition-point deterioration warrants curriculum continuity review, and HSC-specific academic support should be introduced early in the HSC cycle.

03

Focus on Parental Literacy and Family Engagement Programmes

Parental education level is the strongest socio-economic predictor found in this analysis (+0.086 GPA for Secondary vs. Primary-educated parents). Community adult literacy programmes and structured parent-school engagement initiatives may create longer-term academic improvement more effectively than hardware (internet, tuition) investments alone.

04

Re-evaluate the Policy Rationale for Universal Private Tuition Subsidies

Private tuition shows a Cohen's d of 0.021 — negligible — with students scoring 4.019 (with tuition) vs. 4.007 (without). Policy funds currently directed toward private tuition subsidies could yield greater impact if redirected to classroom-level quality improvements, teacher development, or regional equity programmes.

05

Develop Unmeasured Factor Research — The "Unexplained Variance" Problem

Near-zero correlations across all measured predictors (highest r = 0.047 for Previous GPA) indicate that unmeasured variables drive most HSC performance variation. Future data collection should include teacher quality metrics, classroom environment, mental health indicators, peer effects, and learning style assessments to build more predictive models.

06

Optimise Attendance Policy Around the 80–89% Sweet Spot

Students in the 80–89% attendance band achieve the highest mean GPA (4.082), while those with 90–100% attendance actually score lower (3.948). Current absolute-attendance mandates should be reviewed — flexibility for project-based, self-directed study may benefit high-performing students who are penalised for minor absences.
