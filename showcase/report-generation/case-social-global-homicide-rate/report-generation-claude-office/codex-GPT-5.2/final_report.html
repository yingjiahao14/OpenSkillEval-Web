Key question

Is the world becoming safer from intentional homicide, and for whom? This report summarizes global directionality, gender disparity, and the location of high-risk and rapidly changing contexts, while explicitly accounting for uneven reporting coverage.

1) Global Homicide Rate Overview

The “global” series below summarizes country-level homicide rates (victims per 100,000 population) by taking the mean and median across reporting countries each year (sex=both, age=ALL). Because this dataset is an unbalanced panel, changes in the set of reporting countries can shift these aggregates; both central tendency lines should be interpreted together with coverage.

6) Data Limitations & Policy Recommendations

Data limitations (material to interpretation)

  • Unbalanced panel: coverage varies substantially by year; global aggregates are over the set of reporting countries, not a fixed cohort.
  • No interpolation: missing years are absent rather than imputed; trends should not be interpreted as continuous national time series.
  • Age groups constraint: age detail exists only for sex-specific series (male/female). For sex=both, only age_group=ALL is available in this dataset.
  • Territories and UK caveat: some territories are separate entries; UK data may represent England and Wales only (per dataset documentation).
  • Comparability: extreme values can occur; validate outliers against local context and reporting practices before operational decisions.

Actionable policy directions (evidence-aligned)

  • Target high-burden settings: prioritize resources where rates remain high in the latest reporting year, pairing enforcement with violence interruption and prevention.
  • Address gendered risk: sustained male victimization disparities support focused interventions on high-risk male populations, while strengthening prevention and protection for women (including intimate partner violence pathways).
  • Invest in data systems: improve national reporting consistency and timeliness to reduce coverage volatility and enable better cross-country benchmarking.
  • Use distribution-aware KPIs: monitor both mean and median, and track coverage; large shifts in means can be driven by outliers or changing reporting composition.
