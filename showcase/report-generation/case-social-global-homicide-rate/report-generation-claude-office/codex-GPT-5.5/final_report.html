Executive Takeaways

The central tendency in the reporting-country panel points to lower typical homicide rates than at the beginning of the series, but the answer to “is the world becoming safer?” is conditional. The median rate fell from 3.4 in 1990 to 1.8 in 2023, while the reporting-country mean rose from 6.3 to 8.3. This divergence reflects a skewed distribution in which a small set of very high-rate countries heavily influences the mean.

  • Typical-country improvement: the 2023 median is -45.1% below the 1990 median among reporting countries.
  • Persistent concentration: the 2023 top-rate countries are dominated by Caribbean and Latin American countries/territories in the provided data, with several rates above 40 per 100,000.
  • Gender disparity is large: male victimization rates are 6.8 times female rates on an aggregate mean basis in 2023; the country-level median male:female ratio is 3.2×.
  • Young adults are most exposed in latest age-specific reporting: the highest sex-combined mean rate is for ages 25–29 at 1.6 per 100,000 across 27 reporting countries.
  • Interpretation requires caution: 2023 total-rate coverage is 41.8%, lower than peak coverage years, so recent global estimates reflect a changing set of reporting countries.

Policy interpretation

  • Male victimization is consistently higher across the series, indicating that violence-prevention strategies need a strong focus on male risk environments.
  • The gap is not uniform: country-level ratios differ sharply, so national prevention strategies should be tailored to local violence patterns rather than using a single global template.
  • Female homicide rates are lower on average, but this does not imply low policy priority; dedicated domestic, intimate-partner, and community safety responses remain essential where female rates are elevated.

Data Limitations & Policy Recommendations

Data limitations

  • Unbalanced panel: country-year rows are absent when data are unavailable; coverage ranges by year and falls to 84 countries/territories in 2023 for the total-rate panel.
  • No population weighting: the dataset provides rates, not population counts, so this report uses unweighted country means and medians rather than a true population-weighted global homicide rate.
  • Region not included: because no region column is present, the report does not calculate formal regional averages.
  • Age-group comparability: age-specific reporting is much thinner than all-age reporting; latest age findings are based on roughly two dozen reporting countries per age group.
  • Territories and UK caveat: territories are included separately where reported, and the source brief notes that United Kingdom data refer to England and Wales only.

Policy recommendations

  • Prioritize high-rate clusters: focus prevention, investigative capacity, and regional cooperation on the highest-rate countries/territories visible in the most recent data, especially where rates exceed 20 per 100,000.
  • Target male risk environments: integrate gender-responsive prevention into violence-reduction strategies, with attention to young adult male victimization and local drivers of lethal violence.
  • Protect gains in improving countries: countries with large declines should be monitored for sustainability and studied for operational lessons, while avoiding causal claims not supported by this dataset.
  • Improve reporting continuity: strengthen national statistical systems and consistent sex/age disaggregation so trend monitoring is less affected by panel gaps.
  • Use medians alongside means: for executive monitoring, pair the global median with high-rate outlier tracking; the median captures typical-country movement, while the mean flags concentration in severe hotspots.
