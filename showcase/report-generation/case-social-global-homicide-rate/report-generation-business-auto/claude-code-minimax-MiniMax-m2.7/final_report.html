Global Homicide Rate Overview

The global trajectory of intentional homicide rates reveals a nuanced picture of long-term change. After a period of relative stability in the 1990s and an uptick in the 2000s, the 2010s marked a notable decline in global homicide rates, with the trend appearing to stabilize in the early 2020s despite data gaps.

Key Finding: The global mean homicide rate declined from 8.52 per 100,000 in the 2000s to 7.67 per 100,000 in the 2010s—a 10% improvement. However, the 2020s data (based on limited reporting) shows a slight uptick to 7.80, warranting continued monitoring.

The chart above illustrates the year-by-year evolution of global homicide rates from 1990 to 2023. The shaded area represents the interquartile range (25th to 75th percentile), while the line shows the median rate. Notably, both the mean and median have generally trended downward since the early 2000s, suggesting a genuine global improvement rather than an artifact of changing participation.

Gender Disparity in Homicide Victimization

One of the most consistent and stark findings across all countries and time periods is the significant gender disparity in homicide victimization. Men are far more likely to be homicide victims than women, though both sexes remain affected.

Age Group Analysis

Analysis of homicide rates by age group reveals that young adults face the highest risk of homicide victimization across most reporting countries.

Based on the most recent age-disaggregated data available (2022), the patterns are clear:

  • Young adults (20-29) face the highest homicide risk, with rates peaking in the late teens and twenties
  • Middle-aged adults (30-44) experience elevated but declining rates
  • Adolescents (15-17) show moderate but notable rates
  • Children (0-14) and elderly (60+) have the lowest rates

Country Variations in Age Patterns

The age profile of homicide victims varies by country. In countries with high overall homicide rates (e.g., in Central America and the Caribbean), the concentration in young adult males is particularly pronounced. In contrast, countries with lower overall rates tend to show a more evenly distributed age pattern or slightly higher elderly victimization.

Data Limitations & Policy Recommendations

Critical Data Limitations

This analysis is based on an unbalanced panel dataset with significant reporting gaps. Key limitations include:

  • Incomplete time series: Not all countries have observations for every year. Coverage ranges from 84 countries (2023) to 162 countries (2008).
  • Non-random missingness: Countries that fail to report may differ systematically from reporters. This may bias global estimates upward or downward.
  • UK data caveat: UK data refers to England and Wales only, not the full United Kingdom.
  • 2020s data sparsity: Only 84 countries reported data for 2023, limiting the currency of recent trend analysis.
  • Age group data limitations: Age-disaggregated data is not available for all country-year combinations, limiting the precision of demographic analysis.

Policy Recommendations

Based on the evidence presented, we recommend the following priorities for policy analysts and public safety stakeholders:

  1. Prioritize the Americas: The Caribbean and Central/South American regions bear a disproportionate burden of global homicide. International cooperation should prioritize these regions for crime prevention assistance.
  2. Address male youth vulnerability: Males aged 15-44 represent the highest-risk demographic. Prevention programs should target this group specifically, with interventions addressing gang involvement, employment access, and conflict resolution.
  3. Study success cases: Countries like El Salvador, Singapore, and Colombia have achieved dramatic homicide reductions. Systematic study of their approaches—even where context differs—can inform policy design elsewhere.
  4. Strengthen data collection: Improved reporting coverage, particularly in under-reporting regions, is essential for accurate global monitoring. Investment in national statistical systems yields compounding benefits for policy design.
  5. Monitor recent deterioration: The slight uptick in global rates during the 2020s warrants continued monitoring. Whether this reflects a reversal of prior gains or a data artifact cannot yet be determined.
Overarching Conclusion: The evidence suggests that, on balance, the world has become safer from intentional homicide over the past three decades. However, this aggregate improvement masks enormous disparities—by geography, gender, and age. The countries and populations facing the highest risk today are not evenly distributed, and policy attention should be concentrated where the burden is greatest.
