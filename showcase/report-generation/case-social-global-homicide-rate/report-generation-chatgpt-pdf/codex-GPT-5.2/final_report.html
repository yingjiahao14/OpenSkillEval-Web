Interpretation note: “Global” in this report means the distribution of country rates available in each year. The dataset is an unbalanced panel: year-to-year changes reflect both real changes and shifts in which countries reported.

Age Group Analysis

Important limitation: Age-group rates are available for male and female only. The dataset does not provide age-group breakdowns for sex='both', so age-group insights are sex-specific.

Which age groups are highest risk (overall)?

Across all available years and countries with age disaggregation, the highest mean rates concentrate in young-adult and working-age groups for males (notably 20–29 and 30–44), while female rates are markedly lower across all age groups.

This summary reflects a mean across all observations available (unbalanced by country/year). Use it as an indicative pattern, not a population-weighted global estimate.

Data Limitations & Policy Recommendations

Key limitations (directly relevant for interpretation)

  • Unbalanced panel: countries are missing for many years; trends reflect changing sample composition.
  • No interpolation: missing years are absent rather than imputed; this report does not fill gaps.
  • Age group coverage: age-specific rates are only available for male/female, not sex='both'.
  • Territories: some entries are territories reported separately; avoid double-counting with parent states.
  • UK caveat: as documented, UK data correspond to England and Wales only.

Actionable policy directions suggested by the patterns in the data

  • Target the highest-risk male age bands: where age-disaggregated data exist, focus prevention on male 20–29 and 30–44.
  • Use median-based benchmarking: track median across countries to reduce sensitivity to outlier spikes.
  • Strengthen data systems: expand consistent year-to-year reporting; the latest year covers only 41.8% of countries in-file.
  • Prioritize outlier-country support: very high-rate jurisdictions in the latest year warrant tailored assistance and rapid diagnostics.
  • Monitor gender disparities: the male burden remains multiple times the female rate; interventions should address drivers of male victimization.

Recommendations are framed from observed patterns only; this report does not infer causality.

Method note: All computations derive exclusively from benchmark/data.csv. Global aggregates are unweighted across countries due to lack of population denominators in the file.
