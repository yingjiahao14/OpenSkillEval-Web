Executive Summary

The dataset points to a mixed answer to the question “is the world becoming safer from intentional homicide?” The median country in 2023 had a lower homicide rate than in 1990, but the global mean remains elevated by a small number of very high-rate countries and the reporting panel changes substantially over time.

  • Central tendency improved: the median total-population rate fell from 3.35 in 1990 to 1.84 in 2023, a -45.1% change across reporting countries.
  • Average risk is more volatile: the mean changed from 6.33 to 8.29 over the same endpoints (+30.9%), reflecting sensitivity to high-rate outliers and changing country coverage.
  • Gender disparity is large and persistent: in 2023, the cross-country male mean rate was 14.76 versus 2.16 for females, producing a 6.8× ratio.
  • Country-level divergence matters: El Salvador shows the largest absolute decline among countries with at least a 10-year span, while Turks and Caicos Islands shows the largest increase under the same rule.
  • Data completeness is a policy finding: 2023 coverage is only 41.8%, so apparent global movements should be read as trends among reporting countries, not population-weighted world estimates.

1. Global Homicide Rate Overview

The analysis uses sex = both and age_group = ALL for high-level national comparisons. Because the file does not include national population counts, “global” estimates are unweighted cross-country summaries among countries/territories that reported in a given year.

Bars show the share of the 201 countries/territories that ever reported a total-population rate in this dataset. Lines show cross-country mean and median rates among reporting countries for each year.

Trajectory interpretation

The median rate declines over the period, indicating that the typical reporting country became safer by this measure. The mean is consistently higher than the median, showing a right-skewed distribution where a relatively small set of countries experiences much higher homicide rates.

The 2010s had a lower average annual mean than the 2000s, while the partial 2020s average is roughly flat compared with the 2010s. This does not prove a definitive global reversal because the 2020s include only four years and have lower reporting coverage.

3. Gender Disparity in Homicide Victimization

Gender disparity is the clearest demographic pattern in the dataset. Among countries with both male and female total-population rates in 2023, the male cross-country mean rate was 14.76 per 100,000, compared with 2.16 for females.

Key KPI: The 2023 male-to-female homicide rate ratio is 6.8× using the ratio of cross-country mean male and female rates. The median country-level ratio is lower at 3.2×, which indicates that high-rate countries with especially elevated male victimization pull the mean-based KPI upward.

Policy implications

  • Violence prevention should prioritize young and adult men in high-rate settings without neglecting female victimization risks.
  • Monitoring systems should continue sex-disaggregated reporting; one country in 2023 has total data but lacks a complete male/female pair.
  • Mean and median ratios should be tracked together because they answer different questions: system-wide burden versus typical-country disparity.

6. Data Limitations & Policy Recommendations

Reporting coverage and panel balance

The panel is unbalanced: countries enter and leave the reporting set over time. Coverage ranges from 84 reporting countries/territories in 2023 to 162 in 2008. In 2023, only 84 reported a total-population rate.

Limitations

  • No population weights: the file contains rates, not population denominators, so global summaries are unweighted country/territory statistics rather than population-weighted world rates.
  • No region field: formal regional rates are not calculated to avoid importing classifications not present in the provided data.
  • Unbalanced reporting: apparent annual shifts may reflect changes in the mix of reporting countries as well as changes in homicide risk.
  • Age data are sparse: age-specific findings are based on fewer countries than total-population findings, especially in 2023.
  • Source caveat: as documented in source_brief.md, United Kingdom data refer to England and Wales only, and some territories appear separately.

Policy recommendations

  • Prioritize high-outlier countries: countries above the latest-year IQR threshold warrant targeted violence-prevention support, criminal justice capacity review, and sustained monitoring.
  • Target male victimization risk: prevention strategies should focus on the social settings and age bands where male victimization is highest, particularly young adult and adult men.
  • Maintain gains in improving countries: countries with large declines should be studied using local administrative and policy records to identify transferable prevention practices.
  • Strengthen reporting infrastructure: international cooperation should focus on consistent annual total, sex-specific, and age-specific reporting so trend interpretation is less dependent on changing coverage.
  • Use median and outlier metrics together: median trends track typical-country safety, while mean and outlier measures identify concentrated high-burden settings that require resource allocation.

Methodological Notes

  • All statistics are derived directly from /app/benchmark/data.csv.
  • High-level comparisons filter to sex = both and age_group = ALL.
  • The male-to-female KPI uses matched country-year records where both male and female ALL rates are present.
  • Decade-over-decade change uses the average of annual cross-country mean rates; the 2020s decade is partial because the data end in 2023.
  • No missing values are imputed, and absent country-year rows remain absent from all calculations.
