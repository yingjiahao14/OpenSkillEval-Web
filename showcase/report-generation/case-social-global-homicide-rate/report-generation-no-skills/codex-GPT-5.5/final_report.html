Executive Summary

Is the world becoming safer from intentional homicide? In the country-rate panel provided, the typical reporting country is safer than in the early 1990s: the median total-population homicide rate declined from 3.35 in 1990 to 1.84 in 2023 (-45.1%). The mean rate, however, increased from 6.33 to 8.29 (30.9%), indicating that high-rate countries and changing country coverage materially affect the aggregate picture.

  • Risk is highly concentrated. The 2023 top 10 are dominated by Caribbean and Latin American countries, while many of the lowest reported rates are in East Asia, Southern Europe, and small jurisdictions.
  • Gender disparity is central. In 2023, matched countries reported a male mean homicide rate of 15.0 and female mean of 2.2, a 6.8× aggregate ratio. The median country-level ratio was lower, at 3.2×, showing that extreme high-rate settings raise the aggregate disparity.
  • Young adult men face the highest rates. For 2020–2023 age-specific records, male rates peak at ages 25–29 (28.9), followed by 20–24 and 30–44. Female rates are far lower and flatter across adult ages.
  • Outliers are policy-relevant. El Salvador shows the largest long-span decline in the dataset, while several small Caribbean jurisdictions, Ecuador, Haiti, and Jamaica show large increases over their available observation spans.
  • Interpretation must account for gaps. The panel is unbalanced: 2023 coverage is 41.8%, below the peak of 80.6% in 2008. No missing years were filled.

6. Data Limitations & Policy Recommendations

Data limitations

  • Unbalanced panel: Countries enter and exit the reporting set. The total-population panel contains 4215 records across 201 countries/territories, but only 84 report in 2023.
  • No population weights: The CSV provides rates but not population denominators, so global and regional means are unweighted country averages rather than population-weighted homicide rates.
  • Missing years are absent: The analysis does not impute, interpolate, or carry forward missing observations.
  • Age groups are sex-specific: No all-sex age-group rows exist in the file; the age section therefore reports male and female age-specific rates separately.
  • Territories and UK caveat: Territories are included separately when present. The source documentation notes that United Kingdom data refer to England and Wales only, with Scotland and Northern Ireland potentially reported separately.
  • Regional grouping: Broad region labels were assigned for readability from country names; they are not an additional data source for rates.

Policy recommendations

  • Prioritize high-rate clusters: Direct violence-prevention funding, investigative capacity, and cross-border cooperation toward Caribbean and Latin American settings with persistently high recent rates.
  • Target young adult male risk: Combine focused deterrence, firearm and weapon violence prevention, labor-market supports, and community mediation for males aged roughly 18–44, especially 20–29.
  • Maintain gender-sensitive services: Although male rates dominate the aggregate burden, female homicide risk requires dedicated intimate-partner violence prevention, emergency protection, and victim-support systems.
  • Use medians and distributions in monitoring: Track both median and mean rates so policymakers can distinguish broad-based safety improvements from concentrated crises.
  • Invest in reporting completeness: Improve annual country reporting and sex/age disaggregation; coverage gaps directly limit the reliability of global monitoring.
  • Investigate outliers before reallocating resources: Validate abrupt changes against national reporting practices and case-count context, particularly in small jurisdictions.
