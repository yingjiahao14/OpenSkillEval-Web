1) Global Homicide Rate Overview

The global trajectory is mixed. Cross-country mean homicide rates rose from the 1990s to 2000s, declined through the 2010s, then ticked up in the early 2020s (partial decade). The median remains far below the mean in many years, indicating persistent high-rate outliers.

3) Gender Disparity in Homicide Victimization

Gender disparity is structurally large: male victimization rates substantially exceed female rates across virtually all years with matched reporting. In the latest year, the global male-to-female rate ratio is 6.85x.

4) Age Group Analysis

Across reported country-year-age observations, young adult groups (especially 20s) tend to show the highest homicide risk. This aligns with the need for targeted youth and young-adult violence prevention strategies.

6) Data Limitations & Policy Recommendations

  • Unbalanced panel: country coverage varies materially by year; trend interpretation should account for changing reporter composition.
  • Coverage gap: latest-year reporting is 41.8% of all observed countries/territories in this dataset.
  • No imputation: missing years are absent rows, not estimated values; all calculations use available observations only.
  • Comparability caveat: UK observations represent England and Wales in this source framing.

Actionable implications: prioritize prevention and policing resources toward high-rate outlier contexts; sustain male-focused violence interruption while preserving protection responses for women and girls; invest in standardized, sex- and age-disaggregated reporting systems; and benchmark progress with robust distributional metrics (median, percentile spread), not mean alone.
