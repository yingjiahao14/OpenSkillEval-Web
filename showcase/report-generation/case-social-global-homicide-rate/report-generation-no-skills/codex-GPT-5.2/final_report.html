6) Data Limitations & Policy Recommendations

Key limitations (from the dataset structure)

  • Unbalanced panel: many countries do not report every year; coverage changes over time.
  • Comparability: “global” time-series are summaries of reporting countries in that year (not a constant cohort, not population-weighted).
  • Territories: some territories are separate entries; they may not be comparable to sovereign states and should not be double-counted with parent countries.
  • UK caveat: the source documentation notes the “United Kingdom” series covers England and Wales only.
  • Age groups: age-band coverage is much sparser and sex-specific; any age conclusions should be treated as indicative.

Actionable implications for policy stakeholders

  • Use robust baselines: track both median and mean, and report coverage alongside trends.
  • Prioritize male victimization risk: the sex gap is large and persistent; prevention and enforcement strategies should explicitly address male risk contexts (e.g., street violence, organized crime).
  • Target peak-risk ages: where age data exist, young adult cohorts (roughly 18–29) show the highest central tendency of rates across countries.
  • Focus on outlier trajectories: countries with large multi-decade increases or decreases warrant deeper, context-specific review (policy, governance, conflict, criminal markets, policing, public health).
  • Invest in measurement capacity: improving routine reporting is a prerequisite for credible monitoring and international comparison.
