Global Homicide Rate Overview

This report summarizes intentional homicide victimization rates (victims per 100,000 population) reported to UNODC, spanning 1990–2023. The backbone finding is mixed: the cross-country median rate in 2023 is 1.84, while the cross-country mean is 8.29, indicating that a smaller number of very high-rate countries strongly influence global averages.

For context, the average of yearly medians across 1990–1994 is 4.43 (vs 1.84 in 2023), and the average of yearly means across 1990–1994 is 7.93 (vs 8.29 in 2023). Because reporting coverage changes substantially over time, these comparisons should be interpreted as trends among reporting countries rather than a fully representative global time series.

Gender Disparity in Homicide Victimization

Across the countries reporting sex-disaggregated data, homicide victimization is consistently higher for males than females. In 2023, the mean male rate across reporting countries is 14.76 per 100,000 versus 2.16 for females.

As a single executive KPI, the male-to-female homicide rate ratio in 2023 is 6.85× when computed as the ratio of global means (across countries with both-sex data). The country-level distribution is wider; the median country ratio in 2023 is 3.17×.

Why the ratio matters: a high ratio often indicates that violence disproportionately concentrates among men (e.g., interpersonal violence, criminal market violence), but the policy implications depend on local context and should be interpreted alongside absolute female rates.

Male and female trends are shown as global means across reporting countries. The male-to-female ratio uses the ratio of those means (ALL ages).

Age Group Analysis

Age-specific homicide rates in this dataset are available from 2003 to 2023 and are reported by sex (there is no “both sexes” series for age groups). In 2023, the highest mean age-specific rate for males occurs in 20–24 at 2.02 per 100,000; for females it occurs in 25–29 at 1.12.

Coverage for age groups is substantially lower than for all-ages totals (e.g., in 2023 roughly 23–27 countries report each age group, depending on sex). Patterns should therefore be interpreted as indicative among reporting countries rather than globally representative.

Data Limitations & Policy Recommendations

Key data limitations

  • Unbalanced panel: country coverage varies year-to-year. Coverage peaks at 162 reporting countries in 2008, but falls to 84 in 2023.
  • No interpolation: missing years are absent (not imputed). All trends reflect only observed data.
  • Recent-year gaps: 117 of 201 countries have their latest all-ages, both-sex observation before 2023, which can bias “most recent year” comparisons toward countries with stronger reporting capacity.
  • Comparability caveats: territories may appear separately; UK data (where present) may refer to England and Wales only. These factors affect cross-country comparisons.

Action-oriented policy implications (derived from observed patterns)

  • Target high-burden settings: the right tail of the country distribution drives the global mean—prioritize interventions where national rates are persistently high.
  • Focus on gendered risk: male victimization rates exceed female rates in most reporting contexts; prevention strategies should include focused deterrence and violence-interruption approaches tailored to high-risk male groups, while maintaining survivor-centered measures addressing lethal violence against women.
  • Improve measurement capacity: expanding timely reporting (especially post-2019) is itself a safety investment; better surveillance enables earlier detection of deteriorations and more accurate evaluation of interventions.
  • Use robust summaries: for cross-country monitoring, medians and distributional views are more stable than global means because they are less sensitive to outliers.
