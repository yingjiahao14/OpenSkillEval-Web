1. Global Homicide Rate Overview

Executive Summary

Analysis of UNODC data from 201 countries spanning 1990–2023 reveals that the world has become moderately safer over the past three decades. The global median homicide rate declined from approximately 3.4 per 100,000 in 1990 to 1.84 per 100,000 in 2023. However, this global aggregate masks significant regional disparities, with the Americas experiencing sustained high rates while Europe and Asia show sustained improvements.

Global Homicide Rate Trend (1990–2023)

The chart below shows the evolution of the global median homicide rate over the observation period. The global mean is included for comparison, illustrating how high-rate outlier countries skew the average upward.

Key Finding: The global median homicide rate fluctuated between 2.3 and 5.2 per 100,000 over the observation period. A noticeable peak occurred in the early-mid 2000s, followed by a gradual decline. The 2010s decade showed the most consistent improvement.

Data Coverage Over Time

The number of countries reporting homicide data to UNODC has increased from 94 countries in 1990 to a peak of 162 in 2008, then declined to 84 by 2023. This variation in reporting coverage affects trend interpretation.

3. Gender Disparity in Homicide Victimization

Critical Gender Disparity KPI

The male-to-female homicide rate ratio of 3.2x indicates that males are approximately 3.2 times more likely to be homicide victims than females. This ratio has remained remarkably stable across decades (1990s: 3.1x, 2000s: 3.0x, 2010s: 3.3x, 2020s: 3.1x), suggesting structural factors driving gender-based violence risk have not substantially changed.

Male vs. Female Homicide Rates Over Time

Global average homicide rates for males and females show parallel trajectories, with male rates consistently 3x higher than female rates.

Male-to-Female Ratio Evolution

Implication: Prevention strategies must account for the distinct risk profiles between genders. Male homicide victimization is more closely associated with gang violence and drug trade, while female victims more frequently experience domestic violence contexts.

6. Data Limitations & Policy Recommendations

Data Limitations

Important Caveats:
  • Unbalanced Panel: Not all 201 countries have observations for every year. Coverage ranges from 41.8% to 80.6%, with coverage declining in recent years.
  • UK Data Caveat: UK figures refer to England and Wales only, as Scotland and Northern Ireland report separately.
  • No Imputation: Missing years were not interpolated—only available data points were used for calculations.
  • Reporting Quality: National data collection methods vary, potentially affecting comparability.
