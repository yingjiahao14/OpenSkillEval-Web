Executive Summary

The central tendency of homicide in the reporting-country panel is lower in 2023 than at the start of the series: the median total-population rate fell from 3.35 in 1990 to 1.84 in 2023 (-45.1%). The mean, however, rose from 6.33 to 8.29 (+30.9%), reflecting a smaller set of very high-rate countries that pull the average upward.

Bottom line: For the typical reporting country, homicide rates are lower than in the early 1990s, but safety gains are uneven. High homicide burdens are concentrated in specific countries, and male victimization remains several times higher than female victimization.

3. Gender Disparity in Homicide Victimization

Sex-disparity analysis uses country-years with matched male and female total-population records. In 2023, the mean male homicide rate across matched reporting countries was 14.76, compared with 2.16 for females. The aggregate male-to-female ratio was therefore 6.85×; the median country-level ratio was 3.17×.

Policy implication: Male victimization is persistently several times higher than female victimization in the reporting panel. Prevention portfolios should include male-focused violence interruption and firearm/weapon-risk strategies while maintaining dedicated responses to gender-based and domestic violence.

The ratio varies year to year with both actual changes and changes in the set of countries reporting sex-disaggregated records. The KPI uses the latest matched year and divides the cross-country mean male rate by the cross-country mean female rate, making the calculation transparent and reproducible from the dataset.

4. Age Group Analysis

Age-group records are available only for male and female victims in this dataset, not for sex = both. Age coverage begins in 2003 and covers fewer countries than the total-rate panel. The historical age-by-sex profile shows the highest average rates among young adult and adult males, especially ages 20–29 and 30–44.

6. Data Limitations & Policy Recommendations

Data limitations

  • Unbalanced panel: reporting varies by country and year. The total-rate panel has 201 countries/territories overall, but only 84 report in 2023.
  • No interpolation: absent country-years are not filled. Trend lines reflect only observed records.
  • Cross-country, not population-weighted: means and medians treat each reporting country/territory equally because the dataset does not include population counts.
  • Age data limitations: age-group records are sex-specific only and start later than the main panel, with narrower country coverage.
  • United Kingdom caveat: the source documentation states UK data refer to England and Wales only, with Scotland and Northern Ireland potentially reported separately.
  • Territories included: territories may appear separately and should not be merged with parent countries without a documented method.

Policy recommendations

  • Target concentrated high-burden settings: prioritize technical assistance, investigative capacity, violence interruption, and prevention funding in countries with rates far above the 2023 outlier threshold.
  • Focus on male victimization risk: integrate male-focused prevention strategies into national public safety plans while preserving services for women and girls exposed to lethal and non-lethal violence.
  • Invest in youth and young-adult prevention: age-disaggregated data point to elevated risk among young adult males, supporting early intervention, school-to-work supports, and community-based violence prevention.
  • Strengthen reporting systems: improve timeliness, sex and age disaggregation, and consistent annual reporting so trend assessments are less distorted by coverage changes.
  • Use medians alongside means: the gap between the latest mean (8.29) and median (1.84) shows why policy dashboards should report both typical-country experience and high-burden extremes.
  • Pair homicide data with context: country trend outliers should trigger deeper diagnostic reviews using justice, health, socioeconomic, and conflict indicators before selecting interventions.
