1. Global Homicide Rate Overview

The data show a mixed global picture: the typical reporting country became safer by the median measure, while the reporting-country mean remains elevated because a relatively small set of high-rate countries pulls the average upward.

Among countries with total-population observations (sex=both, age_group=ALL), the median homicide rate declined from 3.35 in 1990 to 1.84 in 2023, a -45.1% change. The mean rose from 6.33 to 8.29 over the same endpoint comparison, a +30.9% change, underscoring how high-rate outliers shape the average.

The time series therefore does not support a simple statement that homicide risk has fallen everywhere. It supports a more policy-relevant conclusion: many reporting countries have low or declining rates, but risk is highly concentrated and persistent in specific national and subregional contexts.

3. Gender Disparity in Homicide Victimization

Gender disparity is one of the clearest and most stable findings in the dataset: male homicide rates are several times higher than female homicide rates across the reporting panel.

Headline KPI: In 2023, the mean male homicide rate across countries with sex-disaggregated data was 14.76, compared with 2.16 for females. The male-to-female ratio based on those mean rates was 6.85×, while the median country-level ratio was 3.17×.

This gap means violence-prevention policy should explicitly account for male victimization risk, particularly among young and working-age males in high-rate settings. At the same time, female homicide risk should not be minimized: lower rates can still represent severe household, intimate-partner, and community safety concerns, but this dataset does not identify circumstances or perpetrators.

6. Data Limitations & Policy Recommendations

Data limitations

  • Unbalanced panel: rows are absent when data are unavailable. No missing years were interpolated, and the reporting-country mix changes over time.
  • Partial latest-year coverage: 2023 includes 84 countries/territories, below the observed peak of 162. Comparisons against earlier years should therefore be interpreted as reporting-panel summaries, not fully population-weighted global estimates.
  • Territories and coverage definitions: territories appear separately when reported. The dataset documentation notes that United Kingdom data refer to England and Wales only, while Scotland and Northern Ireland may appear separately in the source.
  • No causal variables: the dataset contains homicide rates by country, year, sex, and age group only. It cannot directly test drivers such as firearms availability, organized crime, policing, conflict, inequality, or justice-system capacity.
  • Age category inconsistency: age-specific reporting is less complete and less harmonized than total-population reporting.

Policy recommendations

  • Target high-rate clusters: prioritize prevention, investigative capacity, and violence-interruption resources in the countries and subregions repeatedly appearing at the top of recent-rate and deterioration lists.
  • Make gender disparity operational: use the male-to-female ratio as a dashboard KPI, while designing interventions for high-risk male victimization without neglecting female safety and domestic-violence prevention.
  • Focus youth and adult prevention: align social services, school-to-work pathways, firearm and weapon controls, and community-based prevention with the age groups showing elevated rates.
  • Separate monitoring from evaluation: use this dataset for trend surveillance and case selection; pair it with local administrative, justice, public-health, and victimization data before evaluating specific interventions.
  • Invest in reporting continuity: improve annual, sex-disaggregated, and age-disaggregated reporting so analysts can distinguish real safety changes from data-coverage changes.
Bottom line: The median reporting country appears safer in 2023 than in 1990, but the world is not uniformly safer. Homicide risk remains sharply concentrated by place, sex, and age, making targeted prevention and better data systems essential.
