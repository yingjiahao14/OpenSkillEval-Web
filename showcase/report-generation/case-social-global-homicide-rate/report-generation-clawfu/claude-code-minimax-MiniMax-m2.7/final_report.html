Executive Summary

Key Finding: The world is becoming moderately safer from intentional homicide. Global average rates declined from 8.24 per 100,000 in the 1990s to 7.80 in the 2020s—a 5.3% improvement over three decades. However, this aggregate progress masks significant regional disparities and demographic inequalities.
Critical Disparity: Males face a homicide rate 3.2 times higher than females globally. Young adults aged 25-29 experience the highest victimization rates, with a median of 3.99 per 100,000—making targeted youth violence prevention programs essential.

The Caribbean and Central America continue to bear a disproportionate burden, with Saint Kitts and Nevis (64.2), Saint Vincent and the Grenadines (51.3), and Jamaica (49.4) recording rates 20-30 times higher than safest nations like Singapore (0.07) or Japan (0.23).

Data Limitations & Policy Recommendations

Data Limitations

  • Unbalanced Panel: Not all 201 countries report data for every year. Coverage peaked at 162 countries in 2008 but dropped to 84 in 2023.
  • Missing Data: Years with no available data are simply absent from the dataset—no interpolation was performed.
  • UK Caveat: United Kingdom data covers England and Wales only, not Scotland and Northern Ireland.
  • Territorial Variations: Some territories (Hong Kong, Macao, Puerto Rico) are reported separately from parent countries.
  • Age Group Data: Disaggregated age data exists only for male/female separately, not for combined "both" sex.

Policy Recommendations

  1. Strengthen Data Collection: Improve national reporting capacity to enable better trend monitoring and cross-country comparisons.
  2. Target High-Risk Demographics: Young males aged 18-29 should be priority beneficiaries of violence prevention programs.
  3. Address Regional Hotspots: The Caribbean and Central America require sustained attention and international cooperation to address persistently high rates.
  4. Learn from Success Stories: Countries showing significant improvements (e.g., Colombia's reduction from 70+ to ~25 per 100,000) offer models worth studying.
  5. Gender-Responsive Approaches: Prevention strategies should account for the 3.2x male-to-female disparity and the female-specific risk of intimate partner violence.
Conclusion: While global homicide rates show modest long-term improvement, the persistent gap between safest and most dangerous nations—along with stark gender and age disparities—demands continued policy attention and international cooperation.
