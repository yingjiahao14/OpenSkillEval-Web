## Poster Brief: The History of Books — From Manuscripts to Mass Production

You're creating an A1 portrait data-report poster that tells the sweeping story of book production across 1,500 years. The audience is intellectually curious — think museum visitors, university open days, or data-journalism readers. The key takeaway: the printing press didn't just change publishing; it fundamentally reshaped civilization, and the data makes that transformation visceral.

### Key Priorities

- **The long arc of data is the star.** The contrast between the slow trickle of manuscripts over ten centuries and the explosive growth after Gutenberg (~1440) should be the most visually striking element on the poster. A well-designed timeline or area chart spanning the 6th century to the present should dominate the upper-to-middle portion.
- **The Gutenberg inflection point** must feel dramatic — use annotation, scale breaks, or visual emphasis so the viewer immediately grasps the magnitude of change.
- **Per-capita modern publishing data** (country-level variation) deserves its own clear, compact visualization lower on the poster, reinforcing that the story continues today.
- The "Books as a Mirror of Society" section should serve as a closing narrative beat — concise text linking book output to literacy, wealth, and culture.

### Visual & Style Guidance

Tone is **data-forward but engaging** — not dry academic. Think rich, warm palette: deep parchment tones, aged-ink browns, and a bold accent color (burnt orange or deep red) for key callouts. Typography should blend editorial elegance (serif headers) with clean sans-serif body text. Subtle paper-texture backgrounds can reinforce the subject matter without competing with charts. White space matters — let the data breathe.
