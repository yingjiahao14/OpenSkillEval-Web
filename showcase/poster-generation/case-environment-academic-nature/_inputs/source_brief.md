# 300 Years of Sclerosponge Thermometry Shows Global Warming Has Exceeded 1.5 °C

## Overview

A 2024 study published in *Nature Climate Change* uses geochemical records preserved in the calcium-carbonate skeletons of sclerosponges to reconstruct sea-surface temperatures over the past ~300 years. The resulting record extends well before the earliest instrumental measurements, revealing that the commonly used 1850–1900 "pre-industrial" reference period already contained significant anthropogenic warming. When warming is measured against a true pre-industrial baseline (circa 1700–1790), total global temperature increase is approximately 0.5 °C greater than conventional estimates — meaning the 1.5 °C Paris Agreement threshold has already been surpassed.

## Why Sclerosponges?

Sclerosponges are long-lived marine organisms found in tropical and subtropical oceans. As they grow, strontium-to-calcium (Sr/Ca) ratios in their aragonite skeletons faithfully track ambient water temperature. Key advantages include:

- **Continuous growth** over centuries, yielding unbroken annual-resolution records
- **Tropical ocean sensitivity** — the Caribbean sites capture large-scale SST variability
- **Independence from instrumental networks**, which only begin around 1850

## Key Temperature Estimates

| Baseline Period | Estimated Warming to ~2020 | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1850–1900 (IPCC standard) | ~1.2 °C | HadCRUT5 / IPCC AR6 |
| 1700–1790 (true pre-industrial) | ~1.7 °C | Sclerosponge reconstruction |
| Difference (hidden warming) | ~0.5 °C | This study |

The table above illustrates the central finding: roughly half a degree of warming occurred before the conventional baseline even begins, meaning the instrumental record systematically underestimates total anthropogenic warming.

## 300-Year Temperature Timeline — Key Milestones

| Period | Observation |
|---|---|
| 1700–1790 | Stable pre-industrial temperatures; defined as the true baseline |
| 1790–1850 | Onset of detectable warming linked to early industrialisation |
| 1850–1900 | Already ~0.5 °C above the 1700–1790 mean |
| 1900–1960 | Gradual warming continues; volcanic cooling episodes visible |
| 1960–2000 | Accelerating warming trend; sclerosponge and instrumental records converge |
| 2000–present | Warming exceeds 1.5 °C relative to the true pre-industrial baseline |

## Implications for Climate Policy

- The Paris Agreement's 1.5 °C target is framed relative to "pre-industrial levels," but the IPCC operationally uses 1850–1900 — a period that was not truly pre-industrial.
- Adopting the sclerosponge-informed baseline reduces the remaining carbon budget for 1.5 °C to effectively zero.
- Even the 2.0 °C guardrail is closer than previously estimated, with only ~0.3 °C of additional warming available rather than ~0.8 °C.
- These findings underscore the urgency of rapid emissions reductions and transparent communication about how baselines affect perceived climate targets.

## Data & Methods Summary

Sclerosponge specimens were collected from Caribbean reef environments at depths of 33–91 m. Sr/Ca ratios were measured via ICP-OES and calibrated against co-located instrumental SST records from 1960 onward (r² > 0.9). The reconstruction was validated against HadCRUT5 and NOAA ERSSTv5 datasets over their overlapping intervals, showing excellent agreement during the instrumental era and divergence only before ~1850, precisely where the hidden warming resides.
