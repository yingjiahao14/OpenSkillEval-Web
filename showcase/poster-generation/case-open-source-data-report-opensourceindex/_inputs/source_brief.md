# Open Source Contributor Index — March 2026

## What Is OSCI?

The Open Source Contributor Index (OSCI) ranks commercial organizations worldwide by the volume of their employees' year-to-date open source contributions on GitHub. As of March 2026, OSCI tracks **239 organizations** across the global technology landscape, providing a transparent, data-driven view of corporate engagement in open source.

Notably, AMD climbed two spots to rank 4th, reflecting significant month-over-month growth.

## Growth Momentum

Every organization in the top 10 showed positive month-over-month growth in both active contributors and total community. The combined active contributors across the top 10 reached **2,721**, with a combined monthly increase of **595 active contributors**. Google alone added 128 new active contributors, the largest single-organization increase. IBM showed strong relative growth, adding 44 active contributors (a roughly 46% increase) and 133 community members.

## Programming Language Distribution

The contributions tracked by OSCI span a diverse set of programming languages. The top six languages by share are:

- **Python** — 20.3%
- **C++** — 14.3%
- **Go** — 11.6%
- **C** — 10.9%
- **TypeScript** — 10.5%
- **Java** — 4.4%

Python leads decisively, reflecting its dominance in data science, AI/ML, and cloud-native tooling. Systems-level languages (C++ and C) together account for over 25% of contributions, underscoring the importance of infrastructure and performance-critical projects in the open source ecosystem.

## License Usage

The most commonly used open source licenses across tracked repositories are:

1. **Apache-2.0** — the most prevalent license
2. **MIT**
3. **GPL-2.0**
4. **Other** — various permissive and copyleft licenses

The dominance of Apache-2.0 and MIT reflects the enterprise-friendly, permissive licensing trend that enables broad commercial adoption of open source software.

## Why It Matters

OSCI provides a transparent benchmark for corporate open source engagement. As Amanda Brock, CEO of Open UK, notes: understanding the value open source software generates in economies and society is critical, and tools like OSCI help gather the necessary base data. The index itself is open source (licensed under GPL v3.0), ensuring transparent criteria and community-driven improvement.
