# Instruction: Hunger Crisis Advocacy Poster

## Overview

Create a bold, emotionally compelling advocacy poster about the hunger crisis in America. This poster is for **public exhibition** — think large-format display where passersby need to be stopped in their tracks. The core message: **48 million Americans face hunger, but collective action is making a massive difference — and viewers can help.**

The audience is the general public. The poster must balance urgency with warmth — it should feel like a rallying cry, not a guilt trip.

## Key Priorities

- **The "48 million" and "5.7 billion meals" statistics should be the visual anchors.** These two numbers tell the story of the problem and the response. Make them impossible to miss.
- **The "$1 = 10 meals" metric is the conversion moment** — it bridges awareness to action. Give it prominent, accessible placement near the calls to action.
- The four calls to action (donate, advocate, fundraise, volunteer) should feel inviting and actionable, not buried.
- The title — *"Hunger Doesn't Wait — Together We Can End It"* — should lead with confidence and urgency.

## Visual & Style Guidance

- **Color palette:** Deep warm tones — think rich oranges, warm reds, and earthy golds against a dark or muted background. These evoke both urgency and human warmth.
- **Mood:** Bold typography, generous whitespace around key stats, and a clear visual hierarchy. The poster should breathe despite its density of information.
- **Layout feel:** Portrait orientation. Think editorial impact — large headline up top, statistics as visual callouts (not buried in paragraphs), and a clear action section anchoring the bottom.
- Avoid clutter. Let the numbers speak. Use typographic scale and color contrast to guide the eye from crisis → impact → action.
