# The State of Global Education: Progress, Gaps, and Inequality

A data-driven comparison of how education access, quality, and equity have evolved worldwide — and where critical challenges remain.

## Two Centuries of Educational Expansion

In the early 1800s, fewer than 1 in 5 adults worldwide had received any formal education. Education was a luxury reserved for a small elite. Over the past two centuries, this ratio has reversed dramatically. Today, more than 4 in 5 adults (aged 15+) have received some formal primary, secondary, or tertiary education. Literacy rates have followed a similar trajectory: two centuries ago, very few could read and write; now, the vast majority of adults possess basic literacy skills.

## The Learning Crisis: Being in School ≠ Learning

International statistics often track enrollment and attendance as markers of progress. However, being enrolled does not guarantee quality learning. In many lower-income countries, fewer than 20% of children achieve minimum reading proficiency by the end of primary school — meaning they cannot identify key ideas in a text or connect them to their own experiences. The data reveals a stark learning crisis hidden behind enrollment numbers.

## 244 Million Children Still Out of School

Despite enormous progress, approximately 244 million children of official school age were not enrolled in primary or secondary education in 2023. A major driver is secondary-level dropout: many children who complete primary school do not continue. The following table summarizes the scale of the challenge:

| Indicator | Value (2023) |
|---|---|
| Children out of school (all levels) | ~244 million |
| Primary-age children out of school | ~60 million |
| Secondary-age adolescents out of school | ~184 million |

## Learning-Adjusted Years of Schooling: A World Divided

Researchers combine the quantity and quality of education into a single metric called "learning-adjusted years of schooling." The disparities are enormous:

| Country Group | Learning-Adjusted Years of Schooling (2020) |
|---|---|
| Poorest countries (parts of Sub-Saharan Africa) | < 3 years |
| South Asia & Sub-Saharan Africa average | < 7 years |
| High-income countries | 10+ years |

Children in the world's poorest nations receive less than a third of the quality schooling available to their peers in wealthy countries. Most of the world's children live in South Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa, where the average remains below 7 years.

## Closing the Gender Gap

Historically, boys were far more likely to attend school than girls. Today, the global gender gap in enrollment has largely closed across primary and secondary education. At the tertiary level, young women are now more likely to be enrolled than young men.

| Education Level | Gender Gap Status (2023) |
|---|---|
| Primary | Nearly closed globally |
| Secondary | Nearly closed globally |
| Tertiary | Women now exceed men in enrollment |

However, significant exceptions persist. In countries like Afghanistan, girls remain far less likely to attend school at any level.

## Key Takeaway

The world has achieved remarkable progress — but the job is far from done. Hundreds of millions of children are either out of school or learning very little while in it. Closing the gap between enrollment and actual learning, and between rich and poor nations, is one of the most pressing challenges of our time.
