## Poster Brief: COVID-19 Emergency Response Process Flow

### Overview

This poster visualizes the WHO-guided COVID-19 emergency response as a clear, sequential process flow — from initial surveillance through long-term recovery. It's intended for **public exhibition** (A1 portrait format), so it must be legible from a distance, visually compelling, and easy to follow for a general audience, including policymakers, health professionals, and informed citizens.

### Key Message

COVID-19 response is not ad hoc — it follows a structured, five-phase process backed by unprecedented global coordination. The poster should make this systematic progression feel both authoritative and accessible.

### Priorities

- **The five-phase flow is the centerpiece.** The viewer's eye should naturally travel through Phases 1–5 in a clear top-to-bottom or cascading sequence. Use strong visual connectors (arrows, numbered nodes, or a timeline spine) so the progression is unmistakable.
- **The "194 WHO Member States Coordinated" and "~1.9 billion COVAX doses" metrics** should be the most visually dominant data points — they convey the extraordinary scale of coordination. Weave the remaining metrics in as supporting evidence without cluttering the flow.
- **Phase 4 (Public Health & Social Measures)** resonates most with general audiences — give it enough visual weight that viewers connect personally with testing, contact tracing, and vaccination.

### Visual & Style Guidance

- **Palette:** WHO blue as the primary anchor, complemented by clean whites, soft grays, and a warm accent (amber or teal) for highlights and call-outs. Avoid overly clinical coldness — keep it professional yet engaging.
- **Mood:** Authoritative but hopeful. The poster should feel like a confident roadmap, not a crisis alarm.
- **Typography:** Bold, modern sans-serif headings; highly readable body text. Phase titles should be prominent.
- **Layout feel:** Structured and orderly — grid-aligned with generous whitespace. Think infographic clarity, not dense report.
