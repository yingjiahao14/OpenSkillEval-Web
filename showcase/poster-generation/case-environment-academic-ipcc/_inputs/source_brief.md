# Climate Change 2023: Key Findings from the IPCC Sixth Assessment Synthesis Report

## Overview

The IPCC Sixth Assessment Report (AR6) Synthesis Report, published in 2023 by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change, integrates findings from three Working Group contributions and three Special Reports produced during the AR6 cycle. It represents the most comprehensive scientific assessment of climate change to date, covering the physical science basis, impacts and adaptation, and mitigation. The report underscores that human-caused climate change is already affecting every region on Earth, with widespread and intensifying consequences.

## Observed Changes and Current State

Global surface temperature rose by approximately **1.1°C** between the period 2011–2020 relative to the pre-industrial baseline of 1850–1900. Atmospheric CO₂ concentrations reached **410 ppm in 2019**, the highest level in at least 2 million years. Total global greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions stood at approximately **59 GtCO₂-equivalent in 2019**, with fossil fuel combustion and land-use change as the dominant sources.

| Indicator | Observed Value | Reference Period |
|---|---|---|
| Global surface temperature rise | +1.1°C | 2011–2020 vs. 1850–1900 |
| Atmospheric CO₂ | 410 ppm | 2019 |
| Global GHG emissions | ~59 GtCO₂e | 2019 |
| Sea level rise | ~0.20 m | 1901–2018 |
| Arctic sea ice (September) decline | ~40% area loss | 1979–2020 |

Every region of the world has experienced increases in the frequency and intensity of extreme weather events, including heatwaves, heavy precipitation, droughts, and tropical cyclones, with clear attribution to human influence.

## Future Projections Under Different Scenarios

The AR6 uses Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSPs) to project future climate outcomes. Under the very low emissions scenario (SSP1-1.9), global warming is likely to reach 1.5°C in the near term before declining slightly by 2100. Under the very high emissions scenario (SSP5-8.5), warming could exceed **4.4°C by 2100**.

| Scenario | Projected Warming by 2100 (best estimate) | Description |
|---|---|---|
| SSP1-1.9 | ~1.4°C | Very low emissions, sustainability focus |
| SSP1-2.6 | ~1.8°C | Low emissions |
| SSP2-4.5 | ~2.7°C | Intermediate emissions |
| SSP3-7.0 | ~3.6°C | High emissions |
| SSP5-8.5 | ~4.4°C | Very high emissions, fossil-fuel intensive |

Sea level is projected to rise by 0.28–0.55 m under SSP1-1.9 and 0.63–1.01 m under SSP5-8.5 by 2100, with continued rise for centuries to millennia regardless of pathway.

## Risks, Vulnerabilities, and Cascading Impacts

Approximately **3.3 to 3.6 billion people** live in contexts highly vulnerable to climate change. Climate hazards increasingly interact with non-climatic stressors—poverty, inequality, ecosystem degradation—to produce cascading and compounding risks. Biodiversity loss accelerates beyond 1.5°C, with up to 14% of terrestrial species facing very high extinction risk at 1.5°C of warming, rising sharply at higher levels.

- **Food security**: Crop yield growth rates have slowed in many regions, and fisheries productivity is declining in tropical zones.
- **Water stress**: Over half the world's population currently experiences severe water scarcity for at least one month per year.
- **Health**: Heat-related mortality and vector-borne disease ranges are expanding.

## Mitigation and Adaptation Pathways

To limit warming to **1.5°C with no or limited overshoot**, global GHG emissions must be reduced by **43% below 2019 levels by 2030** and reach **net zero CO₂ by around 2050**. For a 2°C target, emissions must fall by 27% by 2030 and reach net zero CO₂ by around 2070.

Key mitigation levers include rapid decarbonization of energy systems, electrification of transport, demand-side efficiency improvements, and protection and restoration of carbon sinks. The remaining carbon budget from the start of 2020 for a 50% chance of limiting warming to 1.5°C is approximately **500 GtCO₂**.

## The Narrowing Window for Action

The synthesis report emphasizes that the choices made in this decade will determine whether warming can be held to 1.5°C or 2°C. Delayed action increases costs, locks in high-carbon infrastructure, and reduces feasibility of limiting warming. Climate finance flows remain insufficient—falling short of needs by a factor of 3 to 6 for adaptation alone. Immediate, deep, and sustained emissions reductions across all sectors are essential to secure a liveable and sustainable future for all.

*Source: IPCC, 2023. AR6 Synthesis Report: Climate Change 2023. Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change.*
